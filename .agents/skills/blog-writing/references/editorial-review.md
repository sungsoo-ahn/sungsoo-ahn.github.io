# Editorial review

Read for substantial prose revisions or requests to humanize or remove slop.
Adapted from Anti-Slop Writing and Humanizer; revisions and licenses are in the
[source register](../../../third-party/sources.md).

## Diagnose the passage

Read the paragraph and its neighbors before changing an isolated phrase.
Identify its claim, supporting detail, and relationship to the next paragraph.
A passage can be grammatically clear yet fail to explain why its ideas follow.

- Replace inflated importance with the supported consequence or mechanism.
- Repair a list-like argument by making cause, dependency, contrast, inference,
  or a change of scale explicit where the relationship is real.
- Prefer a precise verb to abstract praise. Preserve technical uses such as
  robust estimation, attention gates, or an energy landscape.
- Watch repeated claim–explanation–summary paragraphs, symmetrical section
  lengths, decorative contrasts, and headings restated in the first sentence.
- Remove chatbot greetings, process reports, fake-candid hooks, and generic
  optimistic endings from the article itself.

These are diagnostic cues, not evidence of who wrote a passage. A colon, dash,
three-item list, formal word, or passive clause is not itself a defect. Keep
earned compression and deliberate repetition. Do not mechanically swap synonyms.

## Preserve voice and meaning

Use the author's supplied sample, or the site's house references when a voice
comparison is needed. Retain distinctive supported details, measured judgment,
and useful variations in sentence length.

Protect negation, quantifiers, causal direction, numerical values, units,
attribution, and uncertainty. Do not turn an association into a causal result
or a heuristic into a proof. Keep necessary limitations even when they make
the prose less punchy. Never change equations or source quotations as a
side effect of polishing the sentences around them.

When specificity needs a missing fact, inspect the available source. If the
fact remains unavailable, retain an accurate narrower sentence or flag the
gap. Ask the author only if the gap prevents the requested result.

## One edit and one comparison

1. Revise around the passage's meaning rather than replacing watched words.
2. Read the result aloud for rhythm and continuity.
3. Compare with the original for added/lost claims, numbers, references,
   conditions, and changes of certainty.
4. Correct the remaining substantive or stylistic defects and finish.

For a review request, explain the most consequential issues with concrete
examples. For a rewrite request, deliver the rewrite without a compulsory
scorecard or intermediate draft. No detector score or claim of human authorship
is a completion criterion.
