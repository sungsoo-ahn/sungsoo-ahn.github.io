## Proteins across sequence, structure, and dynamics

Proteins matter because one molecular class supports catalysis, transport, signaling, recognition, and therapy. Their functions are expressed at several scales: a one-dimensional amino-acid sequence folds into a three-dimensional structure, often changes conformation, and interacts with other proteins, nucleic acids, ions, and small molecules.

Recent breakthroughs in protein science have advanced several connected tasks: structure prediction from sequence, language models for protein sequences, inverse folding, and generative design of new structures.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s04-image5.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Major milestones in protein structure prediction, language modeling, inverse folding, and design." caption="Modern protein design combines progress in predicting structures, representing sequences, and generating both sequences and backbones. The long-term goal is programmable design: specify a desired protein function, use a computational model to propose a sequence, and obtain a molecule that realizes the intended structure and behavior. The difficult step is not merely generating a plausible amino-acid string. A designed sequence must fold, remain stable in the relevant environment, and express the requested function.

A protein is a linear polymer drawn from twenty standard amino acids. The sequence fixes the order of residues, but the physical object is three-dimensional.

### One structure is not the whole protein

Proteins fluctuate over conformational space and often occupy several local minima on an energy landscape. Binding interfaces can reorganize, and large machines such as ATP-binding cassette transporters alternate between functional conformations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s09-image12.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Several conformational states of an ATP-binding cassette transporter." caption="Functional protein motion can connect distinct macroscopic conformations, as illustrated by an ABC transporter cycle. Traditional modeling attacks this problem from two directions. Homology modeling transfers structure from related proteins; molecular dynamics simulates physical motion from an initial structure. The first depends on known templates, while the second is computationally expensive and limited by sampling and force-field accuracy.

Modern learning methods are powered by large sequence and structure databases. UniProt supplies protein sequences and annotations; the Protein Data Bank supplies experimentally determined structures. The model can therefore learn statistical regularities connecting sequence, evolution, and geometry.

### A neural-network–solver hybrid

Predicting torsions independently can accumulate error along the chain. AlphaFold1 therefore converts the network's distance and angle distributions into a differentiable potential and optimizes coordinates against that potential.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s27-image34.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Torsion-angle errors accumulating along a reconstructed protein chain." caption="Small local torsion errors can create large global displacement when coordinates are reconstructed sequentially. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 27." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s27-image35.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold1 neural network producing structural potentials consumed by gradient descent." caption="AlphaFold1 is a model-and-solver system: learned geometric distributions define an energy optimized over structure. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 27." %}

If the network predicts a probability $$p_{ij}(d)$$ for the distance between residues $$i$$ and $$j$$, it can be turned into a potential such as $$-\log p_{ij}(d_{ij}(X))$$. Summing pair, torsion, and structural terms gives an objective over coordinates $$X$$, and gradient descent searches for a compatible structure.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s28-image36.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Residue-pair distance distribution converted into a potential for coordinate optimization." caption="A predicted distance distribution becomes a differentiable structural potential evaluated at candidate coordinates. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 28." %}

This is different from the later coordinate-wise predictors: the neural network supplies potentials, while a separate optimizer supplies the final coordinates.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s29-image37.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Comparison between optimizing learned structural potentials and directly predicting coordinates." caption="AlphaFold1 delegates coordinate construction to optimization, whereas AlphaFold2 and 3 make coordinate prediction part of the learned model. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 29." %}

Physics-derived restraints are added to prevent impossible steric clashes and other local violations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s30-image38.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Steric clash in a protein and physics constraints used to correct it." caption="Learned distance potentials are supplemented by physical restraints that discourage atom overlap. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 30." %}

At CASP13, AlphaFold1 produced a large improvement over other structure-prediction systems, establishing learned residue-pair potentials as a powerful route to folding.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s31-image39.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="CASP13 comparison showing AlphaFold1 performance relative to other groups." caption="AlphaFold1's CASP13 result demonstrated the strength of learned geometric potentials combined with structural optimization. ## AlphaFold2: end-to-end coordinate prediction

### The complete model

The AlphaFold2 pipeline maintains three interacting representations: an MSA representation indexed by sequence and residue, a pair representation indexed by residue pairs, and a three-dimensional structure represented by residue frames. The Evoformer repeatedly exchanges information between the MSA and pair tracks; the structure module converts the resulting single and pair features into coordinates.

Finally, an outer-product mean transfers correlated MSA features into the pair tensor. For each residue pair, outer products of their MSA embeddings are averaged over homologous sequences and projected into the pair channel.

### Structure module and invariant point attention

The structure module consumes the refined single-residue and pair representations. It iteratively updates residue frames and torsion angles, then places backbone and side-chain atoms.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s48-image63.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold2 structure module from single and pair features to residue frames and atom coordinates." caption="The structure module converts abstract sequence and pair states into a full coordinate model. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 48." %}

Invariant point attention attaches learned query, key, and value points to each residue's local frame. Distances between transformed query and key points are invariant to a common rigid transformation of the full input, while the resulting frame updates are equivariant.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s49-image64.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Invariant point attention between query and key points attached to residue frames." caption="IPA performs attention using points expressed in residue-local frames, coupling learned features to three-dimensional geometry. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 49." %}

Repeated IPA and transition blocks refine both the learned residue state and its rigid transformation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s50-image65.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Iterative residue-frame update inside the AlphaFold2 structure module." caption="Structure refinement alternates geometric attention, feature transitions, and rigid-frame updates. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 50." %}

### Local-frame training, equivariance, and recycling

AlphaFold2 trains coordinates with frame-aligned point error. Predicted and true atoms are compared in the local coordinate frame of each residue, so a common rigid transformation does not change the loss.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s51-image66.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Frame-aligned point error comparing predicted and true atoms in residue-local coordinate systems." caption="FAPE evaluates coordinate error after expressing atoms in local residue frames. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 51." %}

The overall model is SE(3)-equivariant: rotating and translating the input frames rotates and translates the predicted structure in the same way.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s52-image67.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein structure prediction before and after a common SE(3) transformation." caption="AlphaFold2's frame-based structure module respects global rotations and translations of its geometric input. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 52." %}

Recycling passes predicted coordinates and internal representations back through the network. The same parameters then revise a previous prediction, turning one forward architecture into an iterative refinement process.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s53-image68.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold2 recycling loop feeding predicted structure and representations back into the model." caption="Recycling lets AlphaFold2 repeatedly reconcile its sequence, pair, and coordinate representations. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 53." %}

At CASP14, AlphaFold2 achieved near-experimental accuracy for many targets and separated sharply from the rest of the field.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s54-image69.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="CASP14 scores and structural examples comparing AlphaFold2 with other prediction groups." caption="AlphaFold2's CASP14 performance established end-to-end learned coordinate prediction as a new reference point. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 54." %}

AlphaFold-Multimer extends the same approach from single chains to protein complexes, where chain identity, pairing, and interfacial geometry become additional modeling problems.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s55-image70.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold-Multimer predictions and performance across protein complexes." caption="AlphaFold-Multimer generalizes residue and pair reasoning to assemblies containing several protein chains. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 55." %}

## AlphaFold3: all-atom diffusion for biomolecular interactions

AlphaFold3 broadens the representation again. It predicts all-atom structures containing proteins, nucleic acids, ligands, ions, and modified residues. Its structure module is generative, using diffusion rather than deterministic equivariant frame updates.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s56-image71.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold3 overview across proteins, nucleic acids, ligands, ions, and modified residues." caption="AlphaFold3 combines all-atom output, multiple biomolecular modalities, a Pairformer, and a diffusion structure module. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 56." %}

### Equivalent structures as a generative target

Rigidly rotated or translated copies describe the same complex. AlphaFold3 treats structure prediction as learning a distribution over equivalent coordinate targets and samples coordinates through a denoising process.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s57-image72.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Comparison between deterministic AlphaFold2 coordinate prediction and AlphaFold3 generative diffusion." caption="AlphaFold3 replaces the deterministic structure module with a generative coordinate model that can represent equivalent targets. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 57." %}

This change supports protein--ligand co-structure prediction from protein and ligand sequences.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s58-image73.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold3 protein-ligand input representation and predicted complex structure." caption="Protein--ligand prediction requires one model to place polymer residues and small-molecule atoms in a shared complex. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 58." %}

### Atom attention and the Pairformer

AlphaFold3 tokenizes heterogeneous biomolecular components while retaining atom-level detail. The atom-attention module aggregates local atomic context into token features and later expands token-level predictions back to atom coordinates.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s59-image74.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold3 atom-attention encoder and decoder around token-level representations." caption="Atom attention bridges detailed all-atom inputs with the more compact token representations used by the trunk. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 59." %}

The pair representation is initialized from token identities, relative positions, bond information, and recycled predictions.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s60-image75.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold3 construction of token-pair representations from sequence, bond, and recycled features." caption="AlphaFold3 pair features must describe relationships among heterogeneous tokens, including covalent connectivity. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 60." %}

AlphaFold3's Pairformer simplifies the AlphaFold2 trunk by removing the full MSA stack from the repeated block. It updates single-token and pair representations through attention and triangle operations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s61-image76.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold3 Pairformer block updating single-token and pair representations." caption="The Pairformer retains pair-triangle reasoning while centering the repeated trunk on single and pair token states. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 61." %}

### Diffusion structure module

The diffusion module starts from noisy atom coordinates and repeatedly denoises them while conditioning on the token and pair representations. Atom attention communicates between noisy local geometry and the trunk's chemical context.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s62-image77.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold3 diffusion module denoising all-atom coordinates conditioned on Pairformer features." caption="The AlphaFold3 structure module generates all-atom coordinates through conditional diffusion. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 62." %}

Training adds noise to experimentally observed complexes, conditions the denoiser on data augmentations and trunk representations, and compares the clean and predicted structures.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s63-image78.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold3 diffusion training pipeline with noisy coordinates, conditioning, and denoising loss." caption="Diffusion training teaches the structure module to reverse coordinate corruption across heterogeneous complexes. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 63." %}

Auxiliary distogram heads remain during training. They regularize the pair representation and are intended to provide lower-variance gradients early in optimization, even though the final coordinates come from diffusion.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s64-image79.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold3 auxiliary distogram heads attached to pair representations during training." caption="Auxiliary distance prediction stabilizes representation learning without replacing the all-atom diffusion output. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 64." %}

Inference uses an FK-steering correction to discourage steric clashes and other low-quality configurations during diffusion sampling.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-12/s65-image80.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold3 FK steering combining a diffusion proposal with a physical or confidence potential." caption="FK steering adjusts diffusion sampling with additional structural preferences such as clash avoidance. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein structure prediction lecture, slide 65." %}

Across the three generations, the changes align with broader trends. AlphaFold1 predicts invariant distributions and uses an external solver. AlphaFold2 predicts residue frames end to end with Evoformer and invariant point attention. AlphaFold3 predicts multimodal all-atom complexes with Pairformer-conditioned diffusion.

## What comes after structural saturation?

Today's models have already exploited a large fraction of the structural information available in the PDB and predicted-structure databases. The final lecture question is therefore about data and targets, not just architecture. Can learning incorporate more physical information from molecular dynamics? Can it use less curated, more direct experimental data? Can it predict thermodynamic quantities such as folding populations and kinetic quantities such as folding rates? Can the same modeling language extend cleanly to DNA, RNA, ligands, ions, and other modalities?

The sequence from AlphaFold1 to BioEmu shows why these questions cannot be reduced to "better structure prediction." The target has moved from invariant pair distributions, to residue frames, to multimodal all-atom samples, binding affinities, and conformational ensembles. Each step asks for a different kind of evidence. A single static coordinate set remains extraordinarily useful, but structure, dynamics, thermodynamics, kinetics, and function are not synonyms.
