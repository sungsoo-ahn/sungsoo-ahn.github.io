## Joint generation in three dimensions

A graph-first molecular generator emits atoms and bonds, leaving coordinates to a separate conformer model. Three-dimensional generation instead constructs atom types and coordinates jointly. Bonds can then be inferred from pairwise distances and chemical rules.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s03-image1.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Three-dimensional molecular structures appearing along a generative process that jointly predicts atom identities and coordinates." caption="Joint 3D generation constructs chemical identity and spatial arrangement in the same model. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 3." %}

For atom types $$Z=(z_1,\ldots,z_n)$$ and coordinates $$R=(r_1,\ldots,r_n)$$, the desired distribution must be unchanged by translating or rotating the whole structure:

$$
p(Z,QR+t)=p(Z,R).
$$

The generated coordinates should also be permutation-consistent: relabeling atoms changes the stored order, not the physical molecule.

### EDM: equivariant diffusion in Cartesian space

EDM applies diffusion directly to the 3D coordinates while using a rotation- and translation-equivariant denoising network.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s04-image2.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="EDM diffusion sequence from coordinate noise to three-dimensional molecular samples." caption="EDM denoises atom coordinates and identities through an equivariant reverse process. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 4." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s04-image3.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="EDM molecular generation samples and evaluation results." caption="The generated molecules are evaluated for validity, uniqueness, and geometric quality. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 4." %}

If a noisy input is rotated by $$Q$$, the predicted coordinate update must also rotate by $$Q$$. Translation is usually removed by centering the point cloud. The model therefore learns molecular shape without privileging a laboratory coordinate frame.

### GeoLDM: diffusion in a learned latent space

GeoLDM first maps a molecule to a latent representation containing invariant and equivariant tensors. Diffusion operates in that compressed latent space, and a decoder reconstructs atom types and coordinates.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s05-image5.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GeoLDM encoder, latent diffusion process, and molecular decoder." caption="GeoLDM separates geometric compression from diffusion by generating invariant and equivariant latent variables. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 5." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s05-image6.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GeoLDM latent-space molecular generation examples." caption="The latent generator must preserve enough chemical and geometric information for the decoder to reconstruct valid 3D molecules. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 5." %}

Compression can make diffusion easier, but the decoder becomes part of the generative contract. A stable latent sample is useful only if it maps back to a chemically and geometrically coherent structure.

### SymDiff: stochastic symmetrization

SymDiff does not require every reverse-process component to be intrinsically equivariant. It symmetrizes the transition kernel by randomly augmenting inputs with rigid transformations and correctly transforming the outputs.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s06-symdiff.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SymDiff stochastic symmetrization procedure and molecular generation results." caption="SymDiff enforces equivariance at the transition-kernel level through stochastic rigid-motion augmentation. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 6." %}

This moves symmetry from the architecture into the sampling procedure. The reverse chain is equivariant after symmetrization even when the underlying network is less constrained.

## Crystal generation and symmetry

Crystals add composition, lattice parameters, fractional coordinates, periodicity, and space-group symmetry. A generator must decide which of these variables to model directly and which constraints to enforce by construction.

### CD-VAE: composition, lattice, and structure

Crystal Diffusion Variational Autoencoder was one of the first models to generate both crystal composition and structure. It proposes composition and lattice parameters through a multilayer perceptron, then uses a diffusion decoder to reconstruct atomic coordinates.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s08-image16.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="CD-VAE architecture for encoding crystals and generating composition, lattice, and atomic structure." caption="CD-VAE combines a crystal latent variable with separate composition, lattice, and coordinate generation components. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 8." %}

The decomposition acknowledges that a crystal is not merely a larger molecule. Its cell is itself a generated geometric variable.

Later models explicitly condition crystal generation on a space group. DiffCSP++ works with Wyckoff positions, while related methods use asymmetric units and symmetry operations to build the full crystal.

## Molecular optimization

Generation now becomes goal-directed. We seek a distribution $$p_\theta(x)$$ over candidates $$x\in\mathcal X$$, evaluated by a reward $$R(x)$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s26-optimization-contract.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Molecular optimization pipeline from candidate generation through property scoring to selected molecules." caption="Molecular optimization couples a generator to a reward and searches for candidates with large scores. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 26." %}

The reward may be a learned predictor, docking calculation, physics simulator, or experiment. The optimization method determines how proposals depend on previous scores and how broadly it explores.

A genetic algorithm is the classical metaheuristic: maintain a population, select high-scoring molecules, mutate or recombine them, and repeat.

A Generative Flow Network is introduced as a framework for sampling objects from an energy-based distribution,

$$
p^*(x)=\frac{\exp R(x)}{Z},
\qquad
Z=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\exp R(x).

Unlike a maximizer, it aims to generate a diverse collection with probability proportional to reward.

## Generative Flow Networks

The final part develops GFlowNets in detail. The target is not the single molecule with the largest reward. It is a sampler whose terminal marginal approximates

$$
p^*(x)=\frac{\exp R(x)}{Z},
\qquad
Z=\sum_{x\in\mathcal X}\exp R(x).
$$

This formulation is related to hierarchical variational inference and to maximum-entropy reinforcement learning under suitable assumptions.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s40-full-content.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Definition of a GFlowNet as a sampler from an energy-based reward-proportional distribution." caption="A GFlowNet targets an entire reward-weighted distribution over constructed objects. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 40." %}

### Why molecular design needs diverse high-reward candidates

In real molecular design, the reward may come from a noisy physics simulator or docking calculation that takes roughly 30 seconds per molecule on a CPU. Many candidates can look good because the score is noisy, and genuinely useful molecules may occur as separate needles in a large chemical haystack. Only a small set can be sent to the wet lab.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s41-full-content.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Real-world molecular design motivation emphasizing noisy docking scores and several distinct high-reward modes." caption="The design objective is to recover several high-scoring molecular modes, not repeat variants of one apparent optimum. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 41." %}

The proxy is fundamentally imprecise. Unknown chemistry can invalidate its ranking, so broad coverage is a hedge against systematic error. The lecture calls this **systematic generalization**: from training data concentrated in one visible region, the generator should reach distinct high-value regions not already represented there.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s42-full-content.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Reward landscape with observed blue data and an unseen red high-reward region." caption="Imprecise proxies motivate casting a wide net across separated candidate modes and underrepresented regions. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 42." %}

This is the distinction between reward-proportional sampling and ordinary optimization. A maximizer can collapse onto one sharp peak. A GFlowNet allocates probability to every peak in proportion to its terminal reward.

### A forward policy constructs objects

Let $$s_0$$ be an initial empty state and let

$$
\tau=(s_0,s_1,\ldots,s_T)
$$

be a construction trajectory terminating at object $$x=s_T$$. A forward policy $$p_F(s_t\mid s_{t-1})$$ induces

$$
p_F(\tau)
=\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_F(s_t\mid s_{t-1}).
$$

If several trajectories terminate at the same object, the object's probability is their sum,

$$
p_F(x)=\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal T(x)}p_F(\tau).
$$

The goal is $$p_F(x)\approx p^*(x)$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s43-full-content.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GFlowNet state graph with forward construction trajectories, trajectory probability, object marginal, and target probability." caption="A forward policy assigns probability to paths; an object's marginal sums all paths that end there. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 43." %}

The marginalization is the hard part. Directly matching $$p_F(x)$$ requires enumerating every trajectory in $$\mathcal T(x)$$, which can be combinatorially large.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s44-full-content.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Equation showing object probability as a sum over all construction trajectories ending at the object." caption="GFlowNet training replaces an intractable object-level sum with local or trajectory-level consistency conditions. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 44." %}

### Introducing a backward policy

Define an auxiliary backward policy

$$
p_B(s_{t-1}\mid s_t)
$$

that reverses a construction trajectory. Begin from a terminal object sampled in proportion to its reward and walk backward toward the root. The unnormalized backward weight of a trajectory is

$$
f_B(\tau)
=\exp R(x)\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_B(s_{t-1}\mid s_t).
$$

Because the backward transition probabilities from a fixed terminal state sum to one over all reverse paths,

$$
\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal T(x)} f_B(\tau)=\exp R(x).
$$

Matching the forward and backward trajectory distributions therefore implies the desired terminal marginal.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s45-full-content.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Backward GFlowNet policy reversing a construction graph from reward-weighted terminal objects to the root." caption="The backward policy distributes each terminal reward over the trajectories that construct that object. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 45." %}

### A small reward-flow example

The lecture carries a finite graph through the construction. Treat the three terminal weights as

$$
w(x_1)=4,\qquad w(x_2)=2,\qquad w(x_3)=1.
$$

The normalizer is $$Z=7$$. A terminal reached by one path carries its full weight on that path. If $$x_2$$ is reached through two reverse paths and the backward policy assigns probability $$1/2$$ to each, its terminal weight 2 is divided into two path flows of 1.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s46-full-content.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Finite GFlowNet example with terminal rewards 4, 2, and 1 and a backward policy over construction paths." caption="Terminal reward is distributed backward across all construction histories according to the backward policy. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 46." %}

After the split, the four illustrated trajectory flows are $$4,1,1,1$$. Normalizing by 7 gives their sampling probabilities. Where paths share a prefix, their flows add: an intermediate state carrying trajectories of weights 4 and 1 has state flow 5 and sends probabilities $$4/5$$ and $$1/5$$ along its outgoing edges.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s47-full-content.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Completed finite GFlowNet example with backward splits and reward-proportional trajectory probabilities." caption="Local forward probabilities are ratios of outgoing path flow to the total flow through the current state. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 47." %}

The example shows why multiple paths are not a nuisance to delete. They are precisely what the flow equations must aggregate.

### From probabilities to unnormalized flows

We know the terminal weights $$\exp R(x)$$ but not the partition function $$Z$$. Introduce a trainable $$Z_\theta$$ and define

$$
f_F(\tau)
=Z_\theta\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_F(s_t\mid s_{t-1}),
$$

$$
f_B(\tau)
=\exp R(x)\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_B(s_{t-1}\mid s_t).
$$

These trajectory-wise unnormalized densities are called flows. Training seeks $$f_F(\tau)\approx f_B(\tau)$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s48-full-content.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Forward and backward trajectory-flow equations with trainable normalizer Z." caption="GFlowNet training matches unnormalized forward and reward-weighted backward path flows. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 48." %}

In the finite example, setting $$Z_\theta=7$$ converts the normalized forward probabilities back into the path flows $$4,1,1,1$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s49-full-content.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Finite GFlowNet example verifying matching forward and backward flows with normalizer seven." caption="At the correct normalizer and policies, forward and backward flow agree on every illustrated trajectory. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 49." %}

### Trajectory balance

Trajectory Balance directly matches the two flows with a squared log-ratio:

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{TB}}(\tau)
=
\left[
\log
\frac{
Z_\theta\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_F(s_t\mid s_{t-1})
}{
\exp R(x)\prod_{t=1}^{T}p_B(s_{t-1}\mid s_t)
}
\right]^2.
$$

For trajectories sampled into a training set or replay buffer $$\mathcal B$$, the parameters minimize the average TB loss. If it is zero for every valid trajectory, the induced terminal distribution matches the reward-proportional target.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s50-full-content.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Trajectory Balance objective for matching forward and backward GFlowNet path flows." caption="Trajectory Balance enforces one global consistency relation along each complete construction path. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 50." %}

The logarithm converts multiplicative path probabilities into sums and compares relative rather than absolute flow.

### Detailed balance

Detailed Balance replaces the complete-trajectory constraint with a local edge relation. Let $$F_\theta(s)$$ estimate the total flow through state $$s$$. For each transition $$s_{t-1}\to s_t$$,

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DB}}(s_{t-1},s_t)
=
\left[
\log
\frac{
F_\theta(s_{t-1})p_F(s_t\mid s_{t-1})
}{
F_\theta(s_t)p_B(s_{t-1}\mid s_t)
}
\right]^2.
$$

Boundary conditions set

$$
F_\theta(s_0)=Z_\theta,
\qquad
F_\theta(x)=\exp R(x)
$$

for terminal $$x$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s51-full-content.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Detailed Balance GFlowNet objective matching local flow through adjacent states." caption="Detailed Balance trains a Bellman-like local conservation relation instead of one complete-path equation. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 51." %}

In the finite graph, the state flows are sums of all path flows passing through each state. The root carries 7; an intermediate branch can carry 5; terminal flows equal their reward weights. Every edge matches incoming forward flow to the corresponding backward flow.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s52-full-content.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Finite GFlowNet example labeled with trajectory flows, state flows, and local detailed-balance equations." caption="The example verifies local flow conservation and the terminal reward boundary at each branch. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 52." %}

Trajectory Balance and Detailed Balance use the same target but assign credit differently. TB observes the product along a whole sampled path; DB trains individual transitions and an explicit state-flow estimator.

### The training loop

The overall algorithm repeats three steps:

1. Collect trajectories with the current forward policy.
2. Store the trajectories in a replay buffer.
3. Sample trajectories from the buffer and minimize a GFlowNet objective such as TB or DB.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s53-full-content.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GFlowNet training algorithm alternating trajectory collection, replay storage, and objective optimization." caption="GFlowNet learning alternates constructive sampling with off-policy training from accumulated trajectories. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol 3D generation and optimization lecture, slide 53." %}

The replay buffer matters in sparse-reward design because useful modes discovered earlier should remain available for training even if the current policy temporarily moves elsewhere.

The closing slide names reward and diversity as the key reported axes. Reward is measured by the average score of the top 100 samples. Diversity is measured through the number of distinct modes, defined by domain-specific structure or by the number of samples above a reward threshold.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-10/s55-full-content.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GFlowNet evaluation curves for number of discovered modes and average top-100 reward." caption="Molecular GFlowNets are evaluated by both high-scoring candidates and the number of distinct high-reward modes recovered. Those two metrics return to the lecture's original motivation. A generator that finds one excellent molecule but repeats its neighborhood has optimized without casting a wide net. A generator that samples broadly but never reaches useful reward has diversity without design value. GFlowNets target the reward-weighted distribution so that both properties arise from the same constructive policy.
