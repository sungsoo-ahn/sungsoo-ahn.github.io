## One-dimensional and two-dimensional benchmark datasets

Molecular property prediction begins with a deceptively simple supervised-learning interface: represent a molecule by a string or graph, pair it with a measured or computed label, and learn a function from one to the other. The difficulty is hidden in the benchmark. A collection decides which chemical space is visible, which labels count as ground truth, and which generalization problem is actually being measured.

### MoleculeNet

MoleculeNet was one of the first broad benchmarks for molecular machine learning. It collected tasks from quantum chemistry, physical chemistry, biophysics, and physiology, then supplied common featurization, splitting, and evaluation conventions. Its enduring contribution was not a single winning architecture. It made heterogeneous molecular tasks comparable enough that graph neural networks, fingerprints, and other representations could be tested on the same collection.

Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 3." %}

The table matters because a score cannot be interpreted without its denominator. Classification and regression tasks use different metrics; scaffold, random, and temporal splits ask different questions; experimental labels and quantum calculations carry different noise. “Performance on MoleculeNet” is therefore shorthand for a vector of results under a specified protocol, not a single universal measure of molecular understanding.

### Therapeutic Data Commons

Therapeutic Data Commons expands the benchmark idea from isolated molecular properties to the drug-development pipeline. Its tasks include small-molecule activity, ADME and toxicity, drug–target interactions, treatment response, and clinical outcomes. This changes the unit of prediction: depending on the task, the input may be a molecule, a protein, a disease, a cell line, a patient cohort, or a relation among them.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s04-image3.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Therapeutic Data Commons map of tasks and datasets across the therapeutic development pipeline." caption="Therapeutic Data Commons broadens molecular benchmarking into a multi-stage therapeutic-development setting. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 4." %}

This broader scope is useful, but it makes task definition even more important. A model that predicts an assay readout is not automatically a model of efficacy; a model trained on approved drugs is exposed to a heavily selected distribution; and a split over molecule–target pairs can leak information if near-identical molecules or homologous targets appear on both sides. The benchmark should tell us what was held out, not merely how many examples were held out.

### ToyMix, LargeMix, and UltraLarge

The next move is from many separate benchmarks to large multi-task corpora. ToyMix, LargeMix, and UltraLarge were proposed as increasingly large mixtures for training foundation models of molecular representations. Rather than optimize one network independently for every endpoint, the model shares an encoder across many assays and datasets and learns from their combined supervision.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s05-image5.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Overview of ToyMix, LargeMix, and UltraLarge molecular multitask data mixtures." caption="Large molecular mixtures turn property prediction into a multi-task representation-learning problem. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 5." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s05-image6.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Comparison table for molecular data mixtures and downstream evaluation tasks." caption="Increasing the number of molecules is only one scaling axis; label diversity and downstream coverage also change. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 5." %}

A mixture does not erase dataset boundaries. Labels can have different units, missingness patterns, assay technologies, and sampling biases. Shared training is attractive precisely because related tasks may transfer, but negative transfer is equally possible. The promise of a molecular foundation model therefore depends on whether the mixture exposes reusable chemical structure rather than simply a larger collection of incompatible columns.

## Three-dimensional molecular and materials datasets

The meaning of a data point changes once coordinates enter. A molecular graph may correspond to many conformers; a crystal is periodic rather than finite; an atomistic trajectory contributes a correlated sequence rather than independent structures; and forces are vector labels attached to every atom. The following datasets illustrate these different regimes in the order used in the lecture.

### QM9: equilibrium molecules and invariant targets

QM9 contains roughly 134,000 stable organic molecules with at most nine heavy atoms and 19 computed quantum properties per molecule. It became a standard benchmark because it is large enough for deep learning yet compact enough for controlled comparison.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s07-image7.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Three-dimensional embedding of QM9 molecules colored by a molecular property." caption="QM9 samples a broad but deliberately small-molecule region of chemical space. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 7." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s07-image8.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Table of the nineteen quantum-chemical target properties in QM9." caption="QM9 couples each equilibrium geometry to several scalar quantum targets. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 7." %}

All of these scalar targets are unchanged when a molecule is translated or rotated. If a model receives positions $$R=(r_1,\ldots,r_n)$$, its prediction should satisfy

$$
f(Z,QR+t)=f(Z,R)
$$

for a rotation $$Q$$ and translation $$t$$. This simple invariance requirement later motivates the architecture families used for 3D property prediction.

### MD17 and revised MD17: local trajectories with force labels

MD17 contains snapshots from *ab initio* molecular-dynamics trajectories of ten small organic molecules, including benzene and aspirin. Each snapshot provides atomic positions, total potential energy, and a force vector for every atom. Revised MD17 selects 100,000 configurations and recomputes energies and forces to remove inconsistencies in the original calculations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s08-image9.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="MD17 molecular structures together with energy and force labels along molecular dynamics trajectories." caption="MD17 changes the unit of supervision from one equilibrium molecule to many correlated configurations around a molecule's trajectory. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 8." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s08-image10.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Aspirin molecular structure from the MD17 collection." caption="A single MD17 system contributes many geometries of the same molecular graph. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 8." %}

For an energy model $$E_\theta(Z,R)$$, conservative forces are obtained by differentiation,

$$
F_i=-\nabla_{r_i}E_\theta(Z,R).
$$

The energy is invariant, while the forces rotate as vectors. MD17 is thus not merely a larger QM9: it asks whether a model can resolve the local shape of a potential-energy surface accurately enough to reproduce derivatives.

### MPtraj and Alexandria: broad chemical coverage and relaxation paths

MPtraj contains about 1.58 million three-dimensional crystal structures drawn from the Materials Project and spans 89 elements. Its purpose is breadth: a potential trained on many inorganic bonding environments may transfer beyond the narrow chemistry of one molecule or material family.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s09-image11.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="MPtraj dataset summary and coverage of materials-project structures." caption="MPtraj draws atomistic training structures from a broad region of inorganic materials space. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 9." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s09-image12.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Periodic-table coverage and dataset statistics for MPtraj." caption="Elemental breadth is one ingredient of a universal interatomic potential. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 9." %}

Alexandria is an open repository of millions of quantum-chemistry calculations for molecules and materials. A particularly useful feature is that it stores intermediate structures from relaxation, including slightly off-equilibrium coordinates and their forces, rather than only final minima.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s10-image13.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Alexandria database logo and scope." caption="Alexandria includes structures encountered along relaxation paths as well as relaxed endpoints. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 10." %}

That distinction is crucial for simulation. A model trained only at minima may interpolate equilibrium energies well while seeing almost no restoring directions. Relaxation frames populate the neighborhoods through which an optimizer or molecular-dynamics integrator actually moves.

### Open Catalyst: adsorption and surface relaxation

The Open Catalyst datasets move from bulk structures to adsorbates on catalytic surfaces. OC20 contains 1,281,040 DFT relaxation trajectories, amounting to roughly 264 million energy-and-force calculations. Its central tasks include structure-to-energy-and-forces (S2EF) and initial-structure-to-relaxed-energy (IS2RE).

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s11-image14.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Open Catalyst dataset overview with adsorbates, surfaces, and chemical coverage." caption="Open Catalyst represents adsorption as a coupled adsorbate–surface problem rather than an isolated molecule. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 11." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s11-image15.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Atomic surface with an adsorbate from the Open Catalyst dataset." caption="A catalytic configuration includes a periodic surface, an attached adsorbate, and the relaxation path between initial and final states. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 11." %}

S2EF asks for the energy and atomic forces at a supplied configuration. IS2RE asks for the final relaxed energy from the initial configuration, thereby folding a whole relaxation trajectory into one prediction. The latter can be useful for screening, but it also asks a network to infer the result of an optimization it does not explicitly execute.

OC22 extends the effort to oxide electrocatalysts. It adds about 62,000 DFT relaxation trajectories and 9.85 million evaluated structures, with total-energy targets across multiple states rather than only adsorption energies. The intention is to encourage a model of more general surface physics instead of a narrow correction for one binding-energy definition.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s12-image16.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="OC22 dataset coverage over oxide electrocatalyst compositions and surface systems." caption="OC22 focuses the Open Catalyst setting on oxide electrocatalysis and a broader collection of total-energy states. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 12." %}

### Phonons as a structured target

A phonon is a quantized vibrational mode of a solid. Low-frequency modes correspond to gentle collective motion; higher-frequency modes correspond to faster atomic vibrations. Their spectra affect thermal conductivity, dynamical stability, and other material properties. The MDR Phonon Benchmark uses computed phonon spectra for roughly 10,000 materials.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s17-image23.gif" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Animated atomic displacement pattern for a phonon mode in a crystal." caption="A phonon target describes a coordinated vibration of atoms in a periodic solid, not merely one scalar attached to a structure. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 17." %}

This benchmark closes the dataset survey with an important reminder: “3D property prediction” includes scalar energies, vector forces, tensor stresses, spectra, and trajectories. The output representation is part of the scientific problem.

## Learning molecular properties from one-dimensional and two-dimensional inputs

Molecular graphs became the main playground for developing graph neural networks because they expose discrete connectivity while avoiding the cost and ambiguity of generating coordinates. The open question in the lecture is whether this success can be scaled into a foundation model for molecules.

### Pretraining a graph encoder

An early strategy separated node-level and graph-level pretraining. Node-level objectives mask atom attributes or predict subgraph context. Graph-level pretraining uses supervised molecular labels, for example from ChEMBL, to force a pooled representation to retain useful chemistry.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s20-image27.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Node-level and graph-level strategies for pretraining graph neural networks." caption="Pretraining can supervise local atom representations, whole-molecule representations, or both. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 20." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s20-image29.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Downstream MoleculeNet results for pretrained graph neural networks." caption="The original pretraining study reported consistent improvements across several MoleculeNet tasks. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 20." %}

The objective decides what information can be discarded. Masked-atom prediction rewards local chemical regularities. A graph-level assay objective rewards distinctions useful to those assays. Neither automatically learns three-dimensional conformational effects.

3D-InfoMax uses available 3D structures as self-supervision for a 2D graph encoder. The idea resembles distillation: a 3D network sees coordinates, a 2D network sees connectivity, and a contrastive objective encourages the two representations of the same molecule to agree.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s21-image30.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="3D-InfoMax contrastive learning between 2D graph and 3D molecular encoders." caption="3D-InfoMax transfers signals available in a three-dimensional view into a two-dimensional molecular encoder. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 21." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s21-image31.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Molecular examples illustrating 2D graphs and alternative 3D conformations." caption="A molecular graph is compatible with multiple conformers, so cross-view agreement cannot make every 3D detail recoverable from 2D alone. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 21." %}

The limitation is structural. If two conformers share the same graph but have different target values, a deterministic 2D encoder cannot know which conformer was intended. Pretraining can teach the *distribution* of plausible geometry associated with a graph; it cannot recover missing instance-specific coordinates.

### A benchmarking crisis

Computational drug-discovery benchmarks remain hard to compare. Datasets and protocols are scattered across papers, split conventions differ, and tuning budgets are rarely identical. A careful study found that many proposed methods underperformed the ECFP4 molecular fingerprint on MoleculeNet tasks.

This is not an argument that graphs are useless. It is an argument that representation learning must earn its complexity against strong fixed features, especially when labels are scarce. The benefit of scale can only be established when the baseline, split, and hyperparameter budget scale with comparable care.

### Multimodal supervision and scale

CLAMP trains a molecular encoder and an assay-text encoder contrastively on ChEMBL bioactivity data. The molecular representation concatenates ECFP and RDKit fingerprints; the assay description is encoded with latent Dirichlet allocation. A positive pair joins an active molecule to the description of its assay, while a negative pair joins a non-active molecule.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s23-image36.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="CLAMP multimodal contrastive architecture connecting molecules and assay descriptions." caption="CLAMP makes the assay itself part of the input, turning many endpoints into one molecule–text compatibility problem. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 23." %}

This formulation can generalize to a new assay description without allocating a new fixed output head. It also makes clear that activity is relational: the same compound can be active in one assay and inactive in another.

Do GNNs benefit from scale? Experiments varying depth, width, molecule count, label count, and pretraining diversity show that molecular graph models can exhibit scaling behavior. LargeMix and phenomic data provide the heterogeneous supervision needed to test those axes.

## Predicting three-dimensional structure from graphs and compositions

A two-dimensional molecular graph does not specify one unique conformation. Structure prediction therefore changes the task from regression to conditional generation: sample stable coordinates given a graph, protein context, or crystal composition.

### Molecular conformers

Conformer generation predicts plausible three-dimensional arrangements of a molecular graph. These coordinates can support stability calculations and molecular-interaction models, and the same basic problem extends toward protein and crystal structures.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s32-image56.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Molecular graph with several possible three-dimensional conformers." caption="One graph can correspond to several stable conformers, so structure prediction should represent a distribution. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 32." %}

GeoDiff takes the direct Cartesian approach: train an equivariant diffusion model that perturbs and denoises atomic coordinates. If $$R_t$$ is a noised geometry, the score or denoising field must rotate consistently with $$R_t$$ so that the generated distribution is independent of the coordinate frame.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s33-image57.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GeoDiff diffusion process for molecular conformer generation." caption="GeoDiff applies equivariant diffusion directly to atomic coordinates of small molecules. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 33." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s33-image58.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Generated molecular conformers compared with reference structures." caption="Conformer generation is evaluated by how well generated structures cover and match reference conformations. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 33." %}

Cartesian coordinates are redundant for many molecules because bond lengths and angles are relatively constrained while rotations around flexible bonds dominate conformational change. Torsional Diffusion generates those bond rotations on a torus. The reduced state has lower dimension, but the method must resolve which bonds are rotatable and remove ambiguity in how a torsion update acts on the molecular graph.

### Protein–ligand docking and protein structure

DiffDock predicts the pose of a small molecule bound to a protein. In the rigid-docking formulation, the protein structure is fixed. Starting from a seed ligand conformer, any admissible pose is reached by translating the ligand, rotating it, and changing its internal torsion angles.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s35-image65.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffDock decomposition of ligand pose into translation, rotation, and torsional changes." caption="DiffDock factorizes a ligand pose into rigid translation, rigid rotation, and internal torsions. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 35." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s35-image66.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffDock predicted ligand poses in protein binding pockets." caption="The generative process is conditioned on the protein environment to place a ligand in its binding pocket. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 35." %}

This decomposition turns docking into diffusion on a product space: Euclidean translation, rotational orientation, and one circle for each torsion. The rigid-protein assumption is also an explicit boundary. Binding modes coupled to large protein rearrangements lie outside that state space.

Protein structure prediction uses related geometric principles at a much larger scale. AlphaFold and later all-atom generative models combine sequence-derived information with geometric reasoning; the lecture previews that connection here before returning to it in the protein-focused lectures.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s36-image67.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Progression of protein structure prediction systems and their generated structures." caption="Molecular conformer, docking, and protein-structure models share geometric generation ideas even though their conditioning information and scale differ. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 36." %}

### Crystal structure prediction with symmetry

DiffCSP generates a crystal structure from its composition. It must model both the lattice and fractional coordinates while respecting rigid-motion symmetry and periodic equivalence. Translating an atom by a lattice vector does not produce a new crystal.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s37-diffcsp-overview.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffCSP joint diffusion of a crystal lattice and periodic fractional atomic coordinates." caption="DiffCSP jointly denoises lattice variables and periodic fractional coordinates while conditioning on composition. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 37." %}

DiffCSP++ allows explicit conditioning on a space group. It first samples a lattice, assigns elements to Wyckoff positions, and then samples the remaining displacements allowed within those sites. Hard symmetry information removes invalid regions of the search space and substantially improves generation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s38-image70.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffCSP++ conditioning crystal generation on space groups and Wyckoff positions." caption="DiffCSP++ generates symmetry-constrained crystals through space groups and occupied Wyckoff sites. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 38." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s38-image71.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffCSP++ performance table across crystal generation benchmarks." caption="Encoding the space-group constraint changes both validity and predictive performance. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 38." %}

SymmCD also conditions on symmetry but keeps the representation small. It separates an asymmetric unit—the minimal independent atom set—from the space-group operations that replicate it after generation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s39-symmcd-overview.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SymmCD diffusion over an asymmetric unit followed by Wyckoff projection and symmetry replication." caption="SymmCD diffuses a compact asymmetric unit and reconstructs the full crystal with the selected symmetry operations. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 39." %}

The three crystal models form a progression. DiffCSP learns periodic structure without a supplied space group; DiffCSP++ builds a requested group through Wyckoff sites; SymmCD compresses the generated object to its independent unit. In every case, the representation determines whether symmetry is learned statistically or enforced by construction.

## Three-dimensional property prediction and machine-learning force fields

The final part returns from generation to prediction. Three-dimensional models can estimate optical, thermal, electronic, thermodynamic, tensile, and elastic properties. Machine-learning potentials specialize this interface to energy, forces, and stress so that the model can drive relaxation and molecular dynamics.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s41-image74.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Examples of three-dimensional molecular and materials property prediction tasks." caption="Three-dimensional prediction spans scalar properties, vector forces, tensor stresses, and experimentally relevant responses. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 41." %}

An interatomic potential approximates a field over atomic configurations. At each state it should provide a potential energy and, often, its derivatives. This is one of the most successful applications of deep learning in molecular science because a trained model can replace many expensive electronic-structure evaluations inside long simulations.

### Four architectural routes

The historical development of 3D molecular models has concentrated heavily on architecture: how should geometry be encoded, and how should symmetry be imposed without making computation prohibitive?

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s43-image81.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Timeline of three-dimensional molecular property prediction architectures." caption="The architecture history branches according to how models represent and preserve three-dimensional symmetry. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 43." %}

**Invariant GNNs** encode quantities that do not change under rigid motion, including distances, bond angles, and dihedral angles. SchNet builds messages from radial distances; DimeNet adds angular structure; GemNet develops directional message passing further.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s44-image82.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GemNet directional message passing based on invariant geometric quantities." caption="Invariant architectures recover directional context through distances, angles, and higher-order message paths. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 44." %}

The advantage is simple scalar computation. The cost is that direction must be reconstructed indirectly through enough invariant relations.

**Cartesian equivariant GNNs** keep geometric quantities that transform as vectors or local frames. EGNN updates coordinates with scalar-weighted displacement vectors. ClofNet and AlphaNet use local coordinate frames and symmetry-preserving inner or outer products.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s45-image83.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Equivariant graph network using displacement vectors and coordinate updates." caption="Cartesian equivariant models construct vector outputs from geometric vectors and invariant coefficients. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 45." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s45-image84.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Complete local-frame construction for an SE(3)-equivariant graph neural network." caption="Local frames turn neighborhood geometry into symmetry-consistent Cartesian features. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 45." %}

**Spherical equivariant GNNs** represent features as irreducible spherical tensors. A type-$$\ell$$ feature rotates through the corresponding representation of $$SO(3)$$. Clebsch–Gordan tensor products combine compatible types while preserving equivariance. Tensor Field Networks, Equiformer, and eSCN follow this route.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s46-image85.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Spherical tensor feature types coupled through Clebsch-Gordan tensor products." caption="Spherical equivariant networks carry typed features and couple them with Clebsch–Gordan products. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 46." %}

This representation is systematic and expressive, but coupling many angular orders can be expensive.

**Unconstrained GNNs with symmetrization** begin with a general architecture and restore symmetry through data augmentation, averaging over rotations, or canonicalization. FAENet, SCN, canonicalization networks, and Orb illustrate variants of this strategy.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s47-image86.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Orb denoising architecture without built-in rotational equivariance." caption="Orb uses a scalable unconstrained architecture and denoising pretraining rather than enforcing full equivariance in every layer. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 47." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s47-image87.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Canonicalization network aligning an input before applying a general model." caption="Canonicalization maps equivalent orientations to a chosen pose before an ordinary predictor is applied. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 47." %}

These four routes are not a simple accuracy ranking. Invariance, Cartesian vectors, spherical tensors, and external symmetrization trade representation completeness, compute, memory, and numerical behavior differently.

### Periodic architectures and long-range interactions

Crystals add periodicity and interactions that may extend beyond a local cutoff. CrystalFormer uses attention with an exponentially decaying periodic structure encoding. Ewald message passing separates short-range interactions from a reciprocal-space component inspired by Ewald summation.

Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 48." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s48-image90.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="CrystalFormer attention formula for periodic structure encoding." caption="The periodic encoding weights repeated interactions while retaining translational equivalence. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol molecular property lecture, slide 48." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s48-image92.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Ewald message passing with real-space and reciprocal-space components." caption="Ewald message passing augments local graph communication with a reciprocal-space route for long-range effects. ### Predictive foundation models

The final sequence asks whether one potential can generalize across elements, temperatures, pressures, and even choices of electronic-structure basis or exchange–correlation functional. MACE-MP-0, MatterSim, Orb-v3, and UMA answer with different combinations of symmetry, scale, data diversity, and task conditioning.

The lecture ends at this frontier because it exposes the central tension of molecular prediction. A useful model must respect geometry, remain stable when embedded in simulation, cover a wide chemical and thermodynamic domain, and still be fast enough to replace the calculation it approximates. MACE-MP-0 emphasizes systematic equivariance and many-body structure; MatterSim emphasizes active learning across conditions; Orb-v3 emphasizes scalable unconstrained computation; UMA emphasizes broad scale with efficient expert routing and energy-derived forces. “Universal” is therefore not one architectural property. It is a claim about the intersection of data coverage, physical constraints, evaluation tasks, and deployment cost.
