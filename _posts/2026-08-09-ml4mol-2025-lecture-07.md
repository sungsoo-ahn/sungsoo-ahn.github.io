## One-dimensional and two-dimensional benchmark datasets

Molecular property prediction begins with a deceptively simple supervised-learning interface: represent a molecule by a string or graph, pair it with a measured or computed label, and learn a function from one to the other. The difficulty is hidden in the benchmark. A collection decides which chemical space is visible, which labels count as ground truth, and which generalization problem is actually being measured.

### MoleculeNet

MoleculeNet was one of the first broad benchmarks for molecular machine learning. It collected tasks from quantum chemistry, physical chemistry, biophysics, and physiology, then supplied common featurization, splitting, and evaluation conventions. Its enduring contribution was not a single winning architecture. It made heterogeneous molecular tasks comparable enough that graph neural networks, fingerprints, and other representations could be tested on the same collection.

The table matters because a score cannot be interpreted without its denominator. Classification and regression tasks use different metrics; scaffold, random, and temporal splits ask different questions; experimental labels and quantum calculations carry different noise. “Performance on MoleculeNet” is therefore shorthand for a vector of results under a specified protocol, not a single universal measure of molecular understanding.

### Therapeutic Data Commons

Therapeutic Data Commons expands the benchmark idea from isolated molecular properties to the drug-development pipeline. Its tasks include small-molecule activity, ADME and toxicity, drug–target interactions, treatment response, and clinical outcomes. This changes the unit of prediction: depending on the task, the input may be a molecule, a protein, a disease, a cell line, a patient cohort, or a relation among them.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s04-therapeutic-data-commons-map.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Therapeutic Data Commons map of tasks and datasets across the therapeutic development pipeline." caption="Therapeutic Data Commons broadens molecular benchmarking into a multi-stage therapeutic-development setting. Source: the <a href='https://www.nature.com/articles/s41589-021-00927-4'>Therapeutics Data Commons paper</a>." %}

This broader scope is useful, but it makes task definition even more important. A model that predicts an assay readout is not automatically a model of efficacy; a model trained on approved drugs is exposed to a heavily selected distribution; and a split over molecule–target pairs can leak information if near-identical molecules or homologous targets appear on both sides. The benchmark should tell us what was held out, not merely how many examples were held out.

### ToyMix, LargeMix, and UltraLarge

The next move is from many separate benchmarks to large multi-task corpora. ToyMix, LargeMix, and UltraLarge were proposed as increasingly large mixtures for training foundation models of molecular representations. Rather than optimize one network independently for every endpoint, the model shares an encoder across many assays and datasets and learns from their combined supervision.

A mixture does not erase dataset boundaries. Labels can have different units, missingness patterns, assay technologies, and sampling biases. Shared training is attractive precisely because related tasks may transfer, but negative transfer is equally possible. The promise of a molecular foundation model therefore depends on whether the mixture exposes reusable chemical structure rather than simply a larger collection of incompatible columns.

## Three-dimensional molecular and materials datasets

The meaning of a data point changes once coordinates enter. A molecular graph may correspond to many conformers; a crystal is periodic rather than finite; an atomistic trajectory contributes a correlated sequence rather than independent structures; and forces are vector labels attached to every atom. The following datasets illustrate these different regimes in the order used in the lecture.

### QM9: equilibrium molecules and invariant targets

QM9 contains roughly 134,000 stable organic molecules with at most nine heavy atoms and 19 computed quantum properties per molecule. It became a standard benchmark because it is large enough for deep learning yet compact enough for controlled comparison.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s07-qm9-target-properties.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Table of QM9 target properties, units, and physical descriptions." caption="QM9 attaches dipole, polarizability, orbital-energy, thermochemical, and spatial targets to each equilibrium geometry. Source: the <a href='https://www.nature.com/articles/sdata201422'>QM9 paper</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s07-qm9-chemical-space.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Projection of QM9 chemical space colored by atomization energy with representative molecules around the map." caption="The chemical-space map makes the dataset's diversity and its atomization-energy gradient visible inside the small-molecule regime. Source: the <a href='https://www.nature.com/articles/sdata201422'>QM9 paper</a>." %}

All of these scalar targets are unchanged when a molecule is translated or rotated. If a model receives positions $$R=(r_1,\ldots,r_n)$$, its prediction should satisfy

$$
f(Z,QR+t)=f(Z,R)
$$

for a rotation $$Q$$ and translation $$t$$. This simple invariance requirement later motivates the architecture families used for 3D property prediction.

### MD17 and revised MD17: local trajectories with force labels

MD17 contains snapshots from *ab initio* molecular-dynamics trajectories of ten small organic molecules, including benzene and aspirin. Each snapshot provides atomic positions, total potential energy, and a force vector for every atom. Revised MD17 selects 100,000 configurations and recomputes energies and forces to remove inconsistencies in the original calculations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s08-md17-overview.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="MD17 molecular structures together with energy and force labels along molecular dynamics trajectories." caption="MD17 changes the unit of supervision from one equilibrium molecule to many correlated configurations around a molecule's trajectory. The exact drawing origin is unclear; MD17 and its revised labels are documented by <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09593'>Christensen and von Lilienfeld</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s08-aspirin-structure.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Two-dimensional free-energy landscape over molecular coordinates with reference trajectories." caption="A trajectory explores a local energy landscape rather than contributing independent equilibrium molecules. The exact drawing origin is unclear; MD17 and its revised labels are documented by <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09593'>Christensen and von Lilienfeld</a>." %}

For an energy model $$E_\theta(Z,R)$$, conservative forces are obtained by differentiation,

$$
F_i=-\nabla_{r_i}E_\theta(Z,R).
$$

The energy is invariant, while the forces rotate as vectors. MD17 is thus not merely a larger QM9: it asks whether a model can resolve the local shape of a potential-energy surface accurately enough to reproduce derivatives.

### MPTrj and Alexandria: broad chemical coverage and relaxation paths

MPTrj contains about 1.58 million three-dimensional crystal structures drawn from the Materials Project and spans 89 elements. Its purpose is breadth: a potential trained on many inorganic bonding environments may transfer beyond the narrow chemistry of one molecule or material family.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s09-mptraj-element-coverage.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Periodic-table heatmap of compound, energy, magnetic moment, force, and stress counts in MPTrj." caption="MPTrj covers most of the periodic table, but its compound and label counts remain strongly nonuniform across elements. Source: the <a href='https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/Materials_Project_Trjectory_MPtrj_Dataset/23713842'>released MPTrj dataset</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s09-materials-project-overview.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Materials Project counts for materials, molecules, users, calculations, and database growth." caption="MPTrj is derived from a much broader Materials Project ecosystem of computed structures and properties. Source: the <a href='https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/Materials_Project_Trjectory_MPtrj_Dataset/23713842'>released MPTrj dataset</a>." %}

Alexandria is an open repository of millions of quantum-chemistry calculations for molecules and materials. A particularly useful feature is that it stores intermediate structures from relaxation, including slightly off-equilibrium coordinates and their forces, rather than only final minima.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s10-alexandria-database.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Alexandria database logo and scope." caption="Alexandria includes structures encountered along relaxation paths as well as relaxed endpoints. Source: the <a href='https://alexandria.icams.rub.de/'>Alexandria database</a>." %}

That distinction is crucial for simulation. A model trained only at minima may interpolate equilibrium energies well while seeing almost no restoring directions. Relaxation frames populate the neighborhoods through which an optimizer or molecular-dynamics integrator actually moves.

### Open Catalyst: adsorption and surface relaxation

The Open Catalyst datasets move from bulk structures to adsorbates on catalytic surfaces. OC20 contains 1,281,040 DFT relaxation trajectories, amounting to roughly 264 million energy-and-force calculations. Its central tasks include structure-to-energy-and-forces (S2EF) and initial-structure-to-relaxed-energy (IS2RE).

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s11-open-catalyst-overview.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Open Catalyst dataset overview with adsorbates, surfaces, and chemical coverage." caption="Open Catalyst represents adsorption as a coupled adsorbate–surface problem rather than an isolated molecule. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09990'>OC20 dataset paper</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s11-adsorbate-surface.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Atomic surface with an adsorbate from the Open Catalyst dataset." caption="A catalytic configuration includes a periodic surface, an attached adsorbate, and the relaxation path between initial and final states. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09990'>OC20 dataset paper</a>." %}

S2EF asks for the energy and atomic forces at a supplied configuration. IS2RE asks for the final relaxed energy from the initial configuration, thereby folding a whole relaxation trajectory into one prediction. The latter can be useful for screening, but it also asks a network to infer the result of an optimization it does not explicitly execute.

OC22 extends the effort to oxide electrocatalysts. It adds about 62,000 DFT relaxation trajectories and 9.85 million evaluated structures, with total-energy targets across multiple states rather than only adsorption energies. The intention is to encourage a model of more general surface physics instead of a narrow correction for one binding-energy definition.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s12-oc22-overview.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="OC22 dataset coverage over oxide electrocatalyst compositions and surface systems." caption="OC22 focuses the Open Catalyst setting on oxide electrocatalysis and a broader collection of total-energy states. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08917'>OC22 paper</a>." %}

### ODAC, OMat24, and Matbench

Open Direct Air Capture, ODAC23/24, focuses on porous materials such as metal–organic frameworks interacting with carbon dioxide. The target application is direct capture of dilute $$\mathrm{CO_2}$$ from air, so the relevant configurations combine guest molecules, pores, and adsorption geometries.

OMat24 instead emphasizes scale and diversity in periodic DFT. It contains more than 110 million calculations over structurally and compositionally diverse bulk materials. Initial structures were generated by perturbing or randomly sampling relaxed structures from Alexandria, expanding the off-equilibrium coverage needed by interatomic potentials.

### Phonons as a structured target

A phonon is a quantized vibrational mode of a solid. Low-frequency modes correspond to gentle collective motion; higher-frequency modes correspond to faster atomic vibrations. Their spectra affect thermal conductivity, dynamical stability, and other material properties. The MDR Phonon Benchmark uses computed phonon spectra for roughly 10,000 materials.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s17-phonon-mode.gif" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Animated atomic displacement pattern for a phonon mode in a crystal." caption="A phonon target describes a coordinated vibration of atoms in a periodic solid, not merely one scalar attached to a structure. The animation's exact origin is unresolved; phonon evaluation context appears in the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12147'>eSEN study</a>." %}

This benchmark closes the dataset survey with an important reminder: “3D property prediction” includes scalar energies, vector forces, tensor stresses, spectra, and trajectories. The output representation is part of the scientific problem.

### Pretraining a graph encoder

An early strategy separated node-level and graph-level pretraining. Node-level objectives mask atom attributes or predict subgraph context. Graph-level pretraining uses supervised molecular labels, for example from ChEMBL, to force a pooled representation to retain useful chemistry.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s20-gnn-pretraining-objectives.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Node-level and graph-level strategies for pretraining graph neural networks." caption="Pretraining can supervise local atom representations, whole-molecule representations, or both. {% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s20-gnn-pretraining-benchmarks.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="MoleculeNet results comparing randomly initialized, node-pretrained, graph-pretrained, and combined graph neural networks." caption="The benchmark separates node-level pretraining, graph-level supervised pretraining, and their combination across eight molecular classification datasets. {% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s20-gnn-pretraining-results.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Title and authors of Strategies for Pre-training Graph Neural Networks." caption="The study formalizes node-level and graph-level pretraining as complementary stages rather than one undifferentiated objective. Source: <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.12265'>Strategies for Pre-training Graph Neural Networks</a>." %}

The objective decides what information can be discarded. Masked-atom prediction rewards local chemical regularities. A graph-level assay objective rewards distinctions useful to those assays. Neither automatically learns three-dimensional conformational effects.

3D-InfoMax uses available 3D structures as self-supervision for a 2D graph encoder. The idea resembles distillation: a 3D network sees coordinates, a 2D network sees connectivity, and a contrastive objective encourages the two representations of the same molecule to agree.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s21-3d-infomax-conformers.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Molecular examples illustrating 2D graphs and alternative 3D conformations." caption="A molecular graph is compatible with multiple conformers, so cross-view agreement cannot make every 3D detail recoverable from 2D alone. {% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s21-3d-infomax-benchmarks.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="QM9 benchmark table comparing 3D-InfoMax with two-dimensional pretraining baselines, RDKit geometry, and a true three-dimensional model." caption="The QM9 comparison distinguishes information transferred into a 2D encoder from the upper bound of a model that receives the true 3D structure at inference time. The limitation is structural. If two conformers share the same graph but have different target values, a deterministic 2D encoder cannot know which conformer was intended. Pretraining can teach the *distribution* of plausible geometry associated with a graph; it cannot recover missing instance-specific coordinates.

### A benchmarking crisis

Computational drug-discovery benchmarks remain hard to compare. Datasets and protocols are scattered across papers, split conventions differ, and tuning budgets are rarely identical. A careful study found that many proposed methods underperformed the ECFP4 molecular fingerprint on MoleculeNet tasks.

The benefit of scale can only be established when the baseline, split, and hyperparameter budget scale with comparable care.

### Multimodal supervision and scale

CLAMP trains a molecular encoder and an assay-text encoder contrastively on ChEMBL bioactivity data. The molecular representation concatenates ECFP and RDKit fingerprints; the assay description is encoded with latent Dirichlet allocation. A positive pair joins an active molecule to the description of its assay, while a negative pair joins a non-active molecule.

## Predicting three-dimensional structure from graphs and compositions

A two-dimensional molecular graph does not specify one unique conformation. Structure prediction therefore changes the task from regression to conditional generation: sample stable coordinates given a graph, protein context, or crystal composition.

### Molecular conformers

Conformer generation predicts plausible three-dimensional arrangements of a molecular graph. These coordinates can support stability calculations and molecular-interaction models, and the same basic problem extends toward protein and crystal structures.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s32-conformer-generation.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Molecular graph with several possible three-dimensional conformers." caption="One graph can correspond to several stable conformers, so structure prediction should represent a distribution. The illustration's exact origin is unresolved; scientific context: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02923'>GeoDiff paper</a>." %}

GeoDiff takes the direct Cartesian approach: train an equivariant diffusion model that perturbs and denoises atomic coordinates. If $$R_t$$ is a noised geometry, the score or denoising field must rotate consistently with $$R_t$$ so that the generated distribution is independent of the coordinate frame.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s33-geodiff-paper.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Title and authors of the GeoDiff molecular conformation generation paper." caption="GeoDiff frames conformer generation as an equivariant reverse diffusion process over Cartesian atom coordinates. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02923'>GeoDiff paper</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s33-geodiff-process.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GeoDiff forward noising and reverse denoising process for a molecular conformer." caption="The forward process disperses an equilibrium conformer, while the learned reverse Markov chain progressively reconstructs molecular geometry. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02923'>GeoDiff paper</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s33-geodiff-benchmark-table.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GEOM-Drugs conformer coverage and matching benchmark comparing GeoDiff with earlier methods." caption="GeoDiff improves both coverage and matching on the displayed GEOM-Drugs evaluation, showing that diversity and geometric fidelity must be reported together. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02923'>GeoDiff paper</a>." %}

Cartesian coordinates are redundant for many molecules because bond lengths and angles are relatively constrained while rotations around flexible bonds dominate conformational change. Torsional Diffusion generates those bond rotations on a torus. The reduced state has lower dimension, but the method must resolve which bonds are rotatable and remove ambiguity in how a torsion update acts on the molecular graph.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s34-torsional-diffusion-paper.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Title and authors of Torsional Diffusion for Molecular Conformer Generation." caption="Torsional Diffusion formulates conformer generation directly on the periodic degrees of freedom around rotatable bonds. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01729'>Torsional Diffusion paper</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s34-torsional-diffusion.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Forward and reverse diffusion over molecular torsion angles with intrinsic and extrinsic score representations." caption="Torsional Diffusion places each rotatable bond angle on a circle and performs score-based generation on the resulting hypertorus. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01729'>Torsional Diffusion paper</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s34-torsion-ambiguity.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Three conventions for changing a torsion angle and the bond-direction ambiguity they create." caption="A torsion update is unchanged by swapping which side of a bond is rotated, so the learning target needs a convention that removes this sign ambiguity. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01729'>Torsional Diffusion paper</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s34-torsional-diffusion-benchmark.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GEOM-Drugs conformer recall and precision table comparing Torsional Diffusion with cheminformatics and learned baselines." caption="The benchmark reports both recall and precision through coverage and average minimum RMSD, separating ensemble breadth from sample quality. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01729'>Torsional Diffusion paper</a>." %}

### Protein–ligand docking and protein structure

DiffDock predicts the pose of a small molecule bound to a protein. In the rigid-docking formulation, the protein structure is fixed. Starting from a seed ligand conformer, any admissible pose is reached by translating the ligand, rotating it, and changing its internal torsion angles.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s35-diffdock-paper.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Title and authors of DiffDock: Diffusion Steps, Twists, and Turns for Molecular Docking." caption="DiffDock reframes docking as generation on a product space of translations, rotations, and torsions. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01776'>DiffDock paper</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s35-diffdock-pipeline.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffDock reverse diffusion from random ligand poses to ranked protein-bound poses." caption="DiffDock begins from randomized ligand configurations and returns ranked poses with confidence estimates after reverse diffusion. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01776'>DiffDock paper</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s35-diffdock-pose-space.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffDock decomposition of ligand pose into translation, rotation, and torsional changes." caption="DiffDock factorizes the pose manifold into Euclidean translations, \(SO(3)\) rotations, and periodic torsional coordinates. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01776'>DiffDock paper</a>." %}

This decomposition turns docking into diffusion on a product space: Euclidean translation, rotational orientation, and one circle for each torsion. The rigid-protein assumption is also an explicit boundary. Binding modes coupled to large protein rearrangements lie outside that state space.

Protein structure prediction uses related geometric principles at a much larger scale. AlphaFold and later all-atom generative models combine sequence-derived information with geometric reasoning; the lecture previews that connection here before returning to it in the protein-focused lectures.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s36-protein-structure-models.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Progression of protein structure prediction systems and their generated structures." caption="Molecular conformer, docking, and protein-structure models share geometric generation ideas even though their conditioning information and scale differ. Source: the <a href='https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07487-w'>AlphaFold 3 paper</a>." %}

### Crystal structure prediction with symmetry

DiffCSP generates a crystal structure from its composition. It must model both the lattice and fractional coordinates while respecting rigid-motion symmetry and periodic equivalence. Translating an atom by a lattice vector does not produce a new crystal.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s37-diffcsp-overview.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffCSP joint diffusion of a crystal lattice and periodic fractional atomic coordinates." caption="DiffCSP jointly denoises lattice variables and periodic fractional coordinates while conditioning on composition. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04475'>DiffCSP paper</a>." %}

DiffCSP++ allows explicit conditioning on a space group. It first samples a lattice, assigns elements to Wyckoff positions, and then samples the remaining displacements allowed within those sites. Hard symmetry information removes invalid regions of the search space and substantially improves generation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s38-diffcspp-denoising.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffCSP++ reverse denoising with crystal-family lattice constraints and Wyckoff-position coordinate constraints." caption="DiffCSP++ applies lattice and Wyckoff constraints during each reverse step instead of filtering invalid symmetry only after generation. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03992'>DiffCSP++ paper</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s38-diffcspp-process.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffCSP++ conditioning crystal generation on space groups and Wyckoff positions." caption="DiffCSP++ generates symmetry-constrained crystals through space groups and occupied Wyckoff sites. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03992'>DiffCSP++ paper</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s38-diffcspp-results.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffCSP++ performance table across crystal generation benchmarks." caption="Encoding the space-group constraint changes both validity and predictive performance. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03992'>DiffCSP++ paper</a>." %}

SymmCD also conditions on symmetry but keeps the representation small. It separates an asymmetric unit—the minimal independent atom set—from the space-group operations that replicate it after generation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s39-symmcd-symmetry-diffusion.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A p4m unit cell partitioned into symmetry sectors and a diffusion sequence over asymmetric-unit variables." caption="SymmCD diffuses the compact asymmetric-unit representation while the space group determines how it expands across the unit cell. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03638'>SymmCD paper</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s39-symmcd-generation-pipeline.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SymmCD denoising, symmetry finding, Wyckoff projection, and replication pipeline." caption="The denoiser generates an asymmetric unit; Wyckoff projection and symmetry replication reconstruct the complete periodic crystal. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03638'>SymmCD paper</a>." %}

The three crystal models form a progression. DiffCSP learns periodic structure without a supplied space group; DiffCSP++ builds a requested group through Wyckoff sites; SymmCD compresses the generated object to its independent unit. In every case, the representation determines whether symmetry is learned statistically or enforced by construction.

### Four architectural routes

The historical development of 3D molecular models has concentrated heavily on architecture: how should geometry be encoded, and how should symmetry be imposed without making computation prohibitive?

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s43-architecture-timeline.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Timeline of three-dimensional molecular property prediction architectures." caption="The architecture history branches according to how models represent and preserve three-dimensional symmetry. This is an author synthesis of the architecture families discussed below." %}

**Invariant GNNs** encode quantities that do not change under rigid motion, including distances, bond angles, and dihedral angles. SchNet builds messages from radial distances; DimeNet adds angular structure; GemNet develops directional message passing further.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s44-gemnet-message-passing.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GemNet directional message passing based on invariant geometric quantities." caption="Invariant architectures recover directional context through distances, angles, and higher-order message paths. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08903'>GemNet paper</a>." %}

The advantage is simple scalar computation. The cost is that direction must be reconstructed indirectly through enough invariant relations.

**Cartesian equivariant GNNs** keep geometric quantities that transform as vectors or local frames. EGNN updates coordinates with scalar-weighted displacement vectors. ClofNet and AlphaNet use local coordinate frames and symmetry-preserving inner or outer products.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s45-cartesian-equivariance.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Equivariant graph network using displacement vectors and coordinate updates." caption="Cartesian equivariant models construct vector outputs from geometric vectors and invariant coefficients. Scientific context: the <a href='https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/du22e.html'>ClofNet paper</a>; the exact origin of the external-field sketch is unclear." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s45-local-coordinate-frames.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Complete local-frame construction for an SE(3)-equivariant graph neural network." caption="Local frames turn neighborhood geometry into symmetry-consistent Cartesian features. Scientific context: the <a href='https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/du22e.html'>ClofNet paper</a>; the exact origin of the external-field sketch is unclear." %}

**Spherical equivariant GNNs** represent features as irreducible spherical tensors. A type-$$\ell$$ feature rotates through the corresponding representation of $$SO(3)$$. Clebsch–Gordan tensor products combine compatible types while preserving equivariance. Tensor Field Networks, Equiformer, and eSCN follow this route.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s46-spherical-tensor-features.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Relative atom positions expanded into type-zero, type-one, and type-two spherical harmonic filters." caption="Tensor Field Networks separate distance-dependent radial filters from direction-dependent spherical harmonics, producing features with prescribed rotation types. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08219'>Tensor Field Networks paper</a>." %}

This representation is systematic and expressive, but coupling many angular orders can be expensive.

**Unconstrained GNNs with symmetrization** begin with a general architecture and restore symmetry through data augmentation, averaging over rotations, or canonicalization. FAENet, SCN, canonicalization networks, and Orb illustrate variants of this strategy.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s47-orb-denoising.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Orb denoising architecture without built-in rotational equivariance." caption="Orb uses a scalable unconstrained architecture and denoising pretraining rather than enforcing full equivariance in every layer. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22570'>Orb paper</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s47-canonicalization.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Canonicalization network aligning an input before applying a general model." caption="Canonicalization maps equivalent orientations to a chosen pose before an ordinary predictor is applied. Source: <a href='https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/kaba23a.html'>Equivariance with Learned Canonicalization Functions</a>." %}

These four routes are not a simple accuracy ranking. Invariance, Cartesian vectors, spherical tensors, and external symmetrization trade representation completeness, compute, memory, and numerical behavior differently.

### Periodic architectures and long-range interactions

Crystals add periodicity and interactions that may extend beyond a local cutoff. CrystalFormer uses attention with an exponentially decaying periodic structure encoding. Ewald message passing separates short-range interactions from a reciprocal-space component inspired by Ewald summation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s48-crystalformer-composite.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="CrystalFormer periodic neighbors and exponentially decaying attention over a one-dimensional crystal." caption="CrystalFormer treats periodic images as attention partners and interprets the decaying weights as learned interatomic potentials over an infinite crystal. Source: the <a href='https://openreview.net/forum?id=fxQiecl9HB'>CrystalFormer paper</a>." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-07/s48-ewald-message-passing-composite.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Real-space and reciprocal-space view of Ewald message passing with Fourier filtering." caption="Ewald message passing supplements local real-space communication with a learned reciprocal-space filter for long-range contributions. Source: the <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04791'>Ewald message-passing paper</a>." %}

The architectural question is now physical as well as geometric. A finite neighbor graph is efficient, but electrostatic and elastic effects can remain important over much longer distances.

### Predictive foundation models

The final sequence asks whether one potential can generalize across elements, temperatures, pressures, and even choices of electronic-structure basis or exchange–correlation functional. MACE-MP-0, MatterSim, Orb-v3, and UMA answer with different combinations of symmetry, scale, data diversity, and task conditioning.

$$
\mathrm{success\ rate}
=
\frac{\text{time or MD steps completed without numerical failure}}
{\text{time or MD steps requested}}.
$$

Predicting forces directly can improve speed and fit, but forces are then not guaranteed to be the gradient of one scalar energy. The conservative choice is not only aesthetic: it determines whether closed-loop work vanishes and whether the dynamics corresponds to a well-defined potential-energy surface.

The lecture ends at this frontier because it exposes the central tension of molecular prediction. A useful model must respect geometry, remain stable when embedded in simulation, cover a wide chemical and thermodynamic domain, and still be fast enough to replace the calculation it approximates. MACE-MP-0 emphasizes systematic equivariance and many-body structure; MatterSim emphasizes active learning across conditions; Orb-v3 emphasizes scalable unconstrained computation; UMA emphasizes broad scale with efficient expert routing and energy-derived forces. “Universal” is therefore not one architectural property. It is a claim about the intersection of data coverage, physical constraints, evaluation tasks, and deployment cost.
