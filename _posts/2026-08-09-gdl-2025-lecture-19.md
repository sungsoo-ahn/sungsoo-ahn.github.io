A protein is not a single structure. It occupies an ensemble of conformations, moves through that ensemble over time, and can be redesigned by changing its sequence, its structure, or both. These statements sound closely related because each involves three-dimensional coordinates. They nevertheless define different probability laws.

An equilibrium model samples individual structures from a distribution. A dynamics model samples transitions or complete trajectories whose temporal ordering matters. A design model samples a new molecular object under a functional condition. The distinction determines the training data, the state representation, the generative process, and the evidence needed to evaluate the output.

The lecture follows those targets in that order. It first surveys protein structure distribution learning, then moves to molecular-dynamics trajectory emulation, and finally develops protein design from sequences through backbones, joint sequence--structure generation, and antigen-aware antibody engineering.

## Learning protein structure distributions

A sequence defines an energy landscape with many accessible basins rather than one unique coordinate set. Temperature, solvent, ligands, cofactors, and other environmental conditions change the probability assigned to those basins. A crystal structure or a deterministic folding prediction can be a useful representative, but it does not specify the full conformational distribution.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s03-conformational-landscape.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A protein target mapped to a target-specific energy landscape and several conformations rather than a unique minimum." caption="One target can occupy several conformations on a target-specific energy landscape. The illustration contrasts the classical unique-minimum picture with a distribution over structures." %}

At equilibrium, a configuration $$\mathbf{x}$$ has Boltzmann density

$$
p_{\mathrm{eq}}(\mathbf{x})
=\frac{1}{Z}\exp\!\left[-\beta U(\mathbf{x})\right],
\qquad
Z=\int \exp\!\left[-\beta U(\mathbf{x})\right]d\mathbf{x},
$$

where $$U(\mathbf{x})$$ is the potential energy and $$\beta=(k_{B}T)^{-1}$$. The normalization constant $$Z$$ is expensive because it integrates over an enormous configuration space. Molecular simulation avoids evaluating $$Z$$ directly, but correlated trajectories may require long runs to cross high free-energy barriers.

### Boltzmann Generators: direct equilibrium sampling

Boltzmann Generators learn an invertible map from a tractable latent density $$p_Z(\mathbf{z})$$ to molecular configurations. If $$\mathbf{x}=f_\theta(\mathbf{z})$$, the change-of-variables formula gives a normalized model density,

$$
\log p_\theta(\mathbf{x})
=\log p_Z\!\left(f_\theta^{-1}(\mathbf{x})\right)
+\log\left\lvert\det J_{f_\theta^{-1}}(\mathbf{x})\right\rvert.
$$

The model combines two sources of information shown in the lecture. Simulation trajectories provide examples of target-specific configurations. The energy function provides an unnormalized physical density that can score configurations beyond the finite training set. After training, the invertible network maps independent latent draws to independent configuration proposals rather than integrating every intermediate physical step.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s04-boltzmann-generator.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Boltzmann Generator with an invertible neural block connecting a Gaussian latent density to molecular configurations, together with simulation training data." caption="A Boltzmann Generator maps a simple latent distribution to target configurations with an invertible neural network. The figure is from Noé et al. (2019); the lecture also asks how well a target-specific generator transfers to other systems." %}

The final question in the slide is the limitation to keep in view: a generator trained on one target-specific trajectory and energy landscape is not automatically a generator for another protein. Fast independent sampling and cross-system generalization are separate claims.

### Two for One: a diffusion model that also defines a force

The diffusion version replaces an invertible map with a denoising process. At noise scale $$\sigma$$, a score network learns

$$
\mathbf{s}_\theta(\mathbf{x},\sigma)
\approx \nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_\sigma(\mathbf{x}).
$$

Reverse diffusion uses this score to generate equilibrium configurations. The same quantity also has a mechanical interpretation. For a Boltzmann density,

$$
\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}\log p_{\mathrm{eq}}(\mathbf{x})
=-\beta\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}U(\mathbf{x})
=\beta\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}),
$$

so the score is proportional to the force. One learned denoising field can therefore support independent equilibrium sampling and supply a coarse-grained force field for dynamics. That is the “two for one” in the paper title.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s05-diffusion-force-field.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A denoising diffusion model trained on coarse-grained data, its implied free-energy landscape, and contact maps from flow, diffusion, and reference samples." caption="The diffusion score provides both a Boltzmann-style sampler and a force derived from the learned coarse-grained free energy. The figure from Arts et al. (2023) also compares contact statistics for five molecular systems." %}

The contact maps on the right test distributional structure rather than the appearance of a few samples. Agreement means that contacts recur with similar frequencies under the generated ensemble. The blank or mismatched panels show that one sampling procedure need not reproduce every system equally well.

### EigenFold: generative structure prediction

EigenFold applies diffusion to conditional protein structure prediction. A harmonic prior initializes coordinates, OmegaFold-derived embeddings encode the sequence, and an $$E(3)$$-equivariant decoder repeatedly updates positions. Sampling the model several times produces a structural distribution rather than a single point estimate.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s06-eigenfold.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="EigenFold sampling structures from a harmonic prior with a score model conditioned on OmegaFold embeddings." caption="EigenFold iteratively transforms a harmonic prior into protein structures with an equivariant score model. The figure from Jing et al. makes repeated sampling part of structure prediction rather than an afterthought." %}

The lecture evaluates this idea on apo and holo structures. An apo structure contains the protein without its activating ligand or cofactor; a holo structure contains the bound complex. Binding can reorganize domains and local active-site geometry, so the two states may differ even though the amino-acid sequence is unchanged.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s06-apo-holo.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Holo and apo conformations of a protein with a magnified active-site region." caption="The holo and apo forms provide a concrete reason to predict a conditional ensemble. The example shows domain rearrangement and local structural changes around the bound state." %}

Training only on the Protein Data Bank and evaluating whether samples cover both apo and holo forms tests whether the model can represent alternative basins. It does not by itself establish equilibrium populations, because structural databases are not Boltzmann-distributed observations.

### ConfDiff: physical guidance during geometric diffusion

ConfDiff begins with an $$\mathrm{SE}(3)$$ diffusion prior over protein conformations and adds energy or force guidance during sampling. The base denoiser supplies a learned structural prior. A guidance field changes the reverse trajectory so that lower-energy regions receive more probability.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s07-confdiff.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ConfDiff combining classifier-free guidance with intermediate energy and force guidance, followed by conformational free-energy plots." caption="ConfDiff adds an energy or force network to an \(\mathrm{SE}(3)\) diffusion model. The ICML 2024 figure compares its generated conformational landscapes with EigenFold and Str2Str variants." %}

The geometric requirement is strict. A scalar energy must be invariant to global rigid motion, while its coordinate gradient must rotate as a vector field. Guidance then changes which conformations are visited without introducing dependence on the arbitrary orientation of the input.

The plotted free-energy surfaces compare more than visual plausibility. They ask whether generated samples occupy the same metastable basins as the reference and whether their relative populations are reproduced. Energy-guided and force-guided variants can change those two properties differently.

### Distributional Graphormer: supervision from samples and energy

Distributional Graphormer separates structure prediction from distribution prediction. A descriptor identifies the molecular system; the distributional model then produces a family of structures rather than one semi-closed, closed, or open conformation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s08-distributional-graphormer.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Distributional Graphormer architecture with descriptor conditioning, diffusion steps, structural representations, and supervision from data and energy." caption="Distributional Graphormer combines sample supervision with supervision from the energy function. The figure from Zheng et al. shows the structural Graphormer inside a diffusion process over an equilibrium distribution." %}

The two supervision paths have different coverage. Data samples anchor the model in observed configurations. Energy supervision can evaluate perturbed structures between observed samples, provided the energy model is accurate in that region. The diffusion process alternates noisy configurations, structural representations, and denoising updates until it reaches the modeled equilibrium distribution.

### AlphaFlow: AlphaFold as a flow-matching denoiser

AlphaFlow changes the role of a folding model. In ordinary sequence-to-structure prediction, AlphaFold maps one sequence to one favored structure. In AlphaFlow, AlphaFold or ESMFold acts repeatedly along a flow-matching trajectory, analogous to how a U-Net is reused across denoising times in image generation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s09-alphaflow.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Analogy between a U-Net text-to-image generator and AlphaFold used as a sequence-to-structure flow-matching denoiser." caption="AlphaFlow reuses AlphaFold as a time-conditioned denoiser for protein ensembles. The ICML 2024 figure follows one sequence through a learned path from a base distribution to multiple conformations." %}

The sequence and folding representation condition every point on the path. Sampling a new initial state and integrating the learned vector field yields another structure for the same protein. The endpoint distribution, not the deterministic prediction of the original network, becomes the object of interest.

### ESMDiff: diffusion in a structure language

ESMDiff inserts a discrete representation between coordinates and generation. A structure language model encodes a conformation into a sequence of structure tokens. Masked diffusion corrupts those tokens toward a mask state, and a bidirectional Transformer learns to denoise them.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s10-esmdiff.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ESMDiff pipeline with a structure language model, masked diffusion over structure tokens, and a bidirectional Transformer initialized from ESM3." caption="ESMDiff generates conformations through masked diffusion over learned structure tokens. The ICLR 2025 figure separates tokenization, corruption, bidirectional denoising, and coordinate decoding." %}

The architecture is initialized from pretrained ESM3, but the generative variable is not an amino-acid sequence. It is a tokenized conformation. A decoder maps the denoised structure-token sequence back to a structural ensemble. This changes the geometry problem: equivariance can be concentrated in the structure tokenizer and decoder, while the diffusion model operates on discrete token identities.

### BioEmu: scaling equilibrium emulation

BioEmu combines protein-sequence conditioning with a denoising diffusion model. A sequence encoder consumes paired evolutionary information and single-sequence representations; a score model then generates structures from the equilibrium distribution conditioned on that sequence.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s11-bioemu-conformations.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Several aligned protein conformations rendered in orange, gray, green, and red on a black background." caption="Multiple aligned BioEmu conformations visualize the model's output as an ensemble. The image preserves the conformational differences that a single predicted structure would collapse." %}

This section ends with equilibrium ensembles, not kinetics. BioEmu may reproduce structural populations or free-energy differences while remaining agnostic about the physical time required to move between conformations. The next part changes the sample from an independent state to a time-dependent transition or trajectory.

## Emulating molecular-dynamics trajectories

Molecular dynamics advances positions and velocities under Newton's equations,

$$
m_i\frac{d^2\mathbf{x}_i(t)}{dt^2}
=-\nabla_{\mathbf{x}_i}U(\mathbf{x}(t)),
$$

then discretizes the evolution with a numerical integrator. The ordered sequence

$$
\mathbf{x}_0,\mathbf{x}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{x}_T
$$

is a trajectory. It contains correlations between nearby times and rates of movement between metastable states. Drawing $$T$$ independent structures from the correct equilibrium density would reproduce the one-time marginal distribution but destroy those temporal relationships.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s13-md-simulation.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Monte Carlo and molecular-dynamics paths on an energy landscape with Metropolis acceptance and Newtonian force diagrams." caption="Monte Carlo and molecular dynamics can visit the same landscape through different path laws. The Str2Str figure emphasizes force evaluation, numerical integration, and ordered trajectories for MD." %}

### CGMD: learn a longer integration step

Traditional MD reaches time $$t+k\Delta t$$ through $$k$$ short updates. CGMD learns a graph-network map that jumps directly across that interval in a coarse-grained representation. An embedding GNN converts fine molecular graphs into latent features, coarse graining produces a reduced graph, and the dynamics and score GNNs predict the future coarse state.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s14-cgmd.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="CGMD multi-scale graph network mapping fine molecular graphs through embedding and coarse graining to a future coarse state." caption="CGMD replaces several conventional MD steps with one learned transition from \(t\) to \(t+k\Delta t\). The TMLR 2023 figure also shows autoregressive bootstrap simulation after one-step training." %}

Training uses future states as prediction targets. At inference, the predicted coarse state is fed back into the simulator to continue the trajectory. This feedback is the central difficulty: a small one-step error changes the next input, and the error can accumulate over a long rollout. Efficient integration therefore requires both accurate transition statistics and stable autoregressive behavior.

### Implicit Transfer Operator: one surrogate, several lag times

A transfer operator propagates observables or densities over a lag $$\tau$$. In kernel language, it is determined by the conditional transition density

$$
p_\tau(\mathbf{x}'\mid\mathbf{x}).
$$

The Implicit Transfer Operator models this density with conditional diffusion and conditions explicitly on the time lag. A single model can therefore represent several resolutions instead of learning one fixed step.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s15-implicit-transfer-operator.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Lag-conditioned implicit transfer operator and free-energy comparisons at 200 picoseconds, 2 nanoseconds, 20 nanoseconds, and 200 nanoseconds." caption="The implicit transfer operator learns lag-conditioned transitions with an \(\mathrm{SE}(3)\)-equivariant PaiNN model. The NeurIPS 2023 figure compares four lag times on small fast-folding proteins." %}

The slide shows $$200$$ ps, $$2$$ ns, $$20$$ ns, and $$200$$ ns. Short lags preserve local continuity and make the next state strongly dependent on the current conformation. Long lags permit basin changes and approach equilibrium independence. Conditioning on $$\tau$$ asks one network to learn that entire change in transition law. The reported experiments use small fast-folders, so multiple-time-resolution capability should not be read as unrestricted transfer to arbitrary proteins.

### TimeWarp: a conditional normalizing-flow transition

TimeWarp represents a time-coarsened transition with a conditional normalizing flow. Given $$\mathbf{x}(t)$$, RealNVP coupling layers transform latent noise into a sample of $$\mathbf{x}(t+\tau)$$. The figure uses $$\tau=0.5\times10^6$$ fs, or $$0.5$$ ns, to illustrate a jump that would contain many ordinary integration steps.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s16-timewarp.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="TimeWarp transition from a peptide state at time t to time t plus tau, with MD and TimeWarp MCMC state-space paths." caption="TimeWarp learns a conditional normalizing-flow proposal for a later molecular state. The NeurIPS 2023 figure compares MD transitions with TimeWarp MCMC on peptides of two to four amino acids." %}

The second panel compares paths through a low-dimensional state projection. TimeWarp is not simply making the MD integrator take a larger deterministic step. It generates a conditional distribution and combines it with MCMC correction, allowing the proposal to traverse between regions while targeting the desired stationary law.

### MDGen: generate the complete trajectory

MDGen changes the generated object again. Instead of sampling one later state, it generates a complete trajectory of three-dimensional coordinates over a $$100$$ ns window. A roto-translationally invariant torsion-angle representation encodes each frame, and a temporal model processes the ordered stack.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s17-mdgen.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="MDGen tasks for forward simulation, interpolation, upsampling, and inpainting, followed by a temporal model over trajectory coordinates." caption="MDGen treats a molecular trajectory as a video-like structured sample. The NeurIPS 2024 figure distinguishes forward simulation, endpoint interpolation, temporal upsampling, and inpainting." %}

The four masks in the figure define different conditional-generation tasks. Forward simulation conditions on an initial segment and generates the future. Interpolation conditions on both endpoints and fills the middle. Upsampling conditions on sparse frames and inserts higher-frequency temporal detail. Inpainting conditions on partial information throughout the sequence. One architecture can express all four, but each task supplies different observations and asks for a different conditional path distribution.

## Protein design targets

Protein design generates molecules intended to realize a desired function. The generated object can be a sequence, a three-dimensional structure, or both. The green fluorescent protein produced with ESM3 in the lecture is an example of the endpoint: a designed sequence and structure associated with a measurable fluorescent phenotype.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s19-esm3-fluorescent-protein.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A green fluorescent protein generated with ESM3, rendered as a ribbon inside a molecular surface." caption="The ESM3-generated green fluorescent protein illustrates function-conditioned protein design. The image shows the molecular object produced after sequence and structure choices have been coupled." %}

The three design formulations expose different variables:

- **Sequence design** generates residue identities, possibly from functional properties, a partial sequence, or a fixed structure.
- **Structure design** generates a three-dimensional backbone or all-atom configuration, often before assigning a compatible sequence.
- **Co-design** generates continuous geometry and discrete residue identity together.

The distinction is operational. A sequence generator still needs folding and functional validation. A backbone generator still needs inverse folding. A co-design model removes that hard boundary but must learn interactions between two modalities during sampling.

Protein sequences are available at a much larger scale than experimentally resolved structures. The lecture's schematic grows the sequence corpus from roughly tens of millions toward hundreds of millions while the structure corpus remains comparatively flat. This difference makes large language-model training easier on sequence, but abundance does not make function labels or paired sequence--structure measurements equally abundant.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s20-sequence-structure-data.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Schematic showing protein sequence data growing from about 20 million toward 280 million samples while protein structure data remain much smaller." caption="Sequence databases outgrow structure databases by a wide margin. The lecture schematic motivates property-conditioned sequence generation and structure-conditioned inverse folding as distinct uses of that data." %}

## Sequence and inverse-folding models

### ProGen: properties to sequence

ProGen conditions an autoregressive protein language model on control tags such as protein family, biological process, and molecular function. A $$1.2$$-billion-parameter Transformer decoder receives the tags and a sequence prefix, then predicts the next amino acid under a negative log-likelihood objective,

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{AR}}
=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log p_\theta(a_i\mid a_{<i},\mathbf{c}),
$$

where $$\mathbf{c}$$ denotes the control tags.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s21-progen.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ProGen protein language model receiving functional control tags and autoregressively producing protein sequences." caption="ProGen maps family and function tags to amino-acid sequences with an autoregressive Transformer. The figure from Madani et al. shows both the conditional generation interface and next-residue training objective." %}

The output is a sequence, not a guaranteed function. The tags bias the learned sequence distribution toward annotated families, while downstream folding and experiments determine whether an individual sample realizes the intended behavior.

### ESM-IF: backbone coordinates to sequence

Inverse folding conditions on a structure instead of a functional tag. ESM-IF receives backbone coordinates and generates a sequence compatible with that backbone. In the training pipeline shown in the lecture, roughly $$12$$ million AlphaFold2-predicted structures supplement about $$16{,}000$$ CATH structures. Span masking and noise regularize the structural input before an invariant geometric vector perceptron encoder and autoregressive Transformer decoder predict residues.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s22-esm-if.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ESM-IF inverse-folding model trained on predicted and CATH structures with invariant geometric features and an autoregressive decoder." caption="ESM-IF generates a sequence from a fixed backbone. The ICML 2022 figure shows how predicted structures and curated CATH structures supervise the same geometric encoder--decoder." %}

The encoder must be invariant to global rotation and translation because the desired amino-acid probabilities cannot depend on how the backbone file is oriented. The decoder is autoregressive because residue compatibility depends on both the geometry and previously selected residue identities.

### ProteinMPNN: residue-graph message passing

ProteinMPNN makes the structural graph explicit. Each residue is a node. Backbone atoms $$N$$, $$C_\alpha$$, $$C$$, and $$O$$ define local geometric features, and directed edges carry pairwise relationships. A backbone encoder alternates edge and node updates before a sequence decoder chooses residues in a random autoregressive order.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s23-proteinmpnn.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ProteinMPNN backbone encoder and sequence decoder with residue nodes, geometric edges, random decoding order, and iterative residue sampling." caption="ProteinMPNN converts backbone geometry into residue probabilities through message passing and autoregressive decoding. The Science 2022 figure exposes the edge updates that distinguish the model from a sequence-only language model." %}

Random decoding order prevents the model from treating the N-to-C chain order as the only legitimate factorization. At each step, known backbone geometry is available everywhere, while only already decoded residue identities are exposed as sequence context.

### SurfPro: molecular surface to sequence

SurfPro replaces a backbone graph with the molecular surface. Local surface patches encode hydrophobicity, charge, polarity, and shape; a hierarchical encoder combines local-perspective modeling with a global landscape representation. An autoregressive decoder then generates the protein sequence.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s24-surfpro.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SurfPro molecular surface with hydrophobicity, charge, and polarity labels, followed by a hierarchical encoder and autoregressive sequence decoder." caption="SurfPro conditions sequence design on the molecular surface rather than only the backbone. The ICML 2024 figure separates local surface patches from the global protein landscape." %}

The surface is a different information interface. It emphasizes the boundary presented to solvent and binding partners, while a backbone representation exposes internal chain geometry more directly. Both tasks output sequences, but they condition on different projections of the same protein.

## Protein structure generation

Generating a complex three-dimensional structure is harder than predicting a token at one sequence position. The output must satisfy bond geometry, steric constraints, global fold organization, and rigid-motion symmetry. The lecture presents structure generators in chronological order because successive models change the geometric state, stochastic process, denoiser, or probability path.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s25-structure-design.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A cloud of perturbed protein backbone atoms surrounding a partially formed central structure." caption="Structure design begins from a noisy three-dimensional state and must recover coherent protein geometry. The image introduces a method sequence that follows the evolution of geometric diffusion and flow models." %}

### FrameDiff: diffusion on residue rigid bodies

FrameDiff represents residue $$n$$ by a rigid frame

$$
T_n=(R_n,\mathbf{x}_n)\in \mathrm{SE}(3),
$$

where $$R_n\in\mathrm{SO}(3)$$ is a rotation and $$\mathbf{x}_n\in\mathbb{R}^3$$ is the $$C_\alpha$$ translation. The frame is constructed from backbone vectors by Gram--Schmidt orthogonalization. Diffusion perturbs the rotations and translations separately, and the denoiser predicts clean frames together with torsion angles used to place atoms such as the backbone oxygen.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s26-framediff.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="FrameDiff residue frame construction, diffusion trajectory, score equations for rotations and translations, and backbone prediction." caption="FrameDiff diffuses per-residue frames in \(\mathrm{SE}(3)^N\) and predicts torsions for atom placement. The ICML 2023 figure shows how frames reduce degrees of freedom while preserving bond geometry." %}

The state reduces the per-residue degrees of freedom relative to generating every backbone atom independently. Bond lengths and angles inside the local residue template are maintained by construction. The score nevertheless has two components: one tangent to $$\mathrm{SO}(3)$$ for rotation and one in $$\mathbb{R}^3$$ for translation.

### FrameFlow: flow matching on the same state

FrameFlow keeps the FrameDiff goal, inputs, and residue-frame output but replaces stochastic reverse diffusion with an ordinary differential equation. Translations follow linear interpolation,

$$
\mathbf{x}_t=(1-t)\mathbf{x}_0+t\mathbf{x}_1,
$$

while rotations follow a geodesic on $$\mathrm{SO}(3)$$,

$$
R_t=\operatorname{Exp}_{R_0}\!\left(t\operatorname{Log}_{R_0}(R_1)\right).
$$

The learned vector field predicts the velocity of these paths.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s27-frameflow.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="FrameDiff and FrameFlow generation trajectories with a backbone-generation results table and rigid-body flow equations." caption="FrameFlow transports residue frames with flow matching rather than reverse diffusion. The figure reports designability, diversity, novelty, and a five-fold reduction in sampling steps relative to FrameDiff." %}

The benchmark table keeps four outcomes separate. Designability asks whether a generated backbone can receive a sequence that folds back to it. Diversity counts distinct structural clusters. Novelty compares generated folds with known structures. Sampling steps measure computational cost. Improving one column does not imply improvement in all four.

### Genie and Genie 2: diffusion in coordinate space

Genie works directly with noisy $$C_\alpha$$ coordinates in Euclidean space. An invariant encoder processes pair and single representations, an equivariant decoder updates coordinate frames, and the denoising objective penalizes coordinate error,

$$
\mathcal{L}
=\mathbb{E}_{t,\mathbf{x}_0,\boldsymbol\epsilon}
\left[
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\left\lVert \boldsymbol\epsilon_i
-\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t)_i\right\rVert^2
\right].
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s28-genie.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Genie invariant encoder and equivariant coordinate decoder for protein backbone diffusion." caption="Genie denoises perturbed \(C_\alpha\) coordinates with invariant pair processing and an equivariant decoder. The ICML 2023 figure contrasts direct coordinate optimization with frame-based generation." %}

The slide reports more novel and diverse structures than FrameDiff, together with a higher sampling cost caused by the larger number of diffusion steps. That is a representation-and-solver trade-off, not a universal ranking of coordinate and frame states.

Genie 2 retains coordinate diffusion and strengthens the denoiser with richer feature processing. It also introduces motif-scaffolding capabilities: a functional motif, target sequence, and partial structural template can condition the generated surrounding backbone.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s29-genie2.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Genie 2 architecture with motif and sequence conditioning, invariant and equivariant processing, and motif-scaffolding examples." caption="Genie 2 extends coordinate diffusion to conditional motif scaffolding and larger structures. The 2024 figure shows generated scaffolds that preserve one or several supplied motifs." %}

The output is still a denoised coordinate set. Conditioning changes which backbones are acceptable by fixing or describing structural content that the generator must accommodate.

### Chroma: correlated diffusion for efficient all-atom generation

Chroma generates all-atom protein structures from corrupted backbone heavy-atom coordinates. Its forward process uses correlated noise designed to reflect protein-chain statistics rather than perturbing every coordinate independently. A random graph neural network parameterizes the covariance, and an equivariant message-passing denoiser predicts the clean geometry.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s30-chroma.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Chroma forward polymer diffusion, reverse generation, equivariant denoising network, and time-dependent protein design conditions." caption="Chroma builds protein correlations into the diffusion covariance and reconstructs all-atom complexes from denoised backbones. The Nature 2023 figure also lists symmetry, substructure, shape, semantics, and other programmable conditions." %}

Correlated perturbations reduce the burden of learning long-range polymer structure from an isotropic-noise baseline. The design network then supplies the all-atom complex, while time-dependent priors and conditions steer generation toward requested symmetries, shapes, substructures, or semantic constraints.

### RFdiffusion: a folding network becomes a denoiser

RFdiffusion perturbs residue frames and adapts RoseTTAFold as the denoising network. The forward process destroys structure; the reverse process repeatedly predicts a cleaner backbone. Self-conditioning feeds a previous structural prediction back into the next denoising step.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s31-rfdiffusion.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RFdiffusion forward and reverse processes, RoseTTAFold denoising architecture, self-conditioning, and conditional design examples." caption="RFdiffusion fine-tunes RoseTTAFold into a backbone denoiser. The Nature 2023 figure connects the same architecture to unconditional generation, symmetric oligomers, binder design, motif scaffolding, and symmetric scaffolding." %}

The applications differ only in the conditioning contract, not in the meaning of success. Unconditional samples are judged as plausible backbones. Symmetric oligomers must satisfy a prescribed assembly symmetry. Binder designs must form a target interface. Motif scaffolds must preserve functional coordinates while generating the rest of the fold.

### FoldFlow: path design matters

FoldFlow returns to flow matching on residue frames and compares three probability paths. The base path uses a direct geometric interpolation. The optimal-transport path chooses a shorter, straighter coupling between base and data frames. The stochastic flow-matching path injects noise along the path to improve robustness in high dimensions.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s32-foldflow.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Three FoldFlow paths labeled Base, optimal transport, and stochastic flow matching, with generated protein frames." caption="FoldFlow compares three transports on \(\mathrm{SE}(3)^N\). The ICLR 2024 figure reports the highest designability for the optimal-transport path, while stochastic flow matching increases novelty and diversity." %}

The endpoints alone do not determine the regression problem. A curved or stochastic conditional path creates a different target velocity field from a short optimal-transport path. FoldFlow uses that fact as an architectural variable rather than treating interpolation as implementation detail.

### FoldFlow 2: sequence information conditions structure

FoldFlow 2 adds a sequence encoder and a multimodal fusion trunk to the structural flow. Single and pair sequence representations enter the same iterative denoising stack as structural representations, and a geometric decoder produces the updated residue frames.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s33-foldflow2.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="FoldFlow 2 structure and sequence encoders, multimodal fusion trunk, combiner module, and geometric decoder." caption="FoldFlow 2 conditions backbone flow on protein sequence through single and pair representations. The figure makes the sequence-to-structure and structure-to-sequence fusion paths explicit." %}

The task remains structure generation: the output is a denoised frame state. Sequence is supplied as conditioning information. This is the bridge to co-design, where sequence stops being fixed and becomes another generated variable.

## Joint sequence--structure co-design

Co-design generates continuous geometry and discrete residue identity at the same time. The lecture's triangle locates it between three one-directional tasks: sequence generation, structure generation, and forward or inverse folding. A joint model must combine continuous and categorical generative processes and must fuse their representations during sampling.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s34-codesign-map.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Triangle connecting sequence generation, structure generation, inverse folding, forward folding, and joint co-generation." caption="Co-design moves along the diagonal of the sequence--structure task map, generating both modalities rather than treating one as fixed conditioning. The lecture diagram preserves the relation to forward and inverse folding." %}

### ProtSeed: iterative geometric co-design

ProtSeed represents each residue with a type, a position, and a local orientation. Context-encoded single and pair features specify the design condition. Each layer updates residue identities and geometric states through sequence-aware and equivariant transformations, then returns the updated variables to the next layer.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s35-protseed.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ProtSeed layer iteratively updating residue-type distributions, orientations, positions, and single and pair context features." caption="ProtSeed co-designs sequence and structure through repeated geometric updates. The ICLR 2023 figure shows a trigonometry-aware encoder and equivariant structure transition inside one encoder--decoder framework." %}

The geometric constraints come from the context features and the equivariant transition. Sequence cannot be updated independently of structure because a residue choice changes local chemical compatibility; structure cannot be updated independently of sequence because residue identity changes the preferred environment.

### ProteinGenerator: relax residue categories into a continuous space

ProteinGenerator embeds a categorical protein sequence into a continuous variable, diffuses that variable, and couples it to a RoseTTAFold structure space. A single denoising system can then update relaxed sequence variables and backbone coordinates while receiving constraints such as a motif, target, or active site.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s36-protein-generator.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ProteinGenerator with a continuous sequence space, diffusion process, RoseTTAFold structure space, self-conditioning, and motif or active-site conditions." caption="ProteinGenerator converts residue categories into continuous variables so sequence and backbone can be denoised together. The Nature Biotechnology 2024 figure shows RoseTTAFold as the structural denoiser." %}

The final sequence still has to return to a discrete alphabet. Continuous relaxation makes gradient-based diffusion convenient, but the conversion to amino-acid identities is part of the generative model rather than a cosmetic rounding step.

### MultiFlow: three flows in one model

MultiFlow assigns a generative process to each component of the residue state. Translations follow Euclidean interpolation. Rotations follow a geodesic exponential-map path. Amino acids follow a discrete flow from a mask-like categorical prior to residue identities.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s37-multiflow.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="MultiFlow translation, rotation, and amino-acid paths with discrete probability flow and jump-rate equations." caption="MultiFlow combines Euclidean translation, manifold rotation, and categorical amino-acid flows on one time axis. The ICML 2024 figure keeps the three mathematical transports separate inside a single generative framework." %}

For a residue category, the discrete state evolves through time-dependent jump rates rather than an ordinary coordinate velocity. The probability vector $$\mathbf{p}_t$$ follows a master equation,

$$
\partial_t\mathbf{p}_t=R_t^{\top}\mathbf{p}_t,
$$

where $$R_t(i,j)$$ is the rate of jumping from category $$i$$ to $$j$$. A small step samples the next residue identity from probabilities derived from these rates. Sharing time and multimodal features allows the three flows to condition one another without pretending that rotations, translations, and amino-acid categories are the same mathematical object.

### Protpardelle: all-atom co-design

Protpardelle raises the structural resolution from backbone frames to all atoms. Its denoising process operates on a superposition of the protein backbone and coordinates for twenty possible side chains at each residue. Backbone atoms, side-chain atoms, and sequence identities can follow different noise schedules.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s38-protpardelle.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protpardelle denoising trajectory over backbone and possible side-chain atoms with different schedules for structural components." caption="Protpardelle generates sequence together with all-atom structure. The PNAS 2024 figure shows why backbone and side-chain variables need separate denoising schedules during diffusion sampling." %}

All-atom output moves chemical detail into the generated state. Side-chain conformations and steric interactions no longer wait for a separate packing stage, but simultaneous updates also increase the dimensionality and coupling of the denoising problem.

## Antibody design and affinity

An antibody binds an antigen through variable domains carried by heavy and light chains. Each variable domain alternates framework regions with three complementarity-determining regions, CDR1--CDR3. The framework supports the fold; the CDR loops form much of the paratope and dominate binding specificity and neutralization.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s39-antibody-anatomy.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Antibody heavy and light chains binding an antigen, with variable heavy and light domains divided into framework and three CDR regions." caption="Antibody design concentrates sequence and geometry changes in the CDRs while retaining framework context. The lecture diagram labels the antigen-facing paratope and the alternating framework/CDR organization." %}

This specialization changes the conditioning problem. General protein co-design can generate an isolated fold. Antibody design must also account for a target antigen, a partial framework, and an interface whose geometry determines binding.

### RefineGNN: CDR sequence--structure co-design

RefineGNN treats the antibody as a graph whose nodes are residues and whose edges encode geometric interactions. A message-passing model predicts amino-acid identities, updates sequence context, predicts coordinates, and updates structure. Coarsened framework sequence provides multi-resolution context around the CDR being generated.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s40-refinegnn.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RefineGNN alternating graph message passing, amino-acid prediction, coordinate prediction, and sequence and structure updates for antibody CDRs." caption="RefineGNN formulates CDR sequence--structure co-design as iterative graph generation. The ICLR 2022 figure shows sequence and coordinate refinement under framework context." %}

The output is both the CDR sequence and its conformation. Framework residues constrain the generated loop even when they are not themselves redesigned, so the model is conditional co-design rather than unconstrained antibody generation.

### HERN: add the antigen to autoregressive generation

HERN adds explicit antigen information. It generates a paratope one residue at a time while conditioning on the epitope. Each partial sequence is embedded together with the antibody--antigen geometry, and a docking model infers the hidden interaction state used to choose the next residue.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s41-hern.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="HERN comparing low- and high-affinity antibodies and generating a CDR paratope conditioned on an antigen epitope." caption="HERN makes the antigen epitope part of paratope generation. The ICML 2022 figure connects affinity, complex geometry, and an autoregressively produced CDR." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s41-hern-docking-step.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="HERN hierarchical encoder and one-step docking update between two autoregressive residue predictions." caption="One HERN decoding step alternates residue prediction with docking. The figure shows the geometric point cloud changing before the next amino acid is selected." %}

The repeated docking step matters because the interface is not fixed while the paratope grows. Adding one residue changes the partial loop and its possible contacts, which changes the geometric context for the next categorical decision.

### DSMBind: learn an affinity landscape to guide design

DSMBind separates proposal from scoring. A generative model proposes CDR sequences and builds antibody--antigen complexes. A second model assigns a binding energy, allowing candidates to be ranked or used to guide the generation process.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s42-dsmbind-affinity.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DSMBind pipeline generating antibody CDR sequences, constructing antigen-bound complexes, and predicting binding energies." caption="DSMBind connects paratope generation to learned antibody--antigen binding energy. The NeurIPS 2023 figure makes candidate proposal and affinity prediction two explicit stages." %}

The affinity model also learns a likelihood over three-dimensional structures with denoising score matching. Random rotations preserve the physical energy, while coordinate perturbations move a stable complex away from a local minimum. The score of a Boltzmann-like structural density satisfies

$$
\nabla_{\mathbf{X}}\log p(\mathbf{X})
=-\beta\nabla_{\mathbf{X}}E(\mathbf{X}),
$$

so the physical descent force $$-\nabla_{\mathbf{X}}E(\mathbf{X})$$ points toward lower-energy structures and transforms equivariantly under rotation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s43-dsmbind-score.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DSMBind energy landscape with a randomly rotated unstable complex, a force toward a stable experimental structure, and a neural binding-energy model." caption="DSMBind learns structural likelihood through denoising score matching. The figure links an invariant binding energy to an equivariant coordinate force that returns perturbed complexes toward local minima." %}

This learned energy is useful only to the extent that it ranks the proposed complexes correctly. Generator and affinity model may share data or geometric assumptions, so agreement between them is computational evidence rather than an independent experiment.

### Wet-lab validation of PD-L1 nanobodies

The lecture ends with designed PD-L1 nanobodies R16 and M24. Their modeled complexes place the designed CDRs at the PD-L1 interface. The table reports their CDR1--CDR3 sequences, and the ELISA assay compares binding signal against the PBST background at $$200$$ nM and $$500$$ nM.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/s44-dsmbind-wetlab.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="R16 and M24 PD-L1 nanobody complexes, their CDR sequences, and ELISA binding box plots at 200 and 500 nanomolar." caption="Wet-lab ELISA measurements test DSMBind-designed PD-L1 nanobodies after generation and computational ranking. M24 is reported at approximately $$200$$ nM affinity despite less than $$0.3$$ sequence identity to known nanobodies. The measured binding signal is the final observable in the pipeline. A plausible structure, a favorable learned energy, and a novel sequence establish intermediate claims; synthesis and assay determine whether a physical candidate binds the intended antigen.

The entire lecture can be read through the object handed from one stage to the next. Ensemble models generate independent conformations. Trajectory models generate lagged transitions or complete ordered paths. Sequence designers output residue identities that still require folding. Backbone generators output geometry that still requires inverse folding. Co-design models output both modalities. Antibody systems add a target interface, an affinity score, and finally an experiment. Geometric deep learning supplies reusable symmetry-aware operations across all of them, but it does not erase the difference between the probability laws or the evidence attached to each output.
