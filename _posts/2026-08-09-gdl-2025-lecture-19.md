## Learning protein structure distributions

A protein does not have one unique structure. Its sequence defines an energy landscape with many accessible conformations, and conditions or binding partners can shift which basins are occupied. A model that returns only one coordinate set cannot represent this distribution.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-3.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="One protein target associated with several conformations rather than one unique structure." caption="Protein structure is a distribution over conformations, not a unique minimum. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 3." %}

Boltzmann Generators train an invertible generative model to sample equilibrium states of a many-body system. Simulation supplies configurations, while the energy function supplies a physical target density. The learned map aims to produce independent equilibrium samples rather than a temporally ordered MD trajectory.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-4.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Boltzmann generator training from simulation and energy for many body configurations." caption="Boltzmann Generators learn an equilibrium sampler using configuration data and target-system energies. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 4." %}

“Two for One” recasts this idea with diffusion. The same score-based model can learn a coarse-grained equilibrium distribution and provide a force field through the gradient of the learned density.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-5.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Diffusion model used as both Boltzmann generator and coarse grained force field." caption="A diffusion score can support both equilibrium sampling and a learned coarse-grained force. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 5." %}

EigenFold uses diffusion for generative structure prediction. Its examples distinguish apo and holo conformations: the unbound and ligand-bound states can occupy different regions of conformational space, so a conditional distribution is more appropriate than one deterministic prediction.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-6.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="EigenFold diffusion samples for apo and holo protein conformations." caption="Generative folding can represent alternative apo and holo conformations rather than one point estimate. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 6." %}

ConfDiff combines an $$\mathrm{SE}(3)$$ diffusion model with energy and force guidance. The learned structural prior proposes conformations, while physical guidance steers samples toward more plausible regions.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-7.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ConfDiff force guided SE3 protein conformation generation." caption="ConfDiff guides geometric diffusion with energy and force information. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 7." %}

Distributional Graphormer predicts equilibrium distributions for molecular systems. Its architecture separates a structural encoder from a distributional decoder and evaluates the generated ensemble against reference structural statistics.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-8.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Distributional Graphormer architecture and molecular equilibrium samples." caption="Distributional prediction targets a set of equilibrium configurations rather than one optimized structure. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 8." %}

AlphaFlow starts from AlphaFold representations and replaces deterministic coordinate prediction with flow matching. Conditioning on sequence and learned folding features yields multiple structures from a generative trajectory.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-9.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFlow combining AlphaFold with flow matching for protein ensembles." caption="AlphaFlow turns a folding architecture into a conditional ensemble generator. ESMDiff treats structure as a language-like sequence of tokens and applies masked diffusion initialized from pretrained ESM-3. A bidirectional Transformer denoises structural tokens to generate alternative conformations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-10.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ESMDiff structure language model and masked diffusion pipeline." caption="A structure tokenizer lets masked diffusion generate conformations in a learned discrete representation. ## Emulating molecular dynamics trajectories

Molecular dynamics solves Newton's equations on an energy landscape. Forces are negative energy gradients, an integrator advances coordinates and velocities, and repeated steps produce a time-ordered trajectory. An equilibrium sampler need not preserve this temporal law.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-13.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Molecular dynamics on an energy landscape with Newtonian force and numerical integration." caption="MD trajectories are generated by force-driven time integration, not by independent equilibrium sampling. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 13." %}

CGMD accelerates coarse-grained simulation by learning time-integrated updates. A multi-scale graph network maps a state at time $$t$$ directly toward a later state, replacing many small integration steps with one learned jump.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-14.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Multi scale graph network for time integrated coarse grained molecular dynamics." caption="CGMD learns a coarse state transition that skips multiple conventional MD steps. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 14." %}

Implicit Transfer Operator learning models several time resolutions with one surrogate. Conditioning on the lag lets the model approximate transitions across short and long intervals rather than fixing one step size.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-15.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Implicit transfer operator learning at multiple molecular dynamics time resolutions." caption="A lag-conditioned transfer operator represents state propagation over several temporal scales. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 15." %}

TimeWarp uses a conditional normalizing flow as a transfer operator. RealNVP coupling layers model a time-coarsened transition and were demonstrated on small peptides of two to four amino acids.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-16.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="TimeWarp conditional normalizing flow for time coarsened peptide dynamics." caption="TimeWarp learns a conditional distribution over a later molecular state rather than integrating every intermediate frame. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 16." %}

MDGen generates a whole molecular dynamics trajectory conditionally, comparing the task to video generation. The output contains correlated frames across a specified time window, so both the marginal conformations and their temporal ordering are modeled.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-17.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="MDGen conditional generation of a complete molecular dynamics trajectory." caption="MDGen treats an MD trajectory as a structured temporal object rather than a set of independent structures. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 17." %}

## Protein design targets

Protein design generates molecules intended to realize a desired function. The lecture separates sequence design, structure design, and joint sequence--structure co-design. ESM-3's generated fluorescent protein illustrates the endpoint, but the three problem formulations expose different variables to the model.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-19.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Generated green fluorescent protein and three protein design task classes." caption="Protein design can choose sequence, structure, or both while conditioning on functional intent. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 19." %}

Sequence data are much more abundant than experimental structures, which makes sequence-model training easier to scale. Sequence generators divide by their input: properties or a partial sequence can condition completion, whereas a backbone conditions inverse folding.

Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 20." %}

Generating a complete three-dimensional protein backbone is harder than decoding a discrete sequence. The architecture history in the lecture follows the broader evolution of geometric diffusion and flow models.

FrameDiff diffuses residue rigid bodies in three dimensions. Its state matches the orientation and translation representation of the backbone rather than applying unconstrained noise to every atom independently.

## Antibody design and affinity

Antibodies bind antigens through variable domains carried by heavy and light chains. Within each variable domain, framework regions support complementarity-determining regions; these CDR loops form much of the paratope and dominate binding specificity.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-39.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Antibody heavy light chains variable domains CDRs and paratope." caption="Antibody design concentrates on CDR sequence and geometry while preserving the surrounding framework. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 39." %}

RefineGNN performs antibody sequence--structure co-design. A geometric graph network iteratively refines the CDR sequence and coordinates within the antibody context.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-40.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RefineGNN antibody sequence structure co-design model." caption="RefineGNN jointly updates CDR residue identities and conformations. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 40." %}

HERN adds explicit antigen information. It generates the paratope autoregressively, adding one residue at a time and using a docking model to infer a geometric hidden state from the partial antibody--antigen interface.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-41.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="HERN antigen conditioned autoregressive paratope generation." caption="HERN conditions each partial CDR extension on its evolving docked relation to the antigen. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 41." %}

DSMBind learns antibody--antigen binding affinity and uses the resulting energy model to guide design. It proposes CDR sequences, constructs the complex, and predicts an interaction score.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-42.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DSMBind antibody CDR generation and binding energy prediction." caption="DSMBind connects sequence proposal to a learned antibody--antigen interaction energy. The affinity model is trained through denoising score matching on three-dimensional complexes. Randomly rotating the protein changes its coordinates but not its energy; the learned force, the negative coordinate gradient of that energy, should transform consistently and move perturbed complexes toward stable structures.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-19/slide-43.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DSMBind denoising score matching energy and force on antibody antigen complexes." caption="Denoising score matching turns structural perturbations into an equivariant force-learning signal for affinity. Cropped from the 2025 GDL protein-ensembles-and-design lecture, slide 43." %}

This closes the design chain from geometric generative modeling through a learned affinity objective to wet-lab binding.
