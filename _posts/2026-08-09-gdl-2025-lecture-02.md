## From data geometry to equivariant networks

Geometric deep learning begins with a design question: given the geometry of a dataset, what is the recipe for a neural architecture?

Geometry studies measurements and relationships on a space. Euclidean, hyperbolic, and Riemannian geometries differ because they define different transformations and invariants.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/s10-image11.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Euclidean plane, hyperbolic surface, and spherical geometry." caption="Different spaces support different notions of distance and symmetry. The geometry of data is specified by transformations that preserve object identity. A cat remains the same cat after changes that the task declares irrelevant.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/s11-image12.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Several transformed images recognized as the same cat." caption="A data symmetry changes the representation without changing the underlying object identity. An equivariant neural network does not merely learn this relationship from augmented examples. Its operations are constructed to commute with the transformations.

## Symmetry of graphs and geometric graphs

An object is symmetric with respect to an operation when applying that operation preserves a specified property. The collection of property-preserving operations forms a group.

### Graph representation and permutation

A graph with $$n$$ nodes can be represented by an adjacency matrix $$A\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$$ and node features $$X\in\mathbb R^{n\times d}$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-18.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Graph, adjacency matrix, node-feature matrix, and molecule example." caption="A stored graph uses node indices even though the scientific object does not assign physical meaning to those indices. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 18." %}

Relabeling vertices by a permutation matrix $$P$$ changes the stored tensors as

$$
X\mapsto PX,
\qquad
A\mapsto PAP^\top,
$$

without changing the graph.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-19.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Same graph before and after vertex permutation with transformed matrices." caption="Permutation symmetry identifies relabeled adjacency and feature matrices as the same graph. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 19." %}

The word permutation is incomplete until its action is specified. The same abstract relabeling acts as $$P$$ on node features and as $$A\mapsto PAP^\top$$ on pairwise features.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-20.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Permutation acting differently on node features and adjacency matrices." caption="One group element has different concrete actions on node-wise and pairwise data spaces. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 20." %}

A graph-level property such as toxicity should be invariant:

$$
f(PAP^\top,PX)=f(A,X).
$$

A node-wise prediction should be equivariant:

$$
F(PAP^\top,PX)=PF(A,X).
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-21.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Permutation-invariant graph property and permutation-equivariant node property." caption="Graph-level outputs remain fixed, while node-level outputs follow the relabeling. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 21." %}

If the output is a distance matrix $$D$$, it transforms as $$D\mapsto PDP^\top$$. Equivariance always depends on both the input and output action.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-22.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Graph neural network predicting a pairwise distance matrix under permutation." caption="A pairwise output carries a two-index permutation action rather than the one-index node action. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 22." %}

### Adding coordinates

A geometric graph adds coordinates $$R\in\mathbb R^{n\times 3}$$. Translation and rotation act on those coordinates, while scalar molecular properties remain invariant and forces transform as vectors. Hamiltonian blocks and other tensor outputs may carry higher-order actions.

## Groups as mathematical structure

Mathematics enriches a set with additional structure. Here two spaces matter: the space of data, such as molecular coordinates, and the space of transformations, such as rotations.

### Algebraic and topological structure

Algebraic structures add operations. A monoid has a closed associative product and identity; a group additionally requires inverses. Rings, fields, and vector spaces add further operations or axioms.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-26.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Hierarchy of algebraic structures and monoid axioms." caption="Groups belong to a hierarchy of sets equipped with algebraic operations. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 26." %}

Topological structures instead formalize closeness through open sets, metrics, simplicial complexes, and manifolds.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-27.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Simplicial complex, metric path on a sphere, and topologically equivalent objects." caption="Topology and metrics describe neighborhood and continuity, complementing algebraic symmetry. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 27." %}

A group $$(G,\cdot)$$ satisfies closure, associativity, an identity element, and an inverse for every element.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-28.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Definition and four axioms of a group." caption="The group axioms formalize composable, reversible transformations. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 28." %}

The product need not commute. Abelian groups satisfy $$gh=hg$$; non-Abelian groups do not. Rotations in three dimensions provide a standard noncommutative example.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-29.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Two three-dimensional rotations applied in opposite orders with different outcomes." caption="Three-dimensional rotations demonstrate why transformation composition can be noncommutative. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 29." %}

### Matrix, permutation, orthogonal, and Euclidean groups

The general linear group $$GL(n,\mathbb R)$$ consists of invertible real $$n\times n$$ matrices under multiplication.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-30.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="General linear group definition and matrix group axioms." caption="Invertible matrices form a group because matrix multiplication is closed, associative, and invertible. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 30." %}

The symmetric group $$S_n$$ consists of all permutations of $$n$$ elements under composition. Permutation matrices embed $$S_n$$ as a subgroup of $$GL(n,\mathbb R)$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-31.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Symmetric group S4 represented by permutation matrices." caption="The symmetric group connects abstract relabelings to linear matrix actions. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 31." %}

The orthogonal group

$$
O(n)=\{Q\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}:Q^\top Q=I\}
$$

contains rotations and reflections that preserve Euclidean distance while fixing the origin. Its special subgroup $$SO(n)$$ has determinant $$+1$$ and excludes reflections.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-32.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Orthogonal and special orthogonal group definitions." caption="Orthogonal transformations preserve inner products and distance; special orthogonal transformations preserve orientation. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 32." %}

The Euclidean group $$E(n)$$ adds translations to orthogonal transformations. $$SE(n)$$ keeps only orientation-preserving rotations and translations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-33.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Euclidean group transformations combining translation, rotation, and reflection." caption="Euclidean isometries preserve distance while moving the origin and possibly reversing orientation. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 33." %}

Representing $$g\in SE(n)$$ as $$(R,t)$$ gives the semidirect product law

$$
(R,t)(R',t')=(RR',Rt'+t),
$$

and inverse $$(R,t)^{-1}=(R^{-1},-R^{-1}t)$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-34.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SE(n) composition, inverse, and homogeneous matrix representation." caption="The Euclidean-group product rotates a translation before adding it, which makes the structure a semidirect product. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 34." %}

Three-dimensional rotations can be represented by an axis and angle, three Euler rotations, a quaternion, or a rotation matrix. These are coordinate systems for the same group element, with different singularities and redundancies.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-35.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Axis-angle and Euler-angle representations of three-dimensional rotation." caption="Several parameterizations encode one SO(3) rotation, each with its own numerical tradeoffs. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 35." %}

## Actions, homogeneous spaces, and quotients

A group becomes relevant to data through an action. A left action maps $$(g,x)$$ to $$g\cdot x$$ and satisfies compatibility $$(gh)\cdot x=g\cdot(h\cdot x)$$ and identity $$e\cdot x=x$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-36.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SO(3) group element acting on a point on the sphere." caption="A group action specifies how an abstract transformation changes an object in a domain. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 36." %}

The same rotation can act on a vector, a triangle, or a molecular coordinate array. The action law guarantees that composing rotations before or after applying them gives the same result.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-37.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Rotation action on vectors and triangles with compatibility law." caption="Group-action compatibility connects transformation composition to repeated action on data. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 37." %}

An action is transitive when every point can be reached from every other point by some group element. A space with a transitive $$G$$-action is homogeneous.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-38.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Transitive and nontransitive group actions illustrated on several spaces." caption="A homogeneous space is generated by moving one reference point through the group action. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 38." %}

The stabilizer of $$x$$ is

$$
\operatorname{Stab}(x)=\{g\in G:g\cdot x=x\}.
$$

For a point on the sphere, rotations about its axis form a stabilizer; when the group acts on itself by left multiplication, only the identity fixes a point.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-39.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Stabilizers of a point on a sphere and of an element under group self-action." caption="A stabilizer records transformations that are invisible at a chosen point. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 39." %}

Given a subgroup $$H\le G$$, a left coset is $$gH=\{gh:h\in H\}$$. Cosets partition the group.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-40.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Group partitioned into cosets of a subgroup." caption="Cosets collect transformations that differ only by multiplication with a subgroup element. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 40." %}

The quotient $$G/H$$ is the set of distinct cosets. A homogeneous space can often be identified with $$G/H$$: transformations differing by a stabilizer element reach the same point.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-41.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Sphere represented as a quotient of rotations by a point stabilizer." caption="A quotient removes the transformations that leave the reference point unchanged. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 41." %}

For a graph example, quotienting $$S_3$$ by its cyclic subgroup groups permutations into two cosets corresponding to the two orientations of a three-cycle.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-42.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="S3 quotient by the cyclic permutation subgroup with two cosets." caption="The finite S3 example makes coset and quotient construction explicit. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 42." %}

## Representations, steerability, and equivariance

A group representation maps each group element to an invertible linear map $$\rho(g)$$ on a vector space, preserving multiplication:

$$
\rho(gh)=\rho(g)\rho(h),
\qquad
\rho(e)=I.
$$

Vectors transforming as $$v\mapsto\rho(g)v$$ are steerable features.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-43.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Definition of a group representation and steerable feature vector." caption="A representation converts abstract group elements into linear transformations of features. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 43." %}

For graphs, node features use $$P$$, while vectorized adjacency features use the Kronecker-product representation $$P\otimes P$$. These are distinct representations of the same permutation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-44.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Permutation representations on graph node features and vectorized adjacency matrices." caption="Feature type is determined by the representation through which the group acts. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 44." %}

### Circular and spherical harmonics

Circular harmonics provide steerable features for planar rotations. For each angular frequency $$k$$, the pair $$(\cos k\theta,\sin k\theta)$$ mixes through a two-dimensional rotation matrix when the input angle changes.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-45.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Circular harmonic vector transformed by a two-dimensional rotation representation." caption="Circular harmonics turn rotation of the argument into a linear transformation of feature coefficients. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 45." %}

The degree controls angular frequency and the corresponding feature transformation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-46.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Circular harmonics of several degrees and their rotation behavior." caption="Higher-degree circular harmonics oscillate more rapidly while remaining steerable under SO(2). Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 46." %}

Spherical harmonics $$Y_{\ell m}$$ form an orthonormal basis for functions on the sphere. Degree $$\ell$$ determines the frequency band, and order $$m=-\ell,\ldots,\ell$$ indexes its $$2\ell+1$$ components.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-47.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Grid of real spherical harmonics arranged by degree and order." caption="Spherical harmonics organize angular functions into finite-dimensional degree blocks. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 47." %}

Under a three-dimensional rotation, the degree-$$\ell$$ block transforms through the Wigner $$D^{(\ell)}(g)$$ matrix:

$$
Y^{(\ell)}(g\cdot \hat r)=D^{(\ell)}(g)Y^{(\ell)}(\hat r).
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-48.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Spherical-harmonic vector transformed by a Wigner D matrix under rotation." caption="Wigner matrices are the SO(3) representations carried by spherical-harmonic feature blocks. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 48." %}

Steerable vectors therefore have types. Scalars, ordinary vectors, and higher-order tensor or harmonic features transform through different representations; tensor products can construct higher-order types from lower-order ones.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-49.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Scalar, vector, tensor, and spherical-harmonic steerable feature types." caption="Feature order is shorthand for the group representation carried by that channel. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 49." %}

Finally, an operator $$\Phi:X\to Y$$ is equivariant when it commutes with the group actions:

$$
\Phi\big(\rho_X(g)x\big)=\rho_Y(g)\Phi(x).
$$

Invariance is the special case in which $$\rho_Y(g)=I$$. The input and output representations may differ, as they do for molecular coordinates, energies, forces, and tensor properties.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-02/slide-50.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Equivariant molecular operator commuting with permutation and rotation actions." caption="Equivariance is a commuting relation between the chosen input action, operator, and output action. Cropped from the 2025 GDL symmetry and equivariance lecture, slide 50." %}

This completes the source's progression. Graph relabeling first shows why one transformation needs several actions. Group theory then formalizes composition and inverse, actions connect transformations to data, stabilizers and quotients describe homogeneous spaces, and representations specify how features transform. Equivariant neural networks are the operators that preserve this entire structure.
