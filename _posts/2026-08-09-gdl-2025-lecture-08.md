## Complete local frames

An equivariant network can safely form new features from invariant nonlinearities, invariant scalar gates, linear combinations of vectors, inner products, and cross products. These operations are useful, but they do not by themselves say how to expose an entire three-dimensional neighborhood to an ordinary nonlinear message-passing network.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-4.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Invariant and equivariant operations used to construct geometric features." caption="Invariant scalar functions, scalar gates, vector combinations, dot products, and cross products form the basic equivariant operation set. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 4." %}

ClofNet constructs a complete local frame for each interacting pair. Given position vectors $$x_i$$ and $$x_j$$, it uses

$$
a_{ij}=\frac{x_i-x_j}{\lVert x_i-x_j\rVert},
\qquad
b_{ij}=\frac{x_i\times x_j}{\lVert x_i\times x_j\rVert},
\qquad
c_{ij}=a_{ij}\times b_{ij}.
$$

An EGNN-like update uses only the relative direction $$x_i-x_j$$. The three frame vectors instead span three-dimensional space whenever the construction is non-degenerate.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-5.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ClofNet local frame constructed from two interacting positions." caption="ClofNet replaces a single relative direction with a local three-axis frame for each interaction. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 5." %}

The layer alternates three operations. Scalarization projects geometric features onto the frame. An ordinary graph network transforms the resulting invariant coefficients. Vectorization reconstructs geometric outputs from those coefficients and the same frame.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-6.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ClofNet scalarization message passing and vectorization sequence." caption="A local frame separates geometric bookkeeping from nonlinear scalar message passing. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 6." %}

For vector inputs, scalarization records their coordinates along the three axes:

$$
s_{ij}^{(t)}=
\bigl(
x_i^{(t)}\!\cdot a_{ij}^{(t)},
x_i^{(t)}\!\cdot b_{ij}^{(t)},
x_i^{(t)}\!\cdot c_{ij}^{(t)},
x_j^{(t)}\!\cdot a_{ij}^{(t)},
x_j^{(t)}\!\cdot b_{ij}^{(t)},
x_j^{(t)}\!\cdot c_{ij}^{(t)}
\bigr).
$$

The same idea extends to higher-order geometric tensors: express the object in a transforming local basis, then learn from its invariant coefficients.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-7.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ClofNet scalarization equations in an edge-local frame." caption="Projection onto an equivariant frame produces invariant coefficients for both endpoints of an edge. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 7." %}

Scalar messages are aggregated with an ordinary nonlinear graph layer. If the learned message returns three coefficients, vectorization maps them back as

$$
\operatorname{Vectorize}(m_{ij},F_{ij})
=m_{ij}^{a}a_{ij}+m_{ij}^{b}b_{ij}+m_{ij}^{c}c_{ij}.
$$

Because the coefficients are invariant while the basis vectors transform equivariantly, their linear combination has the required transformation law.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-8.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ClofNet scalar message and vector reconstruction equations." caption="Nonlinear scalar processing is followed by reconstruction in the edge-local frame. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 8." %}

The full architecture scalarizes once, performs scalar message passing, updates the frames, and then passes vector-valued messages. This keeps the expensive geometric conversion separate from the backbone computation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-9.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Full ClofNet architecture with scalar and vector message paths." caption="ClofNet organizes scalarization, invariant message passing, frame updates, and vectorization as distinct blocks. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 9." %}

The frame is complete only away from degeneracies such as vanishing cross products. Under the non-degeneracy assumptions, vector features are expanded in an orthogonal basis of $$\mathbb R^3$$, which supports the architecture's universality result.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-10.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ClofNet universality statement based on non-degenerate complete frames." caption="Completeness of the local basis is the condition behind ClofNet's universal approximation argument. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 10." %}

ClofNet was evaluated on molecular conformer generation for GEOM-QM9 and GEOM-Drugs, with especially strong results on the larger and more varied drug-like structures.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-11.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ClofNet molecular conformer generation results." caption="The complete-frame construction was tested through conformer generation on GEOM-QM9 and GEOM-Drugs. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 11." %}

## Encoding transitions between local frames

A complete frame for one edge does not settle how information should move between different frames. LEFTNet addresses this question through geometric graph isomorphism. Two configurations are globally isomorphic if one special-Euclidean transformation maps one to the other; local notions compare neighborhoods with increasingly strict geometric information.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-12.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="LEFTNet motivation and its local substructure and frame transition modules." caption="LEFTNet separates edge-local substructure encoding from communication across different local frames. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 12." %}

Tree isometry retains only a tree-shaped neighborhood. Triangular isometry also retains the triangles and angles around the edge. Subgraph isometry asks for an isomorphism of the complete local subgraphs.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-13.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Tree triangular and subgraph notions of local geometric isomorphism." caption="LEFTNet evaluates local expressivity through a hierarchy of increasingly informative neighborhood equivalences. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 13." %}

The hierarchy is strict: two neighborhoods can share their tree structure but differ in triangles, and can share triangular relations without being fully subgraph-isomorphic.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-14.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Hierarchy among tree triangular and subgraph isometry." caption="Each stronger local-isometry notion resolves configurations collapsed by the preceding one. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 14." %}

LEFTNet combines local substructure encoding (LSE) with frame transition encoding (FTE). LSE constructs an edge-specific description of the shared neighborhood. FTE then aggregates messages while accounting for the change of frame between edges or atoms.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-15.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="LEFTNet LSE and FTE architecture blocks." caption="LSE supplies structural weights; FTE transports information between locally defined coordinate systems. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 15." %}

For edge $$(i,j)$$, LSE considers the intersection of the endpoint neighborhoods. It scalarizes every point in this shared substructure in an edge frame, applies an MLP, and averages the resulting node features into a structural weight $$A_{ij}$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-16.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="LEFTNet local substructure encoding equations." caption="Edge-local scalarization encodes triangles shared by the two endpoint neighborhoods. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 16." %}

FTE uses $$A_{ij}$$ inside the message and scalarizes that message in an atom-wise frame before tensorizing it again. The explicit scalarize--tensorize pair is what lets information cross frames without treating their coordinates as directly comparable.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-17.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="LEFTNet frame transition encoding message equations." caption="Frame-transition encoding moves a learned message through invariant coordinates before reconstructing a geometric feature. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 17." %}

LEFTNet reports strong QM9 results under two data splits and strong MD17 energy and force errors. These experiments test whether the added frame-transition information improves both invariant properties and derivative targets.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-18.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="LEFTNet QM9 benchmark results under two splits." caption="QM9 comparisons evaluate the expressivity of LSE and FTE for molecular properties. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 18." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-19.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="LEFTNet MD17 energy and force results." caption="MD17 evaluates whether frame-aware messages also improve learned potential-energy surfaces and forces. ## Scaling local-frame models

Local-frame methods were initially attractive because scalarization and vectorization make their layers simple. More general steerable architectures later became dominant, but AlphaNet argues that frame-based models can remain competitive when the transition and long-range mechanisms are improved.

Its two main additions are rotary positional embeddings for frame transitions and temporal connections for long-range interactions.

## From group averaging to frame averaging

The second half changes scale. Instead of building a coordinate system for every interaction, it asks how to impose symmetry on a backbone that operates in one global coordinate system.

A symmetry belongs to a group: composition is associative, an identity exists, every element has an inverse, and the set is closed under composition.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-24.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Group axioms illustrated with image transformations." caption="The group axioms organize the transformations over which a symmetrization procedure averages. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 24." %}

A representation $$\rho(g)$$ specifies how the same abstract symmetry acts on a particular data space. A 90-degree rotation is one group element, but its matrix representation depends on whether it acts on coordinates, a small image, or a large image.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-25.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Group action and representation on signals of different types." caption="The abstract group element and its data-space representation must be kept distinct. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 25." %}

For a graph with node features $$X$$ and adjacency matrix $$A$$, a permutation matrix $$P$$ acts as

$$
X\mapsto PX,
\qquad
A\mapsto PAP^\top.
$$

The same group-action language covers permutations and Euclidean transformations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-26.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Permutation action on graph nodes features and adjacency." caption="Permutation symmetry acts consistently on node-indexed features and both indices of the adjacency matrix. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 26." %}

The global-frame section compares four approaches: exact frame averaging, stochastic frame averaging for materials, learned canonicalization, and probabilistic symmetrization.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-27.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Overview of global frame based geometric GNN methods." caption="The source moves from averaging over a finite frame to learning deterministic or probabilistic canonicalizers. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 27." %}

Group averaging supplies the general recipe. One can make an ordinary image model invariant to 90-degree rotations by evaluating it on every rotated input and averaging the outputs.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-28.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Rotation averaging applied to an ordinary image model." caption="Averaging a backbone over transformed inputs removes dependence on the chosen representative. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 28." %}

The same construction applies to an arbitrary finite group, but its cost grows with the number of group elements. Frame averaging replaces the full group with a small input-dependent set while preserving the required transformation law.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-29.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Comparison of group averaging and frame averaging." caption="Frame averaging seeks the symmetry guarantee of group averaging with a much smaller evaluation set. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 29." %}

Let $$\rho_1$$ act on inputs and $$\rho_2$$ act on outputs. For a finite group, the invariant average of a backbone $$\phi$$ is

$$
\Phi_{\mathrm{inv}}(X)
=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\phi\!\left(\rho_1(g)^{-1}X\right).
$$

Replacing $$X$$ by $$\rho_1(h)X$$ merely reindexes the sum, so the result is invariant.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-30.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Symmetry operations and data representations used in group averaging." caption="Group averaging requires the group action on the input to be specified explicitly. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 30." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-31.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Proof that group averaging produces an invariant function." caption="The invariance proof is a change of summation index over the group. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 31." %}

For an equivariant output, transform each prediction back into the output coordinate system before averaging:

$$
\Phi_{\mathrm{eq}}(X)
=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}
\rho_2(g)\,
\phi\!\left(\rho_1(g)^{-1}X\right).
$$

The same reindexing proof now leaves a factor $$\rho_2(h)$$ outside the sum.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-32.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Proof that group averaging produces an equivariant function." caption="Output-side transformation converts invariant averaging into an equivariant construction. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 32." %}

Exact averaging is infeasible for continuous groups such as $$E(3)$$. A frame $$F(X)$$ is a finite, input-dependent set of transformations that itself transforms equivariantly:

$$
F\!\left(\rho_1(g)X\right)=gF(X).
$$

This set-valued condition is what lets the finite sum inherit the group-averaging proof.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-33.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Infinite group averaging replaced by a finite input dependent frame." caption="A finite frame replaces an intractable integral over continuous Euclidean transformations. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 33." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-34.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Definition of an equivariant set valued frame." caption="The frame must move with the input as a set, even when no single canonical element is continuous or unique. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 34." %}

Replacing $$G$$ by $$F(X)$$ yields finite invariant and equivariant averages. Their proofs are identical once the set-valued frame condition holds.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-35.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Invariant and equivariant frame averaging equations." caption="Frame averaging retains the transformed-input and transformed-output recipes of full group averaging. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 35." %}

The expressivity theorem states that frame averaging can represent any equivariant function when the unconstrained backbone is sufficiently expressive.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-36.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Universality theorem for frame averaged functions." caption="Frame averaging transfers the capacity of the backbone into an equivariant hypothesis class. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 36." %}

For point clouds, a practical frame comes from PCA. Center the points, compute covariance eigenvectors $$v_1,\ldots,v_d$$, and include every sign choice $$[\alpha_1v_1,\ldots,\alpha_dv_d]$$ with $$\alpha_i\in\{-1,1\}$$. The mean restores the translation component.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-37.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="PCA construction of a finite Euclidean frame." caption="PCA determines axes while the finite frame retains every unresolved eigenvector sign. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 37." %}

## Stochastic frame averaging for materials

FAENet applies frame averaging to crystal representation learning. It uses PCA frames but samples them stochastically to reduce the cost of evaluating every sign choice.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-38.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="FAENet concept and materials performance comparison." caption="FAENet turns the frame-averaging construction into a scalable materials model. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 38." %}

In three dimensions, the PCA axes have $$2^3=8$$ sign combinations. Each transformation sends the centered point cloud into one candidate canonical coordinate system; invariant outputs are averaged directly, while equivariant outputs are rotated back.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-39.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="FAENet PCA frame construction with eight sign choices." caption="A three-dimensional PCA frame contains eight sign-resolved coordinate systems. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 39." %}

Exact frame averaging costs eight backbone evaluations. Stochastic frame averaging samples one frame during a pass and transforms the output consistently. FAENet also modifies the construction for periodic crystal coordinates and cell offsets.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-40.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="FAENet stochastic frame sampling and periodic crystal handling." caption="Sampling one sign frame trades exact finite averaging per pass for lower computation, while periodic offsets require separate treatment. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 40." %}

The reported results show performance comparable to strong materials baselines with better scalability than earlier exact frame-averaging implementations.

## Learned and probabilistic canonicalization

Canonicalization replaces the finite average with a single input-dependent transformation. If $$h(x)$$ maps an input to a group representation and $$f$$ is an unconstrained predictor, the equivariant construction has the form

$$
\Phi(x)=h'(x),f\!\left(h(x)^{-1}x\right),
$$

where $$h'(x)$$ applies the corresponding output transformation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-42.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Learned canonicalization using one forward pass." caption="A learned canonicalizer chooses one pose instead of averaging the backbone over a finite frame. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 42." %}

Equivariance follows when the canonicalizer itself transforms equivariantly, $$h(\rho(g)x)=\rho(g)h(x)$$. In a molecular model, an equivariant auxiliary network can output the rotation used to canonicalize the coordinates.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-43.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Equation and condition for equivariance through learned canonicalization." caption="The transformation law of the canonicalizer is the condition that makes a single canonical pose sufficient. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 43." %}

The method was tested on images, N-body simulation, and point-cloud classification and segmentation, showing that the construction is architecture-agnostic rather than specific to molecules.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-44.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Learned canonicalization results on images N body and point cloud tasks." caption="Learned canonicalization was evaluated across several data types and output transformation laws. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 44." %}

Probabilistic symmetrization generalizes a deterministic canonicalizer into a distribution over group elements. Ambiguous inputs need not be forced into one pose; the model averages predictions over transformations sampled from a learned conditional distribution.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-45.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Probabilistic symmetrization paper and distribution over group elements." caption="Probabilistic symmetrization replaces a single canonical pose with a learned pose distribution. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 45." %}

For $$O(3)$$, the sampler draws standard-normal noise, maps it through an equivariant network to a $$3\times3$$ matrix, and applies Gram--Schmidt orthogonalization. Averaging the transformed-back predictions recovers the symmetrized output.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-46.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Probabilistic symmetrization equation and orthogonal group sampler." caption="An equivariant matrix generator followed by Gram--Schmidt provides samples in the orthogonal group. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 46." %}

The experiments include transferring ImageNet-pretrained backbones to graphs. The point of the construction is that symmetry can be imposed around an architecture chosen for its existing representations rather than by redesigning every layer.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-08/slide-47.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Probabilistic symmetrization experiments using pretrained image models for graphs." caption="Probabilistic symmetrization tests whether non-geometric pretrained backbones can be reused inside an equivariant wrapper. Cropped from the 2025 GDL frame-based-GNN lecture, slide 47." %}

Local and global frames solve different problems. ClofNet and LEFTNet move geometric information through many interaction-specific coordinate systems. Frame averaging, FAENet, and canonicalization place the whole input into one or several global systems so an arbitrary backbone can be symmetrized. Both rely on the same division: the frame carries the transformation law, while the learned backbone operates on coordinates expressed relative to it.
