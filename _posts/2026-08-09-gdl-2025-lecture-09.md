## From scalars and vectors to steerable feature types

Scalar and vector features already obey transformation laws. A scalar is unchanged by rotation; a vector is multiplied by the rotation matrix. Both are steerable features because the transformed feature can be computed by a known linear action.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-3.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Scalar and vector features transforming under rotations." caption="Scalars and ordinary vectors are the first two steerable feature types. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 3." %}

Steerable networks extend this idea to type-$$0$$, type-$$1$$, through type-$$L$$ hidden features. Each type is a vector space with its own prescribed rotation matrix.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-4.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Network layers carrying steerable features of several types." caption="A steerable layer carries multiple feature types rather than only scalars and Cartesian vectors. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 4." %}

The layer must combine these types without violating their transformation laws. Clebsch--Gordan tensor products provide the typed bilinear map used between successive hidden layers.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-5.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Clebsch Gordan tensor products between typed hidden features." caption="Clebsch--Gordan products couple typed inputs into typed outputs. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 5." %}

The deck therefore answers three questions in order: what a steerable feature is, what distinguishes type $$l$$, and how two typed features can be combined linearly into another valid type.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-6.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Three questions about steerable features irreps and Clebsch Gordan products." caption="Group representations, irreducible representations, and Clebsch--Gordan products supply the three required objects. ## Groups, actions, and representations

A group $$(G,\cdot)$$ is a set with a closed associative product, an identity, and an inverse for each element. The definition says how symmetry transformations compose before specifying any data on which they act.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-9.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Definition and four axioms of a group." caption="Closure, associativity, identity, and inverses define the algebra of symmetry operations. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 9." %}

The orthogonal group $$O(n)$$ contains the $$n\times n$$ matrices that preserve Euclidean distance while fixing the origin. Its subgroup $$SO(n)$$ has determinant $$+1$$; in two and three dimensions these are the proper rotation groups.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-10.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Orthogonal and special orthogonal groups." caption="The determinant distinguishes rotations from the reflection-containing orthogonal group. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 10." %}

A group action connects the abstract group to a domain. It satisfies

$$
g\cdot(h\cdot x)=(gh)\cdot x,
\qquad
e\cdot x=x.
$$

For a point cloud, one rotation acts on every point with the same matrix.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-11.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SO2 action on every point of a point cloud." caption="A group action specifies how one symmetry element transforms the complete data object. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 11." %}

A representation $$\rho$$ maps each group element to an invertible linear operator. Compatibility requires

$$
\rho(gh)=\rho(g)\rho(h),
\qquad
\rho(e)=I.
$$

A feature vector transforming as $$v\mapsto\rho(g)v$$ is steerable.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-12.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Definition of a group representation and steerable vector." caption="A representation turns group composition into matrix multiplication on features. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 12." %}

Circular harmonics give a concrete representation of $$SO(2)$$. The pair

$$
v_k(\theta)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos(k\theta)\\
\sin(k\theta)
\end{bmatrix}
$$

rotates by the two-dimensional matrix with angle $$k\alpha$$ when the input angle is shifted by $$\alpha$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-13.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Degree one circular harmonics under planar rotation." caption="Circular harmonics turn a shift of the input angle into a known matrix action on two coefficients. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 13." %}

Higher degree changes the angular frequency. Degree $$k$$ completes $$k$$ oscillations around the circle and therefore transforms with the representation angle $$k\alpha$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-14.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Higher degree circular harmonics and their rotation matrices." caption="The harmonic degree determines both spatial frequency and the associated representation. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 14." %}

## Spherical harmonics as steerable vectors

Spherical harmonics form an orthonormal basis for functions on the sphere. They are indexed by degree $$l$$ and order $$m$$, solve the angular part of Laplace's equation, and factor into a complex azimuthal term and an associated Legendre polynomial:

$$
Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)=N_{lm}e^{im\phi}P_l^m(\cos\theta).
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-15.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Spherical harmonic basis functions indexed by degree and order." caption="Degree controls angular frequency, while order indexes the basis elements within one degree. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 15." %}

All $$2l+1$$ harmonics of one degree form a vector $$Y^{(l)}$$. Under a rotation $$g\in SO(3)$$,

$$
Y^{(l)}(g\cdot \hat r)=D^{(l)}(g)Y^{(l)}(\hat r),
$$

where $$D^{(l)}$$ is the degree-$$l$$ Wigner $$D$$ matrix.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-16.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Spherical harmonics transforming by a Wigner D matrix." caption="A fixed degree is closed under rotation and therefore forms one steerable feature vector. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 16." %}

The degree-one real spherical harmonics are proportional to the Cartesian coordinates on the unit sphere. Ordinary vectors are therefore the type-$$1$$ member of the same family, not a separate geometric object.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-17.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Degree one spherical harmonics identified with xyz coordinates." caption="Type-1 spherical harmonics recover the familiar three Cartesian vector components. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 17." %}

Geometric GNNs use these functions to encode the direction $$\hat r_{ij}$$ of a relative displacement. Radial functions encode distance; spherical harmonics encode how the direction transforms.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-18.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Spherical harmonics used to featurize relative atomic directions." caption="Evaluating spherical harmonics on an edge direction creates typed geometric filter features. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 18." %}

## Cartesian tensors and irreducible representations

There are two routes to higher-order steerable features. Cartesian tensor products build larger arrays from vectors. Spherical tensors instead organize the same representation content into irreducible types and combine them with Clebsch--Gordan products.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-19.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Cartesian and spherical routes to higher order steerable vectors." caption="Cartesian tensor order and spherical irrep type are two organizations of higher-order transformation structure. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 19." %}

Starting from vectors $$u,v,w\in\mathbb R^3$$, a second-order tensor has entries $$M_{ij}=u_iv_j$$ and a third-order tensor has entries $$T_{ijk}=u_iv_jw_k$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-20.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Second and third order Cartesian outer products." caption="Repeated outer products create steerable Cartesian tensors of increasing order. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 20." %}

Under rotation $$R$$,

$$
M\mapsto RMR^\top,
\qquad
\operatorname{vec}(M)\mapsto(R\otimes R)\operatorname{vec}(M).
$$

Each index of a higher-order tensor receives one factor of $$R$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-21.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Rotation laws for Cartesian tensors and their vectorizations." caption="A Cartesian tensor is steerable, with one rotation matrix acting on each index. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 21." %}

This representation is reducible. A rank-two Cartesian tensor decomposes into a scalar trace, an antisymmetric part equivalent to a vector, and a symmetric traceless part. Keeping all nine matrix entries mixes these three distinct response types.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-22.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Rank two Cartesian tensor decomposed into scalar vector and new components." caption="Cartesian tensor order does not provide the most compact classification by rotational response. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 22." %}

Irreducible representations provide the compact classification. They decompose any representation into feature spaces that cannot be split further while respecting the group action.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-23.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Motivation for decomposing steerable vectors into irreducible representations." caption="Irreps categorize features by how they respond to rotation rather than by how they were constructed. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 23." %}

Representations $$\rho_A$$ and $$\rho_B$$ are equivalent if a change of basis $$Q$$ relates them:

$$
\rho_B(g)=Q^{-1}\rho_A(g)Q.
$$

They describe the same group action in different coordinates.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-24.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Equivalent representations connected by a similarity transform." caption="Representation equivalence is a change of feature basis, not a change of symmetry content. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 24." %}

A reducible representation can be put into block-diagonal form,

$$
\rho(g)=Q^{-1}\bigl(\rho_1(g)\oplus\rho_2(g)\bigr)Q.
$$

For $$SO(3)$$, the irreducible blocks are Wigner $$D$$ matrices.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-25.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Reducible representation decomposed into block diagonal irreps." caption="Block diagonalization separates a mixed feature space into independently transforming irreducible types. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 25." %}

For $$SO(2)$$, circular harmonics of each frequency supply the irreducible blocks. Mixing different frequencies in one arbitrary linear combination generally destroys the closure of an individual irrep.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-26.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Block diagonal circular harmonic representations of SO2." caption="Each circular frequency transforms within its own two-dimensional irrep block. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 26." %}

For $$SO(3)$$, $$D^{(l)}(g)$$ is a $$(2l+1)\times(2l+1)$$ matrix. A general finite steerable representation is a direct sum of the blocks $$D^{(0)},D^{(1)},D^{(2)},\ldots$$. A feature on which $$D^{(l)}$$ acts is called type $$l$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-27.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Block diagonal Wigner D representations for SO3." caption="Type <i>l</i> denotes the (2<i>l</i> + 1)-dimensional irreducible representation of three-dimensional rotations. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 27." %}

Type $$0$$ is a scalar and type $$1$$ is a Cartesian vector. Higher types do not have equally simple everyday names; the degree-$$l$$ spherical harmonic vector is the standard realization.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-28.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Examples of type zero type one and higher SO3 steerable vectors." caption="Scalars, vectors, and spherical-harmonic coefficient vectors share one type system. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 28." %}

The spherical-harmonic and Wigner-matrix views coincide: evaluating all degree-$$l$$ harmonics produces a type-$$l$$ vector, and rotating the direction applies $$D^{(l)}$$ to that vector.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-29.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Degree l spherical harmonics paired with the lth Wigner D matrix." caption="Spherical harmonics are concrete functions whose coefficients realize the abstract irreducible representation. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 29." %}

At $$l=1$$, the Wigner matrix is the ordinary three-dimensional rotation matrix, up to the chosen real or complex basis.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-30.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Type one Wigner D matrix identified with a 3D rotation matrix." caption="The familiar vector transformation law is the degree-one case of the Wigner family. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 30." %}

## Clebsch--Gordan tensor products

Typed features must interact without leaving the irrep organization. The Clebsch--Gordan product is the bilinear map that couples two input types and returns one or more valid output types.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-31.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Motivation for Clebsch Gordan products in steerable networks." caption="Clebsch--Gordan coupling generalizes scalar gates, dot products, and cross products to arbitrary types. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 31." %}

An ordinary outer product $$a\otimes b=ab^\top$$ is steerable. If $$a$$ has type $$l_a$$ and $$b$$ has type $$l_b$$, both group actions appear in the transformed tensor.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-32.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Outer product of two steerable vectors and its rotation law." caption="The raw tensor product is equivariant but does not yet expose irreducible output types. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 32." %}

After vectorization, its representation is a Kronecker product of Wigner matrices. That representation is reducible, so the output cannot be labeled by one type until it is block-diagonalized.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-33.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Reducible Kronecker representation of a vectorized tensor product." caption="A raw product carries several irreducible rotational responses at once. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 33." %}

The Clebsch--Gordan change of basis performs this decomposition. Its output is arranged into blocks that transform by individual Wigner $$D$$ matrices.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-34.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Clebsch Gordan basis change into Wigner D blocks." caption="Clebsch--Gordan coefficients project a tensor product onto typed irreducible components. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 34." %}

For inputs $$v^{(l_1)}$$ and $$v^{(l_2)}$$, an output component is

$$
\bigl(v^{(l_1)}\otimes_{\mathrm{CG}}v^{(l_2)}\bigr)_{m}^{(l)}
=\sum_{m_1=-l_1}^{l_1}\sum_{m_2=-l_2}^{l_2}
C_{(l_1,m_1)(l_2,m_2)}^{(l,m)}
v_{m_1}^{(l_1)}v_{m_2}^{(l_2)}.
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-35.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Indexed definition of the Clebsch Gordan tensor product." caption="Each coefficient maps a pair of input representation indices to one output type and index. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 35." %}

Only types satisfying the triangle rule

$$
|l_1-l_2|\le l\le l_1+l_2
$$

can appear. Scalar--scalar, scalar--vector, vector dot product, and vector cross product are the low-order special cases.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-36.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Clebsch Gordan selection rule and elementary products." caption="The triangle rule makes the coupling sparse and recovers familiar invariant and equivariant operations. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 36." %}

The harmonic visualization makes the coupling visible: multiplying two angular patterns decomposes into the allowed output frequencies.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-37.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Spherical harmonic visualization of Clebsch Gordan coupling." caption="Products of angular basis functions decompose into a finite set of output degrees. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 37." %}

The coefficient tensor is fixed by representation theory. Learning chooses channel weights around this map; it does not learn arbitrary couplings between representation indices.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-38.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Sparse Clebsch Gordan coefficient map between typed elements." caption="A Clebsch--Gordan coefficient links one admissible pair of input elements to an output element. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 38." %}

## Tensor Field Networks

Tensor Field Networks were among the first neural architectures to operate directly on steerable feature fields. A tensor field associates typed tensors with every point. TFN couples an angular filter with the current hidden feature through a Clebsch--Gordan product, then applies type-aware nonlinearities.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-40.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Overview of Tensor Field Networks and NequIP." caption="The architecture section begins with TFN and then specializes its construction to interatomic potentials in NequIP. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 40." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-41.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Tensor Field Network feature and filter interaction." caption="TFN featurizes relative directions with spherical harmonics and couples them to typed hidden features. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 41." %}

A hidden feature carries four indices: point $$a$$, channel $$c$$, type $$l$$, and representation index $$m=-l,\ldots,l$$. Channels are learnable multiplicities of the same transformation type; the $$m$$ entries must rotate together.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-42.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Point channel type and representation indices of TFN hidden features." caption="Separating channel multiplicity from representation indices is essential to typed equivariant computation. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 42." %}

The type-$$l_f$$ filter factorizes into a learned radial function and a fixed angular basis:

$$
F_{cm_fm_i}^{(l_f,l_i)}(r)
=R_{cm_fm_i}^{(l_f,l_i)}(\lVert r\rVert)
Y_{m_f}^{(l_f)}(\hat r).
$$

The radial factor is invariant; the harmonic factor supplies the type.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-43.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="TFN filter as radial embedding times spherical harmonic." caption="TFN separates learnable distance dependence from fixed equivariant angular dependence. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 43." %}

Each message couples the filter type $$l_f$$ and input type $$l_i$$ into an allowed output type $$l_o$$, then sums over neighbors. The triangle rule determines which triples $$(l_f,l_i,l_o)$$ exist.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-44.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="TFN Clebsch Gordan message passing equation." caption="A TFN message is a neighbor sum of typed filter--feature Clebsch--Gordan products. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 44." %}

Nonlinearities also respect type. Type $$0$$ channels use ordinary activations. For $$l>0$$, TFN applies a learned scalar function of the invariant norm and uses it to gate the full $$(2l+1)$$-component vector.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-45.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Type wise TFN nonlinearities using scalar norm gates." caption="A scalar norm gate changes feature magnitude without changing its rotational transformation law. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 45." %}

Self-interaction layers act as type-wise $$1\times1$$ convolutions between channels. The full architecture alternates these channel mixers with equivariant convolutions and nonlinearities.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-46.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Complete Tensor Field Network architecture." caption="TFN alternates typed convolution, channel mixing, and order-wise nonlinearities. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 46." %}

The original experiments were proof-of-concept tasks: classifying rotated Tetris-like point sets and predicting a missing point. They demonstrated exact geometric handling before large molecular benchmarks became standard.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-47.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="TFN Tetris classification and missing point prediction experiments." caption="TFN's early experiments test whether typed features preserve the geometry needed for classification and equivariant reconstruction. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 47." %}

## NequIP and reflection parity

NequIP applies a TFN-like architecture to interatomic potentials. It predicts atomic energy contributions and differentiates their sum:

$$
E_{\mathrm{pot}}=\sum_iE_{i,\mathrm{atomic}},
\qquad
F_i=-\nabla_{r_i}E_{\mathrm{pot}}.
$$

Because molecular energy and force laws must also handle reflections, the network is $$O(3)$$-equivariant rather than only $$SO(3)$$-equivariant.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-48.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="NequIP atomic energies forces and O3 equivariance." caption="NequIP turns typed equivariant layers into an invariant energy and its equivariant forces. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 48." %}

An $$O(3)$$ feature therefore needs both degree $$l$$ and parity $$p$$. Even-parity features remain unchanged under inversion; odd-parity features change sign.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-49.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Even and odd parity steerable features under reflection." caption="Parity records the reflection law that rotation degree alone cannot distinguish. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 49." %}

The NequIP filter retains the radial--angular factorization,

$$
S_m^{(l)}(r_{ij})=R(r_{ij})Y_m^{(l)}(\hat r_{ij}),
$$

with $$R$$ produced from a Bessel basis and a smooth polynomial envelope at the cutoff.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-50.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="NequIP radial Bessel envelope and spherical harmonic filter." caption="A smooth learned radial function multiplies the fixed typed angular filter. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 50." %}

Its Clebsch--Gordan layer enforces both selection rules:

$$
|l_i-l_f|\le l_o\le l_i+l_f,
\qquad
p_ip_f=p_o.
$$

The first conserves admissible rotation types; the second conserves reflection parity.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-51.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="NequIP tensor product layer with angular and parity selection rules." caption="NequIP couples only degree and parity combinations allowed by the O(3) representation product. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 51." %}

NequIP's experiments reported lower errors than both deep-learning and kernel baselines, including sGDML and FCHL comparisons. These results established data-efficient equivariant interatomic potentials as more than a proof of symmetry.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-52.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="NequIP benchmark comparison with sGDML." caption="NequIP compares equivariant learned potentials with the sGDML kernel model. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-GNN lecture, slide 52." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-09/slide-53.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="NequIP benchmark comparison with FCHL." caption="Further benchmarks compare NequIP with FCHL and other molecular force models. The same typed machinery appears beyond TFN and NequIP. Cormorant enriches the scalar part of a related covariant molecular network; 3D steerable CNNs apply steerable filters to volumetric data; and Clebsch--Gordan networks use the product as a nonlinear operation in Fourier space.

The construction is cumulative. A group representation defines steerability; irreducible representations define feature types; spherical harmonics provide typed geometric filters; Clebsch--Gordan coefficients couple those types; and parity extends the same bookkeeping from rotations to reflections. TFN and NequIP differ in application and engineering, but they rely on that same representation-theoretic chain.
