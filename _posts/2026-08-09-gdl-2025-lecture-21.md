## Materials science and discovery

Materials science studies how structure, properties, and processing interact across metals, ceramics, polymers, composites, and other material classes. Its applications include semiconductors, batteries, biomaterials, and large-scale industrial manufacturing.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-2.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Materials science examples spanning atomic structures devices polymers and batteries." caption="Materials science connects microscopic organization to measurable properties and engineered applications. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 2." %}

Discovery faces a vast design space, expensive synthesis, and costly first-principles calculations. Traditional trial and error samples only a small fraction of possible compounds. The inverse-design goal is to begin with a desired property and identify a material that realizes it.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-3.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Traditional and database driven materials discovery pipelines." caption="Machine learning targets the gap between a vast candidate space and expensive simulation or synthesis. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 3." %}

Material classes bring different structural and mechanical priors. Metals are conductive and ductile, ceramics tolerate high temperature but are often brittle, polymers are long-chain and lightweight, and composites combine constituents to obtain properties unavailable from either alone.

## Electronic, optical, thermal, magnetic, and mechanical properties

Electronic behavior is organized by band structure. Conductors have mobile carriers and no effective band gap; insulators have a large gap; semiconductors occupy the tunable middle. The band gap separates the valence-band maximum from the conduction-band minimum and plays a role analogous to the molecular HOMO--LUMO gap.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-5.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Electronic band diagrams for metals semiconductors and insulators." caption="Band occupancy and gap size distinguish conducting, semiconducting, and insulating regimes. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 5." %}

Optical transparency requires weak absorption in the visible range, often associated with a wide gap. Luminescence instead emits light after excitation through electron--hole recombination, defects, or dopants.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-6.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Optical transparency and luminescence mechanisms in materials." caption="Optical properties depend on which electronic transitions the material permits. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 6." %}

Thermal transport can be carried by free electrons or by phonons, the quantized modes of atomic vibration. Strong bonding and ordered dense structures can support high conductivity, while disorder, porosity, and low density scatter carriers and insulate.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-7.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Electron and phonon mechanisms for thermal conduction and insulation." caption="Heat transport depends on the propagation and scattering of electronic and vibrational carriers. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 7." %}

Magnetic behavior depends on electronic spins and their ordering. Diamagnets weakly repel applied fields; paramagnets align unpaired electrons weakly; ferromagnets form strongly ordered domains; and antiferromagnets or ferrimagnets contain opposing spin patterns with zero or reduced net magnetization.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-8.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Diamagnetic paramagnetic ferromagnetic antiferromagnetic and ferrimagnetic order." caption="Magnetic classes differ in both local spin response and collective ordering. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 8." %}

Mechanical properties distinguish resistance to deformation, recovery, permanent shape change, and fracture. Hardness, elasticity, plasticity, and toughness are related but separate targets; a hard ceramic can still be brittle, while a ductile metal accommodates dislocations before fracture.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-9.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Hardness elasticity plasticity toughness and brittle fracture properties." caption="Mechanical performance cannot be reduced to one scalar notion of strength. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 9." %}

## Periodic crystals and symmetry

A crystal repeats a finite basis through a lattice matrix $$L=[\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3]$$. If atom $$i$$ has position $$p_i$$ and type $$a_i$$ in one cell, the infinite structure contains

$$
\left\{(p_i+Lk,a_i):k\in\mathbb Z^3\right\}.
$$

The lattice is therefore part of the input representation, not a bounding box around an ordinary point cloud.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-10.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Crystal unit cell basis and periodic lattice repetition." caption="A finite unit cell plus integer lattice translations represents an infinite periodic crystal. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 10." %}

Bravais lattices classify the fourteen distinct three-dimensional translational lattice types. Primitive and centered arrangements belong to crystal systems but do not by themselves specify the atoms decorating the lattice or the full symmetry group.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-11.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Fourteen three dimensional Bravais lattice types." caption="Bravais lattices classify periodic translation structures before chemical decoration is added. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 11." %}

Crystal symmetries include lattice translations and point operations such as rotations, reflections, and inversion. Combining point operations with translations introduces screw axes and glide planes and yields the full space-group symmetry.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-12.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Crystal translation rotation reflection and screw symmetries." caption="Crystal symmetry combines periodic translations with local point transformations. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 12." %}

There are 230 space groups in three dimensions. They enumerate compatible combinations of rotations, mirrors, glide planes, screw axes, and translations after duplicate group descriptions are removed.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-13.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Relation among Bravais lattices point groups and 230 crystal space groups." caption="A space group records the full set of periodic and point symmetries of a crystal. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 13." %}

Within a space group, a Wyckoff position is an orbit of points equivalent under symmetry. Placing one atom on a representative site can imply several atoms throughout the unit cell. Special high-symmetry positions have smaller degrees of freedom and are common in stable crystals.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-14.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Wyckoff positions table and symmetry equivalent sites in a unit cell." caption="Wyckoff positions encode symmetry-equivalent atomic sites and their multiplicities. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 14." %}

## Property prediction and inverse crystal design

Geometric graph models such as CGCNN and MEGNet predict formation energies from crystal structures. Formation energy measures the energy to form a compound from its constituent elements and supports fast screening for thermodynamic candidates.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-15.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Crystal graph neural network formation energy prediction." caption="Crystal property models replace many expensive calculations during stability screening. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 15." %}

Band-gap prediction similarly screens candidates for semiconductor behavior. The model maps periodic structure and composition to the valence--conduction energy separation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-16.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Band gap prediction and conductor semiconductor insulator energy levels." caption="Fast band-gap models identify candidate semiconductors before detailed electronic-structure calculations. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 16." %}

Crystal structure prediction reverses the problem: find a low-energy structure for a composition or a structure with a desired property. Traditional searches such as USPEX and random exploration are expensive. Generative models learn a candidate distribution, and an active loop can propose, evaluate, and refine structures.

## Symmetry-aware crystal generation

DiffCSP conditions a diffusion model on composition and generates lattice and atomic coordinates. Its distribution is equivariant to rotations and translations and invariant to equivalent periodic representations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-18.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffCSP composition conditioned diffusion architecture for crystal structures." caption="DiffCSP generates periodic coordinates and lattice variables while respecting crystal equivalences. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 18." %}

DiffCSP++ adds explicit space-group conditioning. It samples a lattice, chooses occupied Wyckoff positions and their elements, then samples the remaining symmetry-constrained displacements. Encoding symmetry during generation substantially improves valid structure prediction.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-19.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffCSP plus plus space group and Wyckoff conditioned crystal generation." caption="Space-group and Wyckoff variables turn symmetry from an evaluation criterion into a generative constraint. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 19." %}

SymmCD also conditions on symmetry but keeps the generated representation small. It models only the asymmetric unit and applies space-group operations afterward to reconstruct the complete cell.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-20.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SymmCD asymmetric unit and space group reconstruction pipeline." caption="Generating the asymmetric unit avoids repeating symmetry-equivalent atoms in the model state. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 20." %}

## Crystal generative models

Some models generate composition together with lattice and coordinates; others condition on a fixed composition and perform structure prediction. A common design pipeline generates candidates, predicts properties with a fast surrogate, filters or optimizes them, and feeds the results back into generation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-21.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Crystal composition and structure generative design pipeline." caption="Crystal generation can either complete a composition or jointly propose chemistry and geometry. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 21." %}

CDVAE maps a crystal structure into a latent vector and reconstructs it with a diffusion decoder. The latent representation supports sampling and optimization, while the decoder restores periodic atomic structure.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-22.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Crystal Diffusion VAE encoder latent space and diffusion decoder." caption="CDVAE combines latent-variable modeling with a diffusion-based crystal decoder. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 22." %}

CrystalLLM serializes crystal information as a crystallographic information file. An autoregressive Transformer is prompted with a desired composition and writes a plausible CIF structure token by token.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-23.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="CrystalLLM CIF tokenization prompt and generated crystal file." caption="CrystalLLM treats the standardized CIF representation as a generative language. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 23." %}

Existing large language models can be adapted to the same representation. Finetuning LLaMA-70B on CIF files leverages large model capacity and may capture textual regularities such as charge balance and common oxidation states.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-24.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Finetuned large language model workflow for CIF crystal generation." caption="CIF finetuning transfers an optimized autoregressive architecture to crystallographic text. FlowMM uses Riemannian flow matching to generate composition and structure. Its state combines periodic fractional coordinates, lattice variables, and atom types, each with an appropriate continuous or discrete geometry.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-25.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="FlowMM Riemannian flow matching for crystal composition and structure." caption="FlowMM models the product geometry of crystal types, periodic coordinates, and lattice parameters. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 25." %}

MatterGen is a diffusion foundation model for inorganic materials trained across a large portion of the periodic table. It supports fine-tuning or guidance toward property-conditioned material-generation tasks.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-26.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="MatterGen inorganic materials diffusion model and generated crystals." caption="MatterGen scales crystal diffusion across broad chemistry and adapts it to conditional design objectives. The All-Atom Diffusion Transformer learns a multimodal latent distribution spanning molecules and materials. Latent diffusion provides one generative interface while modality-specific encoders and decoders handle different atomic systems.

Chemeleon adds natural-language guidance. A CLIP-like alignment connects text descriptions with crystal representations, letting a language condition steer structural generation.

## Battery materials

A battery stores and releases charge through ion and electron transport. During discharge, lithium ions move from anode to cathode through the separator, while electrons travel through the external circuit. Cathode, anode, electrolyte, and conductive additives impose different material requirements.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-29.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Lithium ion battery cathode anode separator ion and electron flow." caption="Battery operation couples crystal chemistry to ionic transport, electronic conduction, and interfacial stability. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 29." %}

Solid-state electrolytes need high lithium-ion conductivity, stability, and feasible synthesis. MatterGen and Chemeleon both explored the Li--P--S--Cl system; the lecture highlights four generated structures reported as metastable and dynamically stable.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-30.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Generated lithium phosphorus sulfur chlorine solid electrolyte crystal candidates." caption="Generative models propose solid-electrolyte crystals within a targeted chemical system. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 30." %}

Lithium diffusivity can be estimated with neural-accelerated molecular dynamics. A learned force field produces long trajectories, and ionic displacement statistics yield a self-diffusivity estimate.

## Semiconductor materials

Semiconductors have conductivity between conductors and insulators. Donor doping creates n-type material with extra electrons, while acceptor doping creates p-type material with mobile holes. Composition and defect chemistry therefore tune the carrier population.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-32.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Intrinsic n type and p type semiconductor doping and carriers." caption="Dopants control semiconductor carrier type by adding electrons or holes. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 32." %}

Samsung released a dataset for semiconductor analysis with machine-learning force fields. The structures and energy distributions support model development for industrially relevant atomic configurations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-33.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Samsung semiconductor atomic dataset and energy distributions." caption="Industrial semiconductor datasets expose atomic environments for learned-force-field analysis. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 33." %}

The CTMT pipeline combines a generative model, topology evaluator, and interatomic potential. It reports four new topological insulators and sixteen new semimetals, including chiral candidates that are difficult to design by simpler searches.

## Heterogeneous catalysis

In heterogeneous catalysis, a solid catalyst interacts with reactants in another phase. Molecules adsorb on a surface, transform through surface reactions, and desorb as products. The recoverable solid catalyst makes this process central to converters, ammonia synthesis, and refining.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/slide-35.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Adsorption reaction and desorption on a heterogeneous catalyst surface." caption="Surface structure controls the adsorption and reaction sequence of heterogeneous catalysis. Cropped from the 2025 GDL materials lecture, slide 35." %}

One computational task is to find the lowest-energy adsorbate configuration on a slab. The Open Catalyst Project provides surface--adsorbate structures and relaxation data for training models that predict energies, forces, or relaxed geometries across this large configuration space.
