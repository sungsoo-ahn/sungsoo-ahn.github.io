Materials discovery asks for a structure that has a desired property before we know how to make it. That reversal is what makes the problem difficult. A forward calculation starts from a composition and structure, then predicts energy, band gap, conductivity, or another observable. Inverse design starts from the observable and must search over chemical composition, unit-cell geometry, atomic positions, and sometimes processing conditions.

The lecture builds this problem in three stages. First, it identifies the properties and symmetries that distinguish materials from isolated molecules. Second, it follows crystal machine learning from property prediction to structure generation. Third, it checks what a generated crystal must still accomplish in three applications: conduct lithium ions, support semiconductor behavior, or place an adsorbate correctly on a catalytic surface.

## Materials science begins with structure, properties, and processing

Materials science studies how microscopic structure and processing determine macroscopic properties. The scope includes metals, ceramics, polymers, composites, and crystalline compounds used in electronics, energy storage, biomaterials, and manufacturing. This breadth matters for machine learning because “a material” is not one uniform data type. A polymer chain, an amorphous glass, a metal surface, and a periodic inorganic crystal require different representations and expose different observables.

The lecture opens with three examples rather than one abstract definition. Substituting boron into silicon changes its charge-carrier population. Organic molecules with different conjugated structures emit different colors in an OLED. A battery combines several material classes because the cathode, anode, electrolyte, separator, and current collectors have incompatible jobs. In each case, composition alone is insufficient: local bonding, long-range order, interfaces, and defects determine the behavior.

### The discovery bottleneck is a search problem

Only a small fraction of chemically possible compounds have been synthesized. Trial-and-error synthesis is slow, and a failed experiment may reveal little about the rest of the design space. First-principles simulations such as density functional theory provide a more systematic filter, but relaxing and evaluating millions of hypothetical crystals remains expensive.

The traditional workflow combines physical intuition, experiments, and simulation to propose new materials. A database-driven workflow adds a fourth source of evidence: measured and computed structures accumulated across many campaigns. Machine learning turns that archive into a proposal mechanism or a fast surrogate. It does not remove simulation and experiment; it changes where their cost is spent.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s03-discovery-paradigms.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Traditional materials discovery using intuition experiments and simulation beside a database-driven approach using data and machine learning." caption="The database-driven paradigm supplements intuition, experiment, and simulation with reusable data and learned models. Both routes still end with candidate materials that require physical validation." %}

This produces the inverse-design problem:

$$
\text{desired property}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{composition and structure that realize it}.
$$

The arrow hides several distinct tasks. A generator proposes candidates. A property model ranks them. A relaxation method finds nearby local minima. A stability calculation compares each candidate against competing phases. Synthesis and measurement then test whether the predicted material exists and whether the target property survives defects, temperature, interfaces, and fabrication.

### Material classes impose different priors

Metals usually contain delocalized electrons, conduct electricity, deform plastically, and often crystallize. Ceramics are inorganic compounds with ionic or covalent bonding; they can tolerate high temperature but are frequently brittle. Polymers are long-chain organic molecules whose low density and conformational freedom support lightweight, flexible materials. Composites combine constituents so that the resulting structure has properties unavailable from either component alone.

## A property name is not yet a learning target

Material properties arise from different physical carriers and operate at different scales. Band gap is an electronic-structure quantity. Thermal conductivity depends on electron and phonon transport. Magnetization depends on spin response and collective order. Toughness depends on defects, dislocations, and crack propagation. A dataset label called “property” therefore does not specify what information a model needs.

### Electronic properties: conductors, semiconductors, and insulators

In a solid, many atomic orbitals combine into energy bands. At zero temperature, electrons fill states up to the Fermi level. A metal has available states near that level, so an applied field can move charge with little excitation energy. In a semiconductor or insulator, the occupied valence band is separated from the unoccupied conduction band by a band gap

$$
E_g = E_{\mathrm{CBM}} - E_{\mathrm{VBM}},
$$

where $$E_{\mathrm{VBM}}$$ is the valence-band maximum and $$E_{\mathrm{CBM}}$$ is the conduction-band minimum. Semiconductors have a moderate, tunable gap; insulators have a gap large enough that normal thermal or electrical excitation produces few free carriers. The analogy to a molecular HOMO--LUMO gap is useful, but bands belong to an extended periodic system rather than one finite molecule.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s05-band-regimes.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Band diagrams for a metal a semiconductor and an insulator showing valence bands conduction bands the Fermi level and band gaps." caption="Band occupancy and gap size distinguish conducting, semiconducting, and insulating regimes. The metal has overlapping occupied and available states; the semiconductor and insulator separate them by progressively larger gaps." %}

Conductivity can change even when the host lattice remains almost unchanged. Temperature excites carriers across the gap, while doping introduces donor or acceptor states. A useful property predictor must therefore know whether the intended target describes a pristine zero-temperature crystal, a finite-temperature material, or a chemically doped system.

### Optical properties: transmission and emission

Transparency means that visible photons are not absorbed strongly. A wide electronic gap can prevent visible photons from exciting electrons into the conduction band, as in glass or quartz, although scattering, defects, and vibrational absorption also affect real samples. Luminescence instead requires a material to absorb energy and later emit photons. Electron--hole recombination, defect states, and dopants can all create radiative pathways.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s06-oled-layers.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Layered OLED device and energy-level diagram with hole and electron transport layers an emissive layer HOMO and LUMO levels." caption="An OLED is a layered material system, not a single emitter molecule. Holes and electrons enter through different transport layers and recombine in the emissive layer, so both energy-level alignment and device structure control the optical output." %}

The OLED diagram makes the model-to-observable chain explicit. A molecular calculation may predict an emitter's frontier orbitals. A device calculation must additionally account for the hole-transport layer, electron-transport layer, contacts, morphology, and recombination region. Predicting one intermediate quantity does not establish device efficiency.

### Thermal properties: electrons and phonons carry heat

Metals transport heat partly through mobile electrons. Insulators and semiconductors rely more strongly on phonons, the quantized normal modes of collective atomic vibration. Strong bonds and an ordered, dense lattice can support fast propagation. Disorder, pores, boundaries, isotope variation, and anharmonic scattering shorten carrier mean free paths and reduce conductivity.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s07-phonon-transport.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Ordered and disordered lattices illustrating transmitted and scattered vibrational waves." caption="An ordered lattice supports coherent vibrational transport, while disorder increases scattering. Electrons are charged particles; phonons are quantized collective vibrations, and both can carry heat. Original source not identified." %}

This is a reminder that a crystal graph with a small cutoff may predict a static energy while missing the long-wavelength collective modes needed for thermal transport. The correct geometric representation depends on the observable, not only on the material.

### Magnetic properties: local moments and collective order

Diamagnetic materials weakly oppose an applied magnetic field. Paramagnets contain unpaired electrons whose moments align weakly with a field but lack spontaneous long-range order. Ferromagnets form domains with parallel ordering and a strong net magnetization. Antiferromagnets align neighboring spins oppositely so that the net moment cancels, while ferrimagnets have opposing but unequal moments and retain a nonzero total.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s08-magnetic-response.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Comparison of diamagnetic and paramagnetic electron pairing spin response and magnetic-field distortion." caption="Diamagnetic and paramagnetic responses differ in electron pairing and in how an external field changes the local moment. Collective ferromagnetic, antiferromagnetic, and ferrimagnetic order adds a second level beyond this local picture. Original source not identified." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s08-magnetic-classes.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Table comparing spin alignment and net magnetic behavior for ferromagnetic antiferromagnetic ferrimagnetic paramagnetic and diamagnetic materials." caption="Magnetic classes distinguish parallel, alternating, partially cancelling, field-induced, and weak opposing arrangements of local moments. Original source not identified." %}

Predicting magnetic class requires more than atomic positions. Oxidation state, orbital occupation, spin polarization, and the exchange interactions between sites may all enter. MnO and Fe\(_3\)O\(_4\), for example, can have similar-looking periodic representations while exhibiting antiferromagnetic and ferrimagnetic order, respectively.

### Mechanical properties: strength is not one scalar

Hardness measures resistance to local deformation or scratching. Elasticity measures whether a material returns to its original shape after stress is removed. Plasticity describes permanent deformation, often mediated in metals by dislocation motion. Toughness measures resistance to fracture and therefore balances strength with the ability to dissipate energy.

A hard material need not be tough. Ceramics can resist indentation yet fracture with little plastic deformation. A ductile metal can be softer while absorbing more energy before failure. These targets depend on microstructure, defects, grain boundaries, and loading protocol, so an ideal perfect-cell representation may omit the mechanisms that determine the measured value.

## A crystal is a periodic object, not a finite point cloud

The lecture next changes from properties to representation. A finite molecule can be described by atom types and Cartesian positions. A crystal additionally requires a lattice that repeats the unit cell through space.

Let

$$
M=(A,P,L)
$$

denote a crystal with atom types $$A=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$$, positions $$P=(p_1,\ldots,p_n)$$ inside one cell, and lattice matrix

$$
L=[\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3]^\top.
$$

The corresponding infinite structure is

$$
\widehat P
=
\left\{
p_i + k_1\ell_1+k_2\ell_2+k_3\ell_3
\;\middle|\;
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\;
(k_1,k_2,k_3)\in\mathbb Z^3
\right\},
$$

with the atom type $$a_i$$ copied to every translated image. The lattice is not a bounding box. Changing it changes the physical structure and the distances between periodic images.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s10-lattice-basis.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A lattice plus an atomic basis producing a periodic crystal structure." caption="A crystal structure combines a translation lattice with a basis of atoms. The finite pair is a compact representation of an infinite periodic point set." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s10-crystal-cells.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A labeled unit cell and a close-packed crystal showing periodic repetition." caption="A unit cell may be chosen in several equivalent ways. A crystal model must give the same physical prediction after changing the cell origin, translating an atom through a boundary, or selecting another equivalent periodic representation." %}

Fractional coordinates make the periodicity explicit. If $$x_i\in[0,1)^3$$ and $$p_i=L^\top x_i$$, then $$x_i$$ and $$x_i+k$$ represent the same position for any integer vector $$k$$. This periodic equivalence is one of the symmetries that a crystal generator must respect.

### Bravais lattices classify translation structure

A Bravais lattice is the set of all integer combinations of three primitive translation vectors. In three dimensions, lattice geometry and centering produce fourteen Bravais types across seven crystal systems. Primitive, body-centered, face-centered, and base-centered choices specify which translation points belong to the lattice.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s11-bravais-lattices.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="The fourteen three-dimensional Bravais lattices arranged across the seven crystal systems." caption="The fourteen Bravais lattices enumerate distinct periodic translation structures. They do not specify the atoms in the basis, and the lecture emphasizes that a lattice type is not itself the full symmetry group of a decorated crystal." %}

The last distinction prevents a common confusion. “Cubic” constrains lattice lengths and angles. It does not say which species occupy which sites, whether the basis respects every cubic rotation, or which nonsymmorphic operations are present. The decorated crystal can have less symmetry than its underlying lattice.

### Point operations and translations combine into space groups

A symmetry operation maps the infinite crystal onto itself. Lattice translations are unavoidable. Point operations include rotations, reflections, and inversion about fixed points. Combining a point operation with a fractional translation creates a screw axis or glide plane.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s12-nonsymmorphic-operations.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Crystal diagrams showing a mirror plane glide plane and a combined transformation with translation." caption="Mirror and glide operations can look locally similar but differ by an accompanying translation. Such point-plus-translation operations are part of the full space-group symmetry." %}

In affine form, an operation acts on a Cartesian point as

$$
p\longmapsto Rp+t,
$$

where $$R$$ is an orthogonal transformation compatible with the lattice and $$t$$ may contain a fractional lattice translation. The operations that preserve a crystal form its space group.

There are 230 space groups in three dimensions. The lecture motivates this count by combining the fourteen Bravais lattices with the thirty-two crystallographic point groups and removing incompatible or duplicate combinations. The two-dimensional analogue has seventeen wallpaper groups.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s13-wallpaper-groups.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A chart of the seventeen two-dimensional wallpaper groups organized by rotation and reflection content." caption="The seventeen wallpaper groups are the two-dimensional analogue of the 230 three-dimensional space groups. They show why periodic translation alone does not determine the complete symmetry." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s13-symmetry-hierarchy.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Relationship among seven crystal systems fourteen Bravais lattices thirty-two point groups and 230 space groups." caption="Crystal systems constrain lattice geometry; Bravais lattices add translation and centering; point groups describe rotations, reflections, and inversion; space groups combine these ingredients with screw and glide operations." %}

### Wyckoff positions compress symmetry-equivalent atoms

For a fixed space group $$G$$, the orbit of a point $$x$$ is

$$
\operatorname{Orb}_G(x)=\{g\cdot x:g\in G\}.
$$

A Wyckoff position collects points whose stabilizers are conjugate and therefore share the same site symmetry. In practical terms, one representative coordinate can imply several symmetry-equivalent atoms. The orbit size is the Wyckoff multiplicity.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s14-wyckoff-orbits.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Wyckoff positions of the p4mm group showing general and special sites and their symmetry orbits." caption="In the p4mm group, one representative point generates a symmetry orbit. A general site has the largest multiplicity, while special sites lie on symmetry elements and have fewer free coordinates. Original source not identified." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s14-wyckoff-table.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Wyckoff table listing multiplicity site symmetry and equivalent coordinates for positions in a tetragonal space group." caption="A Wyckoff table records each position's multiplicity, letter, site symmetry, and generated coordinates. A compact generative variable can therefore expand into a much larger orbit. Original source not identified." %}

The figure distinguishes the general position from special positions. A generic point $$(x,y)$$ in p4mm generates eight positions. A point constrained to a mirror line, such as $$(x,0)$$, generates four. High-symmetry fixed sites such as $$(0,0)$$ or $$(1/2,1/2)$$ have multiplicity one. Stable crystals often place atoms on special sites because symmetry reduces their degrees of freedom, although symmetry alone does not guarantee energetic stability.

This representation is useful for generation. Sampling every atom independently wastes capacity by repeatedly describing symmetry-equivalent sites. Sampling one representative per occupied Wyckoff orbit and reconstructing the rest enforces symmetry and reduces the state size.

## From property prediction to inverse crystal design

The forward task starts from $$M=(A,P,L)$$ and predicts a property $$y$$. Crystal graph models such as [CGCNN](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevLett.120.145301) and [MEGNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.05055) construct periodic neighborhoods, pass messages between atoms, pool the learned representation, and regress properties such as formation energy, band gap, and elastic moduli.

### Formation energy and the convex hull

Formation energy compares a compound with its constituent elements in their reference states. For a composition with counts $$n_\alpha$$,

$$
\Delta E_f
=
E_{\mathrm{compound}}
-
\sum_\alpha n_\alpha \mu_\alpha,
$$

where $$\mu_\alpha$$ is the reference energy per atom of element $$\alpha$$. A negative value says that the compound is lower in energy than the separated elemental references. It does not prove stability against decomposition into other compounds.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s15-nucleation-growth.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Separate atoms nucleating and growing into a crystalline solid." caption="Formation energy compares the assembled crystal with reference states of its constituent elements. The lecture's nucleation illustration connects that thermodynamic quantity to the physical act of forming a crystal." %}

The convex hull supplies the stronger comparison. At each composition, it takes the lowest energy attainable by any mixture of known phases. The energy above hull is

$$
E_{\mathrm{hull}}(M)
=
E(M)-E_{\mathrm{best\ competing\ mixture}}.
$$

A structure on the hull is thermodynamically stable within the evaluated chemical system at the modeled conditions. A small positive $$E_{\mathrm{hull}}$$ indicates metastability; synthesis may still be possible because kinetics, temperature, pressure, or missing phases can change the outcome.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s15-convex-hull.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Binary formation-energy convex hull with stable points on the hull and unstable structures above tie lines." caption="Negative formation energy is not enough. A candidate is stable only when no combination of competing phases at the same overall composition lies below it on the convex hull." %}

Fast formation-energy models allow millions of hypothetical structures to be screened before expensive relaxation and high-accuracy calculations. The surrogate is useful because it changes the order of computation, not because it makes stability exact.

Band-gap prediction uses the same forward pattern with a different target. A model maps composition and periodic geometry to $$E_g$$, enabling rapid semiconductor screening. The required accuracy depends on the decision: separating likely metals from wide-gap insulators is easier than ranking compounds whose gaps differ by a few tenths of an electronvolt.

### Crystal structure prediction reverses the arrow

Crystal structure prediction asks for low-energy structures at a specified composition. Traditional methods such as USPEX and random structure search generate candidates, relax them on an energy surface, score them, and update the search. The repeated local optimization is costly because most initial arrangements are implausible.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s17-csp-active-loop.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Crystal structure prediction pipeline from composition through candidate generation local optimization machine learning selection and scoring to a final crystal." caption="Crystal structure prediction alternates proposal, local relaxation, scoring, and selection. A learned generator changes the proposal distribution so that more candidates begin near realistic crystal configurations." %}

Machine-learning CSP learns a distribution over stable structures and samples from it. Conditioning on composition turns generation into structure prediction; removing that condition permits de novo composition and structure generation. Active learning can place either model inside a propose--evaluate--refine loop, concentrating expensive labels on candidates that look promising or uncertain.

## Symmetry-aware crystal structure prediction

The first generation methods in the lecture keep composition fixed and focus on periodic structure. Their progression is architectural: DiffCSP builds periodic Euclidean equivariance into diffusion, DiffCSP++ adds an explicit space-group constraint, and SymmCD generates an asymmetric unit that is replicated by symmetry.

### DiffCSP diffuses lattice and fractional coordinates jointly

[DiffCSP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/38b787fc530d0b31825827e2cc306656-Abstract-Conference.html) represents a crystal by lattice $$L$$ and fractional coordinates $$F$$, conditioned on composition $$A$$. The forward process adds noise to both geometric components. A periodic-$$E(3)$$-equivariant denoising network predicts the reverse update.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s18-diffcsp-architecture.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffCSP forward diffusion and reverse generation of lattice and fractional coordinates conditioned on composition." caption="DiffCSP jointly denoises lattice and fractional coordinates. Fractional coordinates encode periodicity directly, while the denoising model preserves rotation, translation, and equivalent periodic descriptions." %}

Three symmetries define the contract:

- Rotating the crystal must rotate its geometry without changing its probability.
- Translating every atom together must not change the physical crystal.
- Replacing a fractional coordinate $$f_i$$ by $$f_i+k$$ for $$k\in\mathbb Z^3$$ must leave the sample unchanged.

The model also respects permutation of same-species atoms. These are distributional constraints on complete crystal samples, not decorative data augmentation.

### DiffCSP++ conditions on a space group

[DiffCSP++](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03992) adds the variable that DiffCSP leaves implicit: a requested space group. The paper reduces the constraint to two tractable pieces. The lattice is restricted to the invariant subspace allowed by the crystal family, and fractional coordinates are constrained by occupied Wyckoff positions.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s19-diffcspp-process.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffCSP plus plus denoising process with crystal-family lattice constraints and Wyckoff-position coordinate constraints." caption="DiffCSP++ constrains the lattice and coordinates during denoising rather than testing symmetry only after generation. Red arrows encode crystal-family lattice constraints; blue arrows encode Wyckoff-position constraints." %}

The generation order in the lecture is:

1. sample a lattice compatible with the crystal family;
2. choose which Wyckoff sites are occupied and assign elements;
3. sample the remaining free coordinates or displacements;
4. reconstruct every symmetry-equivalent atom.

The benchmark shows why explicit symmetry helps. On Carbon-24, DiffCSP++ with predicted constraints reports a match rate of 70.58%, compared with 51.49% for DiffCSP. On MPTS-52, the corresponding rates are 37.17% and 12.19%. Providing ground-truth symmetry raises match rates further, while coordinate RMSE does not improve uniformly; symmetry validity and geometric reconstruction are related but distinct metrics.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s19-diffcspp-benchmark.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Crystal structure prediction table comparing random search Bayesian optimization P-G-SchNet CDVAE DiffCSP and two DiffCSP plus plus variants." caption="Explicit space-group constraints produce the largest match-rate gains on Carbon-24 and MPTS-52; the ground-truth-constraint row also exposes the remaining gap in symmetry prediction. Source: <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03992'>DiffCSP++</a>." %}

### SymmCD generates the asymmetric unit

[SymmCD](https://openreview.net/forum?id=V7x2KZQn2v) decomposes a crystal into a minimal asymmetric unit and the operations that replicate it. Instead of diffusing every atom in the full cell, it learns the joint distribution of the asymmetric-unit content and crystallographic symmetry.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s20-symmcd-asymmetric-unit.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A p4m unit square partitioned into symmetry sectors and a diffusion sequence over asymmetric-unit Wyckoff choices." caption="SymmCD treats the colored sector as an asymmetric unit. Diffusion acts on the compact representative, and the selected space-group operations determine how that representative fills the complete cell." %}

The pre-processing step identifies symmetry and folds a training crystal into its asymmetric unit. The model denoises the compact representation. Post-processing projects predicted coordinates to legal Wyckoff positions and replicates them through the group operations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s20-symmcd-pipeline.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SymmCD pipeline with symmetry finding pre-processing denoising Wyckoff projection and replication." caption="SymmCD's model state and reconstruction state are different objects. The network generates a small asymmetric unit; Wyckoff projection and group replication turn it into the full periodic crystal." %}

The difference from DiffCSP++ is not merely another conditioning label. DiffCSP++ constrains a full crystal representation. SymmCD changes the representation itself so that symmetry-equivalent atoms are not independently generated.

## Generating composition and structure together

The lecture then widens the task. A CSP model receives atom types or composition and predicts geometry. A de novo model must sample composition as well as lattice and coordinates:

$$
(A,F,L)\sim p_\theta.
$$

That adds a discrete chemical variable to the geometric state. The common design pipeline is still generate, predict, filter or optimize, and feed selected results back to the generator. The model papers differ in which object they generate and where they place the difficult constraints.

### CDVAE uses a latent code and a diffusion decoder

[CDVAE](https://openreview.net/forum?id=03RLpj-tc_) encodes a crystal $$M=(A,F,L)$$ into a latent vector $$z$$. Auxiliary heads predict composition, lattice, and atom count from $$z$$. A score-based decoder then reconstructs the atomic structure through Langevin dynamics while conditioning on the latent code.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s22-cdvae-architecture.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="CDVAE encoder latent predictor heads and diffusion decoder for crystal reconstruction and generation." caption="CDVAE separates global latent modeling from geometric decoding. The encoder compresses a crystal, prediction heads recover global attributes, and the score network iteratively denoises atomic structure." %}

The latent space supports random sampling and property-directed optimization, but the decoder must still recover a valid periodic structure. Reconstruction examples across Perov-5, Carbon-24, and MP-20 show that CDVAE improves over a purely coordinate-based VAE while leaving a visible gap from the ground truth on harder structures.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s22-cdvae-samples.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Ground-truth and reconstructed crystals from FTCP conditional diffusion VAE and CDVAE across three datasets." caption="Reconstruction quality varies across perovskites, carbon structures, and MP-20 crystals, showing that structural diversity matters beyond whether a decoder is generative. Source: <a href='https://openreview.net/forum?id=03RLpj-tc_'>CDVAE</a>." %}

### CrystaLLM treats CIF as a language

A crystallographic information file serializes the unit cell, space group, atom types, fractional coordinates, occupancies, and other metadata as text. [CrystaLLM](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-54639-7) tokenizes that text and trains a decoder-only autoregressive Transformer to predict the next token.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s23-crystallm-tokenization.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A CIF file tokenized into an autoregressive input output and target sequence for a language model." caption="CrystaLLM predicts a CIF one token at a time. A composition prompt starts the sequence; the model must continue with mutually consistent lattice, symmetry, species, and coordinate fields." %}

At inference time, a prompt containing the desired composition initiates a CIF. The model then writes a plausible crystal structure. This representation inherits powerful sequence-model infrastructure, but it converts geometric equivalence into a textual learning problem. Two CIF strings can describe the same crystal after changing atom order, cell convention, precision, or symmetry setting.

The benchmark compares one- and twenty-sample settings. More samples increase the chance that at least one candidate matches the target. On Perov-5, CrystaLLM reports a 98.26% match rate with twenty samples, close to DiffCSP's 98.60%. On the more diverse MP-20 set, the corresponding values are 75.14% and 77.93%. The model is competitive without using the same explicit geometric denoising construction.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s23-crystallm-benchmark.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Crystal structure prediction benchmark comparing CDVAE DiffCSP and two CrystaLLM variants at one and twenty generated samples." caption="Match rate increases sharply when twenty candidates are allowed, while RMSE records the geometric fidelity of matched structures. Source: <a href='https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-54639-7'>CrystaLLM</a>." %}

### Fine-tuning a large language model changes scale, not the representation

[Gruver et al.](https://openreview.net/forum?id=0r5DE2ZSwJ) fine-tune pretrained LLaMA-2 models on augmented crystal strings using low-rank adaptation. The same model supports unconditional generation, text-conditioned generation, and infilling for point mutations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s24-llama-cif-pipeline.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Pipeline from stable inorganic crystal strings and prompts through LoRA fine-tuning of LLaMA-2 to generation conditioning and infilling tasks." caption="The model keeps CIF-like string representations but transfers an optimized pretrained language-model architecture. Data augmentation exposes equivalent textual descriptions of the same material. Source: <a href='https://openreview.net/forum?id=0r5DE2ZSwJ'>Gruver et al.</a>" %}

The lecture's argument is that a large language model may internalize recurring chemical constraints such as charge balance and common oxidation states while benefiting from a highly optimized architecture. The table also sets a claim boundary. The largest 70B model has the best metastability rates in the comparison: 49.8% under M3GNet screening and 10.6% under DFT, versus 28.8% and 5.4% for CDVAE. These are evaluated candidate rates, not proof that a language model has learned all crystallographic physics.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s24-llama-cif-benchmark.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Validity coverage property distribution and metastability table for CDVAE language model baselines and several fine-tuned LLaMA-2 sizes." caption="The benchmark separates syntactic and structural validity, coverage, property distribution, and metastability. Scaling improves some columns, but no single metric establishes experimental viability. Source: <a href='https://openreview.net/forum?id=0r5DE2ZSwJ'>Gruver et al.</a>" %}

### FlowMM follows the geometry of each crystal variable

[FlowMM](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/miller24a.html) replaces stochastic diffusion with Riemannian flow matching. Its state is a product of spaces: lattice parameters, periodic fractional coordinates, and atom types. Each component follows a path compatible with its geometry.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s25-flowmm-path.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="FlowMM paths for unit cells periodic coordinates and atom types from base distributions to generated crystal states." caption="FlowMM transports lattice, fractional coordinates, and atom types along different paths. Fractional coordinates live on a periodic torus, while atom types are represented continuously during transport and quantized at the endpoint." %}

For fixed-composition CSP, atom types are conditioning information. For de novo generation, FlowMM transports a continuous relaxation of type variables and quantizes at the end. The learned vector field produces a continuous normalizing flow, giving a finite-time deterministic path from a simple base distribution to the material distribution.

The paper reports that FlowMM uses far fewer integration steps than diffusion baselines and is about three times more efficient at finding stable materials in its quantum-chemistry validation. The lecture keeps both benchmark tables because CSP match rate and de novo stability measure different outputs.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s25-flowmm-benchmarks.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="FlowMM tables for crystal structure prediction and de novo generation comparing match validity coverage property stability and uniqueness metrics." caption="The upper table evaluates composition-conditioned structure prediction; the lower table evaluates de novo composition-and-structure generation and reports integration cost alongside validity, coverage, stability, and uniqueness. Source: <a href='https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/miller24a.html'>FlowMM</a>." %}

### MatterGen conditions a broad diffusion model

[MatterGen](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08628-5) trains a diffusion model over inorganic crystals spanning a broad portion of the periodic table. The forward process corrupts atom types, coordinates, and the lattice. An equivariant score network learns the reverse process.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s26-mattergen-process.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="MatterGen forward corruption reverse denoising and adapter fine-tuning for chemistry symmetry and property conditions." caption="MatterGen first learns an unconditional crystal distribution, then inserts an adapter to condition generation on chemistry, symmetry, or a scalar property. The same geometric core network is reused across design objectives." %}

Fine-tuning with labeled data or guidance steers generation toward a chemical system, space group, magnetic density, or another target. The examples in the lecture include compositions and symmetry classes absent from a narrow single-dataset narrative.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s26-mattergen-examples.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Four MatterGen crystal examples with chemical formulas and space groups." caption="MatterGen's generated examples span different compositions, coordination environments, and space groups. A broad chemical generator still hands each candidate to stability and property validation." %}

Calling MatterGen a foundation model refers to coverage and adaptability, not a guarantee that every conditioned sample is stable, synthesizable, or functional. Its output is the beginning of the design pipeline.

### ADiT shares a latent generator across molecules and materials

[All-atom Diffusion Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03965), or ADiT, asks whether periodic materials and non-periodic molecules can share one generative model. An autoencoder maps either object into a common latent representation. A standard diffusion Transformer models those latent codes, and the decoder restores modality-specific atomic structure.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s27-adit-architecture.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ADiT autoencoder reconstruction stage and latent diffusion Transformer for molecules and periodic materials." caption="ADiT moves the shared modeling problem into latent space. One autoencoder vocabulary covers atom types, coordinates, cell information, and modality labels; a diffusion Transformer generates the latent representation." %}

The benchmark compares an MP20-only model with joint training. Joint ADiT reports 99.74% structural validity, 92.14% compositional validity, and 91.92% overall validity, versus 99.58%, 90.46%, and 90.13% for MP20-only ADiT. Its stable rate is also slightly higher, 15.4% versus 14.1%. The improvement supports positive transfer in this setup without showing that one latent model is universally best for every molecular and periodic task.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s27-adit-benchmark.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ADiT MP20 de novo generation table comparing structure composition validity metastability stability and uniqueness metrics." caption="The benchmark distinguishes validity from energetic stability and uniqueness. Joint molecule–material training improves several MP20 metrics relative to the material-only ADiT row. Source: <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03965'>ADiT</a>." %}

### Chemeleon adds natural-language conditioning

[Chemeleon](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59636-y) couples a text encoder to a crystal diffusion model. Crystal CLIP aligns text embeddings with graph representations of structures. Classifier-free guidance then steers the reverse diffusion process using a prompt that may specify composition, crystal family, space group, or a material description.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s28-chemeleon-architecture.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Chemeleon text encoder Crystal CLIP latent alignment and guided crystal diffusion process." caption="Chemeleon aligns text and crystal embeddings, then injects the text condition into reverse diffusion. The prompt changes the generated distribution; it does not replace structural or energetic verification." %}

Text is useful because experimental goals are often expressed semantically rather than as one scalar. The examples include mineral classes and chemical systems. The Ti--Zn--O candidates in the lecture illustrate how a prompt-guided model can explore several structures within one composition family.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s28-chemeleon-examples.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Chemeleon generated crystal structures across the titanium zinc oxygen chemical system." caption="The Chemeleon examples move from a composition-level request to several candidate structures in the Ti–Zn–O system. Chemical rules and phase stability still decide which candidates merit calculation or synthesis." %}

The sequence from CDVAE to Chemeleon changes the object that absorbs the complexity: a latent code, a CIF token sequence, a pretrained language model, a geometry-aware flow, a broad diffusion backbone, a shared molecular-material latent space, or a text-aligned condition. None eliminates the downstream scientific contract.

## Battery materials: generation must end in ion transport

A lithium-ion battery couples several material functions. During discharge, lithium ions move from the anode through the separator or electrolyte to the cathode. Electrons cannot traverse the electrolyte; they travel through the external circuit and power the device. Conductive additives and current collectors move charge within the electrodes.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s29-battery-anatomy.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Lithium-ion battery cross section with cathode anode separator active materials current collectors and conductive additives." caption="Battery operation couples ionic transport through the electrolyte with electronic transport through electrodes and the external circuit. Each component therefore imposes a different material objective." %}

A liquid-electrolyte battery uses a porous separator soaked with electrolyte. A solid-state battery replaces that liquid with a solid ion conductor, which can improve safety and enable different electrode choices but creates demanding interface and transport constraints.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s29-liquid-solid-electrolytes.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Comparison of lithium-ion and solid-state batteries with liquid and solid electrolytes." caption="A solid electrolyte must transport lithium ions while blocking electrons and remaining compatible with both electrodes. A plausible stable crystal is only one part of that requirement." %}

### Solid electrolytes narrow the inverse-design target

The lecture focuses on Li--P--S--Cl solid electrolytes. A useful candidate needs high lithium-ion conductivity, chemical and electrochemical stability, mechanical compatibility, and a feasible synthesis route. MatterGen and Chemeleon both explored this quaternary system.

Chemeleon reported four generated compositions highlighted in the lecture: Li\(_9\)PS\(_4\)Cl, Li\(_3\)PS\(_3\)Cl, Li\(_6\)PS\(_4\)Cl, and Li\(_4\)PS\(_4\)Cl. The paper classified them as metastable and dynamically stable under its computational tests.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s30-li-ps-s-cl-candidates.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Four generated lithium phosphorus sulfur chlorine crystal candidates." caption="These four Li–P–S–Cl structures are candidate solid electrolytes. Their formulas and geometries define structures to test; they do not by themselves establish conductivity or synthesizability. Source: <a href='https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59636-y'>Chemeleon</a>." %}

Phase diagrams show where known stable and metastable structures lie relative to generated candidates. A generated point can occupy an unexplored part of composition space while still decomposing into nearby phases.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s30-phase-diagrams.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Four ternary projections of the lithium phosphorus sulfur chlorine phase space with known and generated stable and metastable compounds." caption="The phase diagrams compare known and generated candidates across the Li–P–S–Cl system. ### LiFlow connects a crystal to diffusivity

The next step is an observable. Lithium self-diffusivity can be estimated from long-time mean-squared displacement:

$$
D
=
\lim_{t\to\infty}
\frac{1}{6t}
\mathbb E\!\left[
\left\lVert r_i(t)-r_i(0)\right\rVert^2
\right]
$$

for isotropic three-dimensional transport. Conventional molecular dynamics needs enough time for ions to make many hops, which is expensive when forces come from first-principles calculations.

[LiFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01464) treats atomic displacement over a finite lag as conditional generation. A Propagator flow generates the next displacement, while a Corrector flow removes locally unphysical configurations. Conditioning on composition and temperature supports transfer across chemical systems, thermal conditions, and supercell sizes.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s31-liflow-architecture.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="LiFlow propagator and corrector flows compared with a molecular dynamics trajectory and transfer across composition temperature and supercell size." caption="LiFlow learns finite-time atomic displacements rather than one-step forces. The Propagator advances the state; the Corrector repairs local geometry before the next generated interval." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s31-lithium-diffusion.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="LiFlow and reference trajectories of lithium motion in a crystal from zero to twenty-five picoseconds." caption="The trajectory comparison checks the time-ordered object that LiFlow generates. Similar endpoint structures alone would not establish the correct transport dynamics. Source: <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01464'>LiFlow</a>." %}

The paper benchmarks 25-ps trajectories for 4,186 solid-electrolyte candidates at four temperatures. It reports Spearman correlations around 0.7--0.8 for lithium mean-squared displacement on unseen compositions and speedups up to roughly 600,000 times over first-principles simulation. The diffusivity plots also test transfer from small cells to larger supercells.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s31-diffusivity-estimates.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Lithium diffusivity versus temperature and inverse temperature for LiFlow machine-learning potential and ab initio molecular dynamics." caption="LiFlow's downstream check is diffusivity, not visual trajectory quality. The plots compare estimates across temperature, chemical composition, trajectory source, and supercell size. Source: <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01464'>LiFlow</a>." %}

The application completes a model-to-observable handoff: a crystal generator proposes $$M$$; stability calculations filter it; a dynamics model produces trajectories; displacement statistics estimate $$D$$. Every stage can fail independently.

## Semiconductor materials: carriers, atomic environments, and topology

A semiconductor has a controllable carrier population because its band gap is neither zero nor prohibitively large. Donor atoms such as phosphorus contribute extra electrons to silicon and produce n-type material. Acceptor atoms such as boron create holes and produce p-type material.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s32-doping-types.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="N-type and p-type semiconductor regions with extra electrons holes and a depletion zone." caption="Donor and acceptor doping control the mobile carrier type. The central depletion region forms when carriers diffuse across an n–p junction and leave ionized dopants behind." %}

In band language, donor levels sit near the conduction band and require little energy to release an electron. Acceptor levels sit near the valence band and capture an electron, leaving a mobile hole.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s32-semiconductor-bands.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Atomic and band diagrams for phosphorus-doped n-type silicon pure silicon and boron-doped p-type silicon." caption="Substitutional lattice chemistry creates band-level carrier states. Phosphorus adds a donor electron near the conduction band; boron creates an acceptor level and a valence-band hole. Original source not identified." %}

The structure of a device material includes defects, amorphous regions, surfaces, and interfaces. A force field trained only on equilibrium bulk crystals cannot be assumed to describe those environments.

### SAMD23 benchmarks force fields on semiconductor environments

Samsung Advanced Institute of Technology released [SAMD23](https://openreview.net/forum?id=hr9Bd1A9Un), a benchmark containing SiN and HfO datasets and evaluations of ten machine-learning force fields. The structural inventory spans crystalline and amorphous configurations, several crystal phases, point and extended defects, surfaces, isolated clusters, and molecular fragments.

The two bar charts count atomic environments allocated to training, test, and out-of-distribution splits for ten models. The distribution differs between SiN and HfO, so a low aggregate force error can hide a model's weakness on one material or structural regime.

### CTMT turns generation into topological-material discovery

The semiconductor section ends with topological materials. [CTMT](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41535-025-00731-0) combines a generative model, topology screening, an interatomic potential, and first-principles verification. CDVAE first produces 10,000 crystals from a database of topological insulators and semimetals. Novelty and legitimacy checks retain 4,715 valid candidates. A fast Topogivity check reduces the set to 104.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s34-ctmt-pipeline.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="CTMT workflow from topological materials database and CDVAE through novelty topology DFT phonon and topological quantum chemistry checks." caption="CTMT assigns a different scientific question to each stage: generation, structural validity, heuristic topology, energetic stability, phonon stability, and final topological classification. No one model output answers all six." %}

DFT formation energy retains 77 candidates. An energy-above-hull threshold of 0.16 eV per atom leaves 57. M3GNet phonon filtering leaves 32 dynamically stable structures. Band-structure and topological quantum chemistry calculations identify twenty nontrivial materials: four topological insulators and sixteen topological semimetals.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s34-ctmt-funnel.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="CTMT funnel from 104 candidates to 77 negative-formation-energy 57 low-energy-above-hull 32 phonon-stable and 20 topological materials." caption="The CTMT funnel quantifies attrition after generation. Only twenty of 104 topology-screened candidates pass the energetic, dynamical, and electronic verification chain." %}

The result is a better statement than “the generator discovered twenty materials.” Generation produced structures; the remaining pipeline established which claims each structure could support.

## Heterogeneous catalysis: the target is an adsorbate configuration

Heterogeneous catalysis places the catalyst and reactants in different phases, usually a solid surface interacting with gas- or liquid-phase molecules. Reactants reach the surface, weakly physisorb or form stronger chemisorbed bonds, react through surface intermediates, and desorb as products. Catalytic converters, ammonia synthesis, and fuel refining use this pattern because the solid catalyst can be recovered and reused.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s35-surface-catalysis.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A heterogeneous catalytic cycle with physisorption chemisorption bond formation and product desorption on a solid surface." caption="A heterogeneous reaction is a sequence of surface configurations. Adsorption, surface reaction, and desorption depend on the local atomic geometry of the slab and the adsorbate." %}

One computational bottleneck appears before reaction modeling: place an adsorbate on a slab in a configuration that relaxes to the lowest-energy adsorption state. The variables include the adsorption site, molecular orientation, surface coverage, and possible reconstruction. Random placement followed by DFT relaxation wastes calculations on implausible initial states.

### AdsorbDiff generates sites and orientations

[AdsorbDiff](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03962) uses conditional denoising diffusion to predict adsorbate position and orientation on a fixed slab. A reverse process moves a randomly initialized adsorbate toward a predicted configuration. A pretrained machine-learning force field then relaxes the output, checks constraints, and passes the candidate to a DFT single-point calculation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s36-adsorbdiff-pipeline.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AdsorbDiff pipeline from random adsorbate placement through reverse diffusion force-field relaxation constraints and DFT evaluation." caption="AdsorbDiff predicts a rigid adsorbate placement, then delegates local relaxation and energy verification to separate models. Success is defined after this full pipeline, not at the raw diffusion output." %}

The success criterion in the figure requires the predicted adsorption energy to fall within 0.1 eV of the best reference energy. With one proposed site, AdsorbDiff reaches a DFT success rate of 31.8%, compared with 9.1% for AdsorbML. Increasing the number of sites raises AdsorbDiff to 41.0% at ten proposals. AdsorbML eventually reaches 47.7%, exposing a tradeoff between one-shot proposal quality and search breadth.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s36-success-rate.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DFT success rate of AdsorbDiff and AdsorbML as the number of proposed adsorption sites increases." caption="AdsorbDiff is much stronger with one proposal; additional proposals narrow and eventually reverse the comparison with AdsorbML. Source: <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.03962'>AdsorbDiff</a>." %}

The Open Catalyst Project supplies the scale needed to train and evaluate these models: diverse adsorbates, surfaces, initial structures, relaxed structures, energies, and forces. The example panels show that even a single adsorbate species can occupy chemically and geometrically different surfaces.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-21/s36-ocp-adsorbates.gif" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Adsorbates placed on several chemically distinct catalyst surfaces from the Open Catalyst Project." caption="Open Catalyst Project structures expose the diversity hidden by the phrase 'adsorbate on a slab.' Elemental composition, facet, local coordination, adsorption site, and molecular orientation all change the relaxed energy." %}

The lecture closes here because heterogeneous catalysis makes the complete argument visible. A geometric generator is valuable when it proposes the right scientific object. For crystals, that object may be a composition and periodic structure. For battery screening, it must pass stability and transport checks. For semiconductor discovery, it may enter force-field simulation or a topology-verification funnel. For catalysis, it may be an adsorption configuration consumed by relaxation and DFT. Symmetry constrains each model, but the downstream observable decides whether its output is useful.
