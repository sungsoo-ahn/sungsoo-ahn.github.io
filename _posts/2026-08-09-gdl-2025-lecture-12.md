## The datasets change the scientific contract

The lecture focuses on force-field systems at scale: GNoME, MatterSim, Orb, SevenNet, and eSEN. Their performance claims only make sense relative to the data and tasks that precede them.

QM9 is small by current force-field standards: about 134,000 stable organic molecules, at most nine heavy atoms, and nineteen computed quantum properties. Its targets are invariant molecular scalars.

## Periodic crystals need a periodic representation

A crystal is not a finite point cloud. A lattice matrix $$L=[\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3]$$ and the atoms in one cell generate the infinite structure by integer translations:

$$
\mathcal X=\{(p_i+Ln,a_i):i=1,\ldots,N,\ n\in\mathbb Z^3\}.
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-12/slide-15.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Crystal unit cell lattice vectors and periodic copies." caption="A finite cell and lattice generate infinitely many translated atomic images. Cropped from the 2025 GDL force-field-scaling lecture, slide 15." %}

Moving a cell boundary changes which periodic image contains an atom without changing the crystal. Matformer constructs a periodic multi-edge graph from distances to neighboring images and designs its Transformer to be invariant to this choice.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-12/slide-16.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Matformer periodic multi edge graph and boundary invariance." caption="Periodic image edges make the graph independent of the arbitrary unit-cell boundary. Cropped from the 2025 GDL force-field-scaling lecture, slide 16." %}

Its radius graph and periodic pattern encoding expose multiple images of the same atom pair to the model.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-12/slide-17.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Matformer radius graph and periodic pattern encoding." caption="Matformer explicitly represents repeated geometric relations across lattice translations. The source surveys ALIGNN, PotNet, Crystalformer, ComFormer, and reciprocal-space ReGNet, while questioning whether every crystal-specific mechanism yields practical gains.

## Does equivariance matter at scale?

Orb prioritizes scalability and discards explicit invariance and equivariance. Its attention-augmented message-passing network is seven times smaller than MatterSim, can include systems of up to 5,000 atoms during pretraining, and reports state-of-the-art Matbench Discovery results.

Orb uses an attention-augmented Graph Network-based Simulator and diffusion-based materials pretraining.

## Parallel simulation with SevenNet

SevenNet starts from a NequIP-like potential pretrained on Materials Project and designs a parallelization scheme for molecular dynamics.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-12/slide-32.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SevenNet architecture and scalable parallel algorithm." caption="SevenNet targets the systems problem of distributing a short-range equivariant potential. Spatial decomposition divides the simulation cell into processor-owned subdomains. Ghost atoms communicate the neighboring coordinates required by cutoff interactions across processor boundaries.

## eSEN: smoothness and physical property prediction

eSEN builds on eSCN and reports leading Matbench Discovery results while extracting practical rules for force-field training.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-12/slide-35.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="eSEN architecture and smooth potential motivation." caption="eSEN links benchmark performance to smoothness requirements of simulated physical observables. Cropped from the 2025 GDL force-field-scaling lecture, slide 35." %}

Its desiderata are physical, not only statistical. Forces should be conservative, and energy derivatives should remain bounded so numerical solvers conserve energy. The proposed choices are to predict forces as energy gradients, use smooth distance-cutoff envelopes, and prefer Bessel radial bases over Gaussian bases that are more sensitive to coordinate shifts.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-12/slide-36.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="eSEN conservative force and smooth cutoff guidelines." caption="Model smoothness and energy-derived forces determine whether a low static error produces stable simulation observables. Cropped from the 2025 GDL force-field-scaling lecture, slide 36." %}

The benchmark tables report state-of-the-art results across several tasks as of March 2025.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-12/slide-37.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="eSEN benchmark results across materials tasks." caption="eSEN closes the source survey with property, force-field, and discovery comparisons. Cropped from the 2025 GDL force-field-scaling lecture, slide 37." %}

The scaling problem has several denominators. Dataset scale expands chemical and thermodynamic coverage. Periodic graphs define which crystal equivalences the model respects. Active learning controls first-principles labeling. Equivariance changes sample efficiency and compute. Domain decomposition changes the largest simulable system. Smooth energy models change whether trajectories yield stable physical properties. A single leaderboard cannot substitute for these separate contracts.
