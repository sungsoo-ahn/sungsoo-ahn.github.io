## Why proteins matter

Proteins occupy a central position in biology: enzymes catalyze reactions, receptors and signaling proteins coordinate cells, structural proteins build tissues, and antibodies recognize foreign molecules. The same functional diversity gives them therapeutic importance, from engineered enzymes to antibodies and designed binding proteins.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s04-protein-roles.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Biological roles and therapeutic relevance of proteins." caption="Protein function spans cellular machinery and therapeutic intervention." %}

The recent breakthrough sequence combines three capabilities: AlphaFold-scale structure prediction, large protein language models such as ESM, and generative sequence and structure design such as ProteinMPNN and RFdiffusion. Each solves a different map between sequence, structure, and function.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s05-protein-ai-breakthroughs.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Major breakthroughs in protein structure prediction language modeling and design." caption="Modern protein design relies on distinct advances in folding, representation learning, sequence design, and structure generation." %}

Programmable protein design asks for the inverse problem: specify a desired function, then produce a compatible structure and amino-acid sequence. The difficulty is that sequence, three-dimensional structure, dynamics, and biological function are coupled but not interchangeable.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s06-programmable-design-goal.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Desired function mapped by an AI model into protein structure and sequence." caption="Programmable design aims to invert the usual sequence-to-function direction." %}

In probabilistic terms, analysis often moves forward through $$p(\text{structure}\mid\text{sequence})$$ or $$p(\text{function}\mid\text{sequence},\text{structure})$$. Design asks for a reverse conditional such as $$p(\text{sequence},\text{structure}\mid\text{desired function})$$. Bayes' rule does not make that reverse map easy: many sequences can realize similar folds, one fold can support several biochemical contexts, and a function label rarely specifies assay conditions, dynamics, expression, or stability. The model therefore proposes candidates under an incomplete specification; it does not solve function by inverting a deterministic arrow.

## Sequence, structure, and dynamics

A protein begins as a linear chain over twenty amino-acid types. Their side chains create chemical diversity while the peptide backbone provides a repeating scaffold.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s07-peptide-chain.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A linear chain of amino acids connected by peptide bonds." caption="Primary structure supplies an ordered peptide polymer before any three-dimensional folding is considered." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s07-amino-acid-classes.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein primary structure and chemical diversity of twenty amino acids." caption="Amino-acid identity supplies local chemistry along a common peptide backbone." %}

The same chain has a hierarchy of representations: primary sequence, local secondary structure, a folded tertiary structure, and possibly a multichain quaternary assembly. A representation model may target any one of these levels or the relations between them.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s08-structure-hierarchy.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein hierarchy from amino acid sequence through secondary tertiary and quaternary structure." caption="Protein organization turns a one-dimensional sequence into local motifs, a three-dimensional fold, and assemblies." %}

Geometrically, the backbone is the repeating N--Cα--C chain. Side chains attach chemically specific groups, and backbone and side-chain dihedral angles determine much of the local conformation. Cartesian coordinates are therefore one representation of a constrained torsional object.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s09-backbone-torsions.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein backbone side chains and dihedral angle geometry." caption="Backbone connectivity and torsion angles organize the local geometry on which side-chain chemistry acts." %}

Proteins are not rigid. Thermal fluctuations populate an energy landscape, binding interfaces can rearrange, and molecular machines such as ABC transporters move between functional conformations. One static structure is therefore a useful state, not a complete description of protein behavior.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s10-energy-landscape.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein energy landscape conformational fluctuation and transporter motion." caption="Protein function often depends on an ensemble of conformations and transitions between them." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s10-transporter-cycle.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Conformational cycle of an ATP-binding cassette transporter during substrate transport." caption="The transporter's ordered conformational changes make the distinction between a folded structure and a functional cycle concrete." %}

Traditional modeling separated two strategies. Homology modeling inferred structure from related sequences and known templates, but depended on available structural relatives. Molecular dynamics used physical force fields to simulate motion, at considerable computational cost.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s11-traditional-modeling.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Homology modeling and molecular dynamics as traditional protein modeling approaches." caption="Template-based inference and force-field simulation supplied complementary but limited routes to protein structure and motion." %}

Modern learning methods are powered by scale. UniProt supplies billions of protein sequences, while the Protein Data Bank supplies hundreds of thousands of experimentally resolved biomolecular structures. These datasets support different supervision: evolutionary regularity is sequence-rich, whereas geometric supervision remains smaller and more expensive.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s12-sequence-structure-databases.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Large protein sequence and structure databases UniProt and PDB." caption="Sequence and structure databases differ greatly in scale and in the information they provide." %}

The lecture organizes protein reasoning into four representation types: 1D sequences, 2D multiple-sequence alignments and pair relations, 3D structures, and 4D dynamics. Later methods move between these types rather than operating on one universal representation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s13-representation-types.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein sequence MSA structure and dynamics representation types." caption="Sequence, evolutionary relations, structure, and dynamics expose different protein information." %}

These dimensions are information types, not merely tensor ranks. A 1D chain contains residue identity and order. An MSA adds homologous sequences and therefore family-specific evolutionary evidence. A 3D structure supplies coordinates, frames, torsions, surfaces, and contacts at one state. Dynamics adds a distribution or time-indexed process over such states. Converting a structure to a contact matrix makes a two-index tensor, but it does not turn the structure into evolutionary MSA evidence. This distinction prevents the visual shorthand “1D → 2D → 3D → 4D” from being read as a guaranteed hierarchy of increasing completeness.

## A map of protein learning tasks

An MSA aligns homologous sequences. Conserved positions reveal evolutionary constraints, while correlated substitutions suggest residue pairs that are structurally or functionally coupled.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s14-msa.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Multiple sequence alignment with conservation and coevolution information." caption="MSAs turn protein families into evidence about conserved and co-evolving residues." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s14-mutation-function.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A mutation changing a protein sequence and causing a loss of molecular function." caption="Evolutionary conservation matters because changing a constrained site can alter structure or eliminate function." %}

Sequence representation learning treats residues as tokens and uses language-model objectives to produce contextual embeddings. Those representations support downstream property prediction and sequence design.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s15-sequence-encoder.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Transformer style encoding of a protein amino acid sequence." caption="Protein language models transfer token-level context into representations for prediction and design." %}

Structure representation learning instead encodes residues, atoms, surfaces, or geometric graphs. Geometric and graph deep learning make rotations, neighborhoods, and interactions part of the representation rather than expecting a sequence model to infer them implicitly.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s16-structure-learning-pipeline.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein structure represented by geometric graphs surfaces and local features." caption="Three-dimensional encoders choose which geometric entities and relations become learnable features." %}

Structure prediction maps sequence and pair evidence into three-dimensional coordinates. AlphaFold2 is the central example, combining MSA and pair representations with a geometric structure module.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s17-alphafold-map.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold2 pipeline from sequence and pair inputs to protein coordinates." caption="Folding transforms one- and two-dimensional evidence into a three-dimensional structure." %}

Ensemble prediction extends the target from one 3D structure to a distribution over structures or trajectories. Diffusion and flow matching provide generative tools for this 1D-to-4D problem.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s18-ensemble-generation.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein ensemble prediction and dynamics generation methods." caption="Dynamics models target conformational distributions or transitions rather than one folded coordinate set." %}

Sequence design includes unconditional generation and motif conditioning, as well as inverse folding: choose a sequence expected to realize a given backbone. Autoregressive and discrete diffusion models provide different construction processes over the amino-acid vocabulary.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s19-proteinmpnn.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein sequence generation and inverse folding from a backbone." caption="Sequence design either generates a chain directly or conditions on a desired structure." %}

Structure design generates three-dimensional backbones, often with diffusion or flow matching, before a sequence is assigned. Its outputs are hypotheses that still require sequence design, folding checks, and experimental validation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s20-structure-diffusion.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Generated protein backbone structures from three-dimensional diffusion and flow models." caption="Structure generators propose new backbone geometries rather than predicting the fold of a fixed sequence." %}

## Protein language models

The available data establish the modeling question: how should billions of sequences and hundreds of thousands of structures be represented so that information transfers across tasks?

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s22-data-imbalance.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="UniProt PDB and SwissProt scale for protein sequence and structure learning." caption="Protein representation learning begins from an asymmetric supply of sequences and structures." %}

The standard protein language model tokenizes amino acids and applies a Transformer encoder. Next-token or masked-token prediction trains the model to reconstruct sequence context without task-specific labels.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s23-protein-lm.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein language model tokenization Transformer and masked prediction objective." caption="Self-supervision treats amino-acid recovery as the pretraining signal." %}

For masked modeling, sample a set of positions $$M$$, replace their residues by masks or noisy tokens, and minimize

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{MLM}}
=-\sum_{i\in M}\log p_\theta(x_i\mid x_{\setminus M}).
$$

The target is local residue identity, but the context needed to solve it can include motifs, long-range evolutionary regularities, and family membership. That is why structural information can emerge without coordinate labels. It is also why emergence should not be confused with full geometric supervision: the loss only rewards structure insofar as structure helps predict sequence tokens.

The resulting representations support structure, secondary-structure, functional-annotation, family, and variant-effect tasks. Fitness prediction is one particularly important application: mutations are scored by how plausibly they preserve or improve function.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s24-fitness-landscape.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein language model downstream structure function family and fitness tasks." caption="One pretrained sequence representation can be queried by several task-specific heads." %}

[ProGen](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-022-01618-2) uses autoregressive modeling conditioned on protein-family information. Sampling one residue after another makes it a sequence generator as well as a representation model.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s25-progen.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ProGen autoregressive protein family language model." caption="Autoregressive conditioning steers sequence generation toward specified protein families." %}

The corresponding likelihood factorizes in an explicit generation order:

$$
p_\theta(x_{1:L}\mid c)
=\prod_{i=1}^{L}p_\theta(x_i\mid x_{<i},c),
$$

where $$c$$ is the control tag. This yields an exact sequence likelihood under the chosen ordering and makes prefix conditioning natural. The cost is sequential decoding: a decision at position $$i$$ becomes context for every later residue, and errors cannot be revised unless an external search or refinement procedure is added.

[ESM](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2016239118) scales masked language modeling across evolutionary sequence data. Its unsupervised contact-prediction improvement shows that structural regularities emerge from sequence pretraining; ESMFold then turns the learned embeddings into a single-sequence folding model.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s26-esm-masked-lm.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ESM scaling contact prediction and protein sequence processing." caption="Scaling sequence-only masked modeling improves contact information and supports language-model-based folding." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s26-esm-msa-depth.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Unsupervised long-range contact precision improving with MSA depth for ESM models and alignments." caption="In the ESM study, deeper evolutionary coverage helps both language-model and alignment-based contact signals." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s26-esm-scale.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Long-range contact precision plotted against protein language model parameter count." caption="The ESM scaling result shows that larger sequence models recover more structural signal, but scale does not remove the need to define the downstream task and data regime." %}

[DPLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18567) replaces one fixed masking ratio with a discrete diffusion process. Different timesteps correspond to different corruption levels, so the same model supports representation learning and iterative sequence generation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s27-dplm.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DPLM discrete masked diffusion protein language model." caption="A time-indexed masking process unifies protein encoding and sequence denoising." %}

If each residue survives a corruption step with probability $$\alpha_t$$, a simple marginal corruption law is

$$
q(x_i^t\mid x_i^0)
=\alpha_t\,\delta_{x_i^0}
+(1-\alpha_t)\,\delta_{\texttt{[MASK]}}.
$$

Training predicts the clean residue or reverse transition from different $$t$$. Generation begins from a highly masked chain and repeatedly fills or revises positions. Unlike the left-to-right factorization, the intermediate state exposes the whole partially generated sequence. The lecture uses DPLM to make a broader point: representation learning and generation can share a denoising objective, but their evaluation remains different—predictive transfer for the former, validity, diversity, novelty, and function for the latter.

An [MSA Transformer](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/rao21a.html) models an entire protein family. Row attention captures dependencies within each sequence, while column attention compares homologous residues across sequences. The representation therefore has an explicit evolutionary axis absent from a single-sequence model.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s28-msa-transformer.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="MSA Transformer row and column axial attention." caption="Axial attention separates within-sequence context from across-homologue evidence." %}

## Structure and multimodal representation learning

Structure determines many protein functions through shape, chemistry, and interaction surfaces. A useful structure representation must therefore preserve more than a bag of atomic positions.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s29-structure-function.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein enzyme substrate molecular function and antibody binding structures." caption="Protein function depends on geometric arrangements that support catalysis, recognition, and binding." %}

A point cloud preserves coordinates but can neglect residue order and chemical relations. Two proteins may have similar spatial samples while differing in the connectivity and sequential organization that make the fold meaningful.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s30-point-cloud-limit.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein surface point cloud compared with ribbon structure." caption="Coordinates alone do not encode the residue arrangement and relations of a protein chain." %}

[GearNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06125) constructs a multi-relational residue graph. Sequential, k-nearest-neighbor, and radius edges expose chain order and spatial neighborhoods as distinct relation types.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s31-gearnet-graph.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein multi relational graph with sequential nearest neighbor and radius edges." caption="A relational protein graph preserves both covalent order and nonlocal spatial contacts." %}

A generic relational update makes the construction explicit:

$$
h_i'
=\sigma\!\left(
W_0h_i
+\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}
\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_r(i)}
W_r\,m_r(h_i,h_j,e_{ij})
\right).
$$

The relation label $$r$$ distinguishes forward and backward sequence neighbors from spatial k-nearest and radius neighbors. A residue adjacent along the chain and a residue close only after folding need not contribute through the same parameters. Coordinates still require a geometric encoding—distances, angles, torsions, local frames, or equivariant features—because a graph edge alone says that two residues are related, not how they are arranged in space.

Geometric pretraining generates correlated views of the same structure and aligns their representations. Subspace or structural cropping changes what remains visible while encouraging the encoder to retain reusable geometric information.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s32-geometric-pretraining.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GearNet geometric pretraining with correlated protein graph views." caption="Pretraining constructs related structural views and asks the encoder to recognize their shared protein information." %}

Siamese diffusion-trajectory prediction creates another family of correlated views. Conformer simulation supplies structural perturbations, and a mutual-denoising objective learns representations along those trajectories.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s33-diffusion-pretraining.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Siamese sequence structure diffusion trajectory pretraining." caption="Diffusion trajectories turn conformational perturbations into a protein-encoder pretraining signal." %}

Sequence and structure encoders can also be trained jointly. The central question is not whether the modalities can be concatenated, but which fusion design transfers complementary information without discarding the strengths of either pretrained model.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s34-fusion-architectures.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Architectures for joint protein sequence and structure representation learning." caption="Multimodal designs differ in where and how sequence and geometric encoders exchange information." %}

The three fusion patterns in the figure imply different information bottlenecks. Serial fusion lets one encoder's output condition the other, so the second modality sees a compressed summary. Parallel fusion preserves separate encoders and merges their final representations, which protects pretrained features but delays interaction. Cross fusion exchanges information inside the stacks and can model residue-level agreement, at higher memory and optimization cost. None is universally “more multimodal”; the right choice depends on whether the downstream label is mostly sequence-driven, mostly geometric, or defined by their local correspondence.

[SaProt](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/1c42513b8895ab11fbbb5b7e8e6b6b02-Abstract-Conference.html) creates a structure-aware vocabulary. A Foldseek-based tokenizer assigns a structural token at each residue, then the language model consumes paired amino-acid and structural symbols.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s35-saprot-tokenizer.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SaProt structure tokenizer and sequence structure vocabulary." caption="Discrete structural tokens let a language model read local geometry alongside amino-acid identity." %}

[DPLM-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13782) uses discrete masked diffusion over both sequence and three-dimensional structural information. Joint corruption and denoising make the multimodal representation generative rather than only contrastive.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s36-dplm2.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DPLM2 multimodal diffusion over protein sequence and structure." caption="A shared masked diffusion process jointly models protein sequence and geometry." %}

[ESM-3](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ads0018) tokenizes a still wider set of channels: sequence, secondary structure, three-dimensional structure, solvent accessibility, and functional keywords. Masking arbitrary subsets supports conditional generation across modalities.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s37-esm3-architecture.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ESM3 multimodal token tracks for sequence structure SASA and function." caption="ESM-3 casts heterogeneous protein annotations as aligned token tracks for masked generation." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s37-esm3-decoding.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Iterative masked decoding of a protein structure from noise to a completed fold." caption="ESM-3 iteratively unmasks a coherent object across aligned sequence, structure, and function tracks." %}

[ProtST](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/xu23t.html) introduces biomedical text as another modality. Protein sequences and textual descriptions connect evolutionary patterns to functional concepts that are not explicit in sequence alone.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s38-sequence-text.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein sequence and biomedical text information from UniProt and SwissProt." caption="Text descriptions align protein sequences with enzymes, localization, and other semantic annotations." %}

Training uses paired sequence--text data, masked prediction, and contrastive alignment. The generative objectives model each modality, while the contrastive loss brings matching sequence and description embeddings together.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s39-protst.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ProtST masked prediction and contrastive protein text pretraining." caption="Multimodal pretraining combines within-modality reconstruction with cross-modal alignment." %}

## Understanding, fitness, and antibody optimization

The PEER benchmark groups protein understanding into function prediction, localization, structure, interaction, and mutation-effect tasks. This breadth exposes whether a representation transfers beyond the objective used during pretraining.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s40-peer-tasks.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="PEER benchmark taxonomy of protein understanding tasks." caption="Protein understanding includes distinct prediction families rather than one generic representation score." %}

Fitness prediction asks how mutations change a protein's function. A learned model estimates the landscape around a reference sequence and ranks variants that may be beneficial.

[Multi-scale representation learning](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b7d795e655c1463d7299688d489e8ef4-Abstract-Conference.html) combines sequence, structural, and geometric signals for zero-shot fitness prediction. It uses a protein structure encoder alongside representations from large sequence models.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s42-fitness-architecture.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Multi scale sequence and structure representation model for protein fitness." caption="Fitness prediction can combine evolutionary sequence features with local and global structural context." %}

The reported comparison shows that relatively small structure-based pretraining can substantially improve a much larger protein language model. Model scale and modality value are therefore separate axes.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s43-fitness-benchmark.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Zero shot protein fitness benchmark comparing language and structure enriched models." caption="Adding structural learning improves zero-shot fitness beyond increasing sequence-model scale alone." %}

Antibody optimization adds an interaction problem. Given an antigen--antibody complex, the goal is to mutate the antibody so that binding affinity improves. The model must represent two proteins and their interface, not only an isolated sequence.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s44-antibody-objective.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Antigen antibody complex and binding free energy change objective." caption="Affinity maturation targets the mutation-induced binding free-energy change at a protein interface." %}

The slide writes the target as a change of binding free energy,

$$
\Delta\Delta G_{\mathrm{bind}}
=\Delta G_{\mathrm{bind}}^{(\mathrm{mut})}
-\Delta G_{\mathrm{bind}}^{(\mathrm{wt})}.
$$

Under the usual convention, a negative value means the mutation makes binding more favorable. This target is conditional on the complex pose, protonation and assay conditions, and the structural response to mutation. A predictor that freezes the wild-type geometry estimates a narrower quantity than one that permits side-chain repacking or backbone relaxation. The rotamer negatives in the following slide are therefore not incidental augmentation: they expose the model to physically different interface conformations.

The [GearBind model](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-51563-8) uses multi-level message passing and contrastive pretraining. Negative examples come from random residue mutations and side-chain torsions sampled from a rotamer library, so the objective exposes both sequence and conformational changes.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s45-gearbinding.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Geometric graph pretraining for antibody affinity maturation." caption="Interface-aware geometric pretraining distinguishes plausible affinity-improving mutations from sequence and rotamer negatives." %}

Evaluation included both in-silico prediction and wet-lab validation. That final stage matters because improved rank correlation or mutation scoring does not by itself establish that selected antibodies bind better experimentally.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s46-antibody-validation.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Computational and wet lab validation of antibody affinity maturation." caption="Experimental measurements test whether the model's proposed mutations improve antibody affinity in practice." %}

## AlphaFold2 and structure prediction

Structure and dynamics both connect sequence to protein function. Structure prediction targets a coordinate configuration, while dynamics asks which configurations are populated and how they interconvert.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s48-sequence-dynamics-function.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein sequence structure dynamics and function relationship." caption="A predicted structure is one layer in the relation from sequence to dynamics and function." %}

[AlphaFold2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03819-2) represents each residue backbone by a free rigid frame and its side chain by torsion angles. This structured output separates backbone pose from internal side-chain rotation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s49-residue-frames.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold2 residue rigid frames and side chain torsion representation." caption="Residue frames and torsions provide a geometric output space for protein coordinates." %}

Let residue $$i$$ carry a frame $$T_i=(R_i,t_i)$$. A local atom coordinate $$a_{ik}$$ becomes

$$
x_{ik}=R_i a_{ik}+t_i.
$$

A global rigid motion $$(Q,b)$$ sends every frame to $$(QR_i,Qt_i+b)$$ and every atom to $$Qx_{ik}+b$$. Relative geometry expressed through $$T_i^{-1}T_j$$ is unchanged by this common motion. This is the operational reason residue frames are useful: the network can reason locally while the assembled coordinates transform coherently.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s49-af2-predictions.gif" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold2 predicted protein structures compared with experimental structures." caption="The animated comparison shows the coordinate-level output whose internal representation is decomposed into residue frames and torsions." %}

The full system begins with sequence databases, template search, and MSA features; alternates information between MSA and residue-pair representations in the Evoformer; and sends the resulting single and pair features to the structure module.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s50-alphafold2-overview.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold2 full architecture overview from sequence databases to structure." caption="AlphaFold2 couples evolutionary preprocessing, Evoformer reasoning, and geometric decoding." %}

The Evoformer uses row and column attention on the MSA, outer-product updates from MSA to pair features, and triangle operations within the pair representation. These paths let sequence-family evidence and residue-residue geometry constrain one another.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s51-evoformer.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold2 Evoformer MSA pair and triangle update architecture." caption="Evoformer alternates between MSA evidence and pairwise geometric consistency." %}

The triangle update treats each third residue $$k$$ as evidence about a pair $$(i,j)$$. Schematically,

$$
z_{ij}'=z_{ij}
+\sum_k \phi(z_{ik})\odot\psi(z_{kj}),
$$

with learned projections and gates in the actual block. This resembles the consistency relation that the route from $$i$$ to $$j$$ can be checked through $$k$$. Triangle attention then selects which intermediate residues matter. The operation does not directly enforce a realizable Euclidean distance matrix, but it gives the pair representation a computational motif aligned with geometric consistency.

The structure module updates residue frames with invariant point attention and reconstructs atom positions and torsions. Recycling feeds predicted structure information back into the network for iterative refinement.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s52-structure-module.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold2 structure module with invariant point attention and frame updates." caption="The structure module turns learned single and pair features into a chain of residue frames and atoms." %}

Invariant point attention builds query and key points in residue-local frames and compares them after transforming them into the shared global frame. A distance term

$$
\left\lVert R_i q_{ih}+t_i-(R_j k_{jh}+t_j)\right\rVert^2
$$

is unchanged when all frames undergo the same global rigid motion. Its scalar value can therefore enter an attention logit while the frame updates remain geometrically meaningful. IPA is not simply ordinary attention applied to coordinates: it couples learned scalar features to point geometry through an invariant comparison.

The architectural view makes the handoff explicit: input features create MSA and pair states, Evoformer refines them, the structure module predicts coordinates, and recycled structure features initiate another pass.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s53-ipa-details.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold2 architecture with Evoformer structure module and recycling." caption="Recycling couples repeated representation reasoning to repeated geometric refinement." %}

## Beyond one AlphaFold2 pipeline

[RoseTTAFold](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abj8754) uses three interacting tracks for sequence/MSA features, pairwise distances, and three-dimensional coordinates. Information moves among the tracks rather than waiting for one final coordinate decoder.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s54-rosettafold.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RoseTTAFold three track sequence pair and coordinate architecture." caption="RoseTTAFold jointly updates one-dimensional, two-dimensional, and three-dimensional protein representations." %}

[OmegaFold](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-023-02046-5) removes the MSA requirement and predicts structure from a single sequence. Its architecture replaces explicit family alignment with learned sequence representations and a geometric structure module.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s55-omegafold.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="OmegaFold single sequence protein structure prediction architecture." caption="Single-sequence folding trades explicit MSA construction for information learned by a sequence model." %}

[ESMFold](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ade2574) similarly builds on pretrained ESM embeddings. It accelerates large-scale structure prediction by avoiding an MSA search while using evolutionary-scale language-model pretraining.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s56-esmfold.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ESMFold language model based protein structure prediction." caption="ESMFold converts pretrained sequence representations into atomic protein structures." %}

Side-chain packing is a narrower geometric task: given the backbone, predict the coordinates of the side chains. [AttnPacker](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2216438120) performs end-to-end packing and also connects the task to inverse folding.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s57-attnpacker.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AttnPacker side chain packing architecture and packed protein examples." caption="Packing conditions on a known backbone and resolves the remaining side-chain geometry." %}

DiffPack represents side-chain conformation through torsion angles and applies an autoregressive torsional diffusion model. The state space matches the periodic variables that define side-chain rotamers.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s58-diffpack.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DiffPack torsional diffusion model for protein side chain packing." caption="Torsional diffusion generates periodic side-chain angles while conditioning on the backbone." %}

A side chain with $$m_i$$ rotatable bonds lives on a torus $$(S^1)^{m_i}$$, not in an unconstrained box of angles. The values $$-\pi+\varepsilon$$ and $$\pi-\varepsilon$$ describe neighboring conformations. Torsional diffusion respects that periodic identification while the backbone fixes the local chemical scaffold. Cartesian packing predicts more coordinates directly; torsional packing builds bond lengths and angles into the representation but still needs a reconstruction map and steric checks.

[AlphaFold-Multimer](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.10.04.463034v2) extends folding to protein complexes. Its target includes the relative placement and interface between chains, not only the internal fold of each monomer.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s59-alphafold-multimer.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold Multimer protein complex predictions and benchmark." caption="Complex prediction adds interchain assembly and interface accuracy to monomer folding." %}

[RoseTTAFold All-Atom](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adl2528) broadens the token set to residues, ligands, ions, and other chemical components, then predicts the structure of every component. This moves the problem from protein-only folding toward generalized biomolecular modeling and docking.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s60-rfaa-overview.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RoseTTAFold All Atom biomolecular tokens and complex structures." caption="All-atom models represent proteins and nonprotein components in one structural prediction system." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s60-rfaa-benchmark.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RoseTTAFold All-Atom docking benchmark compared with specialized docking models." caption="The metric is the fraction of predictions below a coordinate-error threshold, so this comparison supports a specific docking claim rather than a universal statement about biomolecular accuracy." %}

[AlphaFold3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07487-w) also targets all-atom biomolecular interactions across proteins, ligands, and ions. It replaces the earlier equivariant structure module with a diffusion-based coordinate generator conditioned by the learned pair representation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s61-alphafold3.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold3 all atom multimodal diffusion architecture and benchmark results." caption="AlphaFold3 uses diffusion to generate all-atom complexes across several molecular modalities." %}

This output change is substantial. AlphaFold2 iteratively updates residue frames and torsions; AlphaFold3 denoises atom coordinates for a multimolecular assembly. The conditioning network still reasons through single and pair representations, but the generative head can express uncertainty through multiple samples. Those samples are alternative coordinate predictions under the model, not an equilibrium ensemble with Boltzmann weights or kinetic transition times.

[Proteina](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00710) uses a flow-based architecture for protein backbone generation and trains on filtered clusters derived from AlphaFoldDB. Here the target is not the structure of a given sequence but a distribution of plausible backbones.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s62-proteina-flow.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Proteina flow based protein backbone generation and AlphaFoldDB data curation." caption="Flow-based backbone generation combines a geometric model with large predicted-structure datasets." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s62-proteina-data.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Training-set sizes for PDB and two filtered AlphaFoldDB-derived protein backbone datasets." caption="Proteina's training-data comparison shows that scaling the model also means changing the source and curation of the structural corpus." %}

Open training code makes these systems inspectable and reproducible. [OpenFold](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02272-z) retrains AlphaFold2, while Protenix and Boltz-1 provide open AlphaFold3-style implementations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s63-open-implementations.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="OpenFold Protenix and Boltz1 open protein structure prediction implementations." caption="Open implementations expose training and inference details that cannot be inferred from a web service alone." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s63-boltz1-benchmark.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Boltz-1 benchmark comparison with AlphaFold3 across structure and physical-validity metrics." caption="Open reproduction should be judged on physical validity and molecular task metrics, not only visual similarity of predicted structures." %}

For users, web servers turn models into accessible prediction tools. [ColabFold](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-022-01488-1), ESMFold services, and AlphaFold servers differ in input requirements, speed, and supported molecular systems. Choosing among them still depends on whether the task is monomer folding, rapid single-sequence screening, or all-atom complex prediction.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/s64-structure-servers.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ColabFold ESMFold and AlphaFold web servers for structure prediction." caption="Protein structure servers expose different models and task scopes through practical interfaces." %}

The lecture therefore ends with interfaces rather than another architecture block. A method is usable only when its input contract, output confidence, supported molecular types, databases, and compute requirements match the question. ColabFold accelerates MSA-based folding workflows; ESMFold emphasizes single-sequence throughput; the AlphaFold server broadens the molecular system. None of these interfaces turns structure prediction into fitness prediction, conformational dynamics, or experimental validation. The representation-to-task map established at the start remains the correct way to interpret the final prediction.
