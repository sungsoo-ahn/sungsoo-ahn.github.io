## Why proteins matter

Proteins occupy a central position in biology: enzymes catalyze reactions, receptors and signaling proteins coordinate cells, structural proteins build tissues, and antibodies recognize foreign molecules. The same functional diversity gives them therapeutic importance, from engineered enzymes to antibodies and designed binding proteins.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-4.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Biological roles and therapeutic relevance of proteins." caption="Protein function spans cellular machinery and therapeutic intervention. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 4." %}

The recent breakthrough sequence combines three capabilities: AlphaFold-scale structure prediction, large protein language models such as ESM, and generative sequence and structure design such as ProteinMPNN and RFdiffusion. Each solves a different map between sequence, structure, and function.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-5.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Major breakthroughs in protein structure prediction language modeling and design." caption="Modern protein design relies on distinct advances in folding, representation learning, sequence design, and structure generation. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 5." %}

Programmable protein design asks for the inverse problem: specify a desired function, then produce a compatible structure and amino-acid sequence. The difficulty is that sequence, three-dimensional structure, dynamics, and biological function are coupled but not interchangeable.

## Sequence, structure, and dynamics

A protein begins as a linear chain over twenty amino-acid types. Their side chains create chemical diversity while the peptide backbone provides a repeating scaffold.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-7.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein primary structure and chemical diversity of twenty amino acids." caption="Amino-acid identity supplies local chemistry along a common peptide backbone. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 7." %}

The same chain has a hierarchy of representations: primary sequence, local secondary structure, a folded tertiary structure, and possibly a multichain quaternary assembly. A representation model may target any one of these levels or the relations between them.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-8.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein hierarchy from amino acid sequence through secondary tertiary and quaternary structure." caption="Protein organization turns a one-dimensional sequence into local motifs, a three-dimensional fold, and assemblies. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 8." %}

Geometrically, the backbone is the repeating N--Cα--C chain. Side chains attach chemically specific groups, and backbone and side-chain dihedral angles determine much of the local conformation. Cartesian coordinates are therefore one representation of a constrained torsional object.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-9.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein backbone side chains and dihedral angle geometry." caption="Backbone connectivity and torsion angles organize the local geometry on which side-chain chemistry acts. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 9." %}

Proteins are not rigid. Thermal fluctuations populate an energy landscape, binding interfaces can rearrange, and molecular machines such as ABC transporters move between functional conformations. One static structure is therefore a useful state, not a complete description of protein behavior.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-10.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein energy landscape conformational fluctuation and transporter motion." caption="Protein function often depends on an ensemble of conformations and transitions between them. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 10." %}

Traditional modeling separated two strategies. Homology modeling inferred structure from related sequences and known templates, but depended on available structural relatives. Molecular dynamics used physical force fields to simulate motion, at considerable computational cost.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-11.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Homology modeling and molecular dynamics as traditional protein modeling approaches." caption="Template-based inference and force-field simulation supplied complementary but limited routes to protein structure and motion. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 11." %}

Modern learning methods are powered by scale. UniProt supplies billions of protein sequences, while the Protein Data Bank supplies hundreds of thousands of experimentally resolved biomolecular structures. These datasets support different supervision: evolutionary regularity is sequence-rich, whereas geometric supervision remains smaller and more expensive.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-12.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Large protein sequence and structure databases UniProt and PDB." caption="Sequence and structure databases differ greatly in scale and in the information they provide. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 12." %}

The lecture organizes protein reasoning into four representation types: 1D sequences, 2D multiple-sequence alignments and pair relations, 3D structures, and 4D dynamics. Later methods move between these types rather than operating on one universal representation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-13.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein sequence MSA structure and dynamics representation types." caption="Sequence, evolutionary relations, structure, and dynamics expose different protein information. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 13." %}

## A map of protein learning tasks

An MSA aligns homologous sequences. Conserved positions reveal evolutionary constraints, while correlated substitutions suggest residue pairs that are structurally or functionally coupled.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-14.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Multiple sequence alignment with conservation and coevolution information." caption="MSAs turn protein families into evidence about conserved and co-evolving residues. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 14." %}

Sequence representation learning treats residues as tokens and uses language-model objectives to produce contextual embeddings. Those representations support downstream property prediction and sequence design.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-15.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Transformer style encoding of a protein amino acid sequence." caption="Protein language models transfer token-level context into representations for prediction and design. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 15." %}

Structure representation learning instead encodes residues, atoms, surfaces, or geometric graphs. Geometric and graph deep learning make rotations, neighborhoods, and interactions part of the representation rather than expecting a sequence model to infer them implicitly.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-16.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein structure represented by geometric graphs surfaces and local features." caption="Three-dimensional encoders choose which geometric entities and relations become learnable features. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 16." %}

Structure prediction maps sequence and pair evidence into three-dimensional coordinates. AlphaFold2 is the central example, combining MSA and pair representations with a geometric structure module.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-17.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold2 pipeline from sequence and pair inputs to protein coordinates." caption="Folding transforms one- and two-dimensional evidence into a three-dimensional structure. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 17." %}

Ensemble prediction extends the target from one 3D structure to a distribution over structures or trajectories. Diffusion and flow matching provide generative tools for this 1D-to-4D problem.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-18.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein ensemble prediction and dynamics generation methods." caption="Dynamics models target conformational distributions or transitions rather than one folded coordinate set. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 18." %}

Sequence design includes unconditional generation and motif conditioning, as well as inverse folding: choose a sequence expected to realize a given backbone. Autoregressive and discrete diffusion models provide different construction processes over the amino-acid vocabulary.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-19.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein sequence generation and inverse folding from a backbone." caption="Sequence design either generates a chain directly or conditions on a desired structure. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 19." %}

Structure design generates three-dimensional backbones, often with diffusion or flow matching, before a sequence is assigned. Its outputs are hypotheses that still require sequence design, folding checks, and experimental validation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-20.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Generated protein backbone structures from three-dimensional diffusion and flow models." caption="Structure generators propose new backbone geometries rather than predicting the fold of a fixed sequence. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 20." %}

## Protein language models

The available data establish the modeling question: how should billions of sequences and hundreds of thousands of structures be represented so that information transfers across tasks?

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-22.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="UniProt PDB and SwissProt scale for protein sequence and structure learning." caption="Protein representation learning begins from an asymmetric supply of sequences and structures. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 22." %}

The standard protein language model tokenizes amino acids and applies a Transformer encoder. Next-token or masked-token prediction trains the model to reconstruct sequence context without task-specific labels.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-23.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein language model tokenization Transformer and masked prediction objective." caption="Self-supervision treats amino-acid recovery as the pretraining signal. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 23." %}

The resulting representations support structure, secondary-structure, functional-annotation, family, and variant-effect tasks. Fitness prediction is one particularly important application: mutations are scored by how plausibly they preserve or improve function.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-24.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein language model downstream structure function family and fitness tasks." caption="One pretrained sequence representation can be queried by several task-specific heads. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 24." %}

ProGen uses autoregressive modeling conditioned on protein-family information. Sampling one residue after another makes it a sequence generator as well as a representation model.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-25.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ProGen autoregressive protein family language model." caption="Autoregressive conditioning steers sequence generation toward specified protein families. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 25." %}

ESM scales masked language modeling across evolutionary sequence data. Its unsupervised contact-prediction improvement shows that structural regularities emerge from sequence pretraining; ESMFold then turns the learned embeddings into a single-sequence folding model.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-26.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ESM scaling contact prediction and protein sequence processing." caption="Scaling sequence-only masked modeling improves contact information and supports language-model-based folding. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 26." %}

DPLM replaces one fixed masking ratio with a discrete diffusion process. Different timesteps correspond to different corruption levels, so the same model supports representation learning and iterative sequence generation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-27.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DPLM discrete masked diffusion protein language model." caption="A time-indexed masking process unifies protein encoding and sequence denoising. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 27." %}

An MSA Transformer models an entire protein family. Row attention captures dependencies within each sequence, while column attention compares homologous residues across sequences. The representation therefore has an explicit evolutionary axis absent from a single-sequence model.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-28.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="MSA Transformer row and column axial attention." caption="Axial attention separates within-sequence context from across-homologue evidence. ## Structure and multimodal representation learning

Structure determines many protein functions through shape, chemistry, and interaction surfaces. A useful structure representation must therefore preserve more than a bag of atomic positions.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-29.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein enzyme substrate molecular function and antibody binding structures." caption="Protein function depends on geometric arrangements that support catalysis, recognition, and binding. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 29." %}

A point cloud preserves coordinates but can neglect residue order and chemical relations. Two proteins may have similar spatial samples while differing in the connectivity and sequential organization that make the fold meaningful.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-30.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein surface point cloud compared with ribbon structure." caption="Coordinates alone do not encode the residue arrangement and relations of a protein chain. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 30." %}

GearNet constructs a multi-relational residue graph. Sequential, k-nearest-neighbor, and radius edges expose chain order and spatial neighborhoods as distinct relation types.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-31.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein multi relational graph with sequential nearest neighbor and radius edges." caption="A relational protein graph preserves both covalent order and nonlocal spatial contacts. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 31." %}

Geometric pretraining generates correlated views of the same structure and aligns their representations. Subspace or structural cropping changes what remains visible while encouraging the encoder to retain reusable geometric information.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-32.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GearNet geometric pretraining with correlated protein graph views." caption="Pretraining constructs related structural views and asks the encoder to recognize their shared protein information. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 32." %}

Siamese diffusion-trajectory prediction creates another family of correlated views. Conformer simulation supplies structural perturbations, and a mutual-denoising objective learns representations along those trajectories.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-33.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Siamese sequence structure diffusion trajectory pretraining." caption="Diffusion trajectories turn conformational perturbations into a protein-encoder pretraining signal. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 33." %}

Sequence and structure encoders can also be trained jointly. The central question is not whether the modalities can be concatenated, but which fusion design transfers complementary information without discarding the strengths of either pretrained model.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-34.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Architectures for joint protein sequence and structure representation learning." caption="Multimodal designs differ in where and how sequence and geometric encoders exchange information. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 34." %}

SaProt creates a structure-aware vocabulary. A Foldseek-based VQ-VAE assigns one of twenty structural tokens at each residue, then the language model consumes paired amino-acid and structural symbols.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-35.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SaProt structure tokenizer and sequence structure vocabulary." caption="Discrete structural tokens let a language model read local geometry alongside amino-acid identity. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 35." %}

DPLM-2 uses discrete masked diffusion over both sequence and three-dimensional structural information. Joint corruption and denoising make the multimodal representation generative rather than only contrastive.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-36.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DPLM2 multimodal diffusion over protein sequence and structure." caption="A shared masked diffusion process jointly models protein sequence and geometry. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 36." %}

ESM-3 tokenizes a still wider set of channels: sequence, secondary structure, three-dimensional structure, solvent accessibility, and functional keywords. Masking arbitrary subsets supports conditional generation across modalities.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-37.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ESM3 multimodal token tracks for sequence structure SASA and function." caption="ESM-3 casts heterogeneous protein annotations as aligned token tracks for masked generation. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 37." %}

ProtST introduces biomedical text as another modality. Protein sequences and textual descriptions connect evolutionary patterns to functional concepts that are not explicit in sequence alone.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-38.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein sequence and biomedical text information from UniProt and SwissProt." caption="Text descriptions align protein sequences with enzymes, localization, and other semantic annotations. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 38." %}

Training uses paired sequence--text data, masked prediction, and contrastive alignment. The generative objectives model each modality, while the contrastive loss brings matching sequence and description embeddings together.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-39.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ProtST masked prediction and contrastive protein text pretraining." caption="Multimodal pretraining combines within-modality reconstruction with cross-modal alignment. ## AlphaFold2 and structure prediction

Structure and dynamics both connect sequence to protein function. Structure prediction targets a coordinate configuration, while dynamics asks which configurations are populated and how they interconvert.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-48.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein sequence structure dynamics and function relationship." caption="A predicted structure is one layer in the relation from sequence to dynamics and function. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 48." %}

AlphaFold2 represents each residue backbone by a free rigid frame and its side chain by torsion angles. This structured output separates backbone pose from internal side-chain rotation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-49.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold2 residue rigid frames and side chain torsion representation." caption="Residue frames and torsions provide a geometric output space for protein coordinates. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 49." %}

The full system begins with sequence databases, template search, and MSA features; alternates information between MSA and residue-pair representations in the Evoformer; and sends the resulting single and pair features to the structure module.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-50.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold2 full architecture overview from sequence databases to structure." caption="AlphaFold2 couples evolutionary preprocessing, Evoformer reasoning, and geometric decoding. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 50." %}

The Evoformer uses row and column attention on the MSA, outer-product updates from MSA to pair features, and triangle operations within the pair representation. These paths let sequence-family evidence and residue-residue geometry constrain one another.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-51.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold2 Evoformer MSA pair and triangle update architecture." caption="Evoformer alternates between MSA evidence and pairwise geometric consistency. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 51." %}

The structure module updates residue frames with invariant point attention and reconstructs atom positions and torsions. Recycling feeds predicted structure information back into the network for iterative refinement.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-52.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold2 structure module with invariant point attention and frame updates." caption="The structure module turns learned single and pair features into a chain of residue frames and atoms. Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 52." %}

The architectural view makes the handoff explicit: input features create MSA and pair states, Evoformer refines them, the structure module predicts coordinates, and recycled structure features initiate another pass.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-53.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaFold2 architecture with Evoformer structure module and recycling." caption="Recycling couples repeated representation reasoning to repeated geometric refinement. ## Beyond one AlphaFold2 pipeline

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-55.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="OmegaFold single sequence protein structure prediction architecture." caption="Single-sequence folding trades explicit MSA construction for information learned by a sequence model. Side-chain packing is a narrower geometric task: given the backbone, predict the coordinates of the side chains. AttnPacker performs end-to-end packing and also connects the task to inverse folding.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-18/slide-57.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AttnPacker side chain packing architecture and packed protein examples." caption="Packing conditions on a known backbone and resolves the remaining side-chain geometry. DiffPack represents side-chain conformation through torsion angles and applies an autoregressive torsional diffusion model. The state space matches the periodic variables that define side-chain rotamers.

AlphaFold-Multimer extends folding to protein complexes. Its target includes the relative placement and interface between chains, not only the internal fold of each monomer.

Cropped from the 2025 GDL AI-for-proteins lecture, slide 59." %}

RoseTTAFold All-Atom broadens the token set to residues, ligands, ions, and other chemical components, then predicts the structure of every component. This moves the problem from protein-only folding toward generalized biomolecular modeling and docking.

AlphaFold3 also targets all-atom biomolecular interactions across proteins, ligands, and ions. It replaces the earlier equivariant structure module with a diffusion-based coordinate generator conditioned by the learned pair representation.

Proteina uses a flow-based architecture for protein backbone generation and trains on filtered clusters derived from AlphaFoldDB. Here the target is not the structure of a given sequence but a distribution of plausible backbones.

Open training code makes these systems inspectable and reproducible. OpenFold retrains AlphaFold2, while Protenix and Boltz-1 provide open AlphaFold3-style implementations.

For users, web servers turn models into accessible prediction tools. ColabFold, ESMFold services, and AlphaFold servers differ in input requirements, speed, and supported molecular systems. Choosing among them still depends on whether the task is monomer folding, rapid single-sequence screening, or all-atom complex prediction.
