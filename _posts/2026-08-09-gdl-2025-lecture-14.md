## Density evolution and Langevin dynamics

For

$$
dX_t=f(X_t,t)\,dt+\sigma(t)\,dW_t,
$$

the one-dimensional Fokker--Planck equation is

$$
\partial_t p_t(x)
=-\partial_x\!\left(f(x,t)p_t(x)\right)
+\frac12\sigma^2(t)\partial_{xx}p_t(x).
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-4.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="One dimensional Fokker Planck equation for an SDE." caption="The drift transports probability and the diffusion term spreads it. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 4." %}

In several dimensions with elementwise diffusion, the drift becomes a divergence and the diffusion term contains mixed second derivatives:

$$
\partial_t p_t(x)
=-\sum_i\partial_{x_i}(f_i p_t)
+\frac12\sum_{i,j}\partial_{x_i}\partial_{x_j}
\left(\sigma_i\sigma_j p_t\right).
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-5.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="One and multidimensional Fokker Planck equations." caption="The multidimensional equation supplies the density-level tool used by diffusion models. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 5." %}

Langevin dynamics uses an energy gradient as its drift and isotropic Brownian noise,

$$
dX_t=-\nabla U(X_t)\,dt+\sqrt{2\sigma^2}\,dW_t.
$$

Setting the Fokker--Planck time derivative to zero gives the Boltzmann-type stationary density $$p(x)\propto e^{-U(x)/\sigma^2}$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-6.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Langevin dynamics and its stationary energy based distribution." caption="The balance of energy-gradient drift and diffusion produces a known stationary distribution. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 6." %}

## Forward diffusion and reverse-time SDEs

A diffusion model chooses a forward SDE that carries data at time zero toward a simple noise distribution at time $$T$$:

$$
dX_t=f(X_t,t)\,dt+g(t)\,dW_t,
\qquad X_0\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}.
$$

With a suitable linear drift and noise schedule, $$X_T$$ is approximately standard Gaussian.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-7.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Forward diffusion from data to Gaussian noise." caption="A prescribed SDE encodes the data distribution into a tractable terminal noise law. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 7." %}

Generation requires a reverse-time neural SDE that starts from noise and reconstructs data. Reversing the integration limits is not sufficient because Itô integrals are time-asymmetric.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-8.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Forward noising SDE and learned reverse denoising SDE." caption="The reverse process needs a different drift, not only a negated time step. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 8." %}

Write the forward and backward processes as

$$
dX_t=f(X_t,t)\,dt+g(t)\,dW_t,
\qquad
d\bar X_t=\bar f(\bar X_t,t)\,dt+g(t)\,d\bar W_t,
$$

where $$\bar W_t$$ is a reverse-time Wiener process.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-9.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Forward and reverse SDE definitions." caption="Forward and reverse SDEs share diffusion scale but require compatible drift fields. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 9." %}

One can reparameterize the backward process with a forward-running time variable $$Y_t=\bar X_{T-t}$$. This changes both the drift orientation and the sign of density evolution associated with reverse time.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-10.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Reverse SDE reparameterized in a forward running clock." caption="A forward-running clock makes the reverse process compatible with ordinary SDE notation while preserving its reversed marginals. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 10." %}

Nelson's identity relates the drifts of two SDEs with the same time marginals:

$$
f(x,t)-\bar f(x,t)=g^2(t)\nabla_x\log p_t(x).
$$

The missing reverse drift is therefore determined by the score of the noisy marginal.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-11.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Nelson identity relating forward reverse drifts and score." caption="The score correction is exactly what makes forward and reverse SDEs share the same marginal density path. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 11." %}

The proof equates the forward and backward Fokker--Planck equations. Rearranging their opposite diffusion currents gives

$$
(f-\bar f)p_t=g^2\nabla p_t,
$$

and dividing by $$p_t$$ yields the score.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-12.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Fokker Planck derivation of Nelson identity." caption="Equating the two density PDEs converts the difference of drifts into a logarithmic density gradient. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 12." %}

## Denoising score matching

The forward drift and diffusion schedule are known, so learning the reverse process reduces to estimating $$\nabla_x\log p_t(x)$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-13.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Reverse drift written in terms of the score function." caption="A neural score supplies the only unknown term in the reverse SDE drift. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 13." %}

The marginal score is an expectation of a tractable conditional score:

$$
\nabla_x\log p_t(x)
=\mathbb E\!\left[
\nabla_x\log p_{t\mid0}(x\mid X_0)
\mid X_t=x
\right].
$$

When the forward transition $$p_{t\mid0}$$ is Gaussian and known, samples $$(X_0,X_t)$$ provide supervised denoising-score targets.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-14.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Denoising score identity and score matching loss." caption="Conditional corruption scores make marginal-score learning possible without evaluating the marginal density. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 14." %}

## Continuity equations and flow matching

For the deterministic ODE

$$
dX_t=u(X_t,t)\,dt,
$$

the density follows the continuity equation

$$
\partial_t p_t(x)=-\nabla\cdot\bigl(u(x,t)p_t(x)\bigr).
$$

It is the zero-diffusion case of Fokker--Planck.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-16.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Continuity equation as zero diffusion Fokker Planck." caption="An ODE transports probability without the spreading term of an SDE. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 16." %}

Flow matching learns a velocity field $$u_\theta$$ whose ODE carries a noise law at $$t=0$$ to the data distribution at $$t=1$$. The solution map $$\psi_t$$ is a one-to-one flow and pushes the base density forward, $$(\psi_t)_\#p_0=p_t$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-17.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ODE flow map pushing noise into data." caption="Flow matching reverses the endpoint convention used in the diffusion section: time zero is noise and time one is data. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 17." %}

Instead of defining the marginal path directly, choose tractable conditional paths $$p_t(x\mid x_1)$$ ending at a data sample $$x_1$$, with conditional velocities $$u_t(x\mid x_1)$$. Marginalizing the condition gives

$$
p_t(x)=\int p_t(x\mid x_1)p_{\mathrm{data}}(x_1)\,dx_1.
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-18.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Conditional and marginal probability paths in flow matching." caption="A mixture of tractable conditional transports defines the desired marginal probability path. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 18." %}

Each conditional velocity integrates to a flow $$\psi_t(\cdot\mid x_1)$$ satisfying

$$
\partial_t\psi_t(x\mid x_1)
=u_t(\psi_t(x\mid x_1)\mid x_1).
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-19.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Conditional ODE flow and its probability path." caption="The conditional vector field, flow map, and probability path describe the same tractable transport. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 19." %}

The simplest choice samples independent noise $$x_0$$ and data $$x_1$$ and interpolates linearly,

$$
X_t=(1-t)x_0+tx_1.
$$

Conditioned on $$x_1$$, this gives a Gaussian path with mean $$tx_1$$ and covariance $$(1-t)^2I$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-20.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Gaussian conditional path from noise to data." caption="Linear interpolation induces a tractable Gaussian conditional path and velocity. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 20." %}

The marginal density and velocity are generally intractable even though every conditional density and velocity is tractable. The source keeps these four objects separate because the training trick depends on exactly this mismatch.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-21.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Relation among conditional and marginal paths and velocities." caption="Conditional objects can be sampled and evaluated even when the marginal transport cannot. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 21." %}

## The marginalization trick and conditional loss

The marginal velocity is the conditional velocity averaged under the posterior over endpoints:

$$
u_t(x)=\int u_t(x\mid x_1)p(x_1\mid X_t=x)\,dx_1.
$$

Substituting the conditional continuity equations under the marginalization integral proves that this field generates the marginal density path.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-22.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Proof of the flow matching marginalization trick." caption="Conditional continuity equations sum into one marginal continuity equation whose velocity is a posterior average. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 22." %}

The same identity extends beyond conditioning on one endpoint: one may condition on source samples, endpoint pairs, labels, or other variables as long as their conditional paths marginalize to the target path.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-23.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Flow marginalization under several conditioning choices." caption="Different conditioning variables produce the same marginalization pattern. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 23." %}

Direct regression to $$u_t(x)$$ would require the intractable posterior $$p(x_1\mid X_t=x)$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-24.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Intractable marginal flow matching objective." caption="The ideal target is a posterior average that cannot be evaluated pointwise. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 24." %}

Conditional flow matching instead minimizes

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CFM}}(\theta)
=\mathbb E_{t,x_1,X_t\sim p_t(\cdot\mid x_1)}
\left\lVert u_\theta(X_t,t)-u_t(X_t\mid x_1)\right\rVert^2.
$$

Differentiating the two squared losses shows that conditional and marginal flow matching have identical parameter gradients. The posterior average appears automatically after taking expectation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-25.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Gradient equivalence of conditional and marginal flow matching losses." caption="A tractable conditional regression objective has the same training gradient as regression to the intractable marginal velocity. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 25." %}

## Gaussian and affine conditional flows

Gaussian conditional flow matching makes the mean and standard deviation of the conditional path explicit, revealing how the schedule determines the velocity target.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-26.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Gaussian conditional flow mean variance and velocity diagrams." caption="The Gaussian path connects endpoint distributions through scheduled mean and variance. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 26." %}

An affine conditional flow has

$$
\psi_t(x_0\mid x_1)=\alpha_t x_1+\sigma_t x_0,
$$

with $$\alpha_t$$ increasing from zero to one and $$\sigma_t$$ decreasing from one to zero. Its conditional velocity target is

$$
\dot\alpha_t x_1+\dot\sigma_t x_0.
$$

At the population optimum, the marginal field is the conditional expectation of this target given $$X_t=x$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-27.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Affine conditional flow and its flow matching loss." caption="An affine schedule converts endpoint samples into a closed-form conditional velocity target. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 27." %}

Because $$X_t=\alpha_tX_1+\sigma_tX_0$$, the same marginal velocity can be parameterized through a data prediction $$\mathbb E[X_1\mid X_t=x]$$ or a noise prediction $$\mathbb E[X_0\mid X_t=x]$$. Algebraically eliminating one endpoint yields the conversion among velocity-, data-, and noise-prediction heads.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-14/slide-28.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Velocity data and noise parameterizations for affine flows." caption="The affine path makes velocity, data, and noise predictions convertible through the schedule coefficients. Cropped from the 2025 GDL diffusion-and-flow lecture, slide 28." %}

The diffusion and flow halves share the same statistical pattern. A tractable conditional path is chosen first. The marginal field required for generation is a conditional expectation under an intractable posterior. Denoising score matching and conditional flow matching avoid evaluating that posterior by regressing to conditional targets whose population average recovers the required marginal quantity.
