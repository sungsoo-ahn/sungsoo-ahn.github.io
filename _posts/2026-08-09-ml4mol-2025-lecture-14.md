## From genomes to genomic learning tasks

After molecules and proteins, DNA and RNA provide the next level of biological organization. DNA stores instructions; RNA transmits, regulates, and sometimes executes them; cells interpret both in a context-dependent way.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-14/s03-image1.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Biomolecules connecting small molecules, proteins, DNA, and RNA." caption="Genomic machine learning extends molecular and protein modeling to DNA and RNA. DNA and RNA influence cell formation, function, and regulation. DNA is built from paired nucleotide strands, while RNA is usually single-stranded and can adopt diverse structures.

### DNA, chromosomes, and gene expression

Cells are commonly divided into eukaryotic and prokaryotic forms. Eukaryotic DNA is packaged into chromosomes inside the nucleus, and mitochondria carry their own DNA. Prokaryotic DNA is organized differently and is not enclosed in a nucleus.

### Sequencing genomes and expression

Whole-genome sequencing recovers DNA sequence. The first human genome was completed in 2003 through the Human Genome Project; sequencing cost has since fallen dramatically.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-14/s07-image7.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Human Genome Project timeline and declining genome sequencing cost." caption="Whole-genome sequencing turned the genome from a singular project into a scalable data source. RNA sequencing measures gene expression in a population of cells. RNA is extracted, converted into sequenceable fragments, aligned or quantified, and summarized as expression levels.

### Functional annotation

Variant-effect prediction scores genetic mutations by whether they disrupt function or alter a phenotype.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-14/s09-image9.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Genetic variants linked to molecular and clinical functional effects." caption="Variant-effect models connect sequence changes to molecular dysfunction and phenotype. Promoter and enhancer prediction locates regulatory DNA. Promoters recruit the transcriptional machinery near a gene; enhancers can act from farther away to modulate transcription.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-14/s10-image10.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Promoter and enhancer regions regulating transcription." caption="Promoters and enhancers are sequence elements whose positions and activities shape gene expression. ## Genomic foundation models

Genomic language models train on base-pair sequences. Unlike ordinary text corpora, much of the training data may consist of reference genomes representing species. A single human reference genome contains roughly 3.2 billion bases, already enough to create billions of token positions.

## The virtual-cell objective

A virtual cell is an AI model intended to predict, explain, and discover cellular behavior. The ambition is not merely to embed a cell or classify its type, but to simulate how it responds to interventions.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-14/s35-image54.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Virtual-cell concept connecting molecular inputs, cell state, interventions, and predicted outcomes." caption="A virtual cell aims to integrate heterogeneous biological inputs into a predictive cellular model. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol genomics and virtual cell lecture, slide 35." %}

The desiderata are explicit:

- predict responses to drugs, gene edits, and mutations;
- explain mechanisms of action behind those responses;
- propose new hypotheses for biological discovery.

## Perturbation models and language interfaces

Current virtual-cell work is largely centered on a concrete task: predict the single-cell RNA-seq outcome of a perturbation. The input may include a baseline cell state, drug, gene edit, mutation, cell type, and biological context; the output is a distribution of post-perturbation expression states.

The lecture closes with a widening of interfaces. Genomic models start from nucleotide sequences and predict regulatory or structural consequences. Virtual-cell models start from measured cell states and predict perturbation responses. Language interfaces then translate those states into a form that can be queried and reasoned about. These are not yet equivalent to simulating a complete cell: they operate on particular measurements, perturbations, and evaluation targets. But together they define the path from genomic sequence, through expression and spatial phenotype, to an intervention-conditioned model that can predict, explain, and eventually suggest new experiments.
