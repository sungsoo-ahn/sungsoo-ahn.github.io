## From genomes to genomic learning tasks

After molecules and proteins, DNA and RNA provide the next level of biological organization. DNA stores instructions; RNA transmits, regulates, and sometimes executes them; cells interpret both in a context-dependent way.

DNA and RNA influence cell formation, function, and regulation. DNA is built from paired nucleotide strands, while RNA is usually single-stranded and can adopt diverse structures.

### DNA, chromosomes, and gene expression

Cells are commonly divided into eukaryotic and prokaryotic forms. Eukaryotic DNA is packaged into chromosomes inside the nucleus, and mitochondria carry their own DNA. Prokaryotic DNA is organized differently and is not enclosed in a nucleus.

### Sequencing genomes and expression

Whole-genome sequencing recovers DNA sequence. The first human genome was completed in 2003 through the Human Genome Project. The lecture contrasts that milestone with modern sequencing, which can recover a human genome within days for under 10,000 USD.

RNA sequencing measures gene expression in a population of cells. RNA is extracted, converted into sequenceable fragments, aligned or quantified, and summarized as expression levels.

## Genomic foundation models

## The virtual-cell objective

A virtual cell is an AI model intended to predict, explain, and discover cellular behavior. The ambition is not merely to embed a cell or classify its type, but to simulate how it responds to interventions.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-14/s35-image54.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Virtual-cell concept connecting molecular inputs, cell state, interventions, and predicted outcomes." caption="A virtual cell aims to integrate heterogeneous biological inputs into a predictive cellular model. Source: [Noutahi et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14613)." %}

The desiderata are explicit:

- predict responses to drugs, gene edits, and mutations;

## Cell-level measurements and representations

Whole-genome sequence, molecular localization, fluorescence microscopy, multiplex imaging, single-cell RNA-seq, and spatial transcriptomics expose different projections of a cell. ## Perturbation models and language interfaces

Current virtual-cell work is largely centered on a concrete task: predict the single-cell RNA-seq outcome of a perturbation. The input may include a baseline cell state, drug, gene edit, mutation, cell type, and biological context; the output is a distribution of post-perturbation expression states.

The lecture closes with a widening of interfaces. Genomic models start from nucleotide sequences and predict regulatory or structural consequences. Virtual-cell models start from measured cell states and predict perturbation responses. Language interfaces then translate those states into a form that can be queried and reasoned about. These are not yet equivalent to simulating a complete cell: they operate on particular measurements, perturbations, and evaluation targets. But together they define the path from genomic sequence, through expression and spatial phenotype, to an intervention-conditioned model that can predict, explain, and eventually suggest new experiments.
