The representation system is already fixed. Every hidden feature has a type, every type has a prescribed response to rotation, and Clebsch--Gordan coefficients say which products of types are legal. The architectural question is now narrower and more interesting: **where can a network introduce data-dependent selection and nonlinearity without breaking equivariance?**

This lecture develops two answers. [SE(3)-Transformer](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/15231a7ce4ba789d13b722cc5c955834-Abstract.html) computes invariant query--key scores and uses them to weight steerable values. [SEGNN](https://openreview.net/forum?id=_xwr8gOBeV1) interprets a conditioned Clebsch--Gordan product as a steerable linear map, then composes those maps into nonlinear message and update functions. To see exactly what changes, the lecture begins by rebuilding the common TFN/NequIP baseline.

The contract throughout is

$$
f\!\left(\rho_X(g)x\right)=\rho_Y(g)f(x).
$$

For an invariant molecular target such as energy, $$\rho_Y(g)=1$$. For a vector output such as force, $$\rho_Y(g)=R$$. Every construction below can therefore be read as an answer to the same practical question: which operations commute with these prescribed group actions, and which apparently ordinary neural-network operations would violate them?

## The typed-feature construction in one pass

The recap in the source deck follows the same chain as the preceding article, [Steerable Features, Tensor Products, TFN, and NequIP]({% post_url 2026-08-09-gdl-2025-lecture-09 %}). We will keep that sequence here, because every symbol returns in the attention and SEGNN equations.

### Harmonics turn rotations into matrices

For a planar angle $$\theta$$, degree-$$k$$ circular harmonics form the pair

$$
v_k(\theta)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos(k\theta)\\
\sin(k\theta)
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Rotating the input by $$\alpha$$ produces a known matrix action:

$$
v_k(\theta+\alpha)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos(k\alpha)&-\sin(k\alpha)\\
\sin(k\alpha)&\cos(k\alpha)
\end{bmatrix}
v_k(\theta).
$$

The determinant of this representation is one, so it preserves lengths and oriented area. Degree controls both angular frequency and the representation angle.

Spherical harmonics give the corresponding basis on the unit sphere. A complex basis element has the form

$$
Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)=N_{lm}e^{im\phi}P_l^m(\cos\theta),
$$

and the complete degree-$$l$$ vector transforms by a Wigner matrix:

$$
Y^{(l)}(g\hat r)=D^{(l)}(g)Y^{(l)}(\hat r).
$$

There are $$2l+1$$ orders $$m=-l,\ldots,l$$. Rotation may mix those orders, but it never mixes one degree with another. This makes the degree-$$l$$ vector a type-$$l$$ steerable feature.

On a geometric edge $$i\leftarrow j$$, a radial function sees the invariant distance $$r_{ij}=\lVert x_j-x_i\rVert$$ while a spherical harmonic sees the direction $$\hat r_{ij}=(x_j-x_i)/r_{ij}$$. Their product combines learnable distance dependence with a fixed angular transformation law.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/s10-edge-harmonic-encoding.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Relative atomic directions projected onto spherical harmonics and combined with radial functions." caption="The PowerPoint-native angular-filter illustration repeated in the lecture's recap and sourced to Thomas et al.'s <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08219'>Tensor Field Networks</a>. Distance and direction enter through separate radial and harmonic branches." %}

This edge construction also removes translation before any harmonic is evaluated. Under a common translation $$x_i\mapsto x_i+t$$,

$$
(x_j+t)-(x_i+t)=x_j-x_i.
$$

Consequently both the distance and the direction are unchanged. Under a rotation $$R$$, the distance remains fixed while the direction becomes $$R\hat r_{ij}$$, exactly the transformation handled by $$Y^{(l)}$$. This separation is the reason a layer built from relative positions naturally handles the translational part of $$SE(3)$$ and leaves only the rotation law to the typed features.

### Irreps turn tensor layouts into feature types

Raw Cartesian tensor order is not the same as irreducible rotation type. A $$3\times3$$ matrix transforms as $$M\mapsto RMR^\top$$, but its nine entries split into three independently transforming parts:

$$
M=
\underbrace{\frac{\operatorname{tr}M}{3}I}_{l=0}
+\underbrace{\frac{M-M^\top}{2}}_{l=1}
+\underbrace{\left(\frac{M+M^\top}{2}-\frac{\operatorname{tr}M}{3}I\right)}_{l=2}.
$$

Their dimensions are $$1+3+5=9$$. A change of basis exposes this direct sum. More generally, a reducible representation can be block diagonalized into irreducible representations. For $$SO(3)$$, the irreducible blocks are the Wigner matrices $$D^{(l)}$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/s11-rank-two-decomposition.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A rank-two Cartesian tensor decomposed into trace, antisymmetric, and symmetric traceless parts." caption="The rank-two decomposition repeated in the source lecture, retained from its PowerPoint. The same example is discussed in Duval et al.'s <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.07511'>Hitchhiker's Guide to Geometric GNNs</a>." %}

A finite hidden representation can therefore be organized as

$$
\rho(g)\simeq
\bigoplus_{l=0}^{L}
\left(I_{C_l}\otimes D^{(l)}(g)\right),
$$

where $$C_l$$ counts learnable channels of type $$l$$. Type $$0$$ is a scalar, type $$1$$ is an ordinary vector up to basis convention, and higher types carry progressively richer angular structure.

### Clebsch--Gordan products couple types

The raw tensor product of a type-$$l_1$$ feature and a type-$$l_2$$ feature transforms by $$D^{(l_1)}\otimes D^{(l_2)}$$. That product is reducible:

$$
D^{(l_1)}\otimes D^{(l_2)}
\simeq
\bigoplus_{l=|l_1-l_2|}^{l_1+l_2}D^{(l)}.
$$

Clebsch--Gordan coefficients perform the basis change. The type-$$l$$ output is

$$
\left(v^{(l_1)}\otimes_{\mathrm{CG}}w^{(l_2)}\right)_m^{(l)}
=\sum_{m_1=-l_1}^{l_1}
\sum_{m_2=-l_2}^{l_2}
C_{(l_1,m_1)(l_2,m_2)}^{(l,m)}
v_{m_1}^{(l_1)}w_{m_2}^{(l_2)}.
$$

Only degrees satisfying the triangle rule $$|l_1-l_2|\le l\le l_1+l_2$$ appear. In the standard complex basis, nonzero terms also satisfy $$m=m_1+m_2$$. Scalar multiplication, scalar--vector multiplication, dot products, cross products, and symmetric-traceless products are the low-order cases of this one construction.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/s18-clebsch-gordan-index-map.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Input representation vectors connected by a Clebsch Gordan coefficient tensor to an output representation vector." caption="The native coefficient-map illustration repeated by the source deck. A fixed sparse tensor connects input indices (<i>l</i><sub>1</sub>, <i>m</i><sub>1</sub>) and (<i>l</i><sub>2</sub>, <i>m</i><sub>2</sub>) to an admissible output (<i>l</i>, <i>m</i>)." %}

The recap has now supplied the contract needed by every later layer: a type is a complete irrep block; geometric directions are typed by spherical harmonics; and feature interactions must be built from intertwiners such as Clebsch--Gordan maps.

One further restriction becomes decisive for attention. A type-$$l$$ inner product is invariant because it contracts two copies of the same representation. In general, there is no canonical invariant dot product between type $$l$$ and type $$l'\ne l$$. Queries and keys may mix multiplicity channels within a type, but a scalar attention score must finish by contracting matching irreducible blocks. Flattening all $$m$$ components and sending them through an arbitrary coordinate-wise MLP would forget that restriction.

## The TFN and NequIP baseline

[Tensor Field Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08219) associate typed features with every point. A layer couples a spherical-harmonic filter to a source feature, aggregates the resulting messages, and applies a scalar-controlled nonlinearity.

### TFN feature and filter indices

The hidden state is denoted

$$
V_{acm}^{(l)},
$$

with point index $$a$$, channel index $$c$$, type $$l$$, and representation index $$m=-l,\ldots,l$$. Neural weights may mix channels of the same type. They may not mix $$m$$ components arbitrarily, because those components must transform together under $$D^{(l)}$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/s20-tfn-feature-indices.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Tensor Field Network feature dictionary showing point, channel, type, and representation indices." caption="A native TFN figure retained from the source PowerPoint and linked to <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08219'>Thomas et al.</a> Point and channel indices describe multiplicity; <i>l</i> and <i>m</i> describe the rotation law." %}

For relative position $$r_{ab}=x_b-x_a$$, a filter of type $$l_f$$ factorizes as

$$
F_{c m_f m_i}^{(l_f,l_i)}(r_{ab})
=R_{c m_f m_i}^{(l_f,l_i)}(\lVert r_{ab}\rVert)
Y_{m_f}^{(l_f)}(\hat r_{ab}).
$$

The message into output type $$l_o$$ is

$$
\mathcal L_{a c m_o}^{(l_o)}
=\sum_{b\in\mathcal N(a)}
\sum_{m_f,m_i}
C_{(l_f,m_f)(l_i,m_i)}^{(l_o,m_o)}
F_{c m_f m_i}^{(l_f,l_i)}(r_{ab})
V_{b c m_i}^{(l_i)}.
$$

The three degree labels describe a complete path through the layer. Suppose the source feature is a vector, so $$l_i=1$$, and the angular filter also has $$l_f=1$$. The allowed outputs are $$l_o=0,1,2$$. The scalar branch measures a rotation-invariant relation between feature and edge direction; the type-$$1$$ branch carries an oriented vector-like relation; and the type-$$2$$ branch retains symmetric directional anisotropy. If instead the source is scalar, $$l_i=0$$, then a type-$$1$$ filter can only produce $$l_o=1$$. These are architectural connectivity rules, not learned preferences. Training adjusts radial functions and channel mixing inside the admissible paths but cannot create a forbidden coupling.

This also explains the distinct roles of the indices. The point index $$a$$ is tied to graph permutation. The channel index $$c$$ is an unconstrained multiplicity dimension and may be mixed by learned matrices. The type index $$l$$ chooses a representation block and may change only through a legal tensor product. The component index $$m$$ is a coordinate inside that block and must never be treated as an independent feature channel. Much of steerable-network implementation is simply maintaining this ledger correctly.

The Clebsch--Gordan intertwiner makes each edge message type $$l_o$$; summation preserves that type. For $$l=0$$, TFN uses an ordinary activation. For $$l>0$$, it computes the invariant norm and gates the complete irrep vector:

$$
V_{acm}^{(l)}
\longmapsto
\eta^{(l)}\!\left(\lVert V_{ac}^{(l)}\rVert+b_c^{(l)}\right)
\frac{V_{acm}^{(l)}}{\lVert V_{ac}^{(l)}\rVert}.
$$

The equivariance of the convolution can be checked edge by edge. After a rotation $$g$$, the angular filter and source feature become

$$
F^{(l_f)}(gr)=D^{(l_f)}(g)F^{(l_f)}(r),
\qquad
V^{(l_i)}\mapsto D^{(l_i)}(g)V^{(l_i)}.
$$

The defining intertwiner identity for the Clebsch--Gordan map $$C^{l_o}_{l_f l_i}$$ is

$$
C^{l_o}_{l_f l_i}
\left(D^{(l_f)}(g)\otimes D^{(l_i)}(g)\right)
=D^{(l_o)}(g)C^{l_o}_{l_f l_i}.
$$

Therefore every coupled edge term transforms by $$D^{(l_o)}(g)$$. The same matrix can be pulled through the neighbor sum, so aggregation preserves the type. Finally, orthogonality gives

$$
\left\lVert D^{(l)}(g)V^{(l)}\right\rVert
=\left\lVert V^{(l)}\right\rVert,
$$

which makes the gate an invariant scalar multiplying a complete irrep block. The filter, coupling, sum, and gate are not independent tricks: they close the proof of one full TFN layer.

Type-wise self-interaction layers mix channels as $$1\times1$$ convolutions. The full TFN alternates geometric convolutions, self-interactions, concatenation, and safe nonlinearities.

### NequIP adds parity and an energy objective

[NequIP](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-29939-5) applies the TFN construction to interatomic potentials:

$$
E_{\mathrm{pot}}=\sum_iE_{i,\mathrm{atomic}},
\qquad
F_i=-\nabla_{x_i}E_{\mathrm{pot}}.
$$

Because energy is invariant to rotation, translation, and reflection, hidden types must describe $$O(3)$$ rather than only $$SO(3)$$. NequIP labels a feature by degree $$l$$ and parity $$p\in\{+1,-1\}$$. Even features remain unchanged under inversion; odd features change sign. Degree-one spherical harmonics are odd because $$Y^{(1)}(-\hat r)=-Y^{(1)}(\hat r)$$, while the cross product of two odd vectors is even.

That last example distinguishes polar and axial vectors. A displacement $$r$$ is polar and becomes $$-r$$ under inversion. If momentum $$p$$ is also polar, angular momentum $$L=r\times p$$ is axial:

$$
(-r)\times(-p)=r\times p=L.
$$

Both objects transform as degree one under proper rotations, yet they have opposite parity. An $$SO(3)$$ label alone cannot tell them apart; an $$O(3)$$ label $$(l,p)$$ can.

The angular filter keeps the TFN form

$$
S_m^{(l)}(r_{ij})=R(r_{ij})Y_m^{(l)}(\hat r_{ij}),
$$

with $$R$$ obtained from a neural network over Bessel basis functions multiplied by a smooth cutoff envelope. Its tensor products enforce both

$$
|l_f-l_i|\le l_o\le l_f+l_i,
\qquad
p_fp_i=p_o.
$$

The two NequIP result slides in this lecture repeat the original and revised MD17 tables. Rather than reproduce the same wide tables twice, the [preceding chapter transcribes every value]({% post_url 2026-08-09-gdl-2025-lecture-09 %}#what-the-original-md17-comparisons-showed). Their relevant conclusion here is structural: scalar-only NequIP ($$l=0$$) is much less accurate, adding type-$$1$$ features gives the largest improvement, and increasing the maximum degree further often helps but is not monotonically better in every benchmark cell.

The energy head also turns output symmetry into force symmetry. If

$$
E(Rx_1+t,\ldots,Rx_n+t)=E(x_1,\ldots,x_n),
$$

then differentiating with respect to the transformed coordinates gives

$$
F_i(Rx+t)=-\nabla_{Rx_i+t}E(Rx+t)=R F_i(x).
$$

Differentiating invariance under a common infinitesimal translation gives $$\sum_iF_i=0$$. Because all forces come from the same scalar potential, they are conservative by construction. NequIP therefore does more than attach a vector-valued head: it learns one invariant energy and obtains the physically linked force field by differentiation.

TFN and NequIP therefore establish the baseline to modify: every neighbor passes through a fixed equivariant kernel, messages are summed with uniform coefficients, and nonlinearity is introduced mainly through scalars or invariant norms.

## SE(3)-Transformer: invariant attention over typed values

Standard attention is nonlinear and pair-specific. It asks how relevant source $$j$$ is to receiver $$i$$, then uses that relevance to weight the value. In a steerable network, the weight itself must be invariant. If rotating the input changed $$\alpha_{ij}$$, the model would change not only the orientation of a feature but also which neighbors it listens to.

### Rewriting TFN as a typed kernel

The SE(3)-Transformer paper writes a TFN layer as

$$
f_{\mathrm{out},i}^{(l)}
=\sum_k\sum_{j\ne i}
W^{lk}(x_j-x_i)f_{\mathrm{in},j}^{(k)}.
$$

The kernel $$W^{lk}$$ maps an input type $$k$$ to an output type $$l$$. Its most general TFN form is a sum over the admissible intermediate degrees $$J$$:

$$
W^{lk}(x)
=\sum_{J=|k-l|}^{k+l}
\phi_J^{lk}(\lVert x\rVert)
\sum_{m=-J}^{J}
Y_{Jm}(\hat x)Q_{Jm}^{lk},
$$

where $$\phi_J^{lk}$$ is learned radially and $$Q_{Jm}^{lk}$$ contains fixed Clebsch--Gordan coefficients. This is the earlier filter--feature product written as one matrix-valued kernel.

SE(3)-Transformer separates the update into three terms:

$$
f_{\mathrm{out},i}^{(l)}
=
\underbrace{W_V^{ll}f_{\mathrm{in},i}^{(l)}}_{\text{self-interaction}}
+\sum_{j\in\mathcal N(i)\setminus\{i\}}
\underbrace{\alpha_{ij}}_{\text{invariant attention}}
\underbrace{\sum_kW_V^{lk}(x_j-x_i)f_{\mathrm{in},j}^{(k)}}_{\text{steerable value}}.
$$

The value is a TFN-like equivariant message. Multiplication by a scalar leaves its type unchanged. The only remaining problem is how to construct $$\alpha_{ij}$$ from steerable features.

Notice what this scalar can and cannot do. It can increase or suppress the complete value arriving from one neighbor, but it cannot rotate one component of an irrep independently of the others or select a preferred laboratory axis. If $$v_{ij}^{(l)}\mapsto D^{(l)}(g)v_{ij}^{(l)}$$ and $$\alpha_{ij}$$ is invariant, then

$$
\alpha_{ij}v_{ij}^{(l)}
\mapsto
D^{(l)}(g)\left(\alpha_{ij}v_{ij}^{(l)}\right).
$$

Attention changes the relative importance of edges while the typed value kernel remains responsible for angular transport.

### Why the query--key score is invariant

Queries at node $$i$$ and keys for edge $$i\leftarrow j$$ are built with type-wise linear maps and TFN kernels:

$$
q_i^{(l)}=\sum_kW_Q^{lk}f_{\mathrm{in},i}^{(k)},
\qquad
k_{ij}^{(l)}=\sum_kW_K^{lk}(x_j-x_i)f_{\mathrm{in},j}^{(k)}.
$$

Both transform as type $$l$$. Their same-type inner product is invariant because the Wigner matrices are orthogonal in a real basis, or unitary in a complex basis:

$$
\left(D^{(l)}(g)q_i^{(l)}\right)^\top
\left(D^{(l)}(g)k_{ij}^{(l)}\right)
=q_i^{(l)\top}k_{ij}^{(l)}.
$$

Summing over types gives the scalar score

$$
s_{ij}=\sum_l q_i^{(l)\top}k_{ij}^{(l)},
\qquad
\alpha_{ij}=\frac{\exp(s_{ij})}{\sum_{j'\in\mathcal N(i)\setminus\{i\}}\exp(s_{ij'})}.
$$

With multiple channels, the same rule becomes a sum of valid contractions,

$$
s_{ij}=\sum_l\sum_{c=1}^{C_l}
q_{i,c}^{(l)\top}k_{ij,c}^{(l)},
$$

possibly followed by learned scalar mixing across heads. Channel mixing is safe because channels are repeated copies of the same irrep; contracting a type-$$1$$ query against a type-$$2$$ key is not. This is the typed analogue of the ordinary Transformer dot product: the familiar formula survives, but only after the representation layout has made every contraction legal.

The softmax is applied only to invariant numbers. Therefore the attention pattern is unchanged by global rotation, while each value transforms by its assigned output representation.

For a small numerical example, suppose three neighbors have scores $$0.1$$, $$1.2$$, and $$-0.3$$. Their exponentials are approximately $$1.105$$, $$3.320$$, and $$0.741$$, giving weights

$$
(\alpha_{i1},\alpha_{i2},\alpha_{i3})
\approx(0.214,0.643,0.143).
$$

After rotating the complete point cloud, queries and keys rotate inside each type, but all three inner products and weights remain the same. The weighted value sum rotates as one steerable output.

Take one type-$$1$$ channel and, in a Cartesian basis, let the three values be

$$
v_{i1}=(1,0,0),\qquad
v_{i2}=(0,2,0),\qquad
v_{i3}=(-1,0,1).
$$

The attention output is

$$
0.214v_{i1}+0.643v_{i2}+0.143v_{i3}
=(0.071,1.286,0.143).
$$

For any rotation matrix $$R$$, recomputing the scores on the rotated input gives the same three weights, and the new output is

$$
0.214Rv_{i1}+0.643Rv_{i2}+0.143Rv_{i3}
=R(0.071,1.286,0.143).
$$

The calculation separates invariance from equivariance in one line: the coefficients do not move, while the vector they combine does.

### Nonlinear self-interaction

The attention neighborhood excludes the receiver itself; its own feature enters through a separate self-interaction. TFN used point-independent linear channel weights. SE(3)-Transformer lets invariant summaries of the local feature generate point-dependent channel weights:

$$
f_{\mathrm{out},i,c'}^{(l)}
=\sum_c w_{i,c'c}^{(l)}f_{\mathrm{in},i,c}^{(l)},
$$

where $$w_i$$ is produced by an MLP from invariant inner products or norms such as

$$
\sum_c f_{\mathrm{in},i,c}^{(l)\top}f_{\mathrm{in},i,c}^{(l)}.
$$

The weights depend on the input but remain scalars. The same weight multiplies every representation component $$m$$ within a channel, so the operation is nonlinear in the feature while still commuting with rotation.

The complete layer therefore has two distinct sources of adaptivity. Neighbor attention makes the coefficient of each edge depend on the receiver--sender pair. Nonlinear self-interaction makes channel mixing at the receiver depend on its current invariant state. Both mechanisms are data dependent, yet neither changes the transformation law of the typed values they scale. This is the architectural point of the lecture's SE(3)-Transformer block: nonlinearity enters through invariant control signals rather than through coordinate-wise activation of steerable components.

## SEGNN: steerable MLPs inside message passing

SE(3)-Transformer keeps the value map TFN-like and makes neighbor weighting adaptive. SEGNN changes the internal map itself. Its key observation is that a Clebsch--Gordan product with one input held fixed is a linear transformation of the other input.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/s38-linear-map-analogy.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A standard matrix vector multiplication shown as a linear layer from input features to output features." caption="The lecture's PowerPoint-native linear-layer analogy. SEGNN treats a Clebsch--Gordan product conditioned on a steerable attribute as the typed counterpart of matrix--vector multiplication." %}

### A conditioned Clebsch--Gordan linear map

Let $$h^{(l_i)}$$ be a hidden feature and $$a^{(l_f)}$$ a steerable conditioning attribute. For a chosen output type $$l_o$$, define

$$
\left(h^{(l_i)}\otimes_{\mathrm{CG}}^{w}a^{(l_f)}\right)_{m_o}^{(l_o)}
=w_{l_i l_f l_o}
\sum_{m_i=-l_i}^{l_i}
\sum_{m_f=-l_f}^{l_f}
C_{(l_i,m_i)(l_f,m_f)}^{(l_o,m_o)}
h_{m_i}^{(l_i)}a_{m_f}^{(l_f)}.
$$

For fixed $$a$$, this expression is linear in $$h$$. Collecting all channel multiplicities and admissible output types defines an equivariant linear operator

$$
W_a h:=h\otimes_{\mathrm{CG}}^{W}a.
$$

The learned coefficients $$W$$ mix multiplicity channels; the fixed Clebsch--Gordan tensor handles representation indices. A radial scalar can make the operator distance dependent without changing its transformation law:

$$
W_a(d)h:=h\otimes_{\mathrm{CG}}^{W(d)}a.
$$

The conditioning attribute may include relative position, velocity, force, spin, or any other feature with a known type. This is why the paper's title emphasizes both geometric and physical quantities.

The low-order cases make the operator concrete. Conditioning a scalar hidden feature on a vector attribute produces a vector proportional to that attribute. Coupling two vectors decomposes into

$$
1\otimes1=0\oplus1\oplus2,
$$

corresponding, up to normalization and basis convention, to their dot product, cross product, and symmetric-traceless product. A single conditioned layer may therefore create invariant alignment, an oriented axial response, and a quadrupolar response from the same pair. The triangle and parity rules decide which branches exist; learned channel coefficients decide how strongly each branch is used.

### From a conditioned linear map to a steerable MLP

A standard MLP alternates linear maps and nonlinear activations. SEGNN replaces each linear map by a conditioned equivariant map $$W_a$$ and uses scalar-conditioned activations that act uniformly on a complete irrep block. Schematically,

$$
\operatorname{MLP}_a(h)
=W_a^{(n)}\,\sigma\!\left(
W_a^{(n-1)}\cdots
\sigma\!\left(W_a^{(1)}h\right)
\right).
$$

The scalar channels can pass through ordinary nonlinearities. Higher-order channels may be gated by invariant scalar features. Each layer is equivariant, and composition preserves equivariance. The result is nonlinear in the joint pair $$(h,a)$$ even though it remains linear in $$h$$ when the conditioner and scalar gates are frozen.

The proof is modular. If the conditioner and hidden feature transform as $$a\mapsto\rho_a(g)a$$ and $$h\mapsto\rho_h(g)h$$, the Clebsch--Gordan intertwiner gives

$$
W_{\rho_a(g)a}\,\rho_h(g)h
=\rho_{\mathrm{out}}(g)W_a h.
$$

An invariant scalar gate $$\gamma(h,a)$$ satisfies $$\gamma(\rho_h(g)h,\rho_a(g)a)=\gamma(h,a)$$, so

$$
\gamma(h,a)W_a h
\mapsto
\rho_{\mathrm{out}}(g)\left[\gamma(h,a)W_a h\right].
$$

Composing these conditioned maps and gates repeats the same argument at every layer. SEGNN is nonlinear without requiring a special activation for every representation degree; it routes the nonlinearity through scalar gates while the conditioned CG products carry the typed information.

This distinction matters. A TFN convolution is a linear sum of edge-conditioned maps:

$$
h_i'=\sum_{j\in\mathcal N(i)}W_{a_{ij}}(d_{ij})h_j.
$$

SEGNN can transform the pair $$(h_i,h_j)$$ nonlinearly before aggregation.

### Nonlinear steerable message passing

Let $$\tilde f_i$$ be a direct sum of node features and let $$\tilde a_{ij}$$ be a direct sum of steerable edge attributes. The SEGNN message and update are

$$
\tilde m_{ij}
=\phi_m\!\left(
\tilde f_i,
\tilde f_j,
\lVert x_j-x_i\rVert,
\tilde a_{ij}
\right),
$$

$$
\tilde f_i'
=\phi_f\!\left(
\tilde f_i,
\sum_{j\in\mathcal N(i)}\tilde m_{ij},
\tilde a_i
\right),
$$

where both $$\phi_m$$ and $$\phi_f$$ are steerable MLPs. The sum remains equivariant because all messages share the same output type. Nonlinear pair processing occurs before the sum, and another nonlinear equivariant map occurs after it.

The same equations are also equivariant to relabeling the graph. A node permutation merely reindexes $$i$$ and $$j$$; it does not change the multiset of incoming messages, and the shared functions $$\phi_m$$ and $$\phi_f$$ are applied at every node and edge. The receiver sum is order independent. SEGNN thus combines permutation equivariance over the graph with geometric equivariance inside every feature block.

The attribute interface matters when a system contains more than positions. Velocity and force are polar vectors. Angular momentum is an axial vector. A symmetric stress tensor splits into an invariant trace and a type-$$2$$ symmetric-traceless part. Treating all of these as anonymous triples or matrices would erase physically different transformation laws; supplying the correct irreducible types lets the same steerable MLP combine them without introducing a preferred coordinate frame.

A two-neighbor trace shows where SEGNN differs from attention. Let a scalar receiver feature be $$s_i$$, let neighbor vectors be $$h_j^{(1)}$$, and let edge directions be $$a_{ij}^{(1)}=\hat r_{ij}$$. A conditioned CG map may first form the scalar alignment

$$
u_{ij}^{(0)}
=h_j^{(1)}\otimes_{\mathrm{CG}}^{0}a_{ij}^{(1)}
\propto h_j\cdot\hat r_{ij}.
$$

The message MLP can apply an ordinary nonlinear function to $$u_{ij}^{(0)}$$, use it as a gate, and emit a vector message such as

$$
m_{ij}^{(1)}
=\gamma\!\left(s_i,u_{ij}^{(0)},r_{ij}\right)h_j^{(1)}
+\beta\!\left(s_i,u_{ij}^{(0)},r_{ij}\right)a_{ij}^{(1)}.
$$

Both coefficients are invariant, so the result is a vector. Yet the transformation applied to neighbor $$j$$ depends nonlinearly on its alignment with the edge and on the receiver state before messages are summed. Scalar attention could reweight a precomputed vector value; the conditioned steerable MLP can synthesize a new legal combination of multiple typed inputs on that edge.

The lecture closes by returning to related typed architectures: [Cormorant](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04015), [3D Steerable CNNs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.02547), and [Clebsch--Gordan Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.09231). They use the same representation vocabulary in molecular, volumetric, and spherical-Fourier settings.

The progression across the lecture is now explicit. TFN provides a fixed linear equivariant convolution. NequIP adds parity and an invariant-energy objective. SE(3)-Transformer learns **which neighbor to emphasize** using invariant inner products while keeping the value typed. SEGNN learns **how to transform a neighbor pair** using conditioned Clebsch--Gordan maps before aggregation. Both extensions add adaptivity, but they place it on different sides of the message-passing equation.

That distinction is more useful than treating the architectures as a chronological list. When the main missing ingredient is selective interaction among many neighbors, invariant attention changes the aggregation weights without sacrificing typed values. When the interaction itself must depend nonlinearly on geometric or physical covariates, a steerable message MLP changes the edge transformation before aggregation. In either case, the same discipline remains underneath: use relative geometry for translation, complete irreducible blocks for rotation, parity when reflections matter, invariant scalars for control, and intertwiners for every change of type.
