## The typed-feature construction in one pass

A steerable network carries type-$$0$$, type-$$1$$, through type-$$L$$ features. Each type has a known linear response to rotation, so hidden states can change orientation without losing their semantic meaning.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-3.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Steerable network carrying features of several representation types." caption="Typed hidden features transform by prescribed rotation representations. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 3." %}

Clebsch--Gordan tensor products are the typed interaction used between layers. They combine two steerable inputs and return output components that again transform as irreducible types.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-4.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Clebsch Gordan products between typed hidden layers." caption="Clebsch--Gordan products couple feature types while keeping the output steerable. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 4." %}

The architectures in this deck begin with the simplest high-order implementations: Tensor Field Networks and NequIP, then SE(3)-Transformer and SEGNN.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-5.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Overview of TFN NequIP SE3 Transformer and SEGNN." caption="The source progresses from linear typed convolutions to attention and steerable nonlinear message passing. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 5." %}

Circular harmonics show the basic mechanism in two dimensions. The pair $$(\cos k\theta,\sin k\theta)$$ transforms linearly when the input angle changes, with degree $$k$$ controlling its angular frequency.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-6.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Degree one circular harmonic under planar rotation." caption="A change of input angle becomes a known matrix action on harmonic coefficients. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 6." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-7.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Higher degree circular harmonics and angular frequency." caption="Higher harmonic degree changes the representation angle and the number of oscillations around the circle. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 7." %}

Spherical harmonics provide the corresponding basis on the sphere. Degree $$l$$ contains $$2l+1$$ orders and higher degree represents higher angular frequency.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-8.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Spherical harmonics indexed by degree and order." caption="Spherical harmonics form an orthonormal angular basis for three-dimensional directions. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 8." %}

The vector of degree-$$l$$ harmonics transforms by the Wigner matrix $$D^{(l)}(g)$$:

$$
Y^{(l)}(g\cdot\hat r)=D^{(l)}(g)Y^{(l)}(\hat r).
$$

This makes the harmonic vector a type-$$l$$ feature.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-9.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Spherical harmonics transformed by a Wigner D matrix." caption="One harmonic degree is closed under rotation and supplies an irreducible steerable type. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 9." %}

Evaluating these functions on the direction of a relative displacement produces the angular part of a geometric filter.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-10.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Spherical harmonics used to featurize relative positions." caption="Relative directions become typed edge features through spherical-harmonic evaluation. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 10." %}

Irreducible representations are needed because arbitrary Cartesian tensor products contain several independently transforming parts. The irrep decomposition gives a compact and unique classification by rotational response.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-11.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Motivation for irreducible decomposition of steerable tensors." caption="Irreps categorize a feature by its transformation law rather than its raw tensor layout. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 11." %}

A reducible representation can be block-diagonalized into smaller invariant subspaces. For $$SO(3)$$, those irreducible blocks are Wigner $$D$$ matrices.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-12.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Reducible representation decomposed into irreducible blocks." caption="A change of basis exposes the independently transforming irrep blocks. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 12." %}

Circular harmonics give the $$SO(2)$$ version: every frequency forms one block, whereas arbitrary mixtures of frequencies are not single irreducible features.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-13.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Circular harmonics as irreducible SO2 representations." caption="Each circular frequency closes under planar rotation as its own irrep. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 13." %}

For $$SO(3)$$, type $$l$$ has dimension $$2l+1$$. Type $$0$$ is a scalar, type $$1$$ is an ordinary vector, and higher types carry higher angular structure.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-14.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Wigner D blocks and type l steerable vectors." caption="Wigner matrices define the type system used by every later layer. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 14." %}

A raw tensor product of two typed features is steerable but reducible. Its vectorized transformation is a Kronecker product rather than one Wigner block.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-15.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Reducible vectorized product of two steerable features." caption="The raw tensor product mixes several admissible output types. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 15." %}

The Clebsch--Gordan basis change projects that product into individual irrep outputs.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-16.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Clebsch Gordan product producing irreducible Wigner blocks." caption="Clebsch--Gordan coefficients reorganize a product into typed output blocks. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 16." %}

For input types $$l_1$$ and $$l_2$$, the coupled component is

$$
\bigl(v^{(l_1)}\otimes_{\mathrm{CG}}v^{(l_2)}\bigr)_{m}^{(l)}
=\sum_{m_1,m_2}C_{(l_1,m_1)(l_2,m_2)}^{(l,m)}
v_{m_1}^{(l_1)}v_{m_2}^{(l_2)}.
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-17.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Indexed Clebsch Gordan tensor product equation." caption="A fixed coefficient tensor maps representation-index pairs into a typed output. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 17." %}

Each coefficient selects one admissible mapping $$(l_1,m_1),(l_2,m_2)\mapsto(l,m)$$. This fixed sparse structure is the representation-theoretic core around which the neural layer learns channel weights.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-18.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Clebsch Gordan coefficient visualized as a sparse mapping." caption="The coefficient map couples representation indices without learning an arbitrary rotation-dependent rule. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 18." %}

## The TFN and NequIP baseline

Tensor Field Networks associate typed features with every point. A spherical-harmonic filter encodes each relative direction, a Clebsch--Gordan product couples the filter to the source feature, and a scalar gate supplies the nonlinearity.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-19.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Tensor Field Network architecture concept." caption="TFN establishes the typed convolution that SE(3)-Transformer and SEGNN subsequently modify. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 19." %}

Hidden features are indexed by point, channel, type $$l$$, and representation index $$m$$. Channels may mix freely within a type; the $$m$$ entries must transform together.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-20.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="TFN hidden feature indices and point geometry." caption="Point and channel indices describe multiplicity, while type and representation indices carry the rotation law. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 20." %}

The TFN filter factorizes into a learnable radial function and a fixed harmonic:

$$
F^{(l_f)}(r)=R^{(l_f)}(\lVert r\rVert)Y^{(l_f)}(\hat r).
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-21.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="TFN filter as radial embedding times spherical harmonic." caption="Distance controls an invariant learned coefficient; direction controls a typed harmonic feature. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 21." %}

The layer sums Clebsch--Gordan products of filters and neighbor features for every allowed triple $$|l_f-l_i|\le l_o\le l_f+l_i$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-22.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="TFN typed message passing equation." caption="The TFN convolution is a neighbor sum over allowed filter--input--output type couplings. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 22." %}

Type $$0$$ uses ordinary nonlinear activations. Higher types use invariant norms to generate scalar gates, preserving orientation while changing magnitude.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-23.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="TFN scalar and norm gated nonlinearities." caption="Order-wise gating supplies a safe but restricted nonlinearity for non-scalar features. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 23." %}

Self-interaction layers provide the channelwise $$1\times1$$ convolutions between geometric convolutions.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-24.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Complete Tensor Field Network architecture." caption="TFN alternates geometric convolution, self-interaction, and typed nonlinear blocks. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 24." %}

Its early Tetris classification and missing-point experiments established the proof of concept for rotation-aware feature fields.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-25.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="TFN Tetris classification and missing point experiments." caption="The original TFN tasks test invariant classification and equivariant coordinate reconstruction. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 25." %}

NequIP applies the construction to interatomic potentials. It predicts an invariant atomic-energy sum and differentiates it into forces, requiring equivariance to rotations, translations, and reflections.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-26.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="NequIP atomic energy and force construction." caption="NequIP specializes typed equivariant layers to invariant energies and derivative forces. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 26." %}

Reflection parity augments the rotation type. Even features remain fixed under inversion; odd features change sign.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-27.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Even and odd parity features in NequIP." caption="Parity extends the feature type from SO(3) rotations to the full O(3) transformation group. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 27." %}

NequIP uses a Bessel radial basis, a smooth polynomial cutoff envelope, and spherical harmonics for the angular filter.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-28.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="NequIP Bessel radial envelope and spherical harmonic filter." caption="The filter combines smooth radial learning with an exactly transforming angular basis. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 28." %}

Its tensor products satisfy both the triangle rule and the parity rule $$p_ip_f=p_o$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-29.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="NequIP degree and parity selection rules." caption="Rotation degree and reflection parity jointly determine the legal message couplings. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 29." %}

Energy and force benchmarks compare NequIP with deep-learning and kernel models, including sGDML and FCHL.

## SE(3)-Transformer: invariant attention over typed values

SE(3)-Transformer asks how attention can operate on steerable features. Pairwise attention weights must be invariant; otherwise rotating the input would change which neighbor receives more weight.

The design starts from a TFN-like typed value transform, then learns a scalar attention coefficient for each pair.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-32.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Motivation for invariant attention in SE3 Transformer." caption="Attention may be nonlinear and pair-specific, but its weights must remain invariant. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 32." %}

The TFN convolution can be written as a kernel $$W^{lk}(x_j-x_i)$$ acting on type-$$k$$ source features and producing type-$$l$$ outputs. The kernel contains the radial weights, harmonic filters, and Clebsch--Gordan coefficients.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-33.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="TFN kernel rewritten for SE3 Transformer." caption="SE(3)-Transformer reuses the TFN kernel as its typed value transformation. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 33." %}

SE(3)-Transformer separates self-interaction from neighbor values and multiplies the latter by attention weights:

$$
f_{i,\mathrm{out}}^{(l)}
=W_V^{ll}f_{i,\mathrm{in}}^{(l)}
+\sum_{j\ne i}\alpha_{ij}
\sum_kW_V^{lk}(x_j-x_i)f_{j,\mathrm{in}}^{(k)}.
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-34.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SE3 Transformer attention weighted typed convolution." caption="A scalar attention coefficient modulates each typed TFN-like value without altering its transformation law. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 34." %}

Queries and keys contain direct sums of typed features. Their inner product is invariant, so the softmax

$$
\alpha_{ij}=\frac{\exp(q_i^\top k_{ij})}{\sum_{j'}\exp(q_i^\top k_{ij'})}
$$

is a valid scalar weight. Values remain steerable after weighted summation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-35.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SE3 Transformer invariant query key attention and typed values." caption="Invariant inner products select neighbors while typed kernels carry the equivariant content. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 35." %}

Nonlinear self-interaction layers mix channels. The representation indices inside each type are left intact; learned matrices act on the multiplicity dimension.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-36.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SE3 Transformer nonlinear self interaction layer." caption="Channel mixing and scalar MLPs add capacity without arbitrary mixing of representation coordinates. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 36." %}

QM9 experiments report improvements over the TFN baseline, testing whether adaptive invariant weighting adds useful selectivity to the typed convolution.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-37.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SE3 Transformer QM9 benchmark results." caption="SE(3)-Transformer compares attention-weighted steerable layers with TFN and non-equivariant baselines. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 37." %}

## SEGNN: steerable MLPs inside message passing

SEGNN makes a different change. It interprets a Clebsch--Gordan product with a filter as a matrix--vector multiplication, then uses this operation to build a multilayer perceptron whose hidden states remain steerable.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-38.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SEGNN steerable MLP motivation." caption="SEGNN promotes the typed tensor product from a linear convolution component to the primitive of a steerable MLP. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 38." %}

Given a conditioning steerable attribute $$a_{ij}$$, a Clebsch--Gordan tensor product acts linearly on a hidden feature $$h_{ij}$$ with coefficients determined by $$a_{ij}$$. Collecting every allowed output type produces a typed linear map.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-39.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SEGNN conditioned Clebsch Gordan linear transformation." caption="A geometric attribute supplies the typed weights of a steerable linear transformation. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 39." %}

The resulting steerable MLP composes weighted tensor products with scalar-conditioned nonlinearities. It is nonlinear in the joint input even though each typed transformation remains equivariant.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-40.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SEGNN steerable MLP composition." caption="Repeated conditioned products and scalar nonlinearities create a nonlinear equivariant feature map. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 40." %}

SEGNN places these MLPs inside both the message and node-update functions. Relative positions and other geometric attributes condition the messages; aggregated messages update the receiver state.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-41.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SEGNN message passing with steerable MLPs." caption="Both edge messages and node updates become steerable nonlinear maps in SEGNN. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 41." %}

Compared with TFN's fixed linear neighbor convolution, SEGNN applies a learned nonlinear equivariant transformation before aggregation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-42.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SEGNN contrasted with linear TFN aggregation." caption="The architectural distinction is where nonlinear typed processing occurs relative to neighbor aggregation. Cropped from the 2025 GDL steerable-attention lecture, slide 42." %}

QM9 and OC20 results evaluate the steerable-MLP design on molecular properties and catalyst energy/force prediction.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-43.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SEGNN QM9 and OC20 benchmark results." caption="SEGNN tests nonlinear steerable message passing on both molecular and materials-scale tasks. Related typed architectures include Cormorant, 3D steerable CNNs for volumetric signals, and Fourier-space Clebsch--Gordan networks.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-10/slide-44.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Related steerable architectures listed at the end of the lecture." caption="The source closes by placing TFN, NequIP, SE(3)-Transformer, and SEGNN within a wider typed-equivariant family. The comparison holds the representation system fixed. TFN uses a linear typed convolution with scalar gates. NequIP adds parity and an energy--force objective. SE(3)-Transformer uses invariant query--key scores to weight typed values. SEGNN builds nonlinear message and update maps from conditioned Clebsch--Gordan products. The architectural question is not whether to preserve equivariance, but where adaptive selection and nonlinearity enter without breaking it.
