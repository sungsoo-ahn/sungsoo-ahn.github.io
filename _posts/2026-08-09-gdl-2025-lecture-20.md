Machine learning for genomics is not one task with one natural input. A DNA sequence, an RNA abundance vector, an open-chromatin map, an RNA structure, and a cell state describe different objects. They also support different claims. A model trained on reference genomes can learn sequence regularities without knowing which genes are active in one cell. A model trained on expression can distinguish cell states without recovering the DNA variants that caused them. A structure model can connect an RNA sequence to geometry, while a design model must run that map in reverse.

The chronology in this lecture is therefore more useful than a leaderboard. DNABERT introduces masked pretraining on fixed DNA tokens. Enformer expands the physical span and predicts experimental tracks. GeneBERT adds cell-dependent chromatin context. Pangolin and AlphaMissense narrow the output to splicing and variant pathogenicity. HyenaDNA, Evo, GROVER, Nucleotide Transformer, GENERator, and Evo 2 then change context length, tokenization, evolutionary breadth, and generation. RhoFold+ and RNAFlow move from sequence to three-dimensional RNA. The final step changes the unit again: Geneformer and scCello model transcriptomes and cell identities rather than genomes alone.

## DNA, RNA, and the cellular context

DNA stores genomic information in a sequence of four bases. RNA uses a closely related alphabet while carrying coding information, regulating other molecules, and folding into structures that perform molecular functions. The familiar double helix is consequently only one part of the representational problem.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s03-dna-rna-structure.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DNA and RNA helices surrounded by their nucleotide bases." caption="DNA and RNA share a nucleotide backbone but differ in bases, strand organization, and structural behavior. From <a href='https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Difference_DNA_RNA-EN.svg'>Wikimedia Commons</a>." %}

The genome influences what a cell can build and how it can regulate itself. That influence reaches physical characteristics, health, disease, and behavior through a chain of molecular operations. Sequence is the durable instruction store; expression is the context-dependent use of those instructions.

### The same DNA is organized differently across cells and compartments

Cellular organization determines where genomic material resides. Eukaryotic cells enclose most DNA in a nucleus. The DNA is compacted into chromatin and packaged into chromosomes, which turns a long polymer into an organized object that a cell can copy, repair, and selectively expose. Prokaryotic cells lack the same nuclear compartment and generally organize their main chromosome in the cell body.

Eukaryotic cells also carry a second genomic compartment. Mitochondria contain circular DNA separate from the nuclear chromosomes. A phrase such as “the genome of a cell” can therefore hide both compartment and copy-number choices. Those choices matter when reads are aligned, variants are called, or expression is attributed to a gene.

### Gene expression connects sequence to phenotype

DNA affects a cell through gene expression. A genomic region is transcribed into RNA. Messenger RNA carries a protein-coding message to the ribosome, and the ribosome translates that message into an amino-acid chain. The resulting proteins contribute to traits such as eye color, height, and hair texture, but the same information flow also controls metabolism, signaling, development, and disease.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s05-gene-expression.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1 mx-auto d-block" max-width="760px" zoomable=true alt="DNA transcription into messenger RNA followed by translation at a ribosome." caption="Gene expression passes information from DNA through processed messenger RNA to protein synthesis." %}

The arrows in this diagram should not be read as a claim that DNA alone fixes every downstream outcome. Transcription depends on regulatory proteins and chromatin accessibility. RNA is processed, degraded, localized, and translated at rates that vary with cell state. Protein abundance and activity depend on still more mechanisms. Genomic machine learning enters at several points in this chain, and the input measurement determines which point is actually observed.

## Experiments decide what a model can observe

Sequencing is often discussed as if it produced “biological data” in a generic form. The lecture separates three measurements because they expose different variables: RNA-seq estimates expression, whole-genome sequencing recovers genomic sequence and variation, and ATAC-seq identifies accessible chromatin. A fourth family of experiments supplies three-dimensional structural targets.

### RNA-seq measures abundance after expression has occurred

RNA sequencing begins with a biological sample containing many transcripts. The molecules are isolated, fragmented, converted into a sequencing library, and read as short nucleotide strings. Those reads are then matched to a reference genome or transcriptome. Counting the reads assigned to each gene or transcript produces an expression profile.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s06-rna-seq-workflow.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RNA sequencing from a biological sample through fragments, reads, genome alignment, and expression counts." caption="RNA-seq turns a pool of transcripts into short reads, maps those reads to a reference, and summarizes them as an expression profile." %}

The output is not the DNA sequence of the sample. It is a count-based view of which transcripts were present under the sampled condition. The population in the tube also matters. Bulk RNA-seq averages across cells, so two cell populations can produce the same aggregate even when their constituent cell states differ. The virtual-cell models at the end of the lecture use single-cell transcriptomes precisely because a cell-level model needs cell-level observations.

### Whole-genome sequencing recovers the inherited sequence

Whole-genome sequencing fragments long DNA molecules, reads the fragments, and reconstructs a sequence by assembly or alignment. Overlapping reads provide the evidence needed to order the fragments and identify where the sample differs from a reference.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s07-whole-genome-sequencing.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A large DNA molecule fragmented, sequenced, overlapped, and assembled." caption="Whole-genome sequencing reconstructs long genomic sequence from many overlapping reads. From <a href='https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shotgun_sequencing_lg.jpg'>Wikimedia Commons</a>." %}

The Human Genome Project completed the first human reference genome in 2003 after an international effort. By the time of the lecture, a human genome could be sequenced within days for under 10,000 USD. The practical shift is from one monumental reference-building project to repeatable measurement across individuals. That population scale makes variant-effect prediction possible, but it also creates a distinction that recurs in the model survey: pretraining on one reference genome is not the same as learning variation across thousands of genomes or diversity across species.

### ATAC-seq observes regulatory accessibility, not sequence identity

The same genomic locus can be active in one cell type and inaccessible in another. ATAC-seq measures this regulatory state. Tn5 transposase inserts sequencing adapters preferentially into open chromatin. Sequencing the tagged fragments reveals which regions were physically accessible when the sample was prepared.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s08-atac-seq.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1 mx-auto d-block" max-width="680px" zoomable=true alt="Closed and open chromatin treated with Tn5 transposase in an ATAC-seq assay." caption="ATAC-seq marks accessible DNA because transposase reaches open chromatin more readily than nucleosome-packed regions. From <a href='https://doi.org/10.1186/s41065-019-0105-9'>Sun et al. (2019)</a>." %}

Accessibility varies across cell types, developmental stages, environmental conditions, and even cells assigned the same broad type. A sequence model sees what regulatory motifs could exist. An accessibility measurement says which parts of that sequence were exposed in a particular context. GeneBERT later combines these two kinds of information rather than asking sequence alone to represent both.

### Three-dimensional experiments supply geometric supervision

RNA and protein function often depends on three-dimensional structure. The lecture highlights three established measurement routes that are used for both proteins and nucleic acids: X-ray crystallography, nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, and cryogenic electron microscopy.

These methods do not produce interchangeable targets. A diffraction pattern, an NMR-derived constraint set, a cryo-EM density map, and a curated atomic structure sit at different points in the inference pipeline. A structure-prediction model is usually trained on the resolved structural representation, but the scarcity and biases of that resolved set still reflect the measurement process that created it.

## Prediction and design tasks follow the measurements

The lecture turns from experiments to tasks in a deliberate order. Functional annotation asks what a genomic segment or variant does. Structure prediction asks what geometry a sequence adopts. Sequence generation and design reverse a predictive relation and search for a sequence with a desired regulatory or molecular outcome.

### Functional annotation spans variants, regulation, and RNA processing

Variant-effect prediction assigns a score to a mutation. The desired output may be functional disruption, a molecular phenotype, a disease association, or a change in drug response. A local edit can propagate through several layers: the DNA substitution may alter a regulatory motif, change RNA splicing, replace an amino acid, destabilize a protein, or modify a cellular pathway. The prediction target must name which of these outcomes the score represents.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s10-variant-effects.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1 mx-auto d-block" max-width="620px" zoomable=true alt="An atlas of variant effects connected to drug development, diagnostics, protein science, precision medicine, and disease mechanisms." caption="Variant-effect maps connect genomic changes to molecular, clinical, and therapeutic consequences. From <a href='https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-023-02986-x'>Fowler et al. (2023)</a>." %}

Promoter and enhancer detection asks where transcription is controlled. A promoter is a DNA sequence near a transcription start site that recruits RNA polymerase and transcription factors. An enhancer is a shorter regulatory element that can increase transcription, often through a long-range contact with the promoter. The linear distance between sequence elements therefore need not equal their regulatory distance.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s11-promoter-enhancer.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1 mx-auto d-block" max-width="680px" zoomable=true alt="A DNA enhancer looping toward transcription factors, a promoter, RNA polymerase, and a gene." caption="Enhancer-bound activators contact promoter machinery through a DNA loop, so distal sequence can regulate transcription. From <a href='https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gene_enhancer.svg'>Wikimedia Commons</a>." %}

Transcription-factor binding prediction makes one part of that mechanism explicit. Given a DNA sequence, the model predicts whether a transcription factor binds. A transcription factor is a protein that controls the rate at which genetic information is transcribed from DNA into messenger RNA. The input may contain a recognizable motif, but actual binding can also depend on neighboring motifs, competing proteins, chromatin accessibility, and cell type.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s12-transcription-factors.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Several transcription factors assembled with promoter and enhancer DNA." caption="Transcription-factor binding links sequence motifs to the protein machinery that changes transcription rate." %}

RNA splicing prediction moves the target downstream of transcription. A precursor RNA contains exons and introns. The spliceosome removes introns and joins selected exons to create a mature transcript, which can then be translated. Alternative choices yield different mature RNAs from the same genomic region, and a variant can change a splice site or another regulatory signal without changing the conventional protein-coding frame directly.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s13-rna-splicing.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1 mx-auto d-block" max-width="720px" zoomable=true alt="DNA transcribed into pre-mRNA, spliced into mature mRNA, and translated into protein." caption="Splicing removes introns and joins exons between transcription and translation. From <a href='https://www.yourgenome.org/theme/what-is-rna-splicing/'>Wellcome Connecting Science</a>." %}

These four tasks share an input alphabet but not a prediction contract. Variant pathogenicity is not promoter classification. Promoter classification is not transcription-factor binding. Binding is not splice usage. The chronological model survey keeps returning to this distinction because each architecture is trained and evaluated against a different observable.

### RNA structure prediction adds a geometric target

RNA function is closely tied to structure. A nucleotide sequence first forms base-pairing interactions that define secondary structure, then packs into a three-dimensional fold. Unlike canonical double-stranded DNA, RNA can realize many motifs and global shapes.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s14-rna-structure-levels.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="An RNA sequence mapped to secondary structure and then tertiary structure." caption="RNA structure prediction crosses three representations: nucleotide sequence, base-pairing topology, and three-dimensional coordinates. From <a href='https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1009291'>Zhao et al. (2021)</a>." %}

The lecture places a broader nucleic-acid motif vocabulary beside the RNA folding problem: duplexes, G-quadruplexes, i-motifs, triplexes, and G-triplexes, supported by several base-pair and base-triad chemistries. The reproduced panel is specifically a DNA motif comparison, not a claim that every motif is an RNA structural class. Its point carries over at the right level: nucleic-acid geometry is richer than one standard helix, and a useful structure model must represent both local interactions and the global arrangement they induce.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s14-rna-structural-motifs.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Duplex, G-quadruplex, i-motif, triplex, and G-triplex DNA motifs with their base-pairing chemistries." caption="DNA supports several paired and higher-order motifs rather than one universal double-helix geometry. From <a href='https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms23052407'>Xiong et al. (2022)</a>." %}

### Sequence design reverses the direction of prediction

Design begins with a desired outcome and asks for a sequence. A synthetic enhancer can be designed to recruit chosen transcription factors. Synthetic promoters and enhancers can control gene and cell therapies or probe regulatory logic. RNA sequences can be designed for therapeutics, vaccines, or binding to a molecular target.

The reversal changes what constitutes evidence. A predictor can be evaluated on held-out labeled examples. A designed sequence does not become functional because a model assigns it a high score. The candidate must pass an appropriate forward model and ultimately an experiment. RNAFlow later makes this interface visible: it generates a sequence and structure conditioned on a protein, then evaluates whether the resulting aptamer geometry and sequence recover the target interaction.

### DNABERT starts with masked genomic language modeling

[DNABERT](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btab083) adapts BERT-style masked language modeling to DNA. The GRCh38 human reference genome is split into overlapping 6-mers. A sequence such as `ACGTACG...` becomes a sequence of six-base tokens offset by one base at a time, and the model learns to recover a masked token from the surrounding context.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s16-dnabert-transformer.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1 mx-auto d-block" max-width="420px" zoomable=true alt="A masked DNA token passed through a Transformer and reconstructed from surrounding tokens." caption="DNABERT masks one DNA 6-mer and predicts it from bidirectional sequence context. From <a href='https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btab083'>Ji et al. (2021)</a>." %}

The pretraining corpus is about 3.2 billion bases, but it represents one human reference rather than a population. The downstream question shown in the lecture is promoter identification. Fine-tuning tests whether contextual representations learned without promoter labels can separate promoter sequences from several negative or alternative promoter sets.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s16-dnabert-benchmarks.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DNABERT promoter benchmarks with bars, learning curves, and task comparisons." caption="DNABERT evaluates masked-pretrained representations on several promoter-recognition settings rather than treating pretraining accuracy as the biological result. From <a href='https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btab083'>Ji et al. (2021)</a>." %}

Fixed 6-mers make the analogy to words easy to implement, but they impose a vocabulary and a phase. The choice also multiplies sequence length and can make a one-base insertion or deletion change many neighboring tokens. GROVER later revisits this axis with a learned variable-length vocabulary, while HyenaDNA avoids k-mers by staying at single-nucleotide resolution.

### Enformer makes long-range regulation the supervised target

[Enformer](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-021-01252-x) changes both architecture and supervision. A convolutional front end compresses local sequence patterns. Transformer layers then integrate information across a receptive field spanning roughly 200 kilobases. Organism-specific heads predict thousands of human and mouse genomic tracks.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s17-enformer-architecture.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1 mx-auto d-block" max-width="520px" zoomable=true alt="Enformer convolutional and Transformer layers with human and mouse genomic-track heads." caption="Enformer compresses local sequence with convolution, mixes a long genomic window with attention, and predicts organism-specific experimental tracks. From <a href='https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-021-01252-x'>Avsec et al. (2021)</a>." %}

The long window has a biological purpose. Enhancers can regulate a transcription start site from far away in the linear sequence, so a promoter-centered local model misses part of the relevant context. Enformer is evaluated on gene-expression and epigenomic prediction, including how predicted and measured values correlate across genes and genomic tracks.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s17-enformer-results.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Scatter plots comparing Enformer and Basenji2 across gene expression and epigenomic tracks." caption="The Enformer evaluation separates expression across genes, expression across CAGE experiments, and several epigenomic tracks. From <a href='https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-021-01252-x'>Avsec et al. (2021)</a>." %}

DNABERT and Enformer are therefore not successive versions of the same contract. DNABERT learns a general sequence representation and fine-tunes it for classification. Enformer directly supervises a long sequence-to-measurement map. Context length matters in both, but Enformer attaches the context to experimental outputs that expose distal regulation.

### GeneBERT adds a context that DNA sequence cannot supply

[GeneBERT](https://openreview.net/forum?id=DSCsslei9r) takes two inputs: one-dimensional DNA sequence and a two-dimensional transcription-factor-by-region matrix. The regional input is derived from chromatin-accessibility information, so the model can represent which regulatory regions are active in a cell type rather than encoding only the invariant reference sequence.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s18-genebert-architecture.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GeneBERT sequence pretraining and a region Transformer joined by sequence-region matching." caption="GeneBERT pretrains sequence and region encoders, then aligns their representations so cell-type context can influence regulatory predictions. From <a href='https://openreview.net/forum?id=DSCsslei9r'>Mo et al. (2022)</a>." %}

The model is evaluated on promoter classification and transcription-factor binding across cell lines. On the promoter comparison reproduced in the lecture, GeneBERT reports precision 0.805, recall 0.803, and AUC 0.894, compared with 0.675, 0.637, and 0.693 for DNABERT. The comparison is narrow but informative: adding regulatory context changes the result on a task where the genomic sequence is not the whole state.

The general lesson is not that ATAC-seq should always be appended to a sequence model. It is that cell-dependent regulation cannot be recovered from the reference sequence as a unique answer. When the output depends on cell state, the model needs a measurement or condition that identifies that state.

### Pangolin and AlphaMissense specialize the biological consequence

[Pangolin](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-022-02664-4) predicts RNA splicing from genomic sequence. Its dilated residual convolutional network consumes roughly 10,000 bases of context and outputs splice probabilities or usage for four tissues: heart, liver, brain, and testis. Training spans human, rhesus macaque, mouse, and rat.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s19-pangolin-architecture.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1 mx-auto d-block" max-width="600px" zoomable=true alt="Pangolin genomic input, dilated convolutional layers, four species, and four tissue outputs." caption="Pangolin shares a sequence model across four species while predicting tissue-specific splice usage. From <a href='https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-022-02664-4'>Zeng and Li (2022)</a>." %}

The output is not simply “is this an exon?” It is a probability attached to splice sites and tissues. This makes variant evaluation possible: alter the genomic sequence, recompute the predicted splice behavior, and compare the change with observed RNA processing. The lecture’s benchmark panel reports top-1, top-0.5, and area-under-the-precision-recall-curve results for four tissues against SpliceAI, HAL, MMSplice, and MaxEntScan.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s19-pangolin-results.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Pangolin splice-site prediction comparison across four tissues and five methods." caption="Pangolin is evaluated tissue by tissue against existing splice predictors; the table keeps accuracy-like and precision-recall summaries separate. From <a href='https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-022-02664-4'>Zeng and Li (2022)</a>." %}

[AlphaMissense](https://deepmind.google/research/publications/21083/) follows a different route from DNA variant to effect. A missense variant is a single-nucleotide substitution that changes one amino acid in a protein. AlphaMissense combines the resulting protein sequence with an AlphaFold-derived structural context and protein-language representations, then predicts whether the substitution is likely benign or pathogenic.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s20-alphamissense.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaMissense mapping a DNA substitution to a protein substitution, structure context, and pathogenicity output." caption="AlphaMissense crosses a biological interface: a DNA substitution changes an amino acid, whose sequence and structural context support a pathogenicity score. From <a href='https://deepmind.google/research/publications/21083/'>Cheng et al. (2023)</a>." %}

The proteome-wide output in the lecture labels 57% of possible human missense variants as likely benign, 32% as likely pathogenic, and 11% as uncertain. These are model classifications over a defined variant universe, not observed clinical outcomes for that fraction of people. The denominator is all possible missense substitutions in the human proteome.

Pangolin and AlphaMissense show why “genome foundation model” cannot replace the task name. Pangolin targets tissue-specific RNA processing. AlphaMissense targets a protein-level consequence of coding variation. Both begin from genomic change, but each follows a different mechanistic path and uses different supervision.

### HyenaDNA makes million-base context computationally feasible

Standard dense attention has quadratic cost in sequence length, which makes single-nucleotide genomic context expensive. [HyenaDNA](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/86ab6927ee4ae9bde4247793c46797c7-Abstract-Conference.html) replaces attention with a hierarchy of long convolutions and multiplicative gates. It operates at single-nucleotide resolution and extends context from the 512-to-4,000-token range common in earlier work to as many as one million bases.

Long context is a capacity, not proof that the model uses a particular distant interaction. The biological claim still comes from the downstream task and the construction of its held-out examples. HyenaDNA’s contribution in this lecture is architectural: it makes a much larger context available without abandoning nucleotide resolution.

### Evo changes the corpus from one genome to evolutionary diversity

[Evo](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ado9336) combines long-context sequence modeling with OpenGenome, a corpus containing 2.7 million publicly available prokaryotic and phage genomes. The training material spans coding sequences, regulatory regions, non-coding RNA, and longer genomic neighborhoods. The model consequently encounters DNA, RNA, and protein-coding constraints in their shared genomic context.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s22-evo-long-context.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein, codon, and RNA language models contrasted with a long-context genome foundation model." caption="Evo places regulatory DNA, protein-coding sequence, and non-coding RNA within one long genomic context instead of training one isolated molecular language at a time. From <a href='https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ado9336'>Nguyen et al. (2024)</a>." %}

Evo uses a StripedHyena architecture that interleaves Hyena operators with data-controlled mixing. The model produces a likelihood over the next nucleotide, but the scientific program extends from likelihood to prediction and design at molecular and genome scales.

The shift from HyenaDNA to Evo is therefore not just a bigger sequence window. HyenaDNA is pretrained on the human reference genome. Evo trains across millions of microbial and phage genomes. The input alphabet stays small, but the corpus changes which evolutionary regularities and genomic organizations can be learned.

### GROVER and Nucleotide Transformer change tokenization and population breadth

[GROVER](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00872-0) learns byte-pair-encoded tokens from the entire human genome. Byte-pair encoding begins with short units and repeatedly merges common adjacent pairs, producing a variable-length vocabulary. The learned tokens range from short motifs to longer recurring sequence chunks.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s23-grover-tokenization.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1 mx-auto d-block" max-width="760px" zoomable=true alt="GROVER DNA byte-pair tokenization, variable token lengths, and Transformer architecture." caption="GROVER learns variable-length DNA tokens by byte-pair encoding and passes them through a Transformer. From <a href='https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00872-0'>Sanabria et al. (2024)</a>." %}

The tokenization changes the unit presented to the Transformer. Unlike an overlapping 6-mer vocabulary, BPE does not force every token to have the same length. The evaluation shown in the lecture compares promoter classification under natural and shuffled sequence contexts and tests whether the model’s token and context representations support regulatory prediction.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s23-grover-evaluation.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GROVER promoter-sequence shuffling experiment and classification bars." caption="GROVER tests whether learned context distinguishes promoters from controls whose local or global sequence order has been shuffled. From <a href='https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00872-0'>Sanabria et al. (2024)</a>." %}

[Nucleotide Transformer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02523-z) returns to a Transformer family but expands the training population. The model suite integrates 3,202 human genomes and 850 genomes from other species, with sizes from tens of millions to billions of parameters. The human genomes expose within-species variation; the non-human genomes expose evolutionary diversity.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s24-nucleotide-transformer.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Nucleotide Transformer masked pretraining followed by frozen-feature downstream prediction." caption="Nucleotide Transformer separates masked genomic pretraining from downstream use of frozen token embeddings. From <a href='https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02523-z'>Dalla-Torre et al. (published online 2024)</a>." %}

The figure makes the transfer contract explicit. During pretraining, a masked DNA token is recovered by a language-model head. During downstream prediction, the pretrained encoder can be frozen and its embeddings passed to a task-specific head. A foundation model is useful here only if those embeddings retain information that the downstream predictor can access.

GROVER and Nucleotide Transformer change different axes. GROVER learns a vocabulary on one human genome. Nucleotide Transformer broadens the set of genomes and species while studying scale and transfer. “More genomic data” is too vague to describe either contribution.

### GENERator and Evo 2 make generation and domain coverage central

[GENErator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07272) is a 1.2-billion-parameter autoregressive model with a context of about 98,000 tokens. It trains on roughly 386 billion bases of eukaryotic DNA. The corpus includes intergenic, protein-coding, and RNA-coding regions from many higher organisms rather than only a human reference or prokaryotic genomes.

The downstream ambition is synthetic genomic design. That phrase includes several levels of validity: generated tokens must form coherent DNA; coding regions must translate plausibly; regulatory elements must have the requested activity; and a full construct must function in a biological system. Autoregressive likelihood supplies the generator, while each design claim still needs a matching evaluation.

Evo 2 expands Evo across all domains of life. The 2025 lecture presented the preprint with 9.3 trillion bases from more than 128,000 genomes, including bacteria, archaea, viruses, plants, animals, and humans, together with million-token dependencies. The work has since appeared as [Brixi et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10176-5).

The architecture also changes. The lecture contrasts the first Evo model with Evo 2’s StripedHyena 2 blocks, which combine several sequence-mixing operators, convolution, gates, short segments, and medium- or long-range components.

One evaluation shown in the lecture probes whether the likelihood structure respects coding organization across species. The heat map aligns codon positions and neighboring untranslated or coding regions, revealing sequence likelihood patterns around the start of a coding sequence.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s26-evo2-codon-structure.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Evo 2 nucleotide likelihood heat map aligned around codon positions across species." caption="The codon-aligned likelihood analysis asks whether a genome-scale model recovers coding periodicity across diverse species. From <a href='https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10176-5'>Brixi et al. (2026)</a>." %}

GENErator and Evo 2 both generate DNA, but their corpora and claims differ. GENERator emphasizes long eukaryotic generation with a 98,000-token decoder context. Evo 2 emphasizes generalist modeling across all domains of life with a million-token context. Neither should be summarized by parameter count alone; the relevant differences are what sequences were observed, what span the model can condition on, and what designed object is evaluated.

## RNA models connect sequence, structure, and design

The lecture returns from genome-scale language models to the RNA structure task introduced earlier. The two models occupy opposite directions of the sequence--structure relation. RhoFold+ predicts a three-dimensional structure from sequence. RNAFlow generates an RNA sequence and structure conditioned on a protein target.

### RhoFold+ predicts three-dimensional RNA from sequence

[RhoFold+](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02487-0) combines an RNA language model with a geometric structure module. Its RNA foundation model is pretrained on about 23 million sequences. Sequence and multiple-sequence-alignment features become single and pair representations, which pass through a Rhoformer, an IPA module, and a structure module. Recycling feeds the intermediate structure back through the network ten times.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s27-rhofold-plus.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RhoFold+ RNA language model, Rhoformer, structure module, recycling, and three-dimensional output." caption="RhoFold+ joins RNA sequence pretraining to pair representations and a recycled geometric decoder for direct three-dimensional structure prediction. From <a href='https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02487-0'>Shen et al. (2024)</a>." %}

The output includes an all-atom structure, secondary-structure information, local frames and torsion angles, base-pairing constraints, and a confidence estimate. Sequence pretraining addresses the abundance of unlabeled RNA, while the structure module learns from the much smaller set with geometric supervision. The model does not make experimental structure data unnecessary; it uses language pretraining to compensate for its scarcity.

### RNAFlow co-designs an aptamer around a protein target

[RNAFlow](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/nori24a.html) starts from a protein backbone and noisy RNA coordinates. A joint protein--RNA geometric graph is processed by a graph neural network that predicts a denoised RNA sequence. An inverse-folding model and a fixed RNA structure-prediction network connect that sequence update to a denoised three-dimensional RNA structure.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s28-rnaflow-architecture.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RNAFlow beginning from a protein and noisy RNA complex, predicting sequence, and folding the RNA structure." caption="RNAFlow alternates protein-conditioned sequence prediction with RNA structure prediction along a flow-matching path. From <a href='https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/nori24a.html'>Nori and Jin (2024)</a>." %}

One generated sample is therefore a coupled object: nucleotide identities and a protein-bound RNA conformation. The task is aptamer design, not unconditional RNA generation. The protein supplies the condition, and recovery metrics evaluate whether the generated sequence and structure match the binding problem.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s28-rnaflow-results.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Designed and original RNA-protein structures with RMSD, RNA length, and recovery benchmark bars." caption="RNAFlow compares designed aptamer complexes with target structures and reports structural and sequence-recovery metrics against alternative methods. From <a href='https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/nori24a.html'>Nori and Jin (2024)</a>." %}

RhoFold+ and RNAFlow form a pipeline but not an independent validation loop. RNAFlow uses a pretrained structure predictor inside its design system. A favorable predicted fold is computational evidence produced by a related model, not a binding experiment. The designed aptamer still has to be synthesized and measured if the scientific claim is physical binding.

## A virtual cell changes the unit of modeling

A genomic language model predicts or generates sequence. A virtual cell is meant to simulate cellular behavior. The input must therefore expand from DNA to measurements of molecular state, perturbation, cellular context, and interactions across scales.

[Bunne et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.11.015) organize this program around multimodal observations and universal representations. Whole-genome sequencing describes inherited sequence. RNA microscopy and single-cell RNA-seq describe expression and localization. Imaging and spatial transcriptomics add phenotype and tissue organization. Interventions distinguish correlation from response by showing how the state changes when genes, molecules, or environments are altered.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s29-virtual-cell-modalities.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Multimodal cell measurements feeding an AI virtual-cell foundation model and universal representations." caption="A virtual-cell foundation model must integrate genomic, expression, imaging, and spatial measurements before producing molecular, cellular, and multicellular representations. From <a href='https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.11.015'>Bunne et al. (2024)</a>." %}

The proposed representation is hierarchical. Molecular representations contribute to a cellular representation; cellular states contribute to multicellular representations; modeled interactions connect the levels. One shared latent vector is not enough unless it retains the distinctions needed by each downstream operation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s29-virtual-cell-representations.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Molecular, cellular, and multicellular universal representations connected in an AI virtual cell." caption="The virtual-cell proposal builds representations at molecular, cellular, and multicellular scales and models the interactions between them. From <a href='https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.11.015'>Bunne et al. (2024)</a>." %}

The physical system has the same hierarchy. DNA replication, transcription, translation, and molecular interactions occur inside a cell. Receptor signaling and cell--cell interactions change the cell state. Tissue organization introduces spatial and multicellular constraints. “Simulate all cell behaviors” is therefore an aspiration covering several coupled state spaces, not one benchmark target.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s29-virtual-cell-scales.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Molecular building blocks, one cell, and multicellular tissue organization." caption="The physical object expands from molecular reactions to cellular state and multicellular organization. From <a href='https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2024.11.015'>Bunne et al. (2024)</a>." %}

### Geneformer models one cell as a ranked expression sequence

[Geneformer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06139-9) is pretrained on about 30 million single-cell transcriptomes. It converts each cell’s expression profile into a rank-ordered sequence of genes. Highly expressed genes appear early in the sequence after normalization, and masked learning asks the Transformer to recover omitted genes from the remaining cell state.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s30-geneformer-architecture.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1 mx-auto d-block" max-width="660px" zoomable=true alt="Geneformer rank-value encoding followed by Transformer layers and contextual gene predictions." caption="Geneformer serializes a single-cell transcriptome as ranked gene tokens, then learns contextual gene and cell embeddings through masked pretraining. From <a href='https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06139-9'>Theodoris et al. (2023)</a>." %}

The corpus spans many tissues rather than one homogeneous population. Placenta, adrenal gland, pancreas, immune tissues, brain, lung, liver, and many other sources contribute to the 30-million-cell collection. Tissue diversity gives the model varied expression contexts, although a large corpus still reflects the assays, organisms, and sampling choices used to construct it.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s30-geneformer-tissues.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1 mx-auto d-block" max-width="620px" zoomable=true alt="Pie chart of tissues represented in the Geneformer pretraining corpus." caption="Geneformer’s 30-million-cell corpus combines transcriptomes from many tissues, so pretraining spans heterogeneous cell states rather than one organ. From <a href='https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06139-9'>Theodoris et al. (2023)</a>." %}

Fine-tuning targets network-biology tasks: cell-type and state annotation, gene regulation, disease classification, in silico perturbation, and therapeutic target prediction. Each consumes the pretrained representation differently. A cell-type classifier reads out identity; a perturbation analysis changes or removes gene tokens; a network task interprets relations among genes.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s30-geneformer-tasks.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Geneformer downstream tasks for therapeutics, cell annotation, and gene regulation." caption="Geneformer transfers one transcriptome representation to therapeutic, annotation, and gene-regulation tasks with distinct outputs. From <a href='https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06139-9'>Theodoris et al. (2023)</a>." %}

Geneformer does not ingest the full multimodal state proposed for a virtual cell. It models one observation layer: a single-cell transcriptome. Calling that representation a cell state is useful shorthand only when the downstream question is identifiable from expression and the training distribution covers the relevant context.

### scCello adds a hierarchy of cell identities

[scCello](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/0be40478ab6ee0006ee3b38b158bbc8f-Abstract-Conference.html) adds a cell ontology to transcriptome pretraining. The ontology is a graph in which broad cell categories and more specific cell types are related. The model uses those relations to guide the geometry of cell representations rather than treating every label as an unrelated class.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s31-sccello-pretraining.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="scCello cell ontology, transcriptome matrix, relational alignment, and cell-type coherence objectives." caption="scCello aligns transcriptome representations with a cell-ontology graph so related cell types occupy a structured representation space. From <a href='https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/0be40478ab6ee0006ee3b38b158bbc8f-Abstract-Conference.html'>Yuan et al. (2024)</a>." %}

The relational objective encourages two kinds of organization. Cells with the same label should remain coherent, while distances between different cell types should reflect their ontology relations. This is more informative than a flat classification loss when the labels already form a biological hierarchy.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/s31-sccello-tasks.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="scCello downstream tasks including clustering, integration, classification, cancer drug prediction, novel cell typing, and marker prediction." caption="scCello transfers ontology-guided representations to clustering, batch integration, cell typing, drug prediction, and marker-gene prediction. From <a href='https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/0be40478ab6ee0006ee3b38b158bbc8f-Abstract-Conference.html'>Yuan et al. (2024)</a>." %}

The downstream tasks include cell-type clustering, batch integration, known and novel cell-type classification, marker-gene prediction, and cancer-drug prediction. They share a representation but not an evidence standard. Correct placement in an ontology does not establish that a perturbation response is correct; a useful marker prediction does not establish that the model simulates cell dynamics.

The lecture ends at this boundary. Genomic models can learn sequence regularities across enormous contexts and evolutionary corpora. RNA models can connect sequence to structure and conditioned design. Transcriptome models can organize millions of measured cell states. A virtual cell must connect those components to interventions, dynamics, and multiscale observations. Scaling the encoder is part of that program, but the simulated object is defined by the measurements and transitions the model can actually predict.
