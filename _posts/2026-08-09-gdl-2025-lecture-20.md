## Genomes, expression, and cells

DNA and RNA carry instructions for forming, operating, and regulating cells. Variation in those instructions shapes physical traits, health, and disease, which makes genome modeling both a prediction problem and a potential design problem.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-3.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DNA RNA bases and double stranded molecular structures." caption="DNA and RNA encode and transmit cellular instructions through nucleotide sequences. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 3." %}

Cellular organization changes where DNA resides. Eukaryotic cells package nuclear DNA into chromosomes and also carry a circular mitochondrial chromosome. Prokaryotic cells use a different organization, often with a circular chromosome in the cell body.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-4.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Eukaryotic and prokaryotic cells chromosomes and mitochondrial DNA." caption="Genome organization differs across cell types and cellular compartments. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 4." %}

DNA influences phenotype through gene expression. A region is transcribed into RNA; messenger RNA carries the instructions to ribosomes; and translation produces proteins whose activities shape the cell. The causal chain therefore crosses sequence, regulatory state, RNA abundance, protein, and phenotype.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-5.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Gene expression from DNA transcription to RNA and protein translation." caption="Gene expression connects genomic sequence to RNA and protein production. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 5." %}

## How genomic data are measured

RNA sequencing measures gene expression in a population of cells. Short reads are aligned to a reference genome or transcriptome, then counted to estimate the abundance of known RNA sequences.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-6.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RNA sequencing workflow from biological sample through reads and expression counts." caption="RNA-seq converts short sequencing reads into a gene-expression profile. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 6." %}

Whole-genome sequencing recovers a person's DNA sequence. The first human reference genome required an international project completed in 2003; current sequencing can produce a human genome within days at far lower cost.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-7.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Whole genome sequencing read alignment and variant recovery." caption="Whole-genome sequencing assembles or aligns reads to recover genomic sequence and variation. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 7." %}

ATAC-seq measures chromatin accessibility. Transposase preferentially inserts adapters into open chromatin, making accessible regulatory regions visible to sequencing. Accessibility varies across cell types, conditions, and developmental stages, so the measurement is a context-dependent genomic layer rather than a fixed sequence property.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-8.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ATAC seq assay contrasting closed and open chromatin." caption="ATAC-seq reports which genomic regions are physically accessible in a particular cellular context. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 8." %}

Three-dimensional RNA and protein structures can be measured with X-ray crystallography, NMR spectroscopy, and cryogenic electron microscopy. These measurements provide geometric targets that sequence-only datasets do not contain.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-9.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="X ray crystallography NMR and cryo electron microscopy structural measurement methods." caption="Structural biology adds three-dimensional supervision through X-ray, NMR, and cryo-EM experiments. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 9." %}

## Genomic prediction and design tasks

Variant-effect prediction scores mutations for deleteriousness or phenotypic impact. A useful model must connect a local sequence change to regulatory, RNA, protein, or disease consequences.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-10.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Genetic variant effects spanning drug response protein sequence and disease." caption="Variant annotation asks which genomic substitutions alter molecular or organism-level outcomes. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 10." %}

Promoter and enhancer detection identifies regulatory DNA. Promoters recruit RNA polymerase and transcription factors to initiate transcription, whereas enhancers can raise transcription rates from more distant genomic locations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-11.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Promoter enhancer transcription factors and RNA polymerase regulation." caption="Regulatory annotation locates sequence elements that initiate or amplify transcription. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 11." %}

Transcription-factor binding prediction asks whether a regulatory protein binds a given DNA sequence. The output links sequence motifs to a protein-mediated change in transcription rate.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-12.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Transcription factors bound to regulatory DNA sequences." caption="TF-binding models connect nucleotide context to protein--DNA recognition. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 12." %}

Splicing prediction models which introns are removed and which exons are joined. Variants can change splice sites or regulatory signals and thereby alter the mature RNA transcript.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-13.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Alternative RNA splicing from pre mRNA introns and exons." caption="Splicing models predict how genomic sequence is edited into alternative mature transcripts. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 13." %}

RNA structure prediction maps a nucleotide sequence to secondary and tertiary geometry. Unlike double-stranded DNA, RNA adopts diverse motifs such as duplexes, quadruplexes, i-motifs, triplexes, and other folds tied to cellular function.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-14.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RNA sequence secondary structure tertiary structure and structural motifs." caption="RNA folding spans sequence, base-pairing patterns, and three-dimensional structure. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 14." %}

Design reverses these maps. Synthetic DNA motifs can recruit chosen transcription factors; promoters and enhancers can control gene or cell therapies; and RNA design supports therapeutics, vaccines, and protein-binding aptamers.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-15.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Genomic sequence generation tasks for motifs enhancers promoters and RNA." caption="Genomic design chooses sequences to realize regulatory or molecular functions. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 15." %}

## From DNABERT to genome-scale foundation models

DNABERT was an early masked-language model for DNA. It tokenized the human reference genome into 6-mers and evaluated the learned representation on tasks such as promoter recognition.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-16.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DNABERT six mer tokenization and promoter recognition results." caption="DNABERT brought BERT-style masked pretraining to fixed-length DNA k-mers. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 16." %}

Enformer combined convolution and Transformer layers to model long-range interactions over windows of roughly 200 kilobase pairs. Training against thousands of human and mouse epigenomic tracks made sequence-to-expression prediction a multitask problem.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-17.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Enformer long range DNA sequence model and gene expression prediction." caption="Enformer expands sequence context and supervises it with many regulatory measurement tracks. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 17." %}

GeneBERT added a second modality: a transcription-factor-by-region matrix from chromatin-accessibility data. Joint pretraining on DNA sequence and ATAC-seq context supports regulatory-element prediction across cell types.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-18.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GeneBERT multimodal DNA and ATAC seq transformer architecture." caption="GeneBERT conditions sequence representation on cell-type-specific chromatin accessibility. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 18." %}

Pangolin specializes in RNA splicing and variant-induced splice changes. It learns from multiple tissues across human, macaque, mouse, and rat data, making tissue and species diversity part of the target rather than a post hoc transfer test.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-19.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Pangolin genomic sequence splicing prediction across tissues and species." caption="Pangolin predicts splice usage and the effects of genomic variants across biological contexts. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 19." %}

AlphaMissense uses an AlphaFold-derived model to predict the pathogenicity of missense variants across the human proteome. A single DNA substitution changes one amino acid, and the prediction combines sequence and structural context to classify the consequence.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-20.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="AlphaMissense pathogenesis prediction from a missense DNA variant." caption="AlphaMissense connects a nucleotide substitution to an amino-acid change and a proteome-scale pathogenicity score. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 20." %}

HyenaDNA replaces attention with hierarchical convolution-like operators that scale to sequences of up to one million bases. This increases context from hundreds or thousands of tokens to genomic regions large enough to contain distant regulation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-21.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="HyenaDNA million base context architecture and downstream tasks." caption="HyenaDNA targets single-nucleotide resolution while extending the feasible context length. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 21." %}

Evo moves from one reference genome to evolutionary-scale prokaryotic data. It trains on 2.7 million bacterial, archaeal, and phage genomes and supports sequence modeling and design from molecular to genome scale.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-22.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Evo genomic foundation model training and design pipeline." caption="Evo learns from millions of microbial and phage genomes to model long biological sequences generatively. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 22." %}

GROVER applies byte-pair encoding to the human genome. Learned variable-length tokens capture recurring sequence context, while the model is evaluated on regulatory and genomic tasks.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-23.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GROVER byte pair genome tokenization and model evaluation." caption="GROVER learns a genome-scale vocabulary instead of fixing one nucleotide or k-mer tokenization. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 23." %}

Nucleotide Transformer compares Transformer models trained on 3,202 human genomes and 850 non-human genomes. Its evaluation asks how scale and species composition affect transfer across genomic prediction tasks.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-24.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Nucleotide Transformer pretraining datasets model sizes and genomic tasks." caption="Nucleotide Transformer varies genome diversity and model scale in a broad foundation-model benchmark. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 24." %}

GENErator is a 1.2-billion-parameter long-context generative model with a context of about 98,000 tokens. Its eukaryotic corpus spans roughly 386 billion bases and is optimized for synthetic genomic design.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-25.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GENErator long context eukaryotic genomic foundation model." caption="GENErator combines eukaryotic breadth with a context window designed for long synthetic sequences. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 25." %}

Evo 2 expands the training data to 9.3 trillion bases from more than 128,000 genomes across all domains of life and retains million-token dependencies. The progression from Evo is both a scale change and a domain-coverage change.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-26.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Evo 2 training data across domains of life and million token genomic context." caption="Evo 2 extends genome modeling from prokaryotes and phages to broad organismal diversity. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 26." %}

## RNA structure and sequence design

RhoFold+ integrates an RNA language model with geometric structure learning. Sequence representations, MSA information, and recycled structural features are decoded into three-dimensional RNA coordinates.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-27.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RhoFold plus RNA language model and three dimensional structure prediction." caption="RhoFold+ combines sequence pretraining with a geometric RNA structure module. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 27." %}

RNAFlow addresses co-design: generate an RNA sequence and structure that bind a target protein. Inverse-folding-based flow matching couples the discrete nucleotide choices with a geometric RNA conformation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-28.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RNAFlow protein conditioned RNA sequence structure co-design." caption="RNAFlow co-designs aptamer sequence and structure under a protein-binding condition. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 28." %}

## Toward virtual cells

A virtual cell would combine multiple measurement modalities to simulate cellular behavior. The proposed system must represent cell state, model interventions and context, and connect molecular-scale components to observable cellular responses.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-29.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Roadmap and components for an AI virtual cell model." caption="A virtual-cell model must integrate heterogeneous measurements, interventions, and biological scales. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 29." %}

Geneformer pretrains on 30 million single-cell transcriptomes. Each cell is represented by ranked gene-expression tokens, and masked learning produces a representation for network-biology and perturbation tasks.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-30.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Geneformer ranked gene tokens and single cell transcriptome pretraining." caption="Geneformer turns a single-cell expression profile into a ranked gene sentence for masked pretraining. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 30." %}

scCello adds cell ontology to transcriptome foundation modeling. Its pretraining and classification components use hierarchical relations among cell types, so the representation is guided by a biological taxonomy rather than only by expression similarity.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-20/slide-31.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="scCello cell ontology guided transcriptome foundation model." caption="scCello incorporates hierarchical cell-type knowledge into transcriptome representation learning. Cropped from the 2025 GDL genomics lecture, slide 31." %}
