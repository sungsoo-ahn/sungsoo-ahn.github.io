## Geometric graphs, energies, and forces

A geometric graph attaches positions and features to an ordinary graph,

$$
\mathcal G=(\mathcal V,\mathcal E),
\qquad
\mathcal V=\{1,\ldots,n\},
\qquad
X=\{x_i\}_{i\in\mathcal V},
\qquad
H=\{h_i\}_{i\in\mathcal V}.
$$

The positions $$x_i\in\mathbb R^3$$ form a point cloud. In molecular applications, a vertex is an atom, $$h_i$$ records its chemical identity, and edges often connect pairs within a cutoff radius. The graph is therefore not only a bond diagram: it is a local computational graph constructed from the three-dimensional configuration.

Figure retained from slide 6 of the 2025 GDL lecture deck." %}

{% include figure.liquid path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/s06-aspirin-molecule.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Ball-and-stick model of aspirin." caption="A molecule supplies atom identities together with three-dimensional coordinates. Aspirin model retained from slide 6 of the lecture deck." %}

The same molecule may be translated, rotated, or reflected without changing most scalar properties. A transformation in the Euclidean group has the form

$$
x_i\longmapsto Rx_i+b,
\qquad R^\top R=I.
$$

The full group $$E(3)$$ includes rotations and reflections. The subgroup $$SE(3)$$ keeps only transformations with $$\det R=1$$, so it preserves handedness. A scalar energy should be invariant,

$$
\widehat E(RX+b)=\widehat E(X),
$$

whereas a vector field such as force should be equivariant,

$$
\widehat F_i(RX+b)=R\widehat F_i(X).
$$

The output type decides which transformation law the model must satisfy.

Molecular dynamics repeatedly evaluates a potential energy and the forces derived from it. A classical force-field decomposition separates four familiar contributions,

$$
E=E_{\mathrm{bondlen}}+E_{\mathrm{angle}}+E_{\mathrm{torsion}}+E_{\mathrm{non\mbox{-}bonded}}.
$$

The decomposition already hints at the geometric hierarchy developed later: a bond length is a two-atom quantity, a bond angle uses three atoms, and a torsion uses four.

Illustration retained from slide 10 of the lecture deck." %}

A learned model can predict an invariant energy and obtain forces by differentiation,

$$
\widehat F_i(X)=-\frac{\partial \widehat E(X)}{\partial x_i}.
$$

If $$\widehat E(RX+b)=\widehat E(X)$$, the chain rule makes this gradient equivariant. Energy and force supervision can be combined as

$$
\ell\!\left(\widehat E,(E,F_1,\ldots,F_n)\right)
=
\rho\lVert E-\widehat E\rVert_2^2
+\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
\left\lVert
F_i+\frac{\partial\widehat E}{\partial x_i}
\right\rVert_2^2.
$$

The coefficient $$\rho$$ balances one energy label against the many force components. The scalar architectures in the first half of the lecture construct $$\widehat E$$ from invariant geometry; the vector architectures in the second half retain directional hidden states while still producing the required output type.

## Scalarization and continuous-filter convolution

Scalarization converts coordinate-dependent quantities into invariants before learning. The lecture follows a sequence of increasingly informative choices: [SchNet](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2017/hash/303ed4c69846ab36c2904d3ba8573050-Abstract.html) uses distances, [DimeNet](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=B1eWbxStPH) adds bond angles, [SphereNet](https://openreview.net/forum?id=givsRXsOt9r) adds torsions through spherical coordinates, and [ComENet](https://openreview.net/forum?id=mCzMqeWSFJ) constructs a complete two-hop description more efficiently.

{% include figure.liquid path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/s13-scalarized-geometry.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Diagrams defining a bond length, bond angle, and dihedral angle." caption="Distances, bond angles, and dihedral angles are invariant under rigid translation and rotation. Geometry illustration retained from slide 13 of the lecture deck." %}

A distance $$d_{ij}=\lVert x_i-x_j\rVert$$ describes two-body geometry. An angle $$\angle kij$$ describes the relation between two bonds meeting at $$i$$. A dihedral describes how two planes rotate around their shared bond. Each step exposes information unavailable from the previous scalar alone.

### SchNet: radial information

SchNet initializes an atom feature from its element type and uses the interatomic distance as an edge feature. Its continuous-filter convolution is

$$
h_i^{(\ell+1)}
=\left(h^{(\ell)}*W^{(\ell)}\right)_i
=\sum_{j\in\mathcal N(i)}
h_j^{(\ell)}\odot
W^{(\ell)}\!\left(\lVert x_i-x_j\rVert\right),
$$

where $$\odot$$ is element-wise multiplication. A radial basis expansion feeds the distance to the filter network $$W^{(\ell)}$$. Unlike a discrete image convolution, whose filter is indexed by a finite pixel offset, SchNet evaluates a learned filter at a continuous relative distance.

{% include figure.liquid path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/s14-continuous-filter.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Comparison between a discrete pixel filter and a continuous filter around atom positions." caption="A grid convolution chooses weights from discrete offsets, whereas a molecular continuous filter varies smoothly with atom position. Figure retained from slide 14 of the lecture deck." %}

Distances are invariant under $$E(3)$$, so every scalar hidden state produced by this update is invariant. SchNet can sum atom-wise outputs to obtain an extensive energy. Differentiating that energy supplies equivariant forces without requiring vector hidden features.

## Directional message passing

Distances cannot expose the angular term in the force-field decomposition. DimeNet therefore stores hidden states on directed edges. A message $$m_{ji}$$ points from $$j$$ to $$i$$, and it is updated by messages $$m_{kj}$$ entering its source atom $$j$$:

$$
m_{ji}^{(\ell+1)}
=f_{\mathrm{update}}\!\left(
m_{ji}^{(\ell)},
\sum_{k\in\mathcal N(j)\setminus\{i\}}
f_{\mathrm{int}}\!\left(
m_{kj}^{(\ell)},
e_{\mathrm{RBF}}^{(ji)},
a_{\mathrm{SBF}}^{(kj,ji)}
\right)
\right).
$$

The radial feature $$e_{\mathrm{RBF}}^{(ji)}$$ describes the length of $$j\to i$$. The spherical feature $$a_{\mathrm{SBF}}^{(kj,ji)}$$ describes the angle between $$k\to j$$ and $$j\to i$$.

{% include figure.liquid path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/s19-directional-aggregation.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A directed message entering an atom and aggregating messages from three neighboring directed bonds." caption="DimeNet updates a directed edge state by aggregating the other directed messages entering its source atom. Method diagram retained from slide 19 of the lecture deck." %}

The basis construction starts from the Schrödinger equation for a central potential,

$$
\left(-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2+V(d)\right)\Psi(d)=E\Psi(d).
$$

In spherical coordinates, separation of variables gives radial spherical Bessel functions and angular spherical harmonics,

$$
\Psi(d,\alpha,\varphi)
=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}
\left(a_{\ell m}j_\ell(kd)+b_{\ell m}y_\ell(kd)\right)
Y_\ell^m(\alpha,\varphi).
$$

DimeNet uses the rotationally invariant $$m=0$$ angular functions together with radial eigenfunctions inside a cutoff $$c$$. Before applying a smooth envelope, the Fourier--Bessel basis and radial basis are

$$
\widetilde a_{\mathrm{SBF},\ell n}(d,\alpha)
=
\sqrt{\frac{2}{c^3j_{\ell+1}^2(z_{\ell n})}}
j_\ell\!\left(\frac{z_{\ell n}d}{c}\right)
Y_\ell^0(\alpha),
$$

$$
\widetilde e_{\mathrm{RBF},n}(d)
=\sqrt{\frac{2}{c}}
\frac{\sin(n\pi d/c)}{d}.
$$

The polynomial envelope

$$
u(q)=1-\frac{(p+1)(p+2)}2q^p
+p(p+2)q^{p+1}
-\frac{p(p+1)}2q^{p+2},
\qquad q=\frac dc,
$$

produces $$a_{\mathrm{RBF}}=u\widetilde a_{\mathrm{RBF}}$$ and $$e_{\mathrm{RBF}}=u\widetilde e_{\mathrm{RBF}}$$. The cutoff is not only a sparsity device: the envelope controls smoothness as an atom crosses the neighbor boundary.

{% include figure.liquid path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/s22-radial-bessel-basis.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Five oscillating radial Bessel basis functions that vanish at the cutoff." caption="Radial Bessel functions resolve different distance frequencies inside the cutoff. ## Complete scalar geometry from local frames

Distance and bond angle still leave a rotation around a bond unresolved. The lecture next moves from directional triplets to torsional four-body information, then asks whether the resulting scalar description is complete.

The H$$_2$$O$$_2$$ example makes the missing degree of freedom explicit. The three bond lengths and two bond angles can stay fixed while one O--H group rotates about the O--O bond. The dihedral $$\varphi$$ distinguishes those conformations.

### SphereNet: spherical message passing

SphereNet updates an atom by aggregating over two-hop neighbors. For an edge $$e_k=(s_k,r_k)$$, the edge direction defines the local $$z$$-axis. A neighboring atom $$o$$ fixes a reference plane. Each two-hop atom $$q$$ then contributes a distance, polar angle, and torsion relative to that frame.

{% include figure.liquid path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/s26-spherical-message-passing.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Spherical message passing over a two-hop molecular neighborhood with radius, angle, and torsion labels." caption="SphereNet associates each two-hop interaction with a radius, polar angle, and torsion in an edge-local frame. Method diagram retained from slide 26 of the lecture deck." %}

The sequence across the source slides is constructive. First, the edge $$e_k$$ fixes the axis. Second, atom $$o$$ fixes the zero-torsion plane. Third, each $$q$$ receives a torsion $$\varphi_q$$ measured clockwise from that plane. These coordinates condition the edge update,

$$
e_k'
=\phi^e\!\left(
e_k,v_{r_k},v_{s_k},E_{s_k},
\rho^{p\to e}\!\left(\{r_h\}_{h\in\{r_k,s_k\}\cup\mathcal N'_{r_k}}\right)
\right),
$$

followed by node and graph updates,

$$
v_i'=\phi^v\!\left(v_i,\rho^{e\to v}(E_i)\right),
\qquad
u'=\phi^u\!\left(u,\rho^{v\to u}(V')\right).
$$

SphereNet is more discriminating than a distance--angle construction, but the lecture explicitly records a counterexample to completeness. Two structures may share the same edge-local spherical coordinates even when they are not related by the allowed rigid transformation.

### ComENet: a complete and efficient local frame

ComENet starts from an explicit definition. For two geometric graphs $$G_1=(V,A,P_1)$$ and $$G_2=(V,A,P_2)$$, a geometric representation $$T$$ is complete when

$$
T(G_1)=T(G_2)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists R\in SE(3)\text{ such that }P_1=R(P_2).
$$

The construction targets a two-hop local structure. For every directed edge $$e_{ij}$$, the nearest neighbor of $$i$$ other than $$j$$ and the nearest neighbor of $$j$$ other than $$i$$ select reference directions. Their relative planes define a torsion $$\tau_{ij}$$. The representation also includes the distance $$d_{ij}$$ and local angles $$\theta_{ij}$$ and $$\varphi_{ij}$$.

{% include figure.liquid path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/s35-comenet-local-structure.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A two-hop molecular neighborhood and a local edge rotation angle construction." caption="ComENet selects local reference neighbors to describe a complete two-hop structure around an edge. Method diagram retained from slide 35 of the lecture deck." %}

{% include figure.liquid path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/s36-comenet-edge-torsion.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Atoms around an edge with an orange reference plane and torsion angle tau." caption="The edge-local torsion compares the planes selected at the two endpoints. Method diagram retained from slide 36 of the lecture deck." %}

After associating a torsion with each edge, ComENet uses a DimeNet-like directional update,

$$
v_i'
=g\!\left(
v_i,
\sum_{j\in\mathcal N(i)}
f(v_j,d_{ij},\theta_{ij},\varphi_{ij},\tau_{ij})
\right).
$$

With at most $$k$$ neighbors per atom, the stated complexity is $$O(nk)$$ rather than enumerating every local triplet or quadruplet independently.

### GemNet: universality through directional interactions

[GemNet](https://openreview.net/forum?id=HS_sOaxS9K-) combines Fourier--Bessel radial functions and spherical harmonics with directed-edge and two-hop interactions. Its diagram attaches angles to paths around a central edge and includes a rotation around the ordered pair $$(a,b)$$.

{% include figure.liquid path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/s42-gemnet-interaction.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GemNet directional interaction around a central directed edge with several bond angles and a rotation." caption="GemNet's two-hop interaction pattern exposes the directional relations needed to reconstruct equivariant outputs from invariant coefficients. Method diagram retained from slide 42 of the lecture deck." %}

The lecture states the accompanying universality result in terms of the output representation. Let

$$
h:\mathbb R^{d\times n+h\times n}\longrightarrow\mathbb R^{d\times n}
$$

be permutation and rotation equivariant and translation invariant. For atom $$a$$, suppose the relative vectors $$\{x_{ca}:c\in[1,n]\}$$ do not lie in a $$(d-1)$$-dimensional subspace. Then there exist scalar functions $$f_a^{(c)}$$ such that

$$
h_a(X,H)=\sum_{\substack{c=1\\c\ne a}}^n
f_a^{(c)}(X,H)x_{ca}.
$$

Each coefficient is rotation and translation invariant and permutation equivariant over atom labels. The theorem explains how invariant directional message passing can still produce an equivariant vector: invariant scalars weight a spanning set of relative vectors.

## Vector-based equivariant models

The second half changes the representation strategy. Instead of converting every geometric input to scalars, [EGNN](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/satorras21a.html) updates coordinates, [PaiNN](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/schutt21a.html) maintains scalar and vector channels, and [TorchMD-Net](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02541) builds an equivariant Transformer from both.

### EGNN: coordinate equivariance

The simplest equivariant ingredient is a relative vector. Under $$x_i\mapsto Rx_i+b$$,

$$
x_i-x_j\longmapsto R(x_i-x_j).
$$

Multiplying by an invariant scalar keeps the same transformation law. EGNN uses this fact in a three-stage layer:

$$
m_{ij}
=\phi_e\!\left(
h_i^{(\ell)},h_j^{(\ell)},
\lVert x_i^{(\ell)}-x_j^{(\ell)}\rVert^2,
a_{ij}
\right),
$$

$$
x_i^{(\ell+1)}
=x_i^{(\ell)}
+C\sum_{j\ne i}
\left(x_i^{(\ell)}-x_j^{(\ell)}\right)
\phi_x(m_{ij}),
$$

$$
m_i=\sum_{j\ne i}m_{ij},
\qquad
h_i^{(\ell+1)}=\phi_h\!\left(h_i^{(\ell)},m_i\right).
$$

The edge message is invariant because it sees scalar features and squared distances. The coordinate displacement is equivariant because it is a sum of relative vectors with invariant coefficients. The node features remain invariant.

The source emphasizes the simplicity of this construction. It does not give the best QM9 result in every column, but it obtains competitive errors without spherical harmonics or higher-order representation types.

### PaiNN: scalar and vector channels

PaiNN keeps two feature types at every atom:

$$
s_i=[s_i^m,s_i^u],
\qquad
v_i=[v_i^m,v_i^u].
$$

The scalar channels are invariant. Every vector channel transforms as $$v_i\mapsto Rv_i$$. Safe operations include arbitrary nonlinear functions of scalars, scalar--vector multiplication, linear mixing of vector channels, vector norms and inner products, vector cross products for $$SO(3)$$, and gradients of invariant scalar functions.

The lecture motivates this choice by computational access. A distance-only update can resolve charge-like pair information at cost $$O(|\mathcal N|)$$ but cannot directly resolve an angle. Explicit angle enumeration costs $$O(|\mathcal N|^2)$$. Summing unit directions costs $$O(|\mathcal N|)$$ and preserves enough directional information to distinguish the illustrated environments.

| Geometric input | Local aggregation | Cost | Resolves charge-like pair? | Resolves angle? |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Distances | $$\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}\lVert r_{ij}\rVert$$ | $$O(|\mathcal N|)$$ | No | No |
| Angles | $$\sum_j\sum_k\alpha_{jik}$$ | $$O(|\mathcal N|^2)$$ | No | Yes |
| Directions | $$\sum_{j\in\mathcal N_i}r_{ij}/\lVert r_{ij}\rVert$$ | $$O(|\mathcal N|)$$ | Yes | Yes |

{% include figure.liquid path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/s52-angle-direction-ambiguity.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Two local molecular environments with the same angle-based representation but different directional sums." caption="Angle multisets can coincide while the vector sum of bond directions differs, letting vector channels distinguish the two neighborhoods. Figure retained from slide 52 of the lecture deck." %}

PaiNN alternates an equivariant message block and an atom-wise mixing block. Its message update is

$$
\Delta s_i^m
=\sum_j \phi_s(s_j)\odot W_s(\lVert r_{ij}\rVert),
$$

$$
\Delta v_i^m
=\sum_j v_j\odot\phi_{vv}(s_j)\odot W_{vv}(\lVert r_{ij}\rVert)
+\sum_j\phi_{vs}(s_j)\odot
\nabla_{r_{ij}}W_{vs}(\lVert r_{ij}\rVert).
$$

The first vector term transports existing vector features. The second creates a vector along the pair direction through the radial gradient. The update block then transfers information between types,

$$
\Delta s_i^u
=a_{ss}\!\left(s_i,\lVert Vv_i\rVert\right)
+a_{sv}\!\left(s_i,\lVert Vv_i\rVert\right)
\langle Uv_i,Vv_i\rangle,
$$

$$
\Delta v_i^u
=a_{vv}\!\left(s_i,\lVert Vv_i\rVert\right)Uv_i.
$$

Norms and inner products are invariant, while multiplying a vector by an invariant gate is equivariant.

### TorchMD-Net: equivariant attention

TorchMD-Net asks how to extend these scalar--vector updates with attention. Its first idea is to make attention an invariant, scalar-conditioned nonlinear function. Its second is to inject the bond distance through radial features.

The equivariant update combines attention-weighted vector features, unit edge directions, and a self-interaction:

$$
w_i
=\sum_j s_{ij}^{1}\odot v_j
+s_{ij}^{2}\odot\frac{r_i-r_j}{\lVert r_i-r_j\rVert},
$$

$$
\Delta v_i=w_i+s_i^3\odot U_3v_i,
\qquad
\Delta x_i=q_i^1+q_i^2\odot\langle U_1v_i,U_2v_i\rangle.
$$

The coefficients $$s_{ij}^{1}$$, $$s_{ij}^{2}$$, $$s_i^3$$, $$q_i^1$$, and $$q_i^2$$ are scalars. Vector sums and scalar-gated vector channels therefore remain equivariant; the inner product is invariant and can update scalar features.

{% include figure.liquid path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/s58-torchmd-update.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="TorchMD-Net block updating scalar and vector features with attention, vector mixing, and inner products." caption="TorchMD-Net couples scalar attention, neighbor vectors, bond directions, and vector inner products in one equivariant update. Architecture diagram retained from slide 58 of the lecture deck." %}

The attention block first filters radial-basis features,

$$
D_{ij}^{K}
=\sigma\!\left(W^{D^K}e^{\mathrm{RBF}}(d_{ij})+b^{D^K}\right),
$$

then forms query and key projections and replaces softmax normalization with a cutoff-modulated SiLU score,

$$
Q_i=W^Qx_i,
\qquad
K_j=W^Kx_j,
$$

$$
A_{ij}
=\operatorname{SiLU}\!\left(
\operatorname{dot}(Q_i,K_j,D_{ij}^{K})
\right)\phi(d_{ij}).
$$

The lecture's slide writes the key projection with the same node index as the query; the neighbor-indexed form above makes the intended pairwise attention explicit. The cutoff $$\phi(d_{ij})$$ keeps the interaction local.

{% include figure.liquid path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/s59-torchmd-attention.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="TorchMD-Net attention block with query, key, radial filter, cutoff, and scalar-vector value channels." caption="Distance-filtered keys modify an invariant attention score before scalar and vector values are aggregated. Architecture diagram retained from slide 59 of the lecture deck." %}

The lecture's conclusion is specific: TorchMD-Net improves over EGNN and PaiNN on many reported targets, but does so at a slower running time. Accuracy, geometric sufficiency, and efficiency remain separate axes.

The closing slide points to three neighboring designs rather than treating this list as exhaustive. [Vector Neurons](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/html/Deng_Vector_Neurons_A_General_Framework_for_SO3-Equivariant_Networks_ICCV_2021_paper.html) extends scalar neurons to three-dimensional vectors for point-cloud networks. GemNet uses two-hop directional interactions to recognize torsion. NewtonNet organizes its message passing around Newtonian energy and force relations.

The complete lecture sequence is therefore not a binary choice between invariant and equivariant networks. SchNet exposes pair distances; DimeNet exposes triplet angles; SphereNet and ComENet expose torsion through local frames; GemNet connects invariant coefficients to universal equivariant outputs; EGNN moves coordinates; PaiNN propagates vector channels; TorchMD-Net makes those channels interact through attention. Each architecture changes the geometric information that is available to the learner, the cost of accessing it, and the outputs whose symmetry is built into the model.
