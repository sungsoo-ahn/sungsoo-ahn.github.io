## Geometric graphs, energies, and forces

A geometric graph augments vertices and edges with positions $$R\in\mathbb R^{n\times3}$$. Molecular graphs are the central example: atom type and bond information define graph features, while coordinates define a three-dimensional configuration.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-6.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Molecule represented as a graph with three-dimensional coordinates." caption="A geometric graph combines relational structure with coordinates that transform under rigid motion. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 6." %}

Geometric GNNs are required to respect translations, rotations, and often reflections. Invariant scalar outputs remain unchanged, whereas vector outputs such as forces rotate with the input.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-7.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Recap of invariant and equivariant molecular predictions." caption="The output type determines whether a geometric model should be invariant or equivariant. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 7." %}

Molecular property prediction often targets experimentally measured or quantum-chemically computed scalars such as energy, dipole-related quantities, or thermochemical properties. Those targets should not depend on how the molecule is placed in space.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-8.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Molecular property prediction targets from QM9." caption="Many molecular benchmarks pair a three-dimensional structure with invariant scalar properties. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 8." %}

Molecular dynamics requires forces in addition to energy. A machine-learned potential replaces repeated electronic-structure calculations with a learned energy surface and its force field.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-9.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Machine-learned potential used for molecular dynamics." caption="Molecular simulation evaluates a potential energy and forces repeatedly along a trajectory. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 9." %}

The energy is commonly decomposed into atom-wise contributions, while forces are obtained as coordinate derivatives,

$$
E(R)=\sum_i E_i(R),
\qquad
F_i=-\nabla_{r_i}E(R).
$$

An invariant differentiable energy yields a rotation-equivariant force field.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-10.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Atomic energy decomposition and force gradients." caption="Energy decomposition and differentiation connect invariant property models to equivariant molecular dynamics. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 10." %}

## Scalarization and continuous-filter convolution

The scalar-based family makes the geometric inputs invariant before learning. Distances, bond angles, and torsion or dihedral angles are unchanged by rigid rotations and translations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-12.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Overview of scalar-based geometric GNN architectures." caption="Scalar geometric GNNs differ mainly in which invariant geometric relations they construct and how they enter message passing. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 12." %}

Distances summarize two-body geometry, angles summarize three-body geometry, and dihedrals summarize four-body orientation. Each additional invariant can resolve configurations that the lower-order variables collapse.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-13.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Distance, angle, and torsion scalarization of molecular geometry." caption="Invariant scalarization converts geometric relations into rotation-independent model inputs. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 13." %}

### SchNet: radial information

SchNet uses continuous filters generated from interatomic distance. A message from atom $$j$$ to $$i$$ has the form

$$
m_{ij}=h_j\odot W(r_{ij}),
$$

where a radial network maps the scalar distance to a feature-wise filter.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-14.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SchNet continuous-filter convolution architecture." caption="SchNet conditions messages on a learned continuous function of interatomic distance. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 14." %}

The distance is expanded in radial basis functions before entering the filter network, giving the model a smooth and localized representation of radius.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-15.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SchNet radial basis expansion and continuous filter." caption="Radial basis expansion turns one distance into a smooth bank of localized scalar features. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 15." %}

Because messages depend only on distances, reflected and rotated structures produce the same scalar features. Atom-wise outputs can be summed for an extensive energy.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-16.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SchNet invariant energy and gradient force construction." caption="A distance-based invariant energy can supply equivariant forces through coordinate gradients. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 16." %}

SchNet established strong results on molecular property and materials benchmarks, demonstrating that learned radial filters can replace hand-designed atom-centered descriptors.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-17.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SchNet molecular property benchmark results." caption="SchNet's experiments established continuous-filter message passing as a practical molecular architecture. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 17." %}

## Directional message passing

Distances alone do not determine the angle between two bonds. DimeNet augments messages with triplet geometry so that the interaction around a central atom depends on both radius and direction.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-18.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DimeNet using distances and bond angles." caption="DimeNet resolves directional geometry by adding angles between neighboring bonds. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 18." %}

Its message graph is a line graph: each directed molecular bond becomes a node, and adjacent directed bonds define a triplet interaction. Messages are therefore updated from other messages.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-19.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DimeNet line-graph message passing over directed bonds." caption="Directional message passing lifts atom edges into states that interact through bond triplets. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 19." %}

The radial part uses spherical Bessel functions inside a cutoff, while the angular part uses spherical harmonics. Products of these basis functions provide a structured expansion of triplet geometry.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-20.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DimeNet radial Bessel and spherical harmonic basis functions." caption="DimeNet separates radial and angular dependence through analytic basis expansions. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 20." %}

The basis is motivated by solutions of the Schrödinger equation in a spherical domain, yielding orthogonal radial and angular functions with controlled cutoff behavior.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-21.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Spherical-coordinate decomposition underlying the DimeNet basis." caption="The directional basis inherits its radial--angular separation from spherical eigenfunctions. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 21." %}

An envelope function makes the basis and selected derivatives vanish smoothly at the cutoff, avoiding force discontinuities when neighbors enter or leave the local graph.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-22.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DimeNet smooth cutoff envelope." caption="A smooth envelope controls boundary regularity of the radial filter and derived forces. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 22." %}

The resulting spherical Bessel and radial basis functions form the fixed geometric features supplied to the learned interaction blocks.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-23.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DimeNet basis functions visualized across radius and angle." caption="Analytic basis functions expose radius and angle to the learned directional update. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 23." %}

DimeNet's benchmark tables show substantial improvements over distance-only models on QM9 and molecular-dynamics targets, at higher geometric and computational complexity.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-24.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DimeNet molecular benchmark results." caption="Directional triplet information improves accuracy on several molecular properties and forces. ## Complete scalar geometry from local frames

SphereNet adds torsional information. Distances and bond angles do not always specify a local three-dimensional arrangement; a dihedral can distinguish rotations about a bond.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-25.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SphereNet motivating torsion beyond distance and angle." caption="SphereNet introduces four-body orientation to resolve configurations that share radial and angular features. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 25." %}

The method constructs a spherical coordinate system around each message interaction and represents distance, polar angle, and azimuthal or torsion angle.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-26.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SphereNet spherical coordinate system around molecular bonds." caption="A local spherical frame converts nearby three-dimensional geometry into invariant coordinates. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 26." %}

Choosing a reference plane is necessary to define the torsion. Neighboring atoms determine that plane, making the coordinate system local to the molecular graph.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-27.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Reference plane construction for SphereNet torsion." caption="A neighboring bond fixes the azimuthal reference needed to define a local torsion. The spherical coordinates give a complete local description under the assumptions of the construction: radius locates the shell, angle selects a cone, and torsion selects a point around that cone.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-28.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Radius and polar angle narrowing a local molecular position." caption="Radius and polar angle constrain a neighbor to a circle before torsion resolves its location. ## Vector-based equivariant models

The second part keeps vector information inside the network. EGNN updates coordinates, PaiNN maintains scalar and vector channels, and TorchMD-Net combines equivariant features with Transformer-style interaction.

### EGNN: coordinate equivariance

EGNN constructs invariant edge messages from scalar features and squared distances, then updates coordinates with relative vectors:

$$
x_i' = x_i + \sum_{j\ne i}(x_i-x_j)\phi_x(m_{ij}).
$$

Because scalar coefficients are invariant and relative vectors rotate, the coordinate update is equivariant.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-48.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="EGNN invariant message and equivariant coordinate update." caption="EGNN obtains an equivariant displacement by weighting relative position vectors with invariant learned scalars. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 48." %}

The full block updates edge messages, coordinates, and scalar node states while remaining equivariant to Euclidean transformations and permutations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-49.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Full EGNN layer equations." caption="An EGNN layer alternates invariant message computation with equivariant coordinate motion and scalar feature updates. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 49." %}

Experiments cover physical systems, graph tasks, and molecular data, demonstrating a relatively simple coordinate-equivariant design.

### PaiNN: scalar and vector channels

PaiNN maintains scalar features $$s_i$$ and vector features $$V_i$$. Scalars are invariant; every vector channel transforms by the same rotation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-51.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="PaiNN scalar and vector feature types." caption="PaiNN assigns explicit transformation laws to invariant scalar and equivariant vector channels. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 51." %}

The physical motivation is polarizability: local fields can reorient vector responses even when scalar atom types and distances remain unchanged.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-52.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="PaiNN polarizable interaction motivation and molecular dipoles." caption="Vector channels represent directional responses such as local polarization and dipole-related effects. Cropped from the 2025 GDL geometric-GNN lecture, slide 52." %}

PaiNN alternates an equivariant message block and an atom-wise mixing block. Radial filters generate scalar coefficients, unit direction vectors create geometric vector messages, and vector norms provide invariant inputs to scalar updates.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-07/slide-53.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="PaiNN message and mixing blocks." caption="PaiNN couples scalar and vector channels through operations with known rotation behavior.
