## Protein representation learning

A protein representation should compress a sequence or structure while retaining information useful for function, fitness, interaction, and design. That objective is broader than predicting one label: the representation is intended to transfer across tasks.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s03-image2.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein representation learning connected to sequence, structure, function, and downstream tasks." caption="Protein representation learning seeks a reusable encoding that connects several biological views and tasks. Lecture synthesis by the author." %}

The PEER benchmark organizes protein understanding into five families rather than one generic score. Function tasks include fluorescence, stability, solubility, thermostability, and enzyme activity. Localization tasks ask where a protein resides or whether it is membrane-bound. Structure tasks include secondary structure, contacts, and fold classification. Interaction tasks cover both protein--protein and protein--ligand binding. Fitness tasks measure how mutations change a phenotype such as capsid production or binding.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s04-image3.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="PEER multi-task benchmark for protein sequence understanding." caption="PEER evaluates whether one learned representation transfers across heterogeneous protein-understanding tasks. Source: [Xu et al., 2022](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/e467582d42d9c13fa9603df16f31de6d-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html)." %}

Fitness prediction is a particularly important application. A fitness landscape maps sequence mutations to changes in a measured function, creating a surface over an enormous discrete sequence space.

### Protein language models

Protein language models treat amino acids as tokens and train Transformer encoders by predicting masked or next residues. The objective supplies supervision from unlabeled sequence databases: context must reveal which residues are compatible with a family, fold, or functional constraint.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s06-image5.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Transformer protein language model trained by masked amino-acid prediction." caption="Protein language modeling learns contextual residue embeddings from sequence reconstruction. Lecture synthesis by the author." %}

The learned features support secondary- and tertiary-structure prediction, functional annotation, protein-family classification, and variant-effect prediction. A fitness model can therefore reuse an embedding learned without the task's experimental labels.

[ESM](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2016239118) demonstrates strong gains as protein language models and their training corpora scale. The lecture compares unsupervised long-range contact precision along two axes: the number of homologues found for a sequence and the number of model parameters. More evolutionary coverage helps, but larger sequence models also recover more contact information. ESM embeddings then supply the single-sequence representation used by [ESMFold](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ade2574) for three-dimensional structure prediction.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s08-image6.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ESM masked-language-model architecture for protein sequences." caption="ESM learns protein sequence representations through masked amino-acid recovery. Source: [Rives et al., 2021](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2016239118)." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s08-image7.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Scaling curves for ESM protein language models." caption="ESM reports systematic improvements as model scale increases. Source: [Rives et al., 2021](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2016239118)." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s08-image8.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ESMFold structure prediction from ESM sequence embeddings." caption="ESMFold converts pretrained sequence representations into three-dimensional structures. Source: [Lin et al., 2023](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ade2574)." %}

[DPLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18567) changes the objective from one-step masking to discrete diffusion. Different timesteps correspond to different masking ratios, and the model learns to denoise sequences across this corruption schedule. At low corruption, the hidden states support predictive downstream tasks. At high corruption, the reverse process starts from an almost fully masked chain and generates a sequence iteratively. The same model therefore serves representation learning and unconditional generation without changing the token space.

Source: [Wang et al., 2024](https://openreview.net/forum?id=NUAbSFqyqb)." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s09-image10.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DPLM architecture and downstream representation and generation tasks." caption="A discrete diffusion language model supports both protein understanding and generation. Source: [Wang et al., 2024](https://openreview.net/forum?id=NUAbSFqyqb)." %}

An [MSA Transformer](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/rao21a.html) models a protein family rather than one sequence. Axial attention respects the two alignment axes: row attention captures dependencies within a protein, while column attention compares homologous residues across proteins. Its long-range contact benchmark varies MSA depth from about ten to ten thousand sequences. The comparison asks whether attention across homologues recovers residue pairs that are distant in sequence but close after folding.

### Structure encoders

Sequence is only one view. Structure often determines how a protein binds or catalyzes, and geometric representations can expose this information directly.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s11-image14.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Several protein structures associated with different structural tasks." caption="Structure encoders support tasks such as fold classification, interface prediction, docking, and antibody modeling. The exact montage origin is unclear." %}

A protein cannot be treated as an unordered point cloud without losing the residue arrangement along the chain. The same occupied volume may conceal very different backbone connectivity.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s12-image15.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein surface or point cloud contrasted with an ordered ribbon backbone." caption="Pure point-cloud geometry can discard the sequential and relational organization of a protein chain. The exact visual origin is unclear." %}

[GearNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06125) constructs a multi-relational residue graph. Sequential edges follow the backbone in both directions; radius edges connect spatial neighbors; and k-nearest-neighbor edges provide another geometric relation. Message passing can distinguish these edge types rather than collapsing them into one adjacency. A residue adjacent along the chain and one nearby only after folding therefore enter through different relations.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s13-image16.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GEAR multi-relational protein graph with sequential, radius, and nearest-neighbor edges." caption="GEAR preserves both chain order and spatial neighborhood through typed residue relations. Source: [Zhang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06125)." %}

Geometric pretraining follows a three-stage construction. GearNet first builds the relational graph, then creates correlated views through operations such as subspace cropping, and finally aligns the representations of those views. The encoder must retain the shared protein geometry even though each view hides part of the original structure. This is the pretraining mechanism shown in the lecture; it is not a masked-distance or angle-recovery objective.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s14-image17.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GEAR geometric pretraining tasks over residues, distances, and angles." caption="GEAR pretraining combines residue recovery with geometric relation prediction. Source: [Zhang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06125)." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s14-image18.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein graph pretraining results and learned structural representations." caption="The geometric objectives improve transfer to protein-level and residue-level tasks. Source: [Zhang et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06125)." %}

### Multimodal sequence--structure pretraining

Sequence and structure encoders can be trained jointly. The lecture compares three fusion patterns. **Serial fusion** passes the compressed output of one encoder into the other. **Parallel fusion** preserves separate pretrained encoders and combines their final representations. **Cross fusion** exchanges information inside the two stacks, allowing residue-level agreement to affect both modalities before pooling. These designs differ in where information can interact, not merely in how two feature vectors are concatenated.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s15-image19.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Joint protein sequence and structure representation-learning architectures." caption="Multimodal protein pretraining aligns sequence encoders with geometric structure encoders. Source: [Tan et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.06275)." %}

DiffPreT uses a Siamese objective over sequence and structure diffusion trajectories. Conformer simulation supplies related structural views. Corruption then produces paired trajectories, and mutual denoising or trajectory prediction maximizes the information shared between them. The representation must preserve protein identity while remaining sensitive to conformational changes that are consistent across the paired views.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s16-diffpret-trajectories.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Sequence and structure diffusion trajectories used for protein encoder pretraining." caption="[DiffPreT](https://openreview.net/forum?id=tIzbNQko3c) corrupts residue identities and coordinates jointly so that denoising requires both chemical and geometric context." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s16-diffpret-framework.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Siamese diffusion trajectory prediction between correlated protein conformers." caption="SiamDiff mutually denoises trajectories from correlated conformers to make the representation sensitive to shared protein identity without collapsing conformational variation. Source: [Zhang et al., 2023](https://openreview.net/forum?id=tIzbNQko3c)." %}

[SaProt](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/1c42513b8895ab11fbbb5b7e8e6b6b02-Abstract-Conference.html) offers a simpler fusion interface. Foldseek supplies twenty discrete structural tokens, and each amino acid is paired with one of them. The resulting vocabulary represents both residue identity and its local structural state. The language model can therefore read geometry with the same token-based machinery used for sequence.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s17-image22.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SaProt sequence tokens paired with Foldseek-derived structural tokens." caption="SaProt extends the protein vocabulary by pairing each amino acid with a discrete local-structure token derived from Foldseek. Source: [Su et al., 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/1c42513b8895ab11fbbb5b7e8e6b6b02-Abstract-Conference.html)." %}

[DPLM-2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13782) uses discrete masked diffusion for both sequence and structural tokens. A structure tokenizer and lookup-free quantizer convert three-dimensional geometry into a discrete stream aligned with amino acids. Joint masking and denoising then support sequence-only, structure-only, and cross-modal conditional generation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s18-image23.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DPLM-2 multimodal discrete diffusion over protein sequence and structure tokens." caption="DPLM-2 jointly corrupts and denoises sequence and structural token streams. Source: [Wang et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13782)." %}

[ESM-3](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ads0018) pushes tokenization further: sequence, secondary structure, local three-dimensional structure, solvent-accessible surface area, and function keywords are represented as aligned tracks. Masked diffusion can condition on any observed subset and generate the remainder. The output remains a coupled object: a structure token is local geometry around one residue, not a free-standing coordinate prediction.

### Antibody affinity maturation and learned embeddings

An antibody contains heavy and light chains, each with a variable domain. Framework regions provide a conserved scaffold, while complementarity-determining regions form the antigen-binding surface.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s24-image33.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Antibody heavy and light chains with framework and complementarity-determining regions." caption="Antibody affinity design concentrates on variable-domain loops supported by a more conserved framework." %}

Affinity maturation asks how mutations to an antigen--antibody complex can improve binding. The model must represent interactions between two proteins, not only the isolated antibody. For a wild-type complex and a mutant complex, the target is the change of binding free energy

$$
\Delta\Delta G_{\mathrm{bind}}
=\Delta G_{\mathrm{bind}}^{(\mathrm{mut})}
-\Delta G_{\mathrm{bind}}^{(\mathrm{wt})}.
$$

A negative value indicates more favorable binding under this convention. The complex pose and side-chain conformation are therefore part of the input contract: a sequence mutation can also change the geometry of the interface.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s25-image34.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Antigen-antibody complex and affinity change under antibody mutations." caption="Affinity maturation predicts which antibody mutations improve interaction with a fixed antigen." %}

The [GearBind model](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-51563-8) uses multilevel GearNet message passing and contrastive learning. Negative examples come from random residue mutations and side-chain torsions sampled from a rotamer library. The contrastive task therefore changes both residue identity and plausible local conformation rather than treating every geometric perturbation as equivalent.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s26-image35.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Multilevel GearNet and contrastive negative sampling for antibody affinity maturation." caption="Contrastive pretraining teaches the encoder to separate native antigen--antibody interactions from perturbed alternatives." %}

The work is evaluated both in silico and in the wet lab. On the 5,729-example SKEMPI cross-validation set, the ensemble reports MAE $$1.028\pm0.080$$, RMSE $$1.503\pm0.101$$, Pearson correlation $$0.729\pm0.016$$, and Spearman correlation $$0.643\pm0.030$$. ## Protein design from sequence or structure

Protein design generates molecules with desired functions. The output may be a sequence, an unlabeled backbone structure, or both sequence and structure together.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s29-image41.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein design tasks spanning sequence design, structure design, and joint co-design." caption="Protein design can generate residue identities, backbone geometry, or both variables jointly." %}

### Sequence generation and conditional design

Protein sequences are available at a much larger scale than experimentally resolved structures, making sequence-only generative training comparatively accessible. The lecture schematic places sequence databases near 280 million records while the structure corpus remains far smaller. Depending on the conditioning input, a sequence generator may map a partial sequence or functional properties to a completed sequence, or map a structure to a sequence through inverse folding.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s30-image42.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein sequence data scale and several sequence-generation settings." caption="Sequence generators exploit large protein corpora and differ by which biological or structural variables they condition on." %}

Property-conditioned sequence design asks for sequences associated with a family, biological process, molecular function, or other annotation. [ProGen](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-022-01618-2) uses a 1.2-billion-parameter autoregressive Transformer. Given control tags $$\mathbf{c}$$ and residue prefix $$a_{<i}$$, it minimizes

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{AR}}
=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log p_\theta(a_i\mid a_{<i},\mathbf{c}).
$$

The model outputs a sequence biased toward the requested annotations. Folding and functional testing remain downstream steps; a control tag is not itself evidence that an individual sample has the function.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s31-image43.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Conditional protein sequence generator receiving family and function properties." caption="A property-conditioned generator translates functional or family labels into residue sequences." %}

In practice, many design campaigns follow the RFDiffusion pipeline: choose a target or motif, generate a backbone structure, design a compatible sequence, fold the sequence computationally, and validate the resulting candidate.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s32-image44.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Target-to-structure-to-sequence-to-validation protein design pipeline." caption="The common protein-design workflow separates backbone generation, sequence design, and structural validation." %}

### Inverse folding: structure to sequence

[ESM-IF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/hsu22a.html) predicts a sequence for a given backbone. Its training mixture contains roughly 12 million AlphaFold2-predicted structures and about 16,000 CATH structures. Span masking and coordinate noise regularize both sources. An invariant geometric vector perceptron encoder reads the backbone, and an autoregressive Transformer decoder generates residue identities conditioned on that geometry.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s33-image45.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ESM-IF inverse-folding architecture from protein backbone coordinates to an amino-acid sequence." caption="ESM-IF treats inverse folding as autoregressive sequence generation conditioned on a backbone." %}

[ProteinMPNN](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.add2187) uses residue-level message passing and autoregressive generation. Each node represents one residue, while directed edges encode relations between backbone atoms. The encoder alternates edge and node updates. A randomly ordered decoder then samples residue identities while exposing only already generated sequence context and the complete backbone geometry.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s34-image46.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ProteinMPNN graph message passing and autoregressive sequence design from a backbone." caption="ProteinMPNN communicates geometric context between residues before generating a backbone-compatible sequence." %}

[SurfPro](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/song24g.html) changes the structural input from backbone coordinates to a molecular surface. Local patches encode hydrophobicity, charge, polarity, shape, and perspective. A hierarchical encoder combines these local views with a global landscape representation before an autoregressive decoder generates the sequence.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s35-image47.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SurfPro protein surface encoder and sequence decoder." caption="SurfPro designs sequences from a protein surface, emphasizing the geometry exposed to solvent and binding partners." %}

### Backbone generation

Structure design generates an unlabeled backbone that can later be passed to an inverse-folding model. The motivation is that structure constrains function, so new backbone families may open functional regions that sequence-only generation rarely reaches.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s36-image48.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Unlabeled protein backbone generation followed by sequence design." caption="Backbone generators propose three-dimensional folds before residue labels are assigned." %}

[FrameDiff](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/yim23a.html) diffuses over residue rigid bodies in three dimensions. Residue $$n$$ is represented by

$$
T_n=(R_n,\mathbf{x}_n)\in\mathrm{SE}(3),
$$

where $$R_n\in\mathrm{SO}(3)$$ is a local orientation and $$\mathbf{x}_n\in\mathbb{R}^3$$ is the $$C_\alpha$$ position. Diffusion perturbs rotations and translations separately. The denoiser returns clean frames and torsion angles used to place atoms such as the backbone oxygen. This representation lowers the number of free coordinates while preserving ideal intrarezidue bond lengths and angles.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s37-image49.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="FrameDiff diffusion over residue rigid frames for protein backbone generation." caption="FrameDiff generates a backbone by denoising translations and rotations of residue frames." %}

[FrameFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05297) keeps the same frame state but replaces reverse diffusion with a deterministic flow. Translations follow

$$
\mathbf{x}_t=(1-t)\mathbf{x}_0+t\mathbf{x}_1,
$$

while rotations follow the geodesic interpolation

$$
R_t=\operatorname{Exp}_{R_0}\!\left(t\operatorname{Log}_{R_0}(R_1)\right).
$$

The lecture reports a five-fold reduction in sampling steps relative to FrameDiff. The table also shows why one scalar is insufficient: at 500 ODE steps FrameFlow reaches designability 0.81 and novelty 0.69, while at 10 steps designability falls to 0.33 as diversity rises to 0.54.

| Model | Process | Steps | Designability ↑ | Diversity ↑ | Novelty ↓ |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| GENIE | SDE | 1000 | 0.22 | 0.76 (131) | 0.54 |
| GENIE | SDE | 750 | 0.11 | 0.79 (60) | 0.51 |
| GENIE | SDE | 500 | 0.00 | — | — |
| FrameDiff | SDE | 500 | 0.42 | 0.36 (104) | 0.66 |
| FrameDiff | SDE | 100 | 0.39 | 0.32 (86) | 0.66 |
| FrameDiff | ODE | 100 | 0.26 | 0.43 (76) | 0.65 |
| FrameDiff | ODE | 10 | 0.16 | 0.53 (59) | 0.66 |
| FrameFlow | ODE | 500 | **0.81** | 0.22 (123) | 0.69 |
| FrameFlow | ODE | 100 | 0.77 | 0.28 (147) | 0.67 |
| FrameFlow | ODE | 10 | 0.33 | 0.54 (124) | 0.63 |

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s38-image50.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="FrameFlow rigid-body flow matching and protein-generation results." caption="FrameFlow transports residue frames through a learned continuous-time flow." %}

[Genie](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/lin23a.html) works directly with the $$C_\alpha$$ coordinates $$\mathbf{x}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{x}_N$$. An invariant encoder builds pair features, and an equivariant decoder predicts coordinate noise. The denoising loss is

$$
\mathcal{L}
=\mathbb{E}_{t,\mathbf{x}_0,\boldsymbol\epsilon}
\left[
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\left\lVert
\boldsymbol\epsilon_i
-\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t,t)_i
\right\rVert^2
\right].
$$

The lecture reports greater novelty and diversity than FrameDiff, together with more sampling steps because coordinate generation carries more free variables than a residue-frame state.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s39-image51.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Genie coordinate diffusion architecture for protein backbone generation." caption="Genie generates protein backbones by diffusion in coordinate space." %}

[Genie 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15489) retains coordinate diffusion and strengthens the denoiser to process motif representations. A functional motif, its residue identities, and a partial structural template can be fixed while the surrounding backbone is generated. The examples progress from single-motif to multiple-motif scaffolding, so conditional generation must satisfy several spatial constraints at once.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s40-image52.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Genie 2 architecture, scaling choices, and generated protein examples." caption="Genie 2 develops coordinate diffusion into a larger backbone-generation system." %}

[Chroma](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06728-8) generates all-atom structures from corrupted backbone heavy-atom coordinates. Its forward process uses correlated Gaussian noise,

$$
\mathbf{x}_t\sim
\mathcal{N}\!\left(\alpha_t\mathbf{x}_0,
(1-\alpha_t^2)RR^\top\right),
$$

where the covariance encodes polymer correlations rather than perturbing each atom independently. A long-range message-passing denoiser reconstructs the backbone, and a design network supplies the all-atom structure. The model can also condition on symmetry, substructures, shape, semantics, and other design constraints.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s41-image53.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Chroma protein generative model and conditionally generated structures." caption="Chroma provides an efficient coordinate generator with controls for protein design." %}

[RFdiffusion](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06415-8) perturbs residue frames and adapts RoseTTAFold into the denoising network. Self-conditioning feeds the previous clean-structure estimate into the next reverse step. The same denoiser supports unconditional backbones, symmetric oligomers, binder design, motif scaffolding, and symmetric scaffolding; each task differs through its conditioning information and validation criterion.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s42-image54.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RFDiffusion denoising architecture and several protein design tasks." caption="RFDiffusion reuses a pretrained folding architecture as a powerful conditional backbone denoiser." %}

[FoldFlow](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/618c95f4557c15b253fb0e6f548ea0c0-Abstract-Conference.html) highlights the importance of the probability path used in flow matching on protein manifolds. The base path uses a direct geometric interpolation. The Riemannian optimal-transport path uses a shorter and straighter coupling. Stochastic flow matching adds noise along the path for robustness in high dimensions. The lecture reports the highest designability for the optimal-transport path, while the stochastic path increases novelty and diversity.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s43-image55.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Alternative flow-matching paths between noisy and protein frame distributions in FoldFlow." caption="FoldFlow studies how path design affects generative transport over residue frames." %}

[FoldFlow 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20313) adds sequence information to structure generation. A sequence encoder produces single and pair representations, a multimodal fusion trunk combines them with structural features, and a geometric decoder updates residue frames. Sequence remains conditioning information here; the output is still a backbone. That distinction separates FoldFlow 2 from the co-design models that generate both modalities.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s44-image56.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="FoldFlow 2 coupling protein sequence information to backbone flow matching." caption="FoldFlow 2 conditions structural transport on sequence features." %}

[RFdiffusion2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.09.648075v2) is developed specifically for enzyme design. Its input is an atomic motif or theozyme assembled from a known structure or a quantum-chemical model of the reaction. The generation trajectory preserves catalytic residues, reactants, and any metal cofactor while building the surrounding scaffold. Folding, sequence design, and functional prediction then evaluate whether that catalytic arrangement survives the complete design pipeline.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s45-image57.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RFDiffusion2 enzyme-design pipeline conditioned on catalytic sites." caption="RFDiffusion2 treats catalytic geometry as a fixed functional constraint during scaffold generation." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s45-image58.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RFDiffusion2 designed enzyme structures around catalytic motifs." caption="Generated enzyme scaffolds preserve the requested catalytic configuration while varying the surrounding fold." %}

## Joint sequence--structure co-design

The separated pipeline generates continuous structure first and discrete sequence second. Co-design generates both variables together. This requires one model to couple continuous geometric dynamics with categorical residue dynamics.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s46-image59.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein co-design triangle connecting inverse folding, structure generation, and sequence generation." caption="Co-design closes the loop between continuous backbone geometry and discrete residue identity." %}

### Early geometric and continuous-relaxation models

[ProtSeed](https://openreview.net/forum?id=pRCMXcfdihq) represents residue $$i$$ by a type $$s_i$$, a $$C_\alpha$$ position $$\mathbf{x}_i$$, and a frame orientation $$O_i$$. Context-encoded single and pair features specify the desired structural properties. Each layer updates the residue-type distribution and applies an equivariant structure transition, so sequence and geometry repeatedly exchange information rather than being generated in two disconnected passes.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s47-image60.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ProtSeed geometric encoder and joint protein sequence-structure decoder." caption="ProtSeed builds a shared geometric representation for co-designing residue types and backbone structure." %}

[ProteinGenerator](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-024-02395-w) embeds categorical residue variables in continuous space. RoseTTAFold is fine-tuned as a denoiser that receives noisy sequence embeddings, backbone structure, and optional constraints such as a motif, target, or active site. After denoising, the continuous sequence representation is decoded back to amino-acid identities.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s48-image61.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ProteinGenerator architecture generating continuous structure and relaxed sequence variables." caption="ProteinGenerator relaxes categorical residue identities into a continuous representation for joint generation." %}

[MultiFlow](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/campbell24a.html) assigns one flow to each component of the residue state. Translations use Euclidean interpolation,

$$
\mathbf{x}_t=t\mathbf{x}_1+(1-t)\mathbf{x}_0,
$$

rotations use an $$\mathrm{SO}(3)$$ geodesic, and amino-acid identities use a discrete categorical flow. The categorical probabilities obey a master equation rather than a coordinate ODE,

$$
\partial_t\mathbf{p}_t=R_t^\top\mathbf{p}_t,
$$

where $$R_t(i,j)$$ is the jump rate from residue state $$i$$ to $$j$$. The three processes share time and multimodal features while retaining their different mathematical state spaces.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s49-image62.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="MultiFlow joint flow matching over protein sequence and structure." caption="MultiFlow couples generative transports for residue categories and three-dimensional geometry." %}

[Protpardelle](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2311500121) extends co-design to all-atom structure and sequence generation. At each residue it represents a superposition of the backbone and coordinates for all twenty possible side chains. Backbone atoms and side-chain atoms follow different denoising schedules, allowing sequence identity to resolve as the compatible side-chain trajectory is selected. This handles uncertainty that a backbone-only model can defer to a later packing step.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s50-image63.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protpardelle all-atom protein sequence and structure generation model." caption="Protpardelle generates residue identities together with all-atom protein geometry." %}

### Zero-shot antibody design with Chai-2

[Chai-2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.05.663018v1) applies co-design to de novo antibody engineering. The reported system generates antibodies for new targets without iterative high-throughput screening or affinity maturation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s51-image65.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Chai-2 de novo antibody design concept and generated antibody complex." caption="Chai-2 targets zero-shot antibody generation rather than mutation around a known binder." %}

The lecture summarizes the campaign as fifty targets with at most twenty designs tested per target. The source paper reports 52 targets, a 16% pooled hit rate, and at least one binder for half of the targets. Design, synthesis, and characterization were completed in roughly two weeks without iterative optimization or a high-throughput screening campaign. The denominator matters: the claim concerns tested zero-shot designs, not the fraction of all computational samples that would bind.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s52-image67.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Chai-2 experimental campaign across fifty targets and measured hit rates." caption="Chai-2 evaluates de novo designs through a finite synthesis and laboratory campaign rather than only computational scores." %}

The generated binders span several target complexes.

### BoltzGen and a complete design pipeline

[BoltzGen](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.11.20.689494v2) expresses residue types through an all-atom continuous representation. Each designed residue carries fourteen atoms, including auxiliary markers. The placement of those markers relative to backbone atoms determines the decoded amino acid; the markers are discarded after decoding. This lets the diffusion model treat identity and geometry inside one coordinate state.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s54-image69.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="BoltzGen continuous representation of residue categories and multitask training setup." caption="BoltzGen places sequence and structure inside a shared generative parameterization." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s54-image70.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="BoltzGen training tasks including folding, binder design, motif scaffolding, and unconditional generation." caption="Multitask training exposes BoltzGen to several conditional and unconditional protein-design contracts." %}

Training mixes folding, binder design, motif scaffolding, unconditional design, target-structure conditioning, binding-site conditioning, and secondary-structure conditioning. The pipeline then couples BoltzGen with SolubleMPNN inverse folding, structure prediction, affinity prediction, analysis, filtering, and ranking. Boltz-2 confidence and interaction-style scores—including hydrogen bonds and salt bridges—are used during ranking. The lecture's timing plot separates diffusion from inverse folding and shows that both costs grow with the total number of residues in the design--target complex.

Wet-lab validation provides the final reported evidence across several designed binder classes.

## Antibody-specific generation and filtering

Antibody design returns to the heavy-chain, light-chain, framework, and CDR organization introduced during affinity maturation. The later models specialize the general co-design problem to antigen-aware paratope generation.

### RefineGNN and HERN

[RefineGNN](https://openreview.net/forum?id=LI2bhrE_2A) formulates antibody sequence--structure co-design as conditional graph generation. Residues are nodes, geometric interactions are edges, and framework regions provide the fixed context. Message passing predicts amino-acid identities, updates a coarsened sequence context, predicts coordinates, and refines the CDR structure. The output is therefore a CDR sequence and conformation conditioned on the surrounding antibody rather than a complete unconstrained protein.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s58-image74.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RefineGNN graph-generation model for antibody sequence and structure co-design." caption="RefineGNN jointly generates antibody residue identity and structure through graph refinement." %}

[HERN](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/jin22a.html) adds antigen information. It generates the paratope autoregressively, one residue at a time, while conditioning on the antigen epitope. A docking model infers geometric hidden states from the partial antibody--antigen complex after each residue. Adding one amino acid can change the possible interface geometry, so docking and sequence generation alternate throughout decoding.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s59-image75.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="HERN autoregressive antigen-aware antibody paratope generation." caption="HERN conditions each new paratope residue on an antigen-aware geometric state." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s59-image76.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="HERN docking-guided update from a partial paratope to a completed antibody-antigen complex." caption="A docking module repeatedly updates the interaction geometry as the paratope is constructed." %}

### DSMBind: likelihood-based filtering

DSMBind learns antibody--antigen binding affinity and scores three-dimensional complexes. A generative model first proposes CDR sequences and corresponding complexes. The affinity model then assigns energies—for example, the lecture shows predicted values of $$-10.24$$, $$-8.65$$, and $$-7.33$$ for three paratopes—so candidates can be ranked or used to guide generation before experimental testing.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s60-dsmbind-affinity.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DSMBind antibody-antigen affinity model and table of predicted binding energies." caption="[DSMBind](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/6a45a1b0697ee086bd8bf494cacc6567-Abstract-Conference.html) learns a geometric energy signal from unlabeled complex structures and uses it to rank binding candidates." %}

The model also learns a likelihood over three-dimensional structures through denoising score matching. Random rotation leaves the scalar energy unchanged, while coordinate perturbation moves a stable complex away from a local minimum. For a Boltzmann-like density,

$$
\nabla_{\mathbf{X}}\log p(\mathbf{X})
=-\beta\nabla_{\mathbf{X}}E(\mathbf{X}),
$$

so the descent force $$-\nabla_{\mathbf{X}}E(\mathbf{X})$$ points toward lower-energy structures and rotates equivariantly with the complex. DSM supplies supervision for that force field without requiring a binding-energy label for every structure.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s61-dsmbind-score-matching.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DSMBind denoising score-matching construction for antibody-antigen structure likelihood." caption="Denoising score matching teaches DSMBind the direction from perturbed interfaces back toward higher-density complex geometries." %}

The final evaluation is experimental: designed PD-L1 nanobodies R16 and M24 are synthesized, and ELISA measures binding against the PBST background at $$200$$ nM and $$500$$ nM. M24 is reported at approximately $$200$$ nM affinity despite less than $$0.3$$ sequence identity to known nanobodies. The lecture also records the designed CDR sequences:

| Nanobody | CDR1 | CDR2 | CDR3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| R16 | GCGDWMY | TNTSY | YEWHRREFH |
| M24 | IVIDVIL | MTSGY | VAMHSRYFW |

That closing step completes the source pipeline. Representation learning supplies transferable features; generative models propose sequence and structure; affinity and structural scores filter the proposals; and wet-lab measurements determine whether the designed molecule works. The models differ in what they encode or generate, but each claim must be validated against the object it actually outputs.
