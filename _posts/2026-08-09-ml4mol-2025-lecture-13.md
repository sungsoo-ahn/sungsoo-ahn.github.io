## Protein representation learning

A protein representation should compress a sequence or structure while retaining information useful for function, fitness, interaction, and design. That objective is broader than predicting one label: the representation is intended to transfer across tasks.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s03-image2.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein representation learning connected to sequence, structure, function, and downstream tasks." caption="Protein representation learning seeks a reusable encoding that connects several biological views and tasks." %}

The PEER benchmark organizes protein understanding across several task families, including residue-level prediction, sequence-level properties, protein--protein interaction, and structure-related outputs.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s04-image3.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="PEER multi-task benchmark for protein sequence understanding." caption="PEER evaluates whether one learned representation transfers across heterogeneous protein-understanding tasks." %}

Fitness prediction is a particularly important application. A fitness landscape maps sequence mutations to changes in a measured function, creating a surface over an enormous discrete sequence space.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s05-image4.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein fitness landscape relating sequence mutations to functional measurements." caption="A protein fitness landscape records how mutations move a sequence toward better or worse measured function." %}

### Protein language models

Protein language models treat amino acids as tokens and train Transformer encoders by predicting masked or next residues. The objective supplies supervision from unlabeled sequence databases: context must reveal which residues are compatible with a family, fold, or functional constraint.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s06-image5.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Transformer protein language model trained by masked amino-acid prediction." caption="Protein language modeling learns contextual residue embeddings from sequence reconstruction." %}

The learned features support secondary- and tertiary-structure prediction, functional annotation, protein-family classification, and variant-effect prediction. A fitness model can therefore reuse an embedding learned without the task's experimental labels.

ESM demonstrates strong gains as protein language models and their training corpora scale. ESM embeddings also supply the sequence representation used by ESMFold for single-sequence structure prediction.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s08-image6.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ESM masked-language-model architecture for protein sequences." caption="ESM learns protein sequence representations through masked amino-acid recovery." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s08-image7.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Scaling curves for ESM protein language models." caption="ESM reports systematic improvements as model scale increases." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s08-image8.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ESMFold structure prediction from ESM sequence embeddings." caption="ESMFold converts pretrained sequence representations into three-dimensional structures." %}

DPLM changes the objective from one-step masking to discrete diffusion. Different timesteps correspond to different masking ratios, and the model learns to denoise sequences across this corruption schedule. The same model can serve representation learning and sequence generation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s09-image9.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Discrete masking diffusion process used by DPLM for protein sequences." caption="DPLM corrupts protein sequences at several mask levels and learns the corresponding reverse denoising process." %}

### Structure encoders

Sequence is only one view. Structure often determines how a protein binds or catalyzes, and geometric representations can expose this information directly.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s11-image14.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Several protein structures associated with different structural tasks." caption="Structure encoders support tasks such as fold classification, interface prediction, docking, and antibody modeling." %}

A protein cannot be treated as an unordered point cloud without losing the residue arrangement along the chain. The same occupied volume may conceal very different backbone connectivity.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s12-image15.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein surface or point cloud contrasted with an ordered ribbon backbone." caption="Pure point-cloud geometry can discard the sequential and relational organization of a protein chain." %}

GEAR constructs a multi-relational residue graph. Sequential edges follow the backbone; radius edges connect spatial neighbors; and k-nearest-neighbor edges provide another geometric relation. Message passing can distinguish these edge types rather than collapsing them into one adjacency.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s13-image16.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GEAR multi-relational protein graph with sequential, radius, and nearest-neighbor edges." caption="GEAR preserves both chain order and spatial neighborhood through typed residue relations." %}

Geometric pretraining asks the encoder to recover masked residue identities and geometric quantities such as distances and angles, tying the representation to local three-dimensional structure.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s14-image17.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="GEAR geometric pretraining tasks over residues, distances, and angles." caption="GEAR pretraining combines residue recovery with geometric relation prediction." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s14-image18.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein graph pretraining results and learned structural representations." caption="The geometric objectives improve transfer to protein-level and residue-level tasks." %}

### Multimodal sequence--structure pretraining

Sequence and structure encoders can be trained jointly. A systematic comparison combines protein language models with structural graph encoders and tests how alignment objectives affect downstream transfer.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s15-image19.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Joint protein sequence and structure representation-learning architectures." caption="Multimodal protein pretraining aligns sequence encoders with geometric structure encoders." %}

DiffPreT uses a Siamese objective over sequence and structure diffusion trajectories. Two corrupted views of the same protein are processed jointly so the encoder must predict how their representations evolve during denoising.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s16-diffpret-trajectories.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Sequence and structure diffusion trajectories used for protein encoder pretraining." caption="[DiffPreT](https://openreview.net/forum?id=tIzbNQko3c) corrupts residue identities and coordinates jointly so that denoising requires both chemical and geometric context." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s16-diffpret-framework.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Siamese diffusion trajectory prediction between correlated protein conformers." caption="SiamDiff mutually denoises trajectories from correlated conformers to make the representation sensitive to shared protein identity without collapsing conformational variation." %}

SaProt offers a simpler fusion interface. Foldseek supplies twenty discrete structural tokens, and each amino acid is paired with one of them. The language model then reads a sequence whose vocabulary already contains local structure.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s17-image22.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SaProt sequence tokens paired with Foldseek-derived structural tokens." caption="SaProt extends the protein vocabulary by pairing each amino acid with a discrete local-structure token derived from Foldseek." %}

## Protein design from sequence or structure

Protein design generates molecules with desired functions. The output may be a sequence, an unlabeled backbone structure, or both sequence and structure together.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s29-image41.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein design tasks spanning sequence design, structure design, and joint co-design." caption="Protein design can generate residue identities, backbone geometry, or both variables jointly." %}

### Sequence generation and conditional design

Protein sequences are available at a much larger scale than experimentally resolved structures, making sequence-only generative training comparatively accessible. Depending on the conditioning input, a sequence generator may be unconditional, property-conditioned, or structure-conditioned.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s30-image42.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein sequence data scale and several sequence-generation settings." caption="Sequence generators exploit large protein corpora and differ by which biological or structural variables they condition on." %}

Property-conditioned sequence design asks for sequences associated with a family, biological process, molecular function, or other annotation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s31-image43.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Conditional protein sequence generator receiving family and function properties." caption="A property-conditioned generator translates functional or family labels into residue sequences." %}

In practice, many design campaigns follow the RFDiffusion pipeline: choose a target or motif, generate a backbone structure, design a compatible sequence, fold the sequence computationally, and validate the resulting candidate.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s32-image44.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Target-to-structure-to-sequence-to-validation protein design pipeline." caption="The common protein-design workflow separates backbone generation, sequence design, and structural validation." %}

### Backbone generation

Structure design generates an unlabeled backbone that can later be passed to an inverse-folding model. The motivation is that structure constrains function, so new backbone families may open functional regions that sequence-only generation rarely reaches.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s36-image48.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Unlabeled protein backbone generation followed by sequence design." caption="Backbone generators propose three-dimensional folds before residue labels are assigned." %}

FrameDiff diffuses over residue rigid bodies in three dimensions. Its state uses translations and rotations, matching the frame representation used by structure predictors.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s37-image49.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="FrameDiff diffusion over residue rigid frames for protein backbone generation." caption="FrameDiff generates a backbone by denoising translations and rotations of residue frames." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s38-image50.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="FrameFlow rigid-body flow matching and protein-generation results." caption="FrameFlow transports residue frames through a learned continuous-time flow." %}

BoltzGen expresses discrete residue types through continuous representations and trains across folding, binder design, motif scaffolding, unconditional design, and related tasks.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s54-image69.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="BoltzGen continuous representation of residue categories and multitask training setup." caption="BoltzGen places sequence and structure inside a shared generative parameterization." %}

## Antibody-specific generation and filtering

Antibody design returns to the heavy-chain, light-chain, framework, and CDR organization introduced during affinity maturation. The later models specialize the general co-design problem to antigen-aware paratope generation.

### RefineGNN and HERN

RefineGNN formulates antibody sequence--structure co-design as graph generation, iteratively refining residues and local geometry.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s58-image74.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="RefineGNN graph-generation model for antibody sequence and structure co-design." caption="RefineGNN jointly generates antibody residue identity and structure through graph refinement." %}

HERN adds antigen information. It generates the paratope autoregressively, one residue at a time, while a docking model infers geometric hidden states from the partial antibody and target complex.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s59-image75.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="HERN autoregressive antigen-aware antibody paratope generation." caption="HERN conditions each new paratope residue on an antigen-aware geometric state." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s59-image76.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="HERN docking-guided update from a partial paratope to a completed antibody-antigen complex." caption="A docking module repeatedly updates the interaction geometry as the paratope is constructed." %}

### DSMBind: likelihood-based filtering

DSMBind learns antibody--antigen binding affinity and scores three-dimensional complexes. A designed paratope can be filtered by the predicted energy or affinity before experimental testing.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s60-dsmbind-affinity.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DSMBind antibody-antigen affinity model and table of predicted binding energies." caption="[DSMBind](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/6a45a1b0697ee086bd8bf494cacc6567-Abstract-Conference.html) learns a geometric energy signal from unlabeled complex structures and uses it to rank binding candidates." %}

The model also learns a likelihood over three-dimensional structures through denoising score matching. Random coordinate perturbations define a noisy structure distribution, and the learned score estimates how to move configurations toward the data manifold. This provides a structural plausibility signal alongside binding prediction.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s61-dsmbind-score-matching.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DSMBind denoising score-matching construction for antibody-antigen structure likelihood." caption="Denoising score matching teaches DSMBind the direction from perturbed interfaces back toward higher-density complex geometries." %}

The final evaluation is experimental: designed PD-L1 nanobodies are synthesized and their binding is measured. That closing step mirrors the broader lecture storyline. Representation learning supplies transferable features; generative models propose sequence and structure; affinity and structural scores filter the proposals; and wet-lab measurements determine whether the designed molecule works. The models differ in what they encode or generate, but the scientific claim is completed only by the downstream validation appropriate to that object.
