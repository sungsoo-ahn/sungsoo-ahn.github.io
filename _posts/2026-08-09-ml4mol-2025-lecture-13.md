## Protein representation learning

A protein representation should compress a sequence or structure while retaining information useful for function, fitness, interaction, and design. That objective is broader than predicting one label: the representation is intended to transfer across tasks.

### Protein language models

Protein language models treat amino acids as tokens and train Transformer encoders by predicting masked or next residues. The objective supplies supervision from unlabeled sequence databases: context must reveal which residues are compatible with a family, fold, or functional constraint.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s06-image5.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Transformer protein language model trained by masked amino-acid prediction." caption="Protein language modeling learns contextual residue embeddings from sequence reconstruction. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein representation and design lecture, slide 6." %}

The learned features support secondary- and tertiary-structure prediction, functional annotation, protein-family classification, and variant-effect prediction. A fitness model can therefore reuse an embedding learned without the task's experimental labels.

ESM demonstrates strong gains as protein language models and their training corpora scale. ESM embeddings also supply the sequence representation used by ESMFold for single-sequence structure prediction.

### Structure encoders

Sequence is only one view. Structure often determines how a protein binds or catalyzes, and geometric representations can expose this information directly.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s11-image14.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Several protein structures associated with different structural tasks." caption="Structure encoders support tasks such as fold classification, interface prediction, docking, and antibody modeling. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein representation and design lecture, slide 11." %}

A protein cannot be treated as an unordered point cloud without losing the residue arrangement along the chain. The same occupied volume may conceal very different backbone connectivity.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s12-image15.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein surface or point cloud contrasted with an ordered ribbon backbone." caption="Pure point-cloud geometry can discard the sequential and relational organization of a protein chain. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein representation and design lecture, slide 12." %}

GEAR constructs a multi-relational residue graph. Sequential edges follow the backbone; radius edges connect spatial neighbors; and k-nearest-neighbor edges provide another geometric relation. Message passing can distinguish these edge types rather than collapsing them into one adjacency.

Protein design generates molecules with desired functions. The output may be a sequence, an unlabeled backbone structure, or both sequence and structure together.

### Sequence generation and conditional design

Protein sequences are available at a much larger scale than experimentally resolved structures, making sequence-only generative training comparatively accessible. Depending on the conditioning input, a sequence generator may be unconditional, property-conditioned, or structure-conditioned.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s30-image42.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein sequence data scale and several sequence-generation settings." caption="Sequence generators exploit large protein corpora and differ by which biological or structural variables they condition on. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol protein representation and design lecture, slide 30." %}

Property-conditioned sequence design asks for sequences associated with a family, biological process, molecular function, or other annotation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/s31-image43.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Conditional protein sequence generator receiving family and function properties." caption="A property-conditioned generator translates functional or family labels into residue sequences. ## Antibody-specific generation and filtering

Antibody design returns to the heavy-chain, light-chain, framework, and CDR organization introduced during affinity maturation. The later models specialize the general co-design problem to antigen-aware paratope generation.

RefineGNN formulates antibody sequence--structure co-design as graph generation, iteratively refining residues and local geometry.

### DSMBind: likelihood-based filtering

DSMBind learns antibody--antigen binding affinity and scores three-dimensional complexes. A designed paratope can be filtered by the predicted energy or affinity before experimental testing.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/dsmbind-affinity.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DSMBind antibody-antigen affinity model and table of predicted binding energies." caption="DSMBind filters generated antibody complexes with a learned interaction and affinity model. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol protein representation and design lecture, slide 60." %}

The model also learns a likelihood over three-dimensional structures through denoising score matching. Random coordinate perturbations define a noisy structure distribution, and the learned score estimates how to move configurations toward the data manifold. This provides a structural plausibility signal alongside binding prediction.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-13/dsmbind-score-matching.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="DSMBind denoising score-matching construction for antibody-antigen structure likelihood." caption="Denoising score matching supplies a likelihood-related structural signal for candidate filtering. Cropped from the 2025 ML4Mol protein representation and design lecture, slide 61." %}

The final evaluation is experimental: designed PD-L1 nanobodies are synthesized and their binding is measured. That closing step mirrors the broader lecture storyline. Representation learning supplies transferable features; generative models propose sequence and structure; affinity and structural scores filter the proposals; and wet-lab measurements determine whether the designed molecule works. The models differ in what they encode or generate, but the scientific claim is completed only by the downstream validation appropriate to that object.
