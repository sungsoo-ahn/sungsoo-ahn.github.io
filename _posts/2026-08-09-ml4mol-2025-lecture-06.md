## ODEs, SDEs, and probability paths

An ordinary differential equation specifies a deterministic velocity field,

$$
dx_t=f(x_t,t)\,dt.
$$

The differential notation abbreviates an integral relation,

$$
x_{t+h}-x_t=\int_t^{t+h}f(x_\tau,\tau)\,d\tau.
$$

Given an initial value and suitable regularity, the trajectory is fixed. A random initial condition therefore induces a time-indexed probability distribution even though each conditional trajectory is deterministic.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s04-image1.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="ODE differential equation and its equivalent integral over a short time interval." caption="An ODE moves a state continuously according to a deterministic velocity field. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 4." %}

A stochastic differential equation adds noise,

$$
dX_t=\mu(X_t,t)\,dt+\sigma(X_t,t)\,dW_t.
$$

The drift $$\mu$$ describes systematic motion. The diffusion coefficient $$\sigma$$ sets the noise scale. The stochastic integral is interpreted through Itô calculus rather than as an ordinary Riemann integral.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s05-image2.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="SDE with drift and diffusion terms and its integral interpretation." caption="An SDE combines deterministic drift with Brownian increments. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 5." %}

### Brownian motion

A Wiener process starts from $$W_0=0$$, has Gaussian increments

$$
W_{t+\Delta}-W_t\sim\mathcal N(0,\Delta),
$$

and has independent increments on non-overlapping time intervals. It is the continuous limit of a random walk whose step variance shrinks with the time interval. A typical path is continuous but nowhere differentiable, which is why $$dW_t$$ cannot be treated as an ordinary velocity times $$dt$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s06-image3.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Several sample paths of one-dimensional Brownian motion." caption="Brownian paths accumulate independent Gaussian increments at every time scale. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 6." %}

### The Fokker–Planck equation

The SDE describes random trajectories; the Fokker–Planck–Kolmogorov equation describes their density. In one dimension,

$$
\frac{\partial p(x,t)}{\partial t}
=-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\!\left(\mu(x,t)p(x,t)\right)
+\frac12\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2}
\!\left(\sigma^2(x,t)p(x,t)\right).
$$

An ODE is the special case $$\sigma=0$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s07-image4.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Fokker-Planck equation corresponding to a one-dimensional stochastic differential equation." caption="The Fokker–Planck equation converts drift and diffusion into density evolution. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 7." %}

It is useful to define probability current

$$
J(x,t)=\mu(x,t)p(x,t)
-\frac12\frac{\partial}{\partial x}
\!\left(\sigma^2(x,t)p(x,t)\right),
$$

so that $$\partial_t p=-\partial_xJ$$. Probability in a short interval changes by the incoming current minus the outgoing current. The drift term transports mass at velocity $$\mu$$. The diffusion term moves mass down gradients created by neighboring density and noise strength.

Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 9." %}

### Langevin dynamics and continuity

For an energy $$U(x)$$, overdamped Langevin dynamics has the form

$$
dX_t=-\nabla U(X_t)\,dt+\sqrt{2\tau}\,dW_t.
$$

Its stationary density is $$p_\infty(x)\propto\exp[-U(x)/\tau]$$ under suitable boundary conditions. The energy supplies an attracting drift while diffusion prevents collapse to one minimum.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s11-image11.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Two-dimensional energy landscape and Langevin samples approaching its stationary distribution." caption="Langevin dynamics balances energy descent with thermal diffusion. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 11." %}

For a deterministic velocity $$u(x,t)$$ and random initial condition $$X_0\sim p_0$$,

$$
dX_t=u(X_t,t)\,dt
$$

induces the continuity equation

$$
\partial_t p_t(x)=-\nabla\cdot\bigl(u(x,t)p_t(x)\bigr).
$$

This is the zero-diffusion limit of Fokker–Planck. It is also the governing equation behind continuous normalizing flows and flow matching.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s12-image12.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Continuity equation for the density transported by an ODE velocity field." caption="An ODE transports an entire probability density according to the continuity equation. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 12." %}

## Diffusion models and reverse-time SDEs

A diffusion model begins from data and evolves it toward a simple noise law. A variance-preserving example is

$$
dX_t=-\frac12\beta(t)X_t\,dt+\sqrt{\beta(t)}\,dW_t,
\qquad X_0\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}.
$$

For a sufficiently long or strong schedule, $$X_T$$ is approximately standard Gaussian. The generative problem is to begin from that Gaussian and recover the data distribution by reversing the probability path.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s14-image13.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Data images progressively corrupted into Gaussian noise along a forward diffusion process." caption="The forward SDE gradually erases structure until the endpoint is close to a tractable Gaussian. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 14." %}

Reversing an Itô process is not achieved by placing a minus sign in front of a stochastic integral. Itô integration is time-oriented, and the reverse process needs a different drift.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s15-image14.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Forward noising sequence and reverse neural denoising sequence." caption="A diffusion model learns a reverse-time stochastic process from noise back to data. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 15." %}

### Forward and backward descriptions

Write the forward SDE as

$$
dX_t=f(X_t,t)\,dt+g(t)\,dW_t.
$$

A backward SDE traversing the same one-time marginal densities can be written with backward time and a backward Wiener process. Equivalently, define a new forward clock $$Y_s=X_{T-s}$$. The two notations differ in signs because one integrates from $$T$$ down to zero while the other runs a new variable from zero to $$T$$. Keeping the clock convention explicit prevents an apparent contradiction between reverse-SDE formulas.

### Nelson's identity

Let $$f$$ and $$\bar f$$ be the forward and backward drifts associated with the same density path and diffusion coefficient $$g(t)$$. Nelson's identity gives

$$
f(x,t)-\bar f(x,t)=g(t)^2\nabla_x\log p_t(x).
$$

The extra term is the score of the current marginal density. The identity follows by writing the forward and backward Fokker–Planck equations, equating their time derivatives, and solving for the difference of drifts.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s18-image15.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Nelson identity relating forward drift, backward drift, diffusion, and the score." caption="Forward and backward SDEs with the same marginals differ by a score-dependent drift. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 18." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s19-image17.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Fokker-Planck derivation of Nelson's forward-backward drift identity." caption="Equating the two density equations isolates the score term in the reverse drift. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 19." %}

The reverse-time model must therefore estimate

$$
\bar f(x,t)=f(x,t)-g(t)^2\nabla_x\log p_t(x).
$$

The forward drift and schedule are known. Learning reduces to estimating the score $$\nabla_x\log p_t(x)$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s20-image18.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Reverse drift expressed using the known forward drift and an unknown score function." caption="Score estimation supplies the missing term needed to reverse the diffusion. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 20." %}

### Denoising score matching

Although the marginal score is intractable, the corruption kernel $$p_{t|0}(x_t\mid x_0)$$ is chosen to be tractable. The denoising identity is

$$
\nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(x_t)
=
\mathbb E\!\left[
\nabla_{x_t}\log p_{t|0}(x_t\mid X_0)
\mid X_t=x_t
\right].
$$

Training samples a data point, a time, and a corrupted state, then minimizes

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{DSM}}(\theta)
=\mathbb E\left[
\left\|s_\theta(X_t,t)-
\nabla_{x_t}\log p_{t|0}(X_t\mid X_0)
\right\|^2
\right].
$$

The conditional target has the same conditional expectation as the marginal score, so squared regression learns the required marginal object without evaluating it directly.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s21-image19.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Denoising score identity and its squared score matching objective." caption="Denoising score matching replaces an intractable marginal score with a tractable conditional target. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 21." %}

## Flow matching in Euclidean space

A continuous normalizing flow seeks an ODE

$$
dX_t=u_\theta(X_t,t)\,dt
$$

whose probability path connects a simple prior to the data. The ODE defines an invertible flow map $$X_t=\psi_t(X_0)$$. Direct maximum likelihood requires integrating both the state and a divergence term, which can be expensive during training.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s23-image21.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Continuous normalizing flow transporting a simple density into a data density." caption="A CNF uses an ODE flow map to deform a tractable prior into the target distribution. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 23." %}

Flow matching avoids simulating the learned ODE during training. It begins by choosing a family of tractable conditional probability paths $$p_t(x\mid z)$$, each tied to a target example $$z$$, with a known conditional velocity $$u_t(x\mid z)$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s24-image23.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Several conditional probability paths leading from a prior to different data examples." caption="Each data example defines a tractable conditional path and velocity. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 24." %}

Marginalizing the target example creates the desired probability path,

$$
p_t(x)=\int p_t(x\mid z)p_{\mathrm{data}}(z)\,dz,
$$

with $$p_0$$ equal to the prior and $$p_1$$ equal to the data distribution under the chosen convention.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s25-image24.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Conditional probability paths averaged into one marginal path from noise to data." caption="The marginal probability path is a mixture of all example-conditioned paths. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 25." %}

### Gaussian conditional paths

The simplest construction samples independent endpoints $$x_0\sim p_0$$ and $$x_1\sim p_{\mathrm{data}}$$ and interpolates

$$
x_t=(1-t)x_0+tx_1.
$$

The conditional velocity is constant,

$$
u_t(x_t\mid x_0,x_1)=x_1-x_0.
$$

Gaussian paths can also retain a nonzero time-dependent width. The schedule changes the conditional target and the geometry of the path but not the marginalization principle.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s26-image25.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Linear interpolation between a Gaussian sample and a data point." caption="A linear conditional path has a constant endpoint-to-endpoint velocity. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 26." %}

### The marginalization trick

The marginal velocity is not an unconditional average of conditional velocities. At a particular location $$x$$, it is the posterior average over paths that pass through that location:

$$
u_t(x)=\int u_t(x\mid z)\,p(z\mid X_t=x)\,dz.
$$

Substituting this expression into the continuity equation shows that it generates the marginal density $$p_t$$. The posterior weighting is essential: two conditional paths that visit different regions should not contribute equally at the same $$x$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s28-image391.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Derivation of the marginal velocity as a posterior average of conditional velocities." caption="The marginalization trick averages conditional velocities using the endpoint posterior at the current state. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 28." %}

The same identity applies to other conditional couplings and paths. Independent coupling is convenient but not mandatory; an informed coupling can shorten trajectories or align related structures while preserving the same marginal endpoint distributions.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s29-image40.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Several alternative conditional couplings that induce different flow paths between the same endpoint distributions." caption="Different conditional paths and couplings can produce the same marginal endpoints with different velocities. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 29." %}

### Conditional flow matching

Regressing directly onto $$u_t(x)$$ would require the intractable posterior. Instead train with

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{CFM}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb E_{t,z,X_t\sim p_t(\cdot\mid z)}
\left[
\left\|u_\theta(X_t,t)-u_t(X_t\mid z)\right\|^2
\right].
$$

The conditional and marginal squared losses differ by a term independent of $$\theta$$, so their parameter gradients are identical. This is the flow analogue of the denoising-score identity: both learn an intractable marginal field by regression on a tractable conditional field.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s30-image311.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Intractable marginal flow matching loss containing a posterior-averaged target." caption="The direct marginal flow target contains an intractable posterior integral. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 30." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s31-image41.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Conditional flow matching objective and equality of its gradient with the marginal loss." caption="Conditional flow matching has the same training gradient while exposing a sampleable target. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 31." %}

Gaussian conditional flow matching is therefore a simulation-free training recipe: sample endpoints and time, construct $$x_t$$ analytically, compute its conditional velocity, and regress the neural field. Sampling after training still integrates the learned ODE.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s32-image42.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Gaussian conditional flow matching training pipeline from endpoints to interpolation and velocity target." caption="Gaussian CFM produces states and target velocities without solving the learned ODE during training. ## Geometric flow matching on manifolds

Protein structures do not live in one unconstrained Euclidean vector space. Backbone frames contain positions and orientations, often represented with $$SE(3)$$ elements around residues. Side-chain torsions live on circles or tori. Amino-acid identities are discrete. A model that interpolates all of these as ordinary vectors can leave the valid state space.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s34-image44.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Protein represented by residue frames, side-chain angles, and discrete sequence identities." caption="Protein generation combines Euclidean positions, rotations, periodic torsions, and discrete variables. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 34." %}

Many structure-design models inherit the frame-based representation introduced by AlphaFold2. Flow-based examples include FoldFlow and FrameFlow, followed by systems such as Proteus, MultiFlow, and PEPFlow. Their details differ, but they share the need to define motion on the geometry of the represented variables.

### Manifolds and charts

A manifold $$\mathcal M$$ is a topological space that locally resembles Euclidean space. Each chart

$$
\phi_\alpha:U_\alpha\subset\mathcal M\to V_\alpha\subset\mathbb R^d
$$

provides coordinates on a patch. The manifold need not be a vector space: adding two rotations or two points on a sphere does not generally produce another valid element.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s36-image47.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Curved manifold covered by local coordinate patches and maps to Euclidean charts." caption="Charts provide local Euclidean coordinates without making the whole manifold a vector space. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 36." %}

A smooth manifold requires transition maps $$\phi_\beta\circ\phi_\alpha^{-1}$$ to be infinitely differentiable wherever two charts overlap. This makes derivatives independent of which compatible chart is used.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s37-image49.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Overlapping manifold charts connected by a smooth coordinate transition." caption="Smooth transition maps make calculus consistent across overlapping coordinate patches. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 37." %}

### Tangent spaces and the metric

At $$p\in\mathcal M$$, the tangent space $$T_p\mathcal M$$ is a vector space of local directions. A tangent vector may be defined as a directional-derivative operator acting on smooth functions. It belongs to the tangent space, not generally to the manifold itself.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s38-image51.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Tangent plane attached to a point on a sphere." caption="A tangent space supplies the linear directions available at one manifold point. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 38." %}

A Riemannian metric assigns an inner product

$$
g_p:T_p\mathcal M\times T_p\mathcal M\to\mathbb R
$$

smoothly at every point. It defines vector norms, curve lengths, angles, and gradients.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s39-image53.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Two tangent vectors on a sphere with a point-dependent inner product." caption="A Riemannian metric measures tangent vectors locally across the manifold. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 39." %}

A geodesic is a locally length-minimizing curve. For $$\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathcal M$$,

$$
L(\gamma)=\int_0^1\sqrt{g_{\gamma(t)}(\dot\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t))}\,dt.
$$

On a sphere, great-circle arcs are geodesics; straight chords are not manifold paths.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s40-image55.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Geodesic arc between two points on a curved surface." caption="A Riemannian geodesic is the manifold analogue of a straight line. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 40." %}

### Exponential and logarithmic maps

The exponential map sends a tangent vector $$v\in T_p\mathcal M$$ along the geodesic starting at $$p$$ with initial velocity $$v$$ and returns its point at unit time:

$$
\operatorname{Exp}_p(v)=\gamma_v(1).
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s41-image57.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Tangent vector on a sphere mapped along a geodesic by the exponential map." caption="The exponential map converts a tangent displacement into a valid manifold point. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 41." %}

The logarithmic map locally reverses this operation,

$$
\operatorname{Log}_p(q)\in T_p\mathcal M,
\qquad
\operatorname{Exp}_p(\operatorname{Log}_p(q))=q,
$$

when a unique chosen geodesic connects $$p$$ to $$q$$. At a cut locus the logarithm can be nonunique.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s42-image59.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Point on a sphere mapped back to a base-point tangent vector by the logarithmic map." caption="The logarithmic map expresses a nearby manifold point as a tangent displacement from a base point. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 42." %}

### Riemannian flow matching

An ODE on a manifold requires its velocity to lie in the current tangent space:

$$
\dot x_t=u_t(x_t),
\qquad
u_t(x_t)\in T_{x_t}\mathcal M.
$$

The notation $$dx_t=u_t\,dt$$ is shorthand for finding a curve whose derivative is the given tangent vector field.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s43-image62.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Velocity field tangent to a curved manifold and a path following it." caption="A manifold flow must output a tangent vector at every current state. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 43." %}

A geometric Euler step uses the exponential map,

$$
x_{t+h}\approx\operatorname{Exp}_{x_t}\!\left(h\,u_t(x_t)\right),
$$

which stays on the manifold. A constant-speed geodesic from $$x_0$$ to target $$z$$ is

$$
x_t=\operatorname{Exp}_{x_0}
\!\left(t\operatorname{Log}_{x_0}(z)\right).
$$

At the current point, its conditional velocity can be written

$$
u_t(x_t\mid z)=\frac{1}{1-t}\operatorname{Log}_{x_t}(z).
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s45-image63.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Exponential-map integration step and geodesic interpolation on a sphere." caption="Exponential-map updates replace Euclidean addition, while Log and Exp define a geodesic conditional path. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 45." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s46-image610.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Logarithmic map from a current manifold state toward a target point." caption="The conditional target velocity points along the remaining geodesic to the endpoint. The conditional path induces a marginal density exactly as in Euclidean flow matching. The loss replaces the Euclidean norm with the Riemannian metric,

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{RCFM}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb E\left[
g_{X_t}\!\left(
u_\theta(X_t,t)-u_t(X_t\mid Z),
u_\theta(X_t,t)-u_t(X_t\mid Z)
\right)
\right].
$$

The conditional and marginal losses again have equal parameter gradients. The marginalization trick is statistical; the metric and tangent constraint are geometric.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s48-image390.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Riemannian conditional flow matching loss measured by the local metric." caption="Riemannian CFM keeps the conditional-regression identity while measuring tangent errors intrinsically. ## Discrete flow matching

Some variables should remain discrete throughout generation. Sequence tokens, atom types, bonds, and graph edits can evolve through a continuous-time Markov chain while coordinates evolve through a continuous flow. The lecture develops the discrete construction by translating the same three questions: choose a conditional probability path and velocity, marginalize the conditional velocity, and train with a tractable conditional loss.

### Continuous-time Markov chains

Let the vocabulary be $$\mathcal T$$ and a length-$$d$$ state be $$x=(x^1,\ldots,x^d)\in\mathcal S=\mathcal T^d$$. A continuous-time Markov chain is specified by transition rates $$u_t(y,x)$$ from current state $$x$$ to destination $$y$$. For a small step $$h$$,

$$
\Pr(X_{t+h}=y\mid X_t=x)
=\delta_{yx}+h\,u_t(y,x)+o(h).
$$

Off-diagonal rates must be nonnegative, and the diagonal is set so that $$\sum_yu_t(y,x)=0$$. The state remains unchanged between random jump events.

Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 53." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s54-image72.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Transition-rate matrix for a continuous-time Markov chain." caption="The rate matrix has nonnegative off-diagonal entries and zero column sums in the destination-first convention. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 54." %}

The probability path obeys the Kolmogorov forward equation,

$$
\frac{d}{dt}p_t(y)=\sum_xu_t(y,x)p_t(x).
$$

This is a discrete continuity equation: probability flows into and out of each state along graph edges in state space.

### Conditional and marginal rates

Choose a conditional jump process with rates $$u_t(y,x\mid z)$$ and probability path $$p_t(x\mid z)$$ ending at target $$z$$. Marginalizing over targets gives $$p_t(x)=\sum_zp_t(x\mid z)p(z)$$. The rate analogue of the velocity identity is

$$
u_t(y,x)=\sum_z u_t(y,x\mid z)\,p(z\mid X_t=x).
$$

The proof substitutes the conditional master equations into the derivative of the mixture and applies Bayes' rule.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s57-image630.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Discrete marginalization derivation for conditional and marginal transition rates." caption="Marginal CTMC rates are posterior averages of target-conditioned rates at the current state. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 57." %}

Directly fitting the marginal rate would require that posterior. A discrete flow matching loss instead regresses a model rate vector onto the conditional rate vector sampled from a conditional path. The lecture writes the objective abstractly as a divergence or norm between $$u_t(\cdot,x_t\mid z)$$ and $$u_\theta(\cdot,x_t,t)$$. As in continuous flow matching, the tractable conditional objective targets the marginal generator after averaging.

### Factorized paths and rates

The full state space has size $$|\mathcal T|^d$$, so enumerating every destination is impossible for long sequences. A factorized generator permits one coordinate to change at a time:

$$
u_t(y,x)=
\sum_{i=1}^d
\mathbf 1[y^{-i}=x^{-i}]
u_t^i(y^i,x).
$$

The model outputs $$d$$ categorical rate vectors rather than one vector over the exponential joint space.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s59-image710.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="High-dimensional discrete transition decomposed into coordinate-wise rate vectors." caption="Factorization restricts each infinitesimal jump to one coordinate, avoiding enumeration of the full product state space. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 59." %}

For a small Euler step, the coordinate-wise kernels multiply to first order. Simultaneous changes in two coordinates have probability $$O(h^2)$$, so the product kernel agrees with the single-jump CTMC up to $$o(h)$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s60-image74.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Two coordinate-wise transition matrices used to form a factorized Euler step." caption="Coordinate-wise Euler kernels approximate the factorized continuous-time generator. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 60." %}

A practical conditional path begins from a coupling $$\pi_{0,1}(x_0,x_1)$$ and factorizes across coordinates,

$$
p_t(x\mid x_0,x_1)=\prod_i p_t^i(x^i\mid x_0,x_1).
$$

A simple schedule chooses the endpoint token with probability $$\kappa_t$$ and the source token with probability $$1-\kappa_t$$:

$$
p_t^i(a\mid x_0,x_1)
=\kappa_t\delta_{a,x_1^i}
+(1-\kappa_t)\delta_{a,x_0^i}.
$$

The corresponding conditional rates are obtained by differentiating the path and enforcing the master equation.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/ml4mol-2025-06/s61-image530.webp" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Factorized conditional probability path interpolating source and target tokens." caption="A coordinate-wise categorical path moves probability from each source token toward its coupled target token. Reused from the 2025 ML4Mol diffusion and flow matching lecture, slide 61." %}

After marginalization, it is sufficient to learn a posterior over target tokens given the current state. Plugging the posterior prediction into the analytic conditional-rate expression yields the model generator. This mirrors denoising diffusion: predict the clean endpoint from a corrupted state, then convert that prediction into the local reverse or generative dynamics.

The deck's four parts are variations of one construction. An SDE or ODE induces a probability path. Diffusion learns the score needed to reverse a stochastic path. Flow matching regresses conditional velocities whose posterior average is the marginal velocity. Riemannian flow matching replaces vector addition and Euclidean norms with tangent vectors, Exp/Log maps, and a metric. Discrete flow matching replaces velocities with CTMC rates and the continuity equation with the master equation. The state space changes; conditional-to-marginal regression remains the organizing principle.
