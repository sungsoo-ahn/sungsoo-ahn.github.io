Once a network carries irreducible features, “add more equivariance” is no longer a meaningful design instruction. The symmetry contract is already fixed. The useful questions are architectural: How should neighbors be selected? How many-body correlations should be formed? Which tensor products dominate the cost? How can high-degree features pass through nonlinearities and normalization without becoming unstable?

The lecture answers those questions chronologically. [SE(3)-Transformer](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/15231a7ce4ba789d13b722cc5c955834-Abstract.html) makes TFN messages selective through invariant attention. [Equiformer](https://openreview.net/forum?id=KwmPfARgOTD) turns that idea into a practical Transformer block. [MACE](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/4a36c3c51af11ed9f34615b81edb5bbc-Abstract-Conference.html) forms higher body order explicitly. [eSCN](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/passaro23a.html) reduces an expensive $$SO(3)$$ tensor product to an equivalent $$SO(2)$$ computation. [EquiformerV2](https://openreview.net/forum?id=3o4jU8fWVj) combines that efficient product with revised activation and normalization for high-degree attention.

The resulting progression is not a leaderboard. Each model changes a different part of one typed message-passing pipeline.

## From TFN convolution to steerable attention

The lecture first reconstructs Tensor Field Networks because every later architecture edits this baseline. A point or atom $$a$$ carries features

$$
V_{acm}^{(l)},
$$

where $$a$$ is the point index, $$c$$ is an ordinary multiplicity channel, $$l$$ is the irreducible degree, and $$m=-l,\ldots,l$$ indexes coordinates inside that irrep. Learned matrices may freely mix the $$c$$ channels belonging to one type. They may not treat the $$m$$ entries as unrelated scalar channels, because rotation mixes those entries by the Wigner matrix $$D^{(l)}(R)$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-11/s04-tfn-angular-filter.jpeg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A relative atomic position projected onto spherical harmonics of degrees zero, one, and two and passed through radial filters." caption="The Tensor Field Network angular encoding embedded in the lecture PowerPoint. The original construction is from <a href='https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08219'>Thomas et al.</a>: distance controls a learned radial function, while direction is expanded in a fixed spherical-harmonic basis." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-11/s05-tfn-feature-layout.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="A Tensor Field Network feature dictionary showing point, channel, degree, and representation indices for two particles." caption="The native TFN feature-layout figure retained from the PowerPoint. Point and channel indices describe where a feature lives and how many copies exist; degree and representation indices prescribe how it rotates." %}

For a directed edge $$a\leftarrow b$$ with relative displacement $$r_{ab}=x_b-x_a$$, a type-$$l_f$$ filter separates radius and direction:

$$
F_{c m_f m_i}^{(l_f,l_i)}(r_{ab})
=R_{c m_f m_i}^{(l_f,l_i)}(\lVert r_{ab}\rVert)
Y_{m_f}^{(l_f)}(\hat r_{ab}),
\qquad
\hat r_{ab}=\frac{r_{ab}}{\lVert r_{ab}\rVert}.
$$

The radial factor is invariant. The complete spherical-harmonic vector transforms as type $$l_f$$. Coupling it to a source feature of type $$l_i$$ and summing neighbors gives

$$
\mathcal L_{a c m_o}^{(l_o)}
=\sum_{b\in\mathcal N(a)}
\sum_{m_f,m_i}
C_{(l_f,m_f)(l_i,m_i)}^{(l_o,m_o)}
F_{c m_f m_i}^{(l_f,l_i)}(r_{ab})
V_{b c m_i}^{(l_i)}.
$$

The Clebsch--Gordan coefficient is nonzero only along legal paths

$$
|l_f-l_i|\le l_o\le l_f+l_i.
$$

For example, a type-one directional filter acting on a type-one source feature has

$$
1\otimes1=0\oplus1\oplus2.
$$

The three output dimensions are $$1+3+5=9$$, exactly matching the nine components in the raw outer product of two three-vectors. In Cartesian language, those branches correspond to a dot-product-like scalar, a cross-product-like axial vector, and a symmetric-traceless rank-two tensor. A scalar source coupled to the same filter has only $$1\otimes0=1$$. The network learns how much weight to place on every allowed path, but the representation algebra fixes which paths exist.

This is the recurring three-stage picture in the slides: couple two types, condition the coupling on edge distance and direction, then aggregate the resulting typed messages. The preceding chapter, [Steerable Attention and Nonlinear Message Passing]({% post_url 2026-08-09-gdl-2025-lecture-10 %}), gives the full equivariance proof. Here the important limitation is that the same typed kernel is applied to every neighbor before a uniform sum.

### SE(3)-Transformer makes the neighbor coefficient adaptive

The SE(3)-Transformer rewrites the TFN convolution as a matrix-valued kernel from input type $$k$$ to output type $$l$$:

$$
f_{\mathrm{out},i}^{(l)}
=\sum_k\sum_{j\ne i}
W^{lk}(x_j-x_i)f_{\mathrm{in},j}^{(k)}.
$$

The kernel itself is still assembled from radial functions, spherical harmonics, and fixed Clebsch--Gordan maps:

$$
W^{lk}(x)
=\sum_{J=|k-l|}^{k+l}
\phi_J^{lk}(\lVert x\rVert)
\sum_{m=-J}^{J}Y_{Jm}(\hat x)Q_{Jm}^{lk}.
$$

Attention changes the update to

$$
f_{\mathrm{out},i}^{(l)}
=W_V^{ll}f_{\mathrm{in},i}^{(l)}
+\sum_{j\in\mathcal N(i)\setminus\{i\}}
\alpha_{ij}
\sum_kW_V^{lk}(x_j-x_i)f_{\mathrm{in},j}^{(k)}.
$$

The first term is a pointwise self-interaction. The final term is a TFN-like steerable value. The new coefficient $$\alpha_{ij}$$ must be invariant: if a global rotation changed which neighbor received the largest weight, the layer would not be equivariant.

Queries and keys are therefore typed features,

$$
q_i^{(l)}=\sum_kW_Q^{lk}f_i^{(k)},
\qquad
k_{ij}^{(l)}=\sum_kW_K^{lk}(x_j-x_i)f_j^{(k)},
$$

and their score contracts matching types:

$$
s_{ij}=\sum_l q_i^{(l)\top}k_{ij}^{(l)},
\qquad
\alpha_{ij}
=\frac{\exp(s_{ij})}
{\sum_{j'\in\mathcal N(i)\setminus\{i\}}\exp(s_{ij'})}.
$$

For a real orthogonal Wigner basis,

$$
\bigl(D^{(l)}(R)q\bigr)^\top
\bigl(D^{(l)}(R)k\bigr)
=q^\top D^{(l)}(R)^\top D^{(l)}(R)k
=q^\top k.
$$

The softmax sees only invariant numbers. Its scalar output can suppress or amplify a complete irrep value without changing that value's type. Nonlinear self-interaction uses the same principle: invariant norms or inner products generate data-dependent channel weights, and one scalar weight is shared across every $$m$$ component in the block.

A small exact calculation makes the division of labor visible. Suppose three invariant logits are

$$
(s_{i1},s_{i2},s_{i3})=(0,\log2,\log3).
$$

The softmax weights are

$$
(\alpha_{i1},\alpha_{i2},\alpha_{i3})
=\left(\frac16,\frac26,\frac36\right).
$$

Let one type-one value channel contain

$$
v_{i1}=(1,0,0),\qquad
v_{i2}=(0,1,0),\qquad
v_{i3}=(0,0,2).
$$

The aggregated value is

$$
\sum_j\alpha_{ij}v_{ij}
=\left(\frac16,\frac13,1\right).
$$

After a global rotation $$R$$, the invariant logits and the three fractions are unchanged, while every value becomes $$Rv_{ij}$$. The output is therefore $$R(1/6,1/3,1)$$. Attention has changed which neighbor matters, but the TFN value path still determines how directional information rotates.

## Equiformer: a practical equivariant Transformer

[Equiformer](https://openreview.net/forum?id=KwmPfARgOTD) keeps the residual Transformer layout—attention, feed-forward network, normalization—but replaces each ordinary operation with one that respects irreducible features. It also departs from dot-product attention: its equivariant graph attention uses multilayer-perceptron attention and nonlinear message passing.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-11/s15-equiformer-architecture.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Equiformer architecture with embedding, repeated layer-normalized equivariant graph attention and feed-forward blocks, and an output head." caption="The Equiformer architecture panel embedded in the lecture PowerPoint from <a href='https://openreview.net/forum?id=KwmPfARgOTD'>Liao and Smidt</a>. Its outer residual structure is familiar; every inner operation is typed." %}

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-11/s15-equiformer-attention.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Detailed Equiformer graph-attention block with linear maps, a depth-wise tensor product, invariant attention weights, gating, and value output." caption="The corresponding equivariant graph-attention block from the same paper. A depth-wise tensor product mixes geometric types, scalar features generate attention, and gated values remain steerable." %}

The detailed panel can be read from top to bottom. Receiver and sender features first pass through type-wise linear maps. Their pair interacts with the edge embedding through a depth-wise tensor product. Another linear layer reorganizes the resulting irreps. Scalar channels are reshaped and processed by an ordinary nonlinear network to produce the attention coefficient; the remaining typed channels form the value. A gate lets scalar features control those non-scalar values before the softmax-weighted edge sum. The residual connection then carries the receiver feature around the entire block.

This is “MLP attention” because the scalar logit is not restricted to one query--key dot product. The pair can first undergo an equivariant nonlinear message transformation, after which an MLP acts on the resulting invariant channels. The constraint is on the final logit, not on the complexity of the equivariant computation that produces it.

The design separates operations that mix channels from operations that mix types. A pointwise linear layer acts independently within each degree:

$$
y_{c'm}^{(l)}
=\sum_c W_{c'c}^{(l)}x_{cm}^{(l)}.
$$

The learned weight has no $$m$$ index, so the same channel matrix acts on every coordinate of a type-$$l$$ vector. Scalar channels may use ordinary activations such as SiLU. Non-scalar channels use invariant scalar gates.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-11/s16-equiformer-linear.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Type-wise linear layer connecting channel copies inside degree-zero, degree-one, and degree-two irreducible features." caption="Equiformer's native typed-linear diagram. Dense channel mixing occurs within a degree, not across the representation coordinates of unrelated degrees." %}

Normalization must obey the same layout. Degree-zero scalars can be centered and scaled in the usual way. For $$l>0$$, subtracting a coordinate-wise mean would choose a basis-dependent direction, so Equiformer scales a complete block through an invariant root-mean-square statistic:

$$
\operatorname{RMS}\!\left(x_c^{(l)}\right)
=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2l+1}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}\left(x_{cm}^{(l)}\right)^2+\varepsilon}.
$$

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-11/s16-equiformer-layernorm.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Equiformer layer normalization centering degree-zero scalars and dividing higher-degree irreducible blocks by invariant RMS values." caption="The native Equiformer layer-normalization panel. Scalar and higher-degree blocks require different statistics because only the scalar block has a rotation-invariant componentwise mean." %}

For a type-one block $$x=(1,2,2)$$ and negligible $$\varepsilon$$, the RMS is

$$
\sqrt{\frac{1^2+2^2+2^2}{3}}=\sqrt3.
$$

The normalized vector is $$(1,2,2)/\sqrt3$$. Rotating first preserves the squared norm, so normalization followed by rotation gives the same result as rotation followed by normalization. By contrast, subtracting the component mean $$5/3$$ would not commute with a general rotation.

### Depth-wise tensor products reduce channel cost

A dense tensor product considers every admissible input-channel pair and every output multiplicity. Equiformer's depth-wise tensor product instead makes one output irrep channel depend on a corresponding input path, then restores dense channel mixing with cheaper type-wise linear layers. The analogy is depth-wise separable convolution: separate the expensive structured spatial operation from ordinary channel mixing.

For an admissible path $$l_1\otimes l_2\to l_o$$,

$$
z_{c}^{(l_o)}
=w_{c,l_1l_2l_o}
\left(x_{c}^{(l_1)}
\otimes_{\mathrm{CG}}^{l_o}
y_{c}^{(l_2)}\right).
$$

The channel index $$c$$ is not paired with every other channel inside this expensive contraction. Subsequent linear layers can still communicate across channels.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-11/s17-depthwise-tensor-product.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Three path diagrams showing the depth-wise tensor-product inputs contributing to type-zero, type-one, and type-two outputs." caption="The depth-wise tensor-product paths embedded in the lecture PowerPoint from the Equiformer paper. Restricting multiplicity coupling makes the Clebsch--Gordan stage cheaper while retaining all selected output types." %}

If an input path has $$C$$ channels on each side, a dense product evaluates $$C^2$$ channel pairs before producing output multiplicities. A depth-wise product evaluates only $$C$$ expensive Clebsch--Gordan contractions and delegates later channel mixing to ordinary matrices. For $$C=64$$, that is $$4096$$ versus $$64$$ typed contractions on the path. This arithmetic does not describe the whole runtime—linear layers and the number of degree paths still matter—but it explains why the restriction is valuable precisely where the angular algebra is costly.

## MACE: explicit higher body order

Equiformer changes how an edge message is selected and processed. [MACE](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/4a36c3c51af11ed9f34615b81edb5bbc-Abstract-Conference.html) changes how many neighbors can interact inside one message-passing stage.

The starting point is the atomic cluster expansion. A local energy is organized by body order,

$$
E_i=E_i^{(1)}+E_i^{(2)}+E_i^{(3)}+E_i^{(4)}+\cdots,
$$

where progressively higher terms describe correlations among the center and more neighbors.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-11/s20-ace-body-order.gif" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Atomic cluster expansion of a local energy into one-body, two-body, three-body, and four-body terms." caption="The ACE body-order illustration embedded in the lecture PowerPoint. MACE connects this hierarchy to equivariant message passing; see the <a href='https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/4a36c3c51af11ed9f34615b81edb5bbc-Abstract-Conference.html'>original MACE paper</a>." %}

The lecture describes MACE as two alternating operations. First, a TFN-like layer gathers typed information from the neighborhood. Second, a node-wise layer takes higher-order Clebsch--Gordan products of those already aggregated features. The second operation resembles a geometric $$1\times1$$ convolution: it does not visit neighbors again, but it couples several typed summaries stored at the center.

### First aggregate a typed neighborhood basis

One form of the neighborhood feature is

$$
A_{i,k l_3m_3}^{(t)}
=\sum_{j\in\mathcal N(i)}
\sum_{\substack{l_1m_1\\l_2m_2}}
C_{l_1m_1,l_2m_2}^{l_3m_3}
R_{k l_1l_2l_3}^{(t)}(r_{ji})
Y_{m_1}^{(l_1)}(\hat r_{ji})
\sum_{\tilde k}W_{k\tilde k l_2}^{(t)}
h_{j,\tilde k l_2m_2}^{(t)}.
$$

This is recognizable TFN machinery: radial functions and spherical harmonics form the filter, a Clebsch--Gordan map chooses the output type, and neighbor contributions are summed. The result $$A_i$$ is a typed summary of the entire local environment.

### Then multiply the aggregated summaries

For correlation order $$\nu$$, MACE forms a generalized product

$$
B_{i,\eta_\nu kLM}^{(t)}
=
\sum_{\boldsymbol l,\boldsymbol m}
C_{\eta_\nu,\boldsymbol l\boldsymbol m}^{LM}
\prod_{\xi=1}^{\nu}
\left(
\sum_{\tilde k}
w_{k\tilde k l_\xi}^{(t)}
A_{i,\tilde k l_\xi m_\xi}^{(t)}
\right).
$$

Here $$\boldsymbol l=(l_1,\ldots,l_\nu)$$ and $$\boldsymbol m=(m_1,\ldots,m_\nu)$$. The path label $$\eta_\nu$$ records the intermediate degrees used by the chain of Clebsch--Gordan couplings. For $$\nu=3$$, for example, three copies of $$A_i$$ are coupled sequentially into one output $$LM$$.

The higher-order message sums over correlation orders and coupling paths:

$$
m_{i,kLM}^{(t)}
=\sum_{\nu=1}^{\nu_{\max}}
\sum_{\eta_\nu}
W_{z_i kL,\eta_\nu}^{(t)}
B_{i,\eta_\nu kLM}^{(t)}.
$$

The receiver element $$z_i$$ can choose different linear weights. The node update combines this message with a residual typed feature,

$$
h_{i,kLM}^{(t+1)}
=\sum_{\tilde k}
W_{kL,\tilde k}^{(t)}m_{i,\tilde kLM}^{(t)}
+\sum_{\tilde k}
W_{z_i kL,\tilde k}^{(t)}
h_{i,\tilde kLM}^{(t)}.
$$

These equations preserve the slide's distinction between neighbor aggregation and higher-order node-wise coupling.

### Why products of sums encode several neighbors

The computational advantage appears when the product is expanded. In a scalar caricature, let

$$
A_i=\sum_{j\in\mathcal N(i)}a_{ij}.
$$

Then

$$
A_i^3
=\sum_{j_1,j_2,j_3\in\mathcal N(i)}
a_{ij_1}a_{ij_2}a_{ij_3}.
$$

Products between different neighbors are present even though the implementation first computed one sum. The typed version replaces ordinary multiplication by a chain of legal Clebsch--Gordan products. A correlation order $$\nu=3$$ therefore gives terms involving the center and up to three neighbors—four-body information—without explicitly building a separate tensor for every neighbor triple.

With only two scalar neighbor contributions, $$a_{i1}=1$$ and $$a_{i2}=2$$, aggregation gives $$A_i=3$$. Its second-order product is

$$
A_i^2
=(a_{i1}+a_{i2})^2
=a_{i1}^2+2a_{i1}a_{i2}+a_{i2}^2
=1+4+4=9.
$$

The cross term is the two-neighbor correlation. At third order,

$$
A_i^3
=a_{i1}^3+3a_{i1}^2a_{i2}
+3a_{i1}a_{i2}^2+a_{i2}^3
=27.
$$

The coefficients count repeated assignments of neighbors to product slots. In the full typed model, the coupling-path tensor $$C_{\eta_\nu,\boldsymbol l\boldsymbol m}^{LM}$$ does not merely count assignments: it projects each product onto a chosen output degree and parity. The scalar caricature explains the neighbor combinatorics; the generalized CG product supplies the geometry.

This is why body order and depth are not interchangeable. A two-body edge message followed by several graph layers can eventually correlate distant or multiple neighbors, but the route is indirect and entangled with receptive-field growth. MACE exposes a controlled local body-order hierarchy within each stage. The paper commonly uses $$\nu_{\max}=3$$ and needs only a small number of message-passing iterations.

## eSCN: reducing SO(3) products to SO(2)

A naive all-degree $$SO(3)$$ tensor product grows rapidly with maximum degree $$L$$. Counting input degree, filter degree, output degree, and their magnetic indices leads to an $$O(L^6)$$ angular cost in the formulation analyzed by [Passaro and Zitnick](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/passaro23a.html). eSCN reduces the same equivariant convolution to $$O(L^3)$$ by choosing an edge-aligned coordinate system.

### Rotate every edge to a reference axis

For edge $$s\to t$$, select a rotation $$R_{st}$$ such that

$$
R_{st}\hat r_{st}=e_y.
$$

The source uses the $$y$$-axis convention; a $$z$$-axis convention would be equivalent. Rotate the input feature into that frame:

$$
\tilde x_s^{(l_i)}
=D^{(l_i)}(R_{st})x_s^{(l_i)}.
$$

At the reference axis, the spherical harmonics are sparse:

$$
Y_{m_f}^{(l_f)}(e_y)
\propto \delta_{m_f0}
$$

in the aligned basis convention used by the derivation. Every term with $$m_f\ne0$$ vanishes.

For a concrete frame change, suppose $$\hat r_{st}=e_x$$. A ninety-degree rotation about the global $$z$$ axis maps $$e_x$$ to $$e_y$$. A type-one source vector $$x_s=(a,b,c)$$ becomes $$(-b,a,c)$$ in that aligned frame under the same convention. The angular filter now needs only its axisymmetric component. After the sparse contraction, the inverse rotation returns the output to the original frame. A different global orientation changes both rotations coherently, so it does not change the physical message.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-11/s30-spherical-harmonic-basis.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Spherical harmonic basis functions through degree five arranged by degree and magnetic order." caption="The spherical-harmonic basis image embedded in the eSCN section of the PowerPoint. Aligning an edge with the reference axis selects the axisymmetric column and makes the angular filter sparse." %}

The ordinary coupled feature contains

$$
\left(
x_s^{(l_i)}
\otimes_{l_i,l_f}^{l_o}
h_{l_i l_f l_o}Y^{(l_f)}(\hat r_{st})
\right)_{m_o}
=
\sum_{m_i,m_f}
x_{s,m_i}^{(l_i)}
C_{(l_i,m_i)(l_f,m_f)}^{(l_o,m_o)}
h_{l_i l_f l_o}
Y_{m_f}^{(l_f)}(\hat r_{st}).
$$

In the aligned frame this becomes

$$
\left(
\tilde x_s^{(l_i)}
\otimes_{l_i,l_f}^{l_o}
h_{l_i l_f l_o}\delta^{(l_f)}
\right)_{m_o}
=
\sum_{m_i}
\tilde x_{s,m_i}^{(l_i)}
C_{(l_i,m_i)(l_f,0)}^{(l_o,m_o)}
h_{l_i l_f l_o}.
$$

The filter-index sum has disappeared exactly. Sparsity of the Clebsch--Gordan coefficients reduces the remaining contraction further; in a real basis, each order couples only within a small $$\pm m$$ subspace. Finally, rotate the output back:

$$
a_{st}^{(l_o)}
=D^{(l_o)}(R_{st})^{-1}\tilde a_{st}^{(l_o)}.
$$

### The residual symmetry is SO(2)

Aligning one direction does not determine a complete frame. After fixing the edge axis, rotations about that axis remain possible. The stabilizer of the axis is $$SO(2)$$, and its irreducible features are circular harmonics indexed by order $$m$$.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-11/s33-so3-to-so2.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Spherical harmonic blocks indexed by degree and magnetic order mapped to circular harmonics around an aligned axis." caption="The lecture's embedded SO(3)-to-SO(2) interpretation from eSCN. Once the filter direction is fixed, each magnetic-order column becomes a circular-harmonic channel for rotations around that direction." %}

This viewpoint explains why the reduction is equivariant rather than an arbitrary coordinate shortcut. The aligned-frame computation still respects every residual rotation. Rotating back transports the result to the global frame. The sequence

$$
\text{global irreps}
\;\xrightarrow{D(R_{st})}\;
\text{edge-frame SO(2) channels}
\;\xrightarrow{\text{sparse linear maps}}\;
\text{edge-frame output}
\;\xrightarrow{D(R_{st})^{-1}}\;
\text{global irreps}
$$

commutes with a global rotation of the entire structure.

There is also an apparent frame ambiguity. If $$R_{st}$$ aligns the edge with $$e_y$$, then so does

$$
R_{st}'=H R_{st}
$$

for any rotation $$H$$ about $$e_y$$. The aligned feature produced by $$R_{st}'$$ differs by the residual $$SO(2)$$ action. Let $$K:l_i\to l_o$$ denote the aligned-frame operation. Its required property is

$$
K D^{(l_i)}(H)=D^{(l_o)}(H)K.
$$

Using the alternative alignment then gives

$$
D^{(l_o)}(R_{st}')^{-1}K D^{(l_i)}(R_{st}')
=D^{(l_o)}(R_{st})^{-1}
D^{(l_o)}(H)^{-1}K D^{(l_i)}(H)
D^{(l_i)}(R_{st})
=D^{(l_o)}(R_{st})^{-1}K D^{(l_i)}(R_{st}).
$$

The global output is independent of which valid roll angle was chosen around the edge. This cancellation is the operational meaning of reducing the convolution to the edge stabilizer rather than selecting an arbitrary canonical frame.

The asymptotic saving is large at the degrees targeted by these models. As a crude comparison of the angular powers alone, $$L=6$$ gives

$$
L^6=46{,}656,
\qquad
L^3=216.
$$

The ratio is $$216$$. Real kernels have different constants, channel factors, and rotation overhead, so this is not a wall-clock speedup prediction. It does show why an $$O(L^6)$$ primitive becomes the dominant obstacle before high-degree features become very large, and why moving the structured part to $$O(L^3)$$ changes the feasible design space.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-11/s34-escn-oc20.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="eSCN benchmark table comparing parameter count, training time, OC20 energy and force errors, force within threshold, and IS2RS and IS2RE metrics." caption="The complete eSCN comparison table embedded in the source PowerPoint. The <a href='https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/passaro23a.html'>paper</a> reports both accuracy and GPU-day cost, which is essential because the method is an efficiency result as well as an accuracy result." %}

The result table combines two training regimes and multiple OC20 tasks. The meaningful claim is not that every eSCN cell is smallest. It is that the exact angular reformulation permits higher degrees and moves the accuracy--runtime frontier. That efficient convolution becomes the computational core of EquiformerV2.

## EquiformerV2: high-degree attention at scale

[EquiformerV2](https://openreview.net/forum?id=3o4jU8fWVj) starts from Equiformer, replaces its depth-wise $$SO(3)$$ tensor product plus linear layer with an eSCN convolution, and adds three changes aimed at high-degree features:

- attention re-normalization;
- separable $$S^2$$ activation;
- separable layer normalization.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-11/s35-equiformerv2-architecture.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="EquiformerV2 architecture and equivariant graph-attention block with eSCN convolution, separable layer normalization, and separable spherical activation highlighted." caption="The complete EquiformerV2 architecture embedded in the lecture PowerPoint from <a href='https://openreview.net/forum?id=3o4jU8fWVj'>Liao et al.</a>. Red annotations identify the replacements relative to Equiformer." %}

The first replacement changes the feasible maximum degree. Traditional $$SO(3)$$ products made values beyond small $$L_{\max}$$ expensive on OC20. eSCN's aligned-axis contraction enables EquiformerV2 configurations with $$L_{\max}=6$$ or $$8$$ while preserving the Transformer-style attention block.

### Separable spherical activation

Equiformer's gate allows scalar features to modulate non-scalar blocks, but the nonlinearity itself is controlled mainly by degree zero. A spherical activation instead interprets the irreducible coefficients as a band-limited function on the sphere:

$$
f(\omega)
=\sum_{l=0}^{L}\sum_{m=-l}^{l}
x_m^{(l)}Y_m^{(l)}(\omega).
$$

It samples this function on an $$S^2$$ grid, applies an ordinary pointwise nonlinearity $$\sigma$$, then uses an inverse spherical Fourier transform:

$$
x
\xrightarrow{\mathcal F^{-1}_{S^2}}
f(\omega_q)
\xrightarrow{\sigma}
\sigma(f(\omega_q))
\xrightarrow{\mathcal F_{S^2}}
x'.
$$

This lets all degrees participate in nonlinear interactions. The separable version preserves a direct scalar path while applying the spherical transform to the remaining features, improving efficiency and information flow.

The spectral effect is easy to see with a simple nonlinearity. If the sampled function contains only degrees zero and one,

$$
f(\omega)=aY^{(0)}(\omega)+b^\top Y^{(1)}(\omega),
$$

then the square $$f(\omega)^2$$ contains products $$0\otimes0$$, $$0\otimes1$$, and $$1\otimes1$$. The final product decomposes into degrees zero, one, and two. A pointwise activation can therefore create higher-degree interactions that a scalar gate alone cannot. The forward and inverse spherical transforms translate between the irrep coefficients and the spatial samples where that ordinary nonlinearity is legal.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-11/s36-equiformerv2-activations.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Comparison of gated activation, spherical activation, and separable spherical activation for degree-zero, degree-one, and degree-two features." caption="The three activation designs embedded in the lecture PowerPoint from EquiformerV2. The separable version retains a scalar activation path while letting sampled spherical features from all degrees pass through a pointwise nonlinearity." %}

Because the sphere is sampled at finitely many points, nonlinearities can introduce frequencies above the retained band limit. In practice the grid resolution controls a small numerical equivariance error. This is a computational approximation in the activation, distinct from the exact $$SO(3)$$-to-$$SO(2)$$ reduction used by the eSCN convolution.

### Separable normalization and attention re-normalization

Equiformer normalized each degree with its own RMS statistic. That is equivariant, but when later tensor products combine degrees, independently normalized scales can make the scalar outputs unstable. Separable layer normalization treats degree-zero scalars separately and uses a shared statistic for the combined higher-degree portion.

{% include figure.liquid loading="lazy" path="assets/img/blog/lectures/gdl-2025-11/s37-equiformerv2-normalization.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true alt="Comparison between degree-wise equivariant layer normalization and separable layer normalization using one shared RMS for higher-degree features." caption="The normalization comparison retained from the EquiformerV2 paper through the lecture PowerPoint. Separable normalization keeps scalar centering separate while coupling the scale statistics of non-scalar degrees." %}

Attention re-normalization addresses the same scale mismatch before producing scalar attention logits. Features arriving through different degrees are re-scaled so that high-degree contributions do not dominate merely because their component counts and variances differ. The attention score remains invariant; the change is how its inputs are balanced.

Suppose a type-one block has RMS $$0.1$$ and a type-two block has RMS $$10$$. Normalizing each independently makes both blocks order one, amplifying the former by a factor of ten and shrinking the latter by a factor of ten before they are coupled. That operation is equivariant, but it radically changes their relative scale. A shared higher-degree statistic retains more information about that balance. Attention re-normalization applies the same concern to scalar logits created after mixing several degrees: their variance should be controlled without allowing the number or scale of high-degree components to dictate the softmax temperature.

Results are grouped by training set, and several EquiformerV2 sizes expose how maximum degree and parameter count change throughput and accuracy." %}

The benchmark table establishes the OC20 accuracy claim; the two scatter plots establish the cost claim. EquiformerV2's red-star configurations occupy a better force-MAE frontier than the comparison models in the reported setting. The deck's final phrase—good trade-off between computational cost and accuracy—is therefore more precise than simply calling the model larger or more expressive.

The several EquiformerV2 rows also prevent an easy but wrong reading of the result. Increasing $$L_{\max}$$ changes angular resolution, parameter count, throughput, and the behavior of the activation and normalization simultaneously. The table therefore reports configurations rather than isolating one causal variable. What the architecture demonstrates is that high-degree attention can be made computationally viable and empirically useful; it does not imply that the largest available degree is automatically optimal for every dataset or budget.

## The surrounding design space

The lecture closes by locating these five models among related approaches. LieConv interprets continuous equivariant layers through Lie-group convolution. e3nn supplies the reusable irreducible representations and tensor-product primitives behind many Euclidean-equivariant models. Allegro learns local equivariant representations for scalable atomistic dynamics. BOTNet connects atom-centered potentials to body-ordered tensor networks and helps expose the design space that MACE occupies.

SCN is the spherical-channel predecessor of eSCN; its faster spherical processing is not exactly equivariant in the same sense as the later aligned-axis construction. EQGAT develops equivariant graph attention for molecular property prediction. The Equivariant Transformer similarly adapts attention to neural molecular potentials. These neighboring methods matter because “equivariant Transformer” and “body-ordered potential” each describe a family, not one fixed layer.

The lecture's five-model sequence can now be read as one design ledger:

<div class="table-responsive" markdown="1">

| Model | Part of the pipeline changed | Main gain | Main cost or constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| SE(3)-Transformer | invariant coefficient on TFN values | selective neighbors | TFN-like tensor products remain |
| Equiformer | full attention/FFN/normalization block | practical deep Transformer | dense typed processing is still expensive |
| MACE | node-wise products of aggregated features | explicit higher body order at shallow depth | coupling paths grow with correlation order |
| eSCN | edge-aligned angular contraction | exact $$SO(3)\to SO(2)$$ cost reduction | rotations and high-degree channel mixing remain |
| EquiformerV2 | eSCN plus high-degree activation and normalization | better high-degree accuracy--cost frontier | sampled $$S^2$$ nonlinearities introduce numerical approximation |

</div>

This ledger is the real storyline of the deck. Attention decides **which edge matters**. Higher-order products decide **which neighbor correlations are available within a layer**. Axis alignment decides **how cheaply those typed interactions can be computed**. Spherical activation and separable normalization decide **whether high-degree features remain useful inside a deep Transformer**. The models share one symmetry language, but their gains come from different interventions in the computation.

Three quantities should therefore remain separate when comparing them. The maximum representation degree $$L_{\max}$$ controls the angular types that a hidden state can carry. MACE's correlation order $$\nu$$ controls how many aggregated neighborhood factors are multiplied locally. Message-passing depth controls how often information is transported and recombined across the graph. A model can be high degree but low body order, high body order but shallow, or deep while using only low-degree features. Equiformer, MACE, and eSCN improve different coordinates of this design space; EquiformerV2 combines several of them. That separation is what turns the lecture from a catalog of model names into a reusable way to read the next equivariant architecture.
